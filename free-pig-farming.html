Section 1 — Understanding Pig Farming: The Facts

1.1 Why Pigs? (The Business Case)

FactorDetail
Fast growthA piglet reaches market weight (80–100 kg) in 6–8 months.
Flexible dietPigs eat almost anything — kitchen waste, crop residues, farm by-products — reducing feed costs.
Large littersA sow produces 10–14 piglets per litter, twice per year = 20–28 piglets annually.
Rising demandPork consumption in Africa is growing ~4% per year (FAO data). Hotels, restaurants, butcheries and households all buy.
Premium pricingIn most African markets, pork sells for more per kg than chicken or beef, because supply is lower.

Section 3 — The 12-Week Daily Lesson Plan

Each week has 5 Classroom days (Mon–Fri) and 2 Practical days (Sat–Sun). Spend 30–45 minutes per day.

Week 1 — Setup & Planning

Mon (Class): Why pig farming beats chicken for long-term wealth. Pigs live 10–15 years. One sow can produce 200+ piglets in her lifetime.

Tue (Class): Choosing your breed. Compare Large White vs Local vs Crossbreed. Crossbreed wins for beginners.

Wed (Class): Understanding the market. Visit 2 local butcheries. Ask: How much is pork per kg? Where do you buy from? How many kg do you sell per week?

Thu (Class): Building a pigsty — the 3 rules: dry floor, good airflow, shade from the sun. Pigs cannot sweat — they overheat easily.

Fri (Class): Calculating your start-up cost using the Price Check Formula.

Sat (Practical): Build a simple pigsty. Minimum size 2m × 3m for 5 weaners. Use local materials — mud bricks, thatch roof, raised floor.

Sun (Practical): Visit a local breeder. Inspect 5 piglets. Check: bright eyes, clean skin, active movement, no coughing.

Week 2 — Bringing Piglets Home

Mon (Class): Transporting piglets safely. Use a ventilated truck or cart. Do not transport in midday heat — pigs die from heatstroke.

Tue (Class): The first 48 hours. Provide clean water FIRST, then feed. Stress from transport lowers immunity — add electrolytes to water.

Wed (Class): Quarantine rules. Keep new piglets separate from any existing pigs for 14 days.

Thu (Class): Signs of a healthy piglet: shiny coat, upright ears, good appetite, firm droppings. Signs of sickness: coughing, diarrhoea, loss of appetite, dull eyes.

Fri (Class): Record-keeping. Start a simple notebook: date, weight, feed given, water given, any sickness, temperature.

Sat (Practical): Bring piglets home. Set up water troughs (1 per 3 pigs). Add electrolytes (1 tsp salt + 1 tsp sugar per 5L).

Sun (Practical): Weigh each piglet. Record starting weights. Take photos.

Week 3 — Feeding & Nutrition

Mon (Class): Pig digestive system facts. Pigs are monogastric (one stomach) like humans. They cannot digest grass. They need grains + protein.

Tue (Class): Commercial feed vs homemade feed. Commercial = $0.40/kg. Homemade = $0.20/kg. Homemade saves 50% but must be balanced.

Wed (Class): The iron-clad homemade feed formula (see Section 4).

Thu (Class): How much to feed. Weaners (10–25 kg): 1 kg/day. Growers (25–60 kg): 2.5 kg/day. Finishers (60–100 kg): 3.5 kg/day.

Fri (Class): Feeding schedule. Feed 2× daily: 7 am and 4 pm. Pigs gain faster with 2 meals than 1 big meal.

Sat (Practical): Mix your first 25 kg batch of homemade feed. Weigh every ingredient. Do not guess.

Sun (Practical): Feed the morning and evening meal. Observe which piglets eat fastest — fast eaters are healthy. Slow eaters may be sick.

Week 4 — Water, Health & Vaccines

Mon (Class): Water facts. Pigs drink 3–5 litres of water per kg of feed. A 50 kg pig needs 10–15 litres per day. No water = no growth.

Tue (Class): Common diseases in African pigs: African Swine Fever (ASF), Foot-and-Mouth Disease, Swine Flu, Internal Parasites.

Wed (Class): African Swine Fever — the #1 killer. No vaccine. No cure. 90–100% death rate. Spread by ticks, wild pigs, contaminated feed and human clothing. Prevention is the only option.

Thu (Class): Vaccination schedule. Deworm at 8, 12 and 16 weeks. Vaccinate against Swine Erysipelas, Leptospirosis, Foot-and-Mouth.

Fri (Class): Biosecurity rules. No visitors in the pigsty. Change boots before entering. Wash hands. Do not feed raw meat or kitchen waste from unknown sources. Fence to keep out wild pigs and dogs.

Sat (Practical): Set up a footbath at the pigsty entrance (disinfectant + water). Install fencing if not already done.

Sun (Practical): Deworm all piglets. Buy dewormer from the vet (Ivermectin or Levamisole). Dose: 1 ml per 10 kg body weight.

Week 5 — Housing & Environment

Mon (Class): Temperature facts. Pigs are comfortable at 18–22°C. Above 30°C they stop eating and lose weight. Below 10°C they burn energy to stay warm.

Tue (Class): Cooling systems for hot climates. Wet the floor at midday. Provide wallows (mud pits). Plant shade trees near the pigsty.

Wed (Class): Bedding materials. Use dry grass, straw, wood shavings or rice husks. Change weekly.

Thu (Class): Waste management. Pig manure is gold. One pig produces 2–3 kg of manure per day. Compost for 3 months = organic fertiliser. Or build a biogas digester = free cooking gas.

Fri (Class): Space requirements. Weaners: 0.5 m² per pig. Growers: 1 m² per pig. Finishers: 1.5 m² per pig. Sows: 2.5 m².

Sat (Practical): Build a wallow — 1m × 2m × 0.3m deep. Fill with water + clay soil.

Sun (Practical): Clean the pigsty thoroughly. Remove old bedding. Wash floors with disinfectant. Add fresh bedding.

Week 6 — Growth Monitoring & Records

Mon (Class): Weighing pigs. Use a scale or heart-girth tape. Formula: Weight (kg) = (Heart Girth cm)² × Length cm ÷ 400.

Tue (Class): Growth targets. A healthy pig should gain 600–800 g per day from weaning to market. If below 500 g/day, check feed quality, water or disease.

Wed (Class): Feed Conversion Ratio (FCR). Target FCR = 3:1. If FCR is 5:1, your feed is wrong or pigs are sick.

Thu (Class): Cost per kg of pork. Total feed cost ÷ total weight gain = cost per kg. If pork sells at $3/kg and cost is $1.50/kg, profit is $1.50/kg.

Fri (Class): When to sell. Sell at 80–100 kg live weight (6–7 months old). Do not overfeed beyond 100 kg.

Sat (Practical): Weigh all pigs. Record weights. Calculate average daily gain since Week 2.

Sun (Practical): Calculate your FCR. Total feed given ÷ total weight gained. Below 3.5? Good. Above 4? Fix feed.

Week 7 — Breeding Basics (For Those With a Sow)

Mon (Class): Pig reproductive facts. Sows reach maturity at 8 months. Gestation = 3 months, 3 weeks, 3 days (114 days). A sow can have 2 litters per year.

Tue (Class): Signs of heat (estrus). Sow becomes restless, mounts other pigs, swollen vulva, stands still when pressed on the back (standing heat). Heat lasts 2–3 days. Best time to breed: Day 2 of heat.

Wed (Class): Natural mating vs Artificial Insemination (AI). Natural = put boar with sow for 2 days. AI = buy semen from vet ($5–$10 per dose). AI is safer and uses superior genetics.

Thu (Class): Pregnancy care. Increase feed by 20% in the last month. Provide a clean, quiet environment.

Fri (Class): Farrowing preparation. Build a farrowing crate or pen 1 week before the due date. Provide clean straw. Have clean towels and iodine ready.

Sat (Practical): If you have a sow, check for signs of heat. Record the date.

Sun (Practical): Prepare the farrowing area (if sow is pregnant). Clean and disinfect thoroughly.

Week 8 — Piglet Care & Weaning

Mon (Class): Newborn piglet facts. Piglets are born with no immunity. They must drink colostrum within 2 hours of birth.

Tue (Class): Iron injection. Piglets are born iron-deficient. Give iron injection (1–2 ml) at 1–3 days old.

Wed (Class): Teeth clipping and tail docking. Clip needle teeth at 1 day old. Dock tails at 2–3 days to prevent tail biting (optional but recommended).

Thu (Class): Castration. Castrate male piglets at 7–14 days old. Use a clean, sharp blade and disinfectant.

Fri (Class): Weaning. Wean piglets at 6–8 weeks old (8–12 kg). Reduce sow contact over 3 days. Give starter feed (20% protein) for 2 weeks, then switch to grower feed (18% protein).

Sat (Practical): If piglets were born, ensure all drink colostrum. Mark piglets with ear tags or paint.

Sun (Practical): If piglets are 7 days old, give iron injection and clip teeth.

Week 9 — Disease Prevention & Biosecurity

Mon (Class): African Swine Fever — detailed prevention. ASF virus lives in pork meat for months. Never feed pigs raw pork or swill from restaurants. Cook all kitchen waste at 100°C for 30 minutes before feeding.

Tue (Class): External parasites (mange, lice). Signs: scratching, rough coat, skin sores. Treatment: Ivermectin injection or pour-on. Repeat every 3 months.

Wed (Class): Internal parasites (roundworms, tapeworms). Signs: pot belly, poor growth, diarrhoea. Prevention: deworm every 8 weeks.

Thu (Class): Respiratory diseases (pneumonia). Signs: coughing, fever, difficulty breathing. Cause: wet bedding, poor ventilation, overcrowding.

Fri (Class): Skin diseases (erysipelas). Signs: diamond-shaped red patches, fever, lameness. Vaccinate at 8 weeks and booster at 16 weeks.

Sat (Practical): Inspect every pig for parasites, skin sores or coughing. Treat any issues immediately.

Sun (Practical): Deep-clean one section of the pigsty. Remove all manure. Disinfect with lime wash.

Week 10 — Processing & Slaughter Preparation

Mon (Class): Pre-slaughter care. Stop feeding 12–18 hours before transport (but give water). This empties the gut and improves meat quality.

Tue (Class): Transport to abattoir. Use a clean, ventilated truck. Do not overcrowd. Transport in early morning or evening (cooler).

Wed (Class): Humane slaughter methods. Stunning (electrical or captive bolt) renders the pig unconscious before bleeding. Required by law in most countries.

Thu (Class): Pork cuts and their value. Premium cuts (loin, ham, belly) sell for 2× the price of stewing meat. Learn to butcher or partner with a trained butcher.

Fri (Class): Cold chain. Pork must be chilled to 4°C within 2 hours of slaughter. Without refrigeration, pork spoils in 4–6 hours in African heat.

Sat (Practical): If a pig is ready, arrange transport to the nearest abattoir or arrange a mobile slaughter service.

Sun (Practical): Visit a local abattoir. Observe the process. Ask about costs and requirements.

Week 11 — Preservation & Value-Added Products

Mon (Class): Pork smoking. Cold smoking (20–30°C) for 12–24 hours preserves pork for 2–4 weeks. Hot smoking (70–80°C) cooks and preserves for 1–2 weeks. Use hardwood (oak, hickory, mopane) — never softwood.

Tue (Class): Salt curing. Rub pork with salt (3–5% of meat weight). Store in a cool place for 7–14 days. Rinse and dry. Shelf life: 3–6 months without refrigeration.

Wed (Class): Sausage making. Grind pork (70% lean, 30% fat). Add salt, spices and preservative (nitrite). Stuff into casings. Smoke or freeze.

Thu (Class): Bacon production. Cure pork belly with salt, sugar and spices for 7 days. Smoke at 60°C for 6–8 hours. Slice and package.

Fri (Class): Freezing. Pork can be frozen at −18°C for 6–12 months. Wrap tightly in plastic. Label with date.

Sat (Practical): If you have slaughtered a pig, smoke 5 kg of pork using a simple drum smoker.

Sun (Practical): Make 2 kg of fresh sausages. Grind, season, stuff and freeze.

Week 12 — Sales, Marketing & Scaling

Mon (Class): Pricing your pork. Calculate: (Total cost ÷ total kg produced) + 30% profit margin. Example: cost = $1.50/kg, sell at $2.00/kg.

Tue (Class): Local sales channels. Butcheries, restaurants, hotels, schools, churches, open markets, direct to households.

Wed (Class): Digital sales. List on Jumia, Konga, Takealot. Use WhatsApp Business for orders. Post on Facebook Marketplace.

Thu (Class): Bulk contracts. Approach a school or hotel: "I can supply 50 kg of pork every Friday." Contracts = guaranteed income.

Fri (Class): Scaling up. Reinvest 50% of profits. Buy 2 more sows. Build a second pigsty. Hire a helper when you reach 20+ pigs.

Sat (Practical): Visit 3 potential buyers (butchery, restaurant, hotel). Leave samples. Collect contact numbers.

Sun (Practical): Create a simple WhatsApp Business profile. Take photos of your pigs. Post: "Fresh pork available — farm to table."

9.1 Simple Drum Smoker Build ($15)

Materials: 1 × 200 L metal drum (cut in half lengthwise), 3 × metal rods, 1 × metal mesh, hardwood (oak, mopane, hickory), bricks.

  1. Cut drum in half. Place on bricks, open side up.
  2. Install 3 metal rods across the drum at different heights.
  3. Lay metal mesh on rods to create 3 racks.
  4. Light a small fire of hardwood in the bottom.
  5. Place pork on racks. Cover with the other half of the drum.
  6. Smoke at 60–80°C for 8–12 hours.
  7. Check every 2 hours. Add wood as needed.

Winter Hacks — Cool Highlands (Zimbabwe, Kenya)

  • Add 10% more feed in winter — pigs burn energy to stay warm.
  • Close one side of the pigsty with sacks or plastic sheeting at night.
  • Provide deep bedding (30 cm+) for pigs to burrow into.
  • Do not let water freeze — use warm water in mornings.

Summer Hacks — Hot Climates (Nigeria, Ghana, Northern Kenya)

  • Feed at 6 am and 6 pm — avoid midday heat.
  • Wet the floor 3× per day.
  • Ensure the wallow is full of mud and water.
  • Plant fast-growing shade trees (banana, pawpaw) near the pigsty.

Section 14 — The No-Surprises Reality Check

Before you spend your first dollar, read this out loud.

"I understand that pig farming can make me a profit. I also understand that pig farming can make me lose money if I do not follow the rules.

The rules are:

  1. I will check the price of piglets, feed and vaccines in MY town BEFORE I buy anything. I will call 3 suppliers and take the average.
  2. I will build a proper pigsty with dry floors, good ventilation and shade. I will not keep pigs in a muddy hole.
  3. I will feed my pigs a balanced diet. I will weigh ingredients. I will not guess. I will not feed mouldy maize.
  4. I will give my pigs clean water every single day. No exceptions.
  5. I will deworm my pigs every 8 weeks. I will vaccinate on schedule.
  6. I will NEVER feed raw pork or uncooked swill. African Swine Fever has no cure. One mistake can wipe out my entire farm.
  7. I will keep records. I will weigh my pigs weekly. I will know if I am making a profit or a loss.
  8. I will sell at the right weight (80–100 kg). I will not get greedy and overfeed. I will not panic-sell early.
  9. I will reinvest 50% of my profits. I will not spend everything.
  10. I will ask for help when I don't know something. I will call the vet. I will talk to other farmers. I will not guess when a pig is sick.

If I follow these 10 rules, I have a high chance of success. If I ignore them, I have a high chance of failure.

The choice is mine. The work is mine. The profit is mine."
