SECTION 1: UNDERSTANDING PIG FARMING — THE FACTS

1.1 WHY PIGS? (The Business Case)
- Pigs grow FAST: A piglet reaches market weight (80-100kg) in 6-8 months.
- Pigs eat ALMOST ANYTHING: Kitchen waste, crop residues, farm by-products — reducing feed costs.
- Pigs have LARGE LITTERS: A sow can produce 10-14 piglets per litter, 2 times per year = 20-28 piglets annually.
- Pork DEMAND IS HIGH: Across Africa, pork consumption is growing 4% per year (FAO data).
- Pork SELLS AT A PREMIUM: In most African markets, pork sells for MORE per kg than chicken or beef.

1.2 THE BREEDS YOU NEED TO KNOW
- Large White: Fast growth, 300-400kg adult weight. Best for commercial meat production.
- Landrace: Fast growth, 250-350kg. Lean meat, good mothers.
- Duroc: Very fast growth, 300-400kg. Fast growth, red meat color.
- Hampshire: Fast growth, 250-300kg. Lean meat, good for bacon.
- Local African breeds: Slow growth, 80-150kg. Hardier, disease-resistant.
- Crossbreeds (F1): Fast growth, 200-300kg. Best for small-scale farms.

IRON-CLAD FACT: Crossbreeds (Large White x Local) grow 30% faster than pure local breeds and are 40% more resistant to local diseases. This is the best choice for a beginner with limited capital.

1.3 STARTING NUMBERS (What a Beginner Should Buy)
Option A: The Fast Start (Buy Weaners)
- Buy 5 weaner piglets (8 weeks old, 10-15kg each)
- Raise them for 5-6 months to 80-100kg
- Sell all 5 as pork
- Cost: Lower upfront. Risk: Lower. Profit: Medium.

Option B: The Breeder Start (Buy a Sow + Boar)
- Buy 1 pregnant sow + 1 boar
- Sow produces 10-14 piglets in 3-4 months
- Raise piglets to weaning (8 weeks), keep 2 females as future breeders, sell the rest
- Cost: Higher upfront. Risk: Higher. Profit: Highest long-term.

IRON-CLAD RECOMMENDATION FOR BEGINNERS: Start with Option A (5 weaners). Learn the business. Make your first profit. Then upgrade to Option B.

SECTION 3: THE 12-WEEK DAILY LESSON PLAN

How to use this: Each week has 5 Classroom days (Mon-Fri) and 2 Practical days (Sat-Sun). Spend 30-45 minutes per day.

WEEK 1: SETUP & PLANNING
Mon (Class): Why pig farming beats chicken for long-term wealth. Pigs live 10-15 years. One sow can produce 200+ piglets in her lifetime.
Tue (Class): Choosing your breed. Compare Large White vs Local vs Crossbreed. Crossbreed wins for beginners.
Wed (Class): Understanding the market. Visit 2 local butcheries. Ask: How much is pork per kg? Where do you buy from? How many kg do you sell per week?
Thu (Class): Building a pigsty — the 3 rules: Dry floor, good airflow, shade from sun. Pigs cannot sweat — they overheat easily.
Fri (Class): Calculating your start-up cost using the Price Check Formula.
Sat (Practical): Build a simple pigsty. Minimum size: 2m x 3m for 5 weaners. Use local materials: Mud bricks, thatch roof, raised floor.
Sun (Practical): Visit a local breeder. Inspect 5 piglets. Check: bright eyes, clean skin, active movement, no coughing.

WEEK 2: BRINGING PIGLETS HOME
Mon (Class): Transporting piglets safely. Use a ventilated truck or cart. Do not transport in midday heat — pigs die from heatstroke.
Tue (Class): The first 48 hours. Provide clean water FIRST, then feed. Stress from transport lowers immunity — add electrolytes to water.
Wed (Class): Quarantine rules. Keep new piglets separate from any existing pigs for 14 days.
Thu (Class): Signs of a healthy piglet: Shiny coat, upright ears, good appetite, firm droppings. Signs of sickness: Coughing, diarrhea, loss of appetite, dull eyes.
Fri (Class): Record-keeping. Start a simple notebook: Date, weight, feed given, water given, any sickness, temperature.
Sat (Practical): Bring piglets home. Set up water troughs (1 trough per 3 pigs). Add electrolytes (1 teaspoon salt + 1 teaspoon sugar per 5L).
Sun (Practical): Weigh each piglet. Record starting weights. Take photos.

WEEK 3: FEEDING & NUTRITION
Mon (Class): Pig digestive system facts. Pigs are MONOGASTRIC (one stomach) like humans. They cannot digest grass. They need grains + protein.
Tue (Class): Commercial feed vs homemade feed. Commercial = $0.40/kg. Homemade = $0.20/kg. Homemade saves 50% but must be balanced.
Wed (Class): The iron-clad homemade feed formula (see Section 4).
Thu (Class): How much to feed. Weaners (10-25kg): 1kg/day. Growers (25-60kg): 2.5kg/day. Finishers (60-100kg): 3.5kg/day.
Fri (Class): Feeding schedule. Feed 2x daily: 7am and 4pm. Pigs gain weight faster with 2 meals than 1 big meal.
Sat (Practical): Mix your first 25kg batch of homemade feed. Weigh every ingredient. Do not guess.
Sun (Practical): Feed the morning and evening meal. Observe which piglets eat fastest — fast eaters are healthy. Slow eaters may be sick.

WEEK 4: WATER, HEALTH & VACCINES
Mon (Class): Water facts. Pigs drink 3-5 liters of water per kg of feed. A 50kg pig needs 10-15 liters per day. NO WATER = NO GROWTH.
Tue (Class): Common diseases in African pigs: African Swine Fever (ASF), Foot-and-Mouth Disease, Swine Flu, Internal Parasites.
Wed (Class): African Swine Fever (ASF) — THE #1 KILLER. No vaccine. No cure. 90-100% death rate. Spread by ticks, wild pigs, contaminated feed, and human clothing. Prevention is the ONLY option.
Thu (Class): Vaccination schedule. Deworm at 8 weeks, 12 weeks, and 16 weeks. Vaccinate against: Swine Erysipelas, Leptospirosis, Foot-and-Mouth.
Fri (Class): Biosecurity rules. No visitors in pigsty. Change boots before entering. Wash hands. Do not feed raw meat or kitchen waste from unknown sources. Fence to keep out wild pigs and dogs.
Sat (Practical): Set up footbath at pigsty entrance (disinfectant + water). Install fence if not already done.
Sun (Practical): Deworm all piglets. Buy dewormer from vet (e.g., Ivermectin or Levamisole). Dose = 1ml per 10kg body weight.

WEEK 5: HOUSING & ENVIRONMENT
Mon (Class): Temperature facts. Pigs are comfortable at 18-22C. Above 30C, they stop eating and lose weight. Below 10C, they burn energy to stay warm.
Tue (Class): Cooling systems for hot climates. Wet the floor at midday. Provide wallows (mud pits). Plant shade trees near the pigsty.
Wed (Class): Bedding materials. Use dry grass, straw, wood shavings, or rice husks. Change weekly.
Thu (Class): Waste management. Pig manure is GOLD. One pig produces 2-3kg of manure per day. Compost for 3 months = organic fertilizer. Or build a biogas digester = free cooking gas.
Fri (Class): Space requirements. Weaners: 0.5m2 per pig. Growers: 1m2 per pig. Finishers: 1.5m2 per pig. Sows: 2.5m2.
Sat (Practical): Build a wallow 1m x 2m x 0.3m deep. Fill with water + clay soil.
Sun (Practical): Clean the pigsty thoroughly. Remove all old bedding. Wash floors with disinfectant. Add fresh bedding.

WEEK 6: GROWTH MONITORING & RECORDS
Mon (Class): Weighing pigs. Use a scale or heart girth tape. Formula: Weight (kg) = (Heart Girth in cm)^2 x Length (cm) / 400.
Tue (Class): Growth targets. A healthy pig should gain 600-800g per day from weaning to market. If below 500g/day, check feed quality, water, or disease.
Wed (Class): Feed conversion ratio (FCR). Target FCR = 3:1. If FCR is 5:1, your feed is wrong or pigs are sick.
Thu (Class): Cost per kg of pork. Total feed cost / total weight gain = cost per kg. If pork sells at $3/kg and cost is $1.50/kg, profit is $1.50/kg.
Fri (Class): When to sell. Sell at 80-100kg live weight (6-7 months old). Do not overfeed beyond 100kg.
Sat (Practical): Weigh all pigs. Record weights. Calculate average daily gain since Week 2.
Sun (Practical): Calculate your FCR. Total feed given / total weight gained. Below 3.5? Good. Above 4? Fix feed.

WEEK 7: BREEDING BASICS (For Those Who Bought a Sow)
Mon (Class): Pig reproductive facts. Sows reach maturity at 8 months. Gestation = 3 months, 3 weeks, 3 days (114 days). A sow can have 2 litters per year.
Tue (Class): Signs of heat (estrus). Sow becomes restless, mounts other pigs, swollen vulva, stands still when pressed on back (standing heat). Heat lasts 2-3 days. Best time to breed: Day 2 of heat.
Wed (Class): Natural mating vs Artificial Insemination (AI). Natural = put boar with sow for 2 days. AI = buy semen from vet ($5-$10 per dose). AI is safer and uses superior genetics.
Thu (Class): Pregnancy care. Increase feed by 20% in last month. Provide clean, quiet environment.
Fri (Class): Farrowing preparation. Build a farrowing crate or pen 1 week before due date. Provide clean straw. Have clean towels and iodine ready.
Sat (Practical): If you have a sow, check for signs of heat. Record the date.
Sun (Practical): Prepare farrowing area (if sow is pregnant). Clean and disinfect thoroughly.

WEEK 8: PIGLET CARE & WEANING
Mon (Class): Newborn piglet facts. Piglets are born with NO immunity. They MUST drink colostrum within 2 hours of birth.
Tue (Class): Iron injection. Piglets are born iron-deficient. Give iron injection (1-2ml) at 1-3 days old.
Wed (Class): Teeth clipping and tail docking. Clip needle teeth at 1 day old. Dock tails at 2-3 days to prevent tail biting (optional but recommended).
Thu (Class): Castration. Castrate male piglets at 7-14 days old. Use clean, sharp blade and disinfectant.
Fri (Class): Weaning. Wean piglets at 6-8 weeks old (8-12kg). Reduce sow contact over 3 days. Give starter feed (high protein, 20%) for 2 weeks, then switch to grower feed (18% protein).
Sat (Practical): If piglets were born, ensure all drink colostrum. Mark piglets with ear tags or paint.
Sun (Practical): If piglets are 7 days old, give iron injection and clip teeth.

WEEK 9: DISEASE PREVENTION & BIOSECURITY
Mon (Class): African Swine Fever — detailed prevention. ASF virus lives in pork meat for months. NEVER feed pigs raw pork or swill from restaurants. Cook all kitchen waste at 100C for 30 minutes before feeding.
Tue (Class): External parasites (mange, lice). Signs: Scratching, rough coat, skin sores. Treatment: Ivermectin injection or pour-on. Repeat every 3 months.
Wed (Class): Internal parasites (roundworms, tapeworms). Signs: Pot belly, poor growth, diarrhea. Prevention: Deworm every 8 weeks.
Thu (Class): Respiratory diseases (pneumonia). Signs: Coughing, fever, difficulty breathing. Cause: Wet bedding, poor ventilation, overcrowding.
Fri (Class): Skin diseases (erysipelas). Signs: Diamond-shaped red patches, fever, lameness. Vaccinate at 8 weeks and booster at 16 weeks.
Sat (Practical): Inspect every pig for parasites, skin sores, or coughing. Treat any issues immediately.
Sun (Practical): Deep clean one section of the pigsty. Remove all manure. Disinfect with lime wash.

WEEK 10: PROCESSING & SLAUGHTER PREPARATION
Mon (Class): Pre-slaughter care. Stop feeding 12-18 hours before transport (but give water). This empties the gut and improves meat quality.
Tue (Class): Transport to abattoir. Use clean, ventilated truck. Do not overcrowd. Transport in early morning or evening (cooler).
Wed (Class): Humane slaughter methods. Stunning (electrical or captive bolt) renders pig unconscious before bleeding. Required by law in most countries.
Thu (Class): Pork cuts and their value. Premium cuts (loin, ham, belly) sell for 2x the price of stewing meat. Learn to butcher or partner with a trained butcher.
Fri (Class): Cold chain. Pork must be chilled to 4C within 2 hours of slaughter. Without refrigeration, pork spoils in 4-6 hours in African heat.
Sat (Practical): If a pig is ready, arrange transport to the nearest abattoir or arrange a mobile slaughter service.
Sun (Practical): Visit a local abattoir. Observe the process. Ask about costs and requirements.

WEEK 11: PRESERVATION & VALUE-ADDED PRODUCTS
Mon (Class): Pork smoking. Cold smoking (20-30C) for 12-24 hours preserves pork for 2-4 weeks. Hot smoking (70-80C) cooks and preserves for 1-2 weeks. Use hardwood (oak, hickory, mopane) — NOT softwood.
Tue (Class): Salt curing. Rub pork with salt (3-5% of meat weight). Store in cool place for 7-14 days. Rinse and dry. Shelf life: 3-6 months without refrigeration.
Wed (Class): Sausage making. Grind pork (70% lean, 30% fat). Add salt, spices, and preservative (nitrite). Stuff into casings. Smoke or freeze.
Thu (Class): Bacon production. Cure pork belly with salt, sugar, and spices for 7 days. Smoke at 60C for 6-8 hours. Slice and package.
Fri (Class): Freezing. Pork can be frozen at -18C for 6-12 months. Wrap tightly in plastic. Label with date.
Sat (Practical): If you have slaughtered a pig, smoke 5kg of pork using a simple drum smoker.
Sun (Practical): Make 2kg of fresh sausages. Grind, season, stuff, and freeze.

WEEK 12: SALES, MARKETING & SCALING
Mon (Class): Pricing your pork. Calculate: (Total cost / total kg produced) + 30% profit margin. Example: Cost = $1.50/kg. Sell at $2.00/kg.
Tue (Class): Local sales channels. Butcheries, restaurants, hotels, schools, churches, open markets, direct to households.
Wed (Class): Digital sales. List on Jumia, Konga, Takealot. Use WhatsApp Business for orders. Post on Facebook Marketplace.
Thu (Class): Bulk contracts. Approach a school or hotel: I can supply 50kg of pork every Friday. Contracts = guaranteed income.
Fri (Class): Scaling up. Reinvest 50% of profits. Buy 2 more sows. Build a second pigsty. Hire a helper when you reach 20+ pigs.
Sat (Practical): Visit 3 potential buyers (butchery, restaurant, hotel). Leave samples. Collect contact numbers.
Sun (Practical): Create a simple WhatsApp Business profile. Take photos of your pigs. Post: Fresh pork available — farm to table.

SECTION 5: FACTORS THAT CAUSE NEGATIVE RESULTS

Factor / What Happens / Prevention
African Swine Fever (ASF): 90-100% death rate in 3-10 days. No vaccine. No cure. Prevention: NO raw pork/swill. Biosecurity fence. Disinfect everything.
Overcrowding: Stress, fighting, tail biting, disease spread, poor growth. Prevention: 0.5m2 per weaner, 1.5m2 per finisher.
Poor ventilation: Ammonia buildup, pneumonia, eye infections, coughing. Prevention: Open sides, roof ventilation gap.
Wet bedding / floors: Foot rot, pneumonia, parasites. Prevention: Dry bedding changed weekly. Raised floors.
Heat stress (above 30C): Pigs stop eating, lose weight, can die. Prevention: Shade, wallows, wet floors at midday.
Cold stress (below 10C): Pigs burn energy to stay warm, grow 20% slower. Prevention: Closed sides at night, deep bedding.
Moldy feed: Aflatoxin poisoning — liver failure, death in 2-4 weeks. Prevention: Smell maize before mixing. Discard moldy.
No water: Pigs stop eating, dehydration, death in 2-3 days. Prevention: 3-5L water per kg feed. Clean daily.
Skipping deworming: Worms eat 30% of feed — pigs look fat but don't gain weight. Prevention: Deworm every 8 weeks without fail.
Inbreeding: Weak piglets, deformities, poor growth, higher disease. Prevention: Replace boar every 2 years.
Late castration: Stress, poor meat quality, boar taint in meat. Prevention: Castrate at 7-14 days old.
Selling underweight: Buyers pay less per kg. You lose profit. Prevention: Sell at 80-100kg live weight minimum.
No record-keeping: You don't know if you're profitable or losing money. Prevention: Weigh weekly. Record everything.

6.1 BASIC PIGSTY DESIGN (For 5 Weaners)
Dimensions: 3m (length) x 2m (width) x 2m (height)
Floor: Raised 0.5m off ground (prevents flooding, parasites, easier cleaning)

9.1 SIMPLE DRUM SMOKER BUILD ($15)
Materials: 1 x 200L metal drum (cut in half lengthwise), 3 x metal rods (for racks), 1 x metal mesh (for racks), hardwood (oak, mopane, hickory), bricks (for support).

Method:
1. Cut drum in half. Place on bricks, open side up.
2. Install 3 metal rods across the drum at different heights.
3. Lay metal mesh on rods to create 3 racks.
4. Light a small fire of hardwood in the bottom.
5. Place pork on racks. Cover with the other half of the drum.
6. Smoke at 60-80C for 8-12 hours.
7. Check every 2 hours. Add wood as needed.

SECTION 13: TEMPERATURE & ENVIRONMENT CHART

Pig Type / Comfortable Temp / Danger Zone / What To Do
Newborn piglets: 30-32C. Below 28C: heat lamp, deep bedding, huddle with sow.
Weaners (8 wks): 25-28C. Below 20C: closed sides at night. Above 32C: wallow, wet floor.
Growers (25-60kg): 20-25C. Below 15C: deep bedding, draft-free. Above 30C: shade, wallow, ventilation.
Finishers (60-100kg): 18-22C. Below 10C: closed pen, extra feed. Above 30C: wallow, early morning feed.
Pregnant sows: 18-22C. Below 10C: warm, dry farrowing area. Above 30C: cool water, shade.

WINTER HACK (Zimbabwe highlands, Kenya highlands):
- Add 10% more feed in winter — pigs burn energy to stay warm.
- Close one side of the pigsty with sacks or plastic sheeting at night.
- Provide deep bedding (30cm+) for pigs to burrow into.
- Do not let water freeze — use warm water in mornings.

SUMMER HACK (Nigeria, Ghana, Northern Kenya):
- Feed at 6am and 6pm — avoid midday heat.
- Wet the floor 3x per day.
- Ensure wallow is full of mud and water.
- Plant fast-growing shade trees (banana, pawpaw) near pigsty.

15.4 EMERGENCY SIGNS — CALL THE VET IMMEDIATELY
Pig not eating 24+ hours: Fever, infection, blockage. Isolate. Check temperature. Call vet.
Diarrhea (watery, bloody, or smelly): E. coli, parasites, ASF (if bloody). Isolate. Give electrolytes. Call vet IMMEDIATELY.
Coughing + fever: Pneumonia, flu. Isolate. Improve ventilation. Call vet.
Lameness + swollen joints: Arthritis, erysipelas. Isolate. Antibiotics. Call vet.
Skin turning purple/blue (ears, belly): AFRICAN SWINE FEVER. ISOLATE ALL PIGS. CALL VET + GOVERNMENT VET IMMEDIATELY. Do NOT move pigs. Quarantine farm.
Sudden death of multiple pigs: ASF, anthrax, poisoning. Do NOT touch carcass. Call government vet.
Abortion in sow: Brucellosis, stress, leptospirosis. Isolate sow. Call vet.

SECTION 17: GLOSSARY (Layman's Terms)

Weaner: A piglet that has stopped drinking mother's milk (8 weeks old).
Sow: A female pig that has given birth.
Boar: A male pig used for breeding.
Gilt: A young female pig that has not yet given birth.
Farrowing: Giving birth to piglets.
Dressing: The percentage of live weight that becomes saleable meat (usually 72% for pigs).
FCR: Feed Conversion Ratio — kg of feed needed to make 1kg of pork.
Biosecurity: Rules to keep diseases OUT of your farm.
Wallow: A mud pit where pigs cool down.
Colostrum: The first milk from a sow — full of antibodies for piglets.
Castration: Removing testicles from male piglets (prevents bad meat taste).
Deworming: Giving medicine to kill worms inside the pig.
Aflatoxin: Poison from moldy maize — kills pigs slowly.
ASF: African Swine Fever — the deadliest pig disease. No cure.
