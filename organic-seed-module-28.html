🎁 Reveal a strong answer

Tomato, pepper, amaranth, African eggplant, and kale sell for 70 to 120 dollars per kilogram. Vegetable seed is different because fruits must ripen fully, seed must often be fermented or extracted carefully, and isolation distances are larger. The three hardest are onion, carrot, and beetroot - they are biennial and take two seasons.

📖 Farai Masters Three Vegetables

Farai had grown cereal seed for three years. He was earning 900 dollars per hectare. He wanted to earn more.

His extension officer suggested vegetable seed. The numbers were 5 to 10 times higher per hectare.

Farai chose three crops to try in his first vegetable season: tomato, kale, and amaranth.

Tomato was the first lesson. Farai harvested ripe red tomatoes, cut them open, and dried the seed on a plate in the sun.

The seed stuck together. It was pale. His germination test showed 55 percent.

His extension officer explained. Tomato seed has a gel coating that prevents germination until it is removed. The traditional method is fermentation: mash the tomatoes in a bucket, add a little water, leave for 2 days. The gel dissolves. The good seed sinks. Pour off the floating pulp and bad seed. Wash the sunken seed. Then dry in the shade.

Farai tried again. Germination: 89 percent.

Kale was simpler. He let the pods dry on the plant. When 70 percent of the pods had turned light brown, he cut the whole plant at the base, tied it in a bundle, and hung it upside down in his shed. Ten days later, the pods had all split. The seed fell into a cloth he had tied under the bundle. 92 percent germination.

Amaranth was the hardest. The seed head is tiny, and the seed shatters at the slightest touch. Farai learned to harvest in the early morning while the dew was still on the plant - this stopped the seed from shattering. He cut the whole seed head, put it in a paper bag, and let it dry for a week before shaking the seed loose.

By the end of the season he had 8 kilograms of tomato seed, 12 kilograms of kale seed, and 15 kilograms of amaranth seed.

Revenue: tomato at 80 = 640 dollars. Kale at 30 = 360 dollars. Amaranth at 70 = 1,050 dollars. Total: 2,050 dollars from 0.6 hectares.

His cereal crop had earned him 900 dollars per hectare. His vegetable crop earned him 3,400 per hectare. The learning curve was steep but the numbers were 4 times higher.

💡 The Big Idea

Vegetable seed is worth the effort

Vegetable seed is difficult, but it pays 4 to 10 times more per hectare than cereal seed. The three main challenges are correct extraction (tomato fermentation), correct isolation (100+ metres for many crops), and correct drying (which takes precision). Master these three and vegetable seed is the most profitable crop a small African producer can grow.

📚 Three Deep Lessons

1

Tomato seed production in detail

Tomato seed production requires a fruit crop first. You do not harvest the fruit for eating - you let it fully ripen for seed. This is called "seed set." The tomatoes stay on the plant 2 to 3 weeks longer than market tomatoes.

Extraction method: cut the fruit, scoop out the seed and pulp into a plastic bucket. Add a little water. Leave for 24 to 48 hours in a warm place. The pulp ferments and the seed separates from the gel coating. Good seed sinks. Bad seed and pulp float. Pour off the floating material. Wash the sunken seed 3 times in clean water. Dry in the shade on a screen for 3 to 5 days. Do not dry in direct sun - the seed coat cracks.

Isolation distance: 100 metres from other tomato varieties. Yield: 20 to 40 kilograms of seed per hectare. Price: 60 to 90 dollars per kilogram.

2

Kale, cabbage, and brassica seed production

Brassica crops (kale, cabbage, broccoli, cauliflower) are biennial - they take two seasons. In the first season, the plant grows a head or leaves. In the second season, it flowers and sets seed.

In cool-climate areas (highland Zimbabwe, Kenya, Ethiopia), you can let the plant overwinter and flower the second season. In hot climates, you plant at the end of the cool season so flowering happens in the dry season.

Harvest: when 70 percent of the pods have turned light brown and are beginning to split, cut the whole plant at the base. Bundle and hang upside down in a dry shed for 10 days. The seed continues maturing in the bundle. When fully dry, beat the bundles on a tarp and collect the seed.

Isolation: 400 metres from other brassica varieties. Yield: 40 to 80 kilograms per hectare. Price: 20 to 40 dollars per kilogram.

3

Amaranth and traditional vegetables

Amaranth is the highest-value African vegetable seed. It sells at 70 to 100 dollars per kilogram. Traditional African vegetables - African eggplant, spider plant, jute mallow, African cabbage - also command premium prices of 60 to 120 dollars per kilogram.

Amaranth production: plant in the early rainy season. Thin to 30 cm spacing. Do not harvest leaves for eating - allow the seed head to form. When the seed head turns brown, harvest in early morning while dew is still on the plant. Cut whole heads. Bag them. Dry for a week. Rub the heads between your hands to release the seed. Sieve to remove debris.

Isolation: amaranth crosses with other amaranth species, but not with most other crops. 200 metres from other amaranth varieties is safe. Yield: 60 to 90 kilograms per hectare. Price: 70 to 100 dollars per kilogram.

🛠 Hands-On - Ferment 1 Kilogram of Tomato Seed

You need

  • 5 kilograms of fully ripe tomatoes
  • A plastic bucket
  • Water
  • A sieve or strainer
  • A screen or tray
  • A notebook
  1. Cut the tomatoes in half.
  2. Scoop the seed and pulp into a bucket.
  3. Add 1 cup of water. Stir.
  4. Leave in a warm place for 24 to 48 hours.
  5. Stir twice a day. The pulp will float.
  6. Pour off the floating pulp and bad seed.
  7. Wash the sunken seed 3 times in clean water.
  8. Spread on a screen and dry in the shade for 3 to 5 days.
  9. Test germination at day 14. It should be above 85 percent.
Why it works: Tomato fermentation is the single most important skill in vegetable seed production. Master it once and you have it forever.
