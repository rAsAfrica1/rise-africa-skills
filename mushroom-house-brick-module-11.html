Module 11 — Pest and Disease Management in the Mushroom House

Flies, mites and moulds — and the routine that keeps them out of a warm, wet, dark building.

Home › Mushroom House — Brick › Module 11

Learning outcomes

  • Identify the main insect pests, mites and diseases of cultivated mushrooms.
  • Monitor with sticky traps, visual inspection and crop records.
  • Apply integrated pest management rather than reaching for chemicals first.
  • Design the building and the schedule to make infestation unlikely.
  • Respond to an outbreak without spreading it across the house.

Flies are the number one commercial pest

A sciarid fly lays up to 200 eggs in a week. One generation takes 3–4 weeks in warm conditions. Two flies on a sticky trap in week 1 become hundreds by week 6. Monitor every week, and respond the same week you see a rise — not after mushrooms start coming out with maggots.

2. Mites

MiteSignDamage
Mushroom miteTiny white or translucent specks on substrate and myceliumChews mycelium; heavy infestations stop colonisation

Tarsonemid miteVery small, hard to see without magnificationSevere in commercial systems; destroys pinning

Mites spread by walking on contaminated tools, gloves, hands and clothing. Sanitation between bags and between rooms is the primary control. Some mites cling to flies and hitch a ride.

4. Integrated pest management

  1. Keep pests out. Insect screens on every opening; HEPA-filtered supply air; sealed doors; positive pressure in the growing room.
  2. Monitor. Sticky traps (yellow for flying pests, blue for thrips). One trap per 20 m² or one per shelf bay. Count weekly.
  3. Keep the room dry on the surface. No standing water, no wet floors, no condensation dripping. Fix leaks immediately.
  4. Remove spent substrate promptly. Spent bags attract flies, harbour moulds and become the next generation's nursery. Remove the day the third flush ends.
  5. Clean and disinfect between crops. Full empty, wash down, fog, dry, re-establish. No shortcuts.
  6. Quarantine suspect material. Any bag with a suspect spot is removed immediately — before you can see the spores.
  7. Use chemicals only as a last resort. Many are not registered for food use; residues fail inspection. Check local rules.

Why a weekly trap count works

Sciarid fly life cycle at 22 °C: egg 4 days, larva 8 days, pupa 5 days, adult 5–7 days. Total: 3 weeks.
If you catch 5 flies in week 1, by week 4 you could have 5 × 100 offspring × 50 % survival = 250 flies.
If you catch 20 flies in week 1 and do nothing, you have an outbreak by week 4.

Acting at 5 flies/trap is a nuisance job. Acting at 50 is a disaster recovery. Count and act weekly.

The door is the weak point

Even with a HEPA inlet, an open door for 30 seconds during harvest can let in a hundred flies. Train every worker: door closed when not in use; enter and exit quickly; do not stand in the doorway. A self-closing hinge costs very little and stops a very expensive problem.

7. Outbreak response

  1. Identify the pest or disease. Look at a sample under a hand lens or with a torch. The response differs by pest.
  2. Isolate. Remove all affected bags and everything within 500 mm. Bag the material and remove it from the property.
  3. Clean the area. Wash the shelf, the surrounding shelves and the floor with a sporicidal or appropriate disinfectant. Replace shelf liners.
  4. Check the rest. Inspect every bag in the room. If two or three are affected in one row, suspect a shared cause — substrate, spawn, water.
  5. Adjust conditions. If flies: lower humidity, increase airflow. If blotch: stop misting, reduce RH. If cobweb: increase airflow, lower RH.
  6. Treat if necessary. Only approved products; only as directed; never during harvest.
  7. Record. Date, pest, affected bags, actions, outcome. Use the record to spot patterns.

Assignment 11

  1. List the insect pests you are most likely to see and the key sign of each.
  2. List the diseases and the key sign of each. State which require the room conditions to be changed and which require bag removal.
  3. Draw your monitoring plan: number of sticky traps, where they go, how often you check, threshold for action.
  4. Write your IPM plan in at least 6 measures.
  5. Write your outbreak response in 6 steps or fewer.
  6. List the design features of your mushroom house that reduce pest entry. Note any that you would add if rebuilding.
  7. Design a weekly monitoring record sheet with at least 6 fields, and fill in one week.
  8. State your contamination rate target and your fly catch target. State your action when each is exceeded.
