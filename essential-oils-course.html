Practical Reference

This enrichment block consolidates the operational numbers, safety protocols and market signals that turn a coffee agribusiness plan into a bankable, repeatable enterprise. Whether you are a smallholder aggregator in Nyeri, a washing station manager in Jimma, or a trader moving parchment between Mbeya and Dar es Salaam, the figures below are the ones buyers, lenders and certification bodies will ask you to defend. Use them to build your cost model, set your farmgate price, and protect the people and product that generate your margin.

â  SAFETY WARNING â Chemical, Mycotoxin and Food-Safety Hazards

Coffee processing and pest control involve real health risks. Apply the following controls without exception:

  • Pesticides: Always wear gloves, goggles, boots and a respirator when mixing or spraying. Never eat, drink or smoke during application. Observe the pre-harvest interval (PHI) on the label â commonly 14â21 days for coffee insecticides â and store chemicals in a locked, ventilated store away from food and children.
  • Mycotoxins (ochratoxin A): Never dry coffee on bare ground or on tarpaulins contaminated with soil or animal waste. Keep drying beds raised, covered at night and during rain. Reject any parchment or green bean showing black, mouldy or musty beans â ochratoxin A is a regulated food contaminant in the EU and Japan, and a single rejected container can end a buyer relationship.
  • Processing water and effluent: Pulping uses large volumes of water and produces acidic, high-BOD effluent. Do not discharge untreated pulp water into streams or community water sources; use settling ponds or compost the pulp. Wear waterproof footwear in wet processing areas to prevent fungal skin infections.
  • Dust and machinery: Hulling and milling generate fine dust that can cause respiratory irritation. Use dust extraction or N95 masks. Guard all belts, pulleys and augers; lock out machinery before clearing jams.
  • Heavy metals and adulteration: Do not use lead-soldered equipment or contaminated jute bags. Buyers increasingly test for cadmium and lead; source fertilisers and bags from reputable suppliers with certificates of analysis.

Quick Reference Checklist

  1. Confirm your outturn before you price. Take a 1 kg sample of cherry through pulping, fermentation and drying, then weigh the green bean. Use your own conversion factor, not a textbook one.
  2. Test moisture at every stage. Buy a calibrated moisture meter (target 10.5â11.5% for green bean). Log readings per lot and per drying day.
  3. Build a per-hectare cost model using the column in Table 1 that matches your country, then stress-test it against a 20% price fall.
  4. Secure a buyer before the harvest. A signed letter of intent or forward contract at a floor price protects you from the post-harvest price dip.
  5. Register your business and your lots. Obtain a tax ID, export licence (where applicable) and traceability records that link each bag to a farm and a date.
  6. Train every worker on chemical handling and keep a signed attendance register. This is both a legal and a certification requirement.
  7. Monitor the ICO composite price weekly and track the ArabicaâRobusta spread to decide what to plant and what to sell.
  8. Reinvest 10â15% of net margin into drying beds, moisture meters, and staff training â the three interventions with the fastest payback in African coffee.
