🧠 Brain Opener

Every small business has a hundred tiny tasks that are done by hand and could be done by machine. Finding one of them and automating it is the fastest way to add value.

  • What is automation?
  • What kinds of tasks can be automated today?
  • Why is a small automation worth more than a big one?
🎁 Reveal a good answer

Automation is making a machine do a task that used to be done by hand. Recipes, reminders, report generation, data entry, formatting. A small automation is worth more than a big one because it works every day, and small improvements compound.

📖 Chiedza Automates the Shop

Chiedza, fourteen, in Harare, spent every Saturday helping her mother run a small grocery shop. Every customer bought something. Every sale had to be written into a notebook. The notebook was the shop's only record.

The problem was simple. At the end of each week, her mother spent two hours adding up the notebook. She checked every line. She counted the money. She made a summary. It was slow, and it was often wrong.

One Saturday, Chiedza asked her mother a question: "What if the summary made itself?"

Chiedza opened a spreadsheet on her phone. She set up five columns: date, item, quantity, price, total. She showed her mother how to type one line for each sale. Then she added a formula at the bottom that added every total automatically.

The first week, her mother still used the notebook - she did not trust the phone yet. At the end of the week, she compared the two. The spreadsheet was faster and it was correct.

The second week, her mother used only the spreadsheet. She saved one and a half hours on Saturday. The third week, Chiedza added a second sheet that showed which items sold the most. Her mother used this to plan her next order.

By the end of the month, the shop was making more money. Not because prices changed, but because her mother could see what was selling and what was not. The automation saved time. The information saved money.

💡 The Big Idea

One automation, run every day

Most people try to automate something big and fail. The professional automates one small thing and runs it every day. A two-hour weekly task becomes a ten-minute check. Over a year, that is eighty hours saved - one full working fortnight. Small automations compound.

📚 Three Deep Lessons

1

What can be automated today

With a phone and a free account, you can automate: (1) spreadsheets with formulas that calculate automatically, (2) reminders that send themselves, (3) templates that fill in with data you type once, (4) reports that update themselves, (5) simple messages that repeat.

You do not need to code for most of these. A spreadsheet, a free account on a scheduling tool, or a single Claude prompt can replace hours of manual work. Start with the task you hate most.

2

The six-step automation method

First, write down the task you want to automate. Second, list every single step in order. Third, find the steps that repeat the same way every time. Fourth, find a tool that does those steps. Fifth, run the automation alongside the manual task for one week. Sixth, if it works, switch over.

The fifth step is the one people skip. Always run the automation alongside the manual version for one week. Compare. If the automation makes any mistake, fix it before you switch.

3

Why small beats big

A big automation has more moving parts, more ways to break, and more risk. A small one that saves five minutes a day is worth more than a big one that saves an hour a month. And once one small automation works, adding a second one is easy.

The best automations are boring. Nobody notices them. The task was going to be done anyway. Now it is done without thinking. That is the whole goal.

🛠 Hands-On - Automate One Real Task This Week

You need

  • A phone or laptop
  • A free spreadsheet app
  • A notebook
  1. Pick one task you do every week that takes at least twenty minutes.
  2. List every step in order in your notebook.
  3. Circle the steps that are always the same.
  4. Choose a tool for those steps (spreadsheet, reminder app, template).
  5. Build the automation. Run it alongside the manual version for one week.
  6. If it worked, switch. If not, fix it and try again.
Why it works: The best automation is boring. Nobody notices it. The task was going to be done anyway. Now it happens without thinking.

🎮 Game - Automation Sprint

2 to 6 players - 30 min

Each player picks a task they do every week. In thirty minutes, everyone writes the steps, builds the simplest possible automation, and tests it. At the end, each player shows their automation and says how many minutes it will save in a year. The biggest saving wins - but only if the automation actually works.

🤖 AI-Era Skill

Claude can write spreadsheet formulas, help you design a workflow, and suggest tools. It cannot click buttons for you.

Try this prompt: "I spend two hours every Saturday adding up sales for a small shop. I want to automate the calculation using a free spreadsheet on my phone. What are the exact steps, and what formula should I use to add every sale total automatically?"

Test the formula with fake numbers first. Add five rows manually and check the total with a calculator. If the number matches, the formula works.
