1. Common Weed Species in Soybean Fields

Weeds are the biggest yield robbers in soybean. In Africa, you will see many types. The most common are:

1. **Pigweed (Amaranthus spp.)** fast-growing, broadleaf, can grow 2-3 cm per day. It competes for light and nutrients.
2. **Blackjack (Bidens pilosa)** has yellow flowers and sticky seeds. It emerges early and can reduce yields by up to 50% if not controlled.
3. **Couch grass (Cynodon dactylon)** a perennial grass with deep roots. It spreads by rhizomes and is very hard to remove by hand.
4. **Nutgrass (Cyperus rotundus)** a sedge with underground tubers. It thrives in moist soils and can puncture plastic mulch.
5. **Mexican marigold (Tagetes minuta)** a strong-smelling weed that can grow 2 m tall. It produces many seeds and allelopathic chemicals that suppress soybean growth.
6. **Witchweed (Striga spp.)** a parasitic weed that attaches to soybean roots. It is a serious problem in northern Nigeria, Ghana, and parts of East Africa.

**Why identification matters:** Different weeds need different control methods. For example, hand-pulling works for pigweed but not for nutgrass because tubers remain in the soil. Herbicides are selective: 2,4-D kills broadleaf weeds but not grasses.

**Scouting:** Walk your field in a 'W' pattern. Check 5 spots per hectare. Count weeds in a 1 m x 1 m square. If you find more than 5 weeds per square meter, you need to act.

**Critical period:** Soybeans are most sensitive to weed competition from 2 to 6 weeks after planting. Weeds that emerge during this time cause the biggest yield loss.

**Local names:** In Nigeria, pigweed is 'arowo'; in Kenya, blackjack is 'kihia'; in Tanzania, couch grass is 'kisinde'. Knowing local names helps you communicate with other farmers.

**Prevention:** Always clean your boots and tools before moving to a new field. Weed seeds stick to mud and can spread.

**Action step:** This week, walk your field and identify at least three weed species. Take photos or note their leaf shapes. This will help you choose the right control method.

2. Integrated Weed Management Approaches

Integrated Weed Management (IWM) means using many methods together, not just one. This is the best way to control weeds in soybean because it reduces costs, slows herbicide resistance, and protects the environment.

**The four pillars of IWM:**

1. **Cultural** crop rotation, cover crops, competitive varieties, and proper spacing.
2. **Mechanical** hand weeding, hoeing, and tillage.
3. **Biological** using living organisms like insects or fungi to eat weeds (rare in Africa but possible with goats grazing after harvest).
4. **Chemical** herbicides, used wisely and rotated.

**How to combine methods:**

- **Step 1:** Prepare land well. Plough and harrow to kill early weeds.
- **Step 2:** Plant soybean in rows 50-60 cm apart. This allows you to hoe between rows.
- **Step 3:** Apply a pre-emergence herbicide like pendimethalin (e.g., Stomp) within 3 days of planting. This stops weed seeds from germinating.
- **Step 4:** Scout at 2 weeks. If weeds escape, hand-pull or hoe.
- **Step 5:** At 4-6 weeks, if grasses are a problem, apply a post-emergence herbicide like fluazifop-p-butyl (e.g., Fusilade) but only if you have a sprayer and protective gear.
- **Step 6:** After harvest, plant a cover crop like mucuna or cowpea to smother weeds.

**Real example:** In Zambia, farmers who used IWM (rotation + hand weeding + one herbicide) got 2.5 tonnes/ha, while those who only hand-weeded got 1.8 tonnes/ha.

**Costs:** Hand weeding a hectare takes 20-30 person-days. Herbicides cost about $30-50 per hectare. IWM can reduce total weeding costs by 30%.

**Key rule:** Never rely on one method. If you only use one herbicide, weeds will become resistant. If you only hand-weed, you will not control deep-rooted weeds.

**Action step:** Plan your IWM for next season. Write down which methods you will use at each stage: pre-planting, planting, 2 weeks, 6 weeks, and after harvest.

3. Herbicide Selection and Application Timing

Herbicides are chemicals that kill weeds. They must be chosen carefully and applied at the right time. Wrong choice or wrong timing wastes money and can damage your soybean.

**Types of herbicides:**

- **Pre-emergence (PRE):** Applied to soil before weeds and soybean emerge. Examples: pendimethalin (Stomp), metribuzin (Sencor), and S-metolachlor (Dual Gold). They form a barrier that kills weed seeds as they germinate.
- **Post-emergence (POST):** Applied after weeds and soybean have emerged. Examples: fluazifop-p-butyl (Fusilade) for grasses, fomesafen (Flex) for broadleaf weeds, and bentazon (Basagran) for broadleaf.
- **Non-selective:** Kills all plants. Example: glyphosate (Roundup). Only use before planting or with a shielded sprayer.

**Timing is critical:**

- **Pre-emergence:** Apply within 3 days after planting, before weeds emerge. If it rains heavily after application, the chemical may leach away.
- **Post-emergence:** Apply when weeds are small 2 to 4 leaves. If weeds are bigger than 10 cm, they may survive. For grasses, apply when they are 5-10 cm tall.

**Sprayer calibration:** You must know how much water your sprayer puts out. Fill a 10-litre bucket with water. Spray a 10 m x 10 m area (100 m). Measure how much water you used. If you used 1 litre, then you need 100 litres per hectare (10,000 m). Adjust nozzle or speed to get 150-200 litres per hectare.

**Mixing:** Always read the label. Use the recommended rate. For example, pendimethalin at 1.5-2.0 litres per hectare. Mix in a bucket first, then pour into the sprayer tank.

**Safety:** Wear gloves, boots, long trousers, and a mask. Do not eat or drink while spraying. Wash your hands after.

**Common mistake:** Spraying when it is windy (above 10 km/h) causes drift that can kill nearby crops. Spray early morning or late evening when wind is calm.

**Action step:** Buy one pre-emergence herbicide and one post-emergence herbicide for your next soybean crop. Practice calibration with water before using chemicals.

4. Herbicide Resistance Management

Herbicide resistance happens when weeds survive a herbicide that used to kill them. It is a serious problem in Africa, especially with glyphosate-resistant pigweed in South Africa and Zimbabwe. Once resistance develops, you cannot control those weeds with that chemical again.

**How resistance develops:**

- You spray the same herbicide every year.
- A few weeds have natural genes that make them survive.
- Those survivors produce seeds.
- Next year, more weeds are resistant.
- After 3-5 years, the herbicide stops working completely.

**Signs of resistance:**

- You spray and most weeds die, but a few survive and look healthy.
- The same weed species survives across the field.
- You have used the same herbicide for more than 3 years.

**How to prevent resistance:**

1. **Rotate herbicides:** Use different chemicals with different modes of action. For example, if you used pendimethalin (a dinitroaniline) this year, use metribuzin (a triazine) next year.
2. **Mix herbicides:** Apply two herbicides with different modes of action together. Example: pendimethalin + metribuzin.
3. **Use multiple methods:** Do not rely only on chemicals. Hand-weed, hoe, and rotate crops.
4. **Clean equipment:** Remove weed seeds from your sprayer, boots, and tools before moving to a new field.
5. **Scout after spraying:** If you see survivors, pull them out by hand and burn them. Do not let them produce seeds.

**Real example:** In South Africa, farmers who rotated herbicides and used hand weeding kept glyphosate working for 15 years. Those who used only glyphosate lost it in 5 years.

**What to do if you suspect resistance:**

- Stop using that herbicide.
- Switch to a different mode of action.
- Hand-pull the surviving weeds and destroy them.
- Report to your extension officer.

**Action step:** Write down every herbicide you use this season. Next season, choose a different one. Never use the same herbicide two years in a row.

5. Mechanical Weed Control Methods

Mechanical weed control means using tools, hands, or machines to remove weeds. It is the oldest and most common method in Africa. It is effective if done at the right time and with the right tool.

**Hand weeding:**

- Use a hoe or hand. Best for small fields (less than 1 hectare) and for weeds between soybean plants.
- Timing: First weeding at 2-3 weeks after planting. Second weeding at 5-6 weeks.
- Labour: One person can weed about 0.1 hectare per day. So 1 hectare needs 10 person-days per weeding.
- Cost: In Nigeria, a labourer costs about $3-5 per day. So two weedings cost $60-100 per hectare.

**Hoeing:**

- Use a hoe to cut weeds just below the soil surface. Do not dig deep or you will damage soybean roots.
- Best for row-planted soybean. Keep the hoe 5-10 cm away from the soybean stem.
- Do not hoe when soil is wet it creates clods and spreads weed seeds.

**Mechanical cultivators:**

- For larger farms (above 5 hectares), use a tractor-drawn cultivator or a push-type weeder.
- A cultivator has tines that uproot weeds between rows.
- Set the tines to work 3-5 cm deep. Deeper will damage roots.
- Cultivate when weeds are small (2-4 leaves).

**Mowing:**

- For tall weeds like Mexican marigold, slash them before they produce seeds.
- Mowing does not kill perennial weeds like couch grass it only sets them back.

**Mulching:**

- Spread dry grass, straw, or plastic mulch between rows. This blocks sunlight and stops weed seeds from germinating.
- Use 5-10 cm thick layer of organic mulch.
- Plastic mulch (black or silver) is expensive but very effective.

**Timing is everything:**

- Weeds compete most from 2 to 6 weeks after planting. So your first weeding must be done by week 3.
- If you delay, yield loss can be 30-50%.

**Action step:** Plan your weeding schedule now. Mark on a calendar: first weeding at 2-3 weeks, second at 5-6 weeks. Train your labourers to hoe shallow and not damage soybean roots.

6. Cultural Practices for Weed Suppression

Cultural practices are things you do before and during the growing season that make it harder for weeds to grow. They are cheap, effective, and do not require chemicals.

**1. Crop rotation:**

- Do not plant soybean in the same field year after year. Rotate with maize, sorghum, or cowpea.
- Different crops have different growth habits and root systems. This breaks weed life cycles.
- Example: If you rotate soybean with maize, you can use atrazine (a herbicide for maize) that kills broadleaf weeds that soybean cannot tolerate.

**2. Cover crops:**

- Plant a cover crop like mucuna, lablab, or cowpea before soybean.
- These grow fast and smother weeds. They also add nitrogen to the soil.
- Plant cover crops 4-6 weeks before soybean. Then kill them by slashing or rolling.

**3. Competitive soybean varieties:**

- Some soybean varieties grow faster and shade the ground earlier. Ask your seed supplier for 'early canopy' varieties.
- In Nigeria, TGX varieties are known to be competitive.

**4. Proper spacing and plant population:**

- Plant soybean at 50-60 cm between rows and 5-10 cm within rows.
- Aim for 400,000 to 500,000 plants per hectare.
- A dense crop shades the soil and reduces weed germination.
- Do not plant too wide weeds will take over.

**5. Fertilizer and soil health:**

- Healthy soybean grows faster and competes better with weeds.
- Apply phosphorus and potassium based on soil test.
- Add organic manure to improve soil structure.

**6. Timely planting:**

- Plant at the start of the rains when soil is moist. Soybean will germinate quickly and get a head start on weeds.
- If you plant late, weeds will already be established.

**7. Sanitation:**

- Clean your plough, harrow, and boots before entering a new field.
- Remove weed seeds from harvested soybean before storing.
- Do not let weeds go to seed. One pigweed plant can produce 100,000 seeds.

**Real example:** In Malawi, farmers who rotated soybean with maize and used a cover crop of mucuna reduced weed pressure by 60% and increased soybean yield by 40%.

**Action step:** This season, plan a rotation. Next season, plant soybean after maize or sorghum. Also, try a cover crop on one small plot to see the difference.

<h2>Practical Reference: Weed Control Strategies for Soybean Farming in Africa</h2>

<p>Weeds are the single most underestimated threat to soybean profitability across Africa. Unlike insects or diseases, weeds compete silently from emergence, stealing water, nutrients, light, and space. In West African Guinea savannahs, uncontrolled weeds can cut soybean yields by 4080%. In East African highlands, the same story plays out in smallholder and commercial fields alike. For agribusinesses, the message is clear: every naira, shilling, or cedi lost to weeds is a direct hit to margin, loan repayment capacity, and farmer loyalty. This practical reference gives you field-ready tools to protect soybean yield and your bottom line.</p>

<h3>Why Weed Control Is a Business Decision</h3>
<p>Weed control is not just an agronomic taskit is a financial lever. A farmer who spends US$60 per hectare on effective herbicide and one timely weeding pass can protect 1.52.5 tonnes of soybean worth US$6001,000 at farmgate. The return on investment is 10:1 or better. Conversely, delayed weeding during the critical period (first 46 weeks after planting) can reduce yields by 1% per day of delay. For agribusinesses supplying inputs, offering credit, or buying grain, promoting integrated weed management (IWM) is a portfolio risk-reduction strategy.</p>

Post-emergence herbicides work best on young, actively growing weedsusually 24 leaf stage. In Africa, the most widely available and effective products for soybean include glyphosate (pre-plant burndown), pendimethalin and S-metolachlor (pre-emergence), and fomesafen, bentazon, and clethodim (post-emergence).</p>

<h3>Integrated Weed Management: The Business Case</h3>
<p>No single method wins the weed war. Integrated Weed Management (IWM) combines chemical, mechanical, cultural, and biological tools to reduce weed pressure, delay resistance, and lower long-term costs. For agribusinesses, IWM reduces reliance on any one herbicide (protecting your product portfolio), stabilises farmer yields (reducing credit default risk), and builds trust as a knowledge partner.</p>

<p><strong>Mechanical methods:</strong> Hand hoeing remains the backbone for smallholders. Two timely weedingsat 23 weeks and 56 weeks after emergencecan protect 8090% of yield potential. For larger farms, inter-row cultivation with a tractor-mounted cultivator at 34 weeks is cost-effective. However, mechanical control fails against <em>Imperata</em> and <em>Cyperus</em> because rhizomes and tubers regenerate.</p>

<p><strong>Cultural practices:</strong> Competitive soybean varieties with rapid canopy closure suppress late weeds. Narrow row spacing (4050 cm vs. 75 cm) shades soil faster. Crop rotation with maize or sorghum breaks weed cyclesespecially for <em>Striga</em>. Cover crops like cowpea or mucuna smother weeds and fix nitrogen. Mulching with crop residues reduces emergence by 4060%.</p>

<p><strong>Herbicide resistance management:</strong> Resistance is rising in Africa, especially in <em>Echinochloa</em> and <em>Cyperus</em>. Rotate herbicide modes of action (MOA) every season. Use full labelled ratesnever cut rates. Tank-mix herbicides with different MOAs. Include mechanical and cultural controls. Scout fields weekly and remove escapes by hand before they seed.</p>

<ul style="list-style-type:none; padding-left:0;">
<li style="margin-bottom:10px;"> <strong>Scout fields weekly</strong> from planting to canopy closureidentify weeds early.</li>
<li style="margin-bottom:10px;"> <strong>Apply pre-emergence herbicide</strong> within 48 hours of planting on moist soil.</li>
<li style="margin-bottom:10px;"> <strong>Time post-emergence sprays</strong> at 24 leaf stage of weedsnever delay.</li>
<li style="margin-bottom:10px;"> <strong>Rotate herbicide MOAs</strong> every season to prevent resistance.</li>
<li style="margin-bottom:10px;"> <strong>Combine chemical with mechanical</strong>at least one hoeing or cultivation pass.</li>
<li style="margin-bottom:10px;"> <strong>Use competitive varieties</strong> and narrow rows to shade out late weeds.</li>
<li style="margin-bottom:10px;"> <strong>Keep records</strong> of weeds, products, rates, and dates for every field.</li>
<li style="margin-bottom:10px;"> <strong>Train workers on PPE</strong> and safe herbicide handling before every spray season.</li>
</ul>
