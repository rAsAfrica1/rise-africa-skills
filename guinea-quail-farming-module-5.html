5. Quail Health and Production

Quail are surprisingly hardy but tiny problems cost you whole batches. Here is the prevention routine.

Module 5 of 12 · Guinea Fowl and Quail Farming

Why quail die young

Quail chicks are the size of a bumblebee. Small problems become big losses fast:

ProblemImpactPrevention
Drowning in drinkerLoses chicks in first weekMarbles or stones in drinker base
ChillingLoses chicks in first 3 days35-37 C brooder, pre-heat 24 hrs
Escape through meshChicks lost, cold1 cm mesh maximum
CoccidiosisBloody droppings, deathsDry litter, coccidiostat in feed
CannibalismPecked vents, feet, headsLow light, more space, greens to peck
Ulcerative enteritisSudden deaths in cagesClean water, hygiene, treatment

Ulcerative enteritis (quail disease)

This is the quail-specific disease that kills without warning. Caused by Clostridium colinum in dirty cages.

  • Sudden death with no symptoms
  • White watery droppings
  • Ruffled feathers, drooping wings
  • Weight loss before death

Prevention:

  • Clean cages daily - manure tray out twice a week
  • Fresh water twice daily - never let drinkers grow bacteria
  • Do not overcrowd
  • Treat with streptomycin or bacitracin if diagnosed

Lighting for year-round production

Quail lay on long daylight (14-16 hours). In short-day months, add artificial light:

  • Use a 40 W bulb per 10 m2 cage space
  • Turn on for 2-3 hours at dawn and 2-3 hours at dusk
  • Total light: 14-16 hours per day
  • Keep consistent - changing light suddenly stops laying
The light rule: Never reduce light suddenly. Quail that go from 16 to 10 hours of light stop laying within a week and take 4-6 weeks to recover.

Cage management

  1. Remove manure tray twice a week - ammonia builds fast in cages
  2. Wash drinkers daily with clean water
  3. Replace wet feed immediately
  4. Check for cracked or broken eggs in the roll-out tray - remove daily
  5. Remove any dead bird immediately - quail will peck dead cage-mates
  6. Replace any broken cage wire right away - sharp edges cause injuries

Vaccination

Quail rarely need vaccination if biosecurity is good. For high-density commercial cages:

AgeVaccine
Day 1 (hatchery)Not usually needed
Day 10-14Newcastle (optional)
Day 21Boosters if needed

Most quail farms rely on strict hygiene rather than vaccination.
