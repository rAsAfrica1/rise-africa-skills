â  SAFETY WARNING â Chemicals, Aflatoxins & Food Safety
  • Aflatoxin: Never sell or consume maize, groundnuts, or cottonseed that shows mould, discolouration, or a musty smell. Aflatoxin B1 is a known carcinogen and is not destroyed by normal cooking. Test kits are available through national bureaus of standards.
  • Pesticides: Always wear gloves, a mask, and long sleeves when handling agrochemicals. Never mix or store pesticides in food or drink containers. Observe the pre-harvest interval (PHI) on the label â selling sprayed produce early is illegal and dangerous.
  • Veterinary drugs: Respect withdrawal periods for antibiotics and dewormers in poultry, milk, and meat. Residues can cause market rejection and antibiotic resistance.
  • Water & hygiene: Use clean, potable water for processing and washing produce. Contaminated water spreads cholera, typhoid, and E. coli â a single outbreak can shut down your market access.
  • Storage: Fumigation tablets (e.g., aluminium phosphide) are highly toxic. Only trained, licensed operators should use them, and never in or near living quarters.

Quick Reference Checklist

  1. Test your inputs: Verify chick/fingerling/seed quality from a certified supplier and keep the receipt. Poor inputs wipe out margins before you start.
  2. Keep daily records: Track feed, mortality, weight, and sales. If you cannot measure it, you cannot manage or finance it.
  3. Calculate your break-even: Know your cost per kg or per unit before you set a selling price. Use the benchmarks in Table 1 as a starting point.
  4. Plan for losses: Budget 5â15% post-harvest loss depending on commodity. Invest in drying, cooling, or hermetic storage where the payback is under two cycles.
  5. Secure a buyer before you produce: A verbal or written off-take agreement reduces price risk and strengthens loan applications.
  6. Comply with standards: Register with the relevant authority, follow withdrawal periods, and keep certificates visible. Compliance opens institutional markets.
  7. Manage cash flow: Separate business and household money. Reinvest a fixed percentage each cycle for inputs and equipment.
  8. Train continuously: Attend extension sessions on biosecurity, feed formulation, and financial literacy. Skills compound faster than equipment.
