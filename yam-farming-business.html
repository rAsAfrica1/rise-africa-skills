1. The Business Idea

You are training to run one of these real businesses around yam:

  • Fresh food yam production — grow, grade, store, sell to urban markets and festivals
  • Seed yam multiplication — highest margin in the yam value chain, 2–3x food price
  • Yam processing — pounded yam flour, chips, crisps — 3–8x raw yam value
  • Certified seed yam producer — top of the seed market, premium prices

2. Three Capital Tiers

Tier 1 — Home Plot

–

200–500 m². Buy clean seed yam, prepare mounds, stake, mulch. Sell fresh food yam at local market. Learn the crop.

Tier 2 — Smallholder Producer

– ,500

0.5–1 hectare. Serious mixed food and seed yam production. Mounds or ridges, stakes, complete feeding programme, storage in a yam barn.

Tier 3 — Seed Yam Enterprise

,000 – ,000

2–5 hectares under seed multiplication. Clean seed yam in, strict nematode control, grading, formal or informal certification. Sell to other farmers at 2–3x food price.

Rule: start at the tier your land, labour and market can support. Yam is labour-intensive — do not over-extend in year one.

3. Value-Addition Ladder

ProductMultiplier
Uncleaned field-run yam1x baseline

Stored food yam (off-season sale)1.5–2.2x
Festival-season food yam1.8–2.5x
Certified seed yam2–3x
Pounded yam flour4–6x
Yam chips / crisps5–8x

For most smallholders, storage and seed yam are the first two value steps. Both pay back in one season.

4. Buyers You Will Approach

  • Local market vendors — daily cash purchases
  • Urban retailers and supermarkets — weekly or festival contract
  • Festival buyers — West African festival markets, premium prices
  • Other farmers (seed yam) — highest price, highest quality requirement
  • Flour and chip processors — bulk, processing-grade yam
  • Diaspora export buyers — premium for pounded yam flour
Rule: name three buyers before you plant. The festival market is seasonal — plan your harvest to hit it if you can.
