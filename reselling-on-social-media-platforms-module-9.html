1. Accepting Payments Securely (PayPal, Venmo, etc.)

When selling on social media, you need to accept payments safely. In Africa, popular options include PayPal, Payoneer, and local mobile money like M-Pesa, MTN Mobile Money, and Airtel Money. PayPal is widely used for international buyers, but fees are about 3.4% + $0.30 per transaction. Payoneer is good for receiving payments from marketplaces like Amazon and Fiverr, but for direct sales, you can send an invoice. Mobile money is instant and low-cost: M-Pesa charges about 1.5% for transfers. Always use a business account if possible to avoid mixing personal and business funds. Never accept payment via friends and family on PayPalâyou lose seller protection. For mobile money, confirm the transaction SMS before releasing goods. Be wary of overpayment scams: if a buyer sends more than the agreed amount and asks for a refund, it's a scam. Use escrow services like Escrow.com for high-value items. Always keep records: screenshots of payment confirmations, chat logs, and receipts. Set clear payment terms in your posts: 'Payment via M-Pesa or PayPal only. No cash on delivery unless local pickup.' For international buyers, consider Wise (formerly TransferWise) for lower fees. Always factor fees into your price: if you want $100, charge $104 to cover PayPal fees. Test your payment links before sharing. Finally, never share your password or PIN with anyone. Use two-factor authentication on all payment accounts.

2. Packaging Products for Shipping

Packaging protects your product and creates a good impression. In Africa, you can find affordable packaging materials at local markets or stores like Nakumatt (Kenya), Shoprite (Nigeria), or online on Jumia. For small items like phone cases, use padded envelopes (about $0.20 each). For clothing, poly mailers (waterproof plastic bags) cost around $0.10 each. For fragile items, use bubble wrap ($2 per roll) and a sturdy cardboard box. Always use a box that fits snuglyâtoo much space means the item shifts and breaks. Add cushioning like newspaper, foam peanuts, or air pillows. Seal with strong packing tape, not masking tape. For electronics, wrap in anti-static bags first. Label the package clearly with the recipient's address and your return address. If shipping internationally, use a customs declaration form (available at post offices). For local deliveries within your city, consider using a motorbike courier like Uber Connect or Bolt Send. They charge based on distance, typically $2-$5. For bulk orders, negotiate with a packaging supplier. Reuse boxes from your own online orders to save money. Always take a photo of the packed item before sealingâthis helps if there's a dispute. Write 'FRAGILE' in red on the box. For liquids, double-bag and use absorbent material. Test your packaging by shaking the box gentlyâif you hear movement, add more cushioning. Finally, keep packaging costs low but never compromise on protection.

3. Calculating Shipping Costs and Options

Shipping costs can eat your profits if you don't calculate them properly. Start by weighing and measuring your package. Use a kitchen scale for small items; for larger ones, a bathroom scale. Measure length, width, and height in centimeters. Then use online calculators: for international, try DHL, FedEx, or your local post office (e.g., Kenya Post, Nigeria Post). For domestic, use courier apps like Sendy (Kenya) or GIG Logistics (Nigeria). Rates depend on weight, dimensions, and destination. For example, sending a 1kg package from Nairobi to Mombasa via G4S costs about $5; to Kampala, Uganda, via DHL costs about $30. Always compare at least three options. The post office is cheapest but slow (7-14 days). Couriers are faster (1-3 days) but pricier. For bulky but light items, dimensional weight applies: a large box of pillows may cost more than its actual weight. Offer free shipping by building the cost into your price: if shipping is $10, add $10 to your item price and advertise 'Free Shipping'. For local pickup, you save on shipping, so offer a discount. For multiple items, combine shipping: charge full shipping for the first item and 50% for each additional. Use flat-rate boxes from the post office if they fit your itemâthey have one price regardless of weight. Always get a tracking number. For high-value items, add insurance (about 1% of value). Keep a shipping log to track costs and adjust your prices. Finally, communicate shipping times clearly to buyers to avoid disputes.

4. Using Shipping Labels and Discounts

Shipping labels are essential for tracking and professionalism. You can create labels online using platforms like PayPal ShipStation, Pirate Ship (for USPS in the US), or local courier apps. In Africa, many couriers like DHL and FedEx have online portals where you can generate labels. For example, DHL Express has a 'MyDHL' account where you can create shipments, print labels, and schedule pickups. You get discounts of up to 30% compared to retail rates. For local deliveries, apps like Sendy and GIG Logistics provide digital labels you can print or show on your phone. Always use a thermal label printer if you ship oftenâthey cost about $100 but save on tape and paper. If you don't have one, print on plain paper and tape it securely with clear packing tape. Never write addresses by handâit can cause delays. Include the tracking number on the label and share it with the buyer immediately. For discounts, open a business account with couriers: DHL offers up to 40% off for small businesses. Also, use aggregators like Shippo or EasyPost that compare rates and give you discounted labels. For mobile money payments, some couriers offer discounts if you pay online. Always ask for a receipt and keep a copy of the label. If you ship internationally, use a commercial invoice (3 copies) and attach it to the package. For returns, use prepaid labels to make it easy for buyers. Finally, track your label expenses and claim any business deductions. Using labels correctly reduces errors and builds trust.

5. Local Pickup vs. Shipping

Deciding between local pickup and shipping depends on your product, location, and customer base. Local pickup is free for you and fast for the buyer. It works best for bulky items (furniture, appliances) or high-value items (phones, laptops) where shipping is risky or expensive. Meet in a safe public place like a mall, police station, or coffee shop. Always inspect the item with the buyer and get a signed receipt or confirmation message. For payment, use mobile money or cash on pickupâbut beware of fake notes. In Africa, local pickup is common on Facebook Marketplace and WhatsApp groups. Offer a small discount (5-10%) for pickup to encourage it. Shipping, on the other hand, expands your market beyond your city. It's essential for small, light items (jewelry, phone cases) and for international buyers. Shipping costs money and time, but you can charge the buyer. Use tracked shipping to avoid disputes. For local pickup, set clear hours and a location. For shipping, provide a tracking number and estimated delivery date. Consider a hybrid: offer both and let the buyer choose. For example, 'Pickup in Nairobi CBD for free, or shipping via G4S for $5.' Always communicate clearly: if pickup, say 'No shipping available.' If shipping, say 'Ships within 2 business days.' For high-value items, require signature on delivery. Finally, track which method is more popular and adjust. Many resellers start with local pickup to build trust, then add shipping as they grow.

6. Tracking Orders and Managing Delays

Tracking orders keeps buyers happy and protects you. Always use a shipping method with tracking, even if it costs a little more. When you ship, get the tracking number and send it to the buyer immediately via the same platform (e.g., WhatsApp, Instagram DM). For international shipments, use tracking that works across borders, like DHL or FedEx. For local, use courier apps that provide real-time tracking. Check the tracking status daily and update the buyer if there's a delay. Delays happen: customs, weather, or courier issues. If a delay occurs, inform the buyer proactively with a new estimated delivery date. Apologize and offer a small discount or free shipping on their next order. If the package is lost, file a claim with the courier. Most couriers have a claims process; you'll need the receipt and tracking number. For high-value items, insure the package. If you use PayPal, you must provide tracking to win a dispute. For mobile money, keep screenshots. Always keep buyers informed: 'Your package is delayed due to customs, expected in 3 more days.' Use a spreadsheet to track all orders: order number, buyer name, tracking number, status. Set reminders to follow up. If a buyer complains, respond within 24 hours. Never ignore messages. For repeated delays, switch couriers. Finally, learn from each delay: was it your packaging, wrong address, or courier? Adjust your process. Good tracking and communication turn a bad experience into a loyal customer.

Practical Reference: Payment, Shipping, and Logistics for African Social Media Resellers

You can sell out of a WhatsApp status or an Instagram Live and still lose money on the back end if payments, packaging, and delivery are handled loosely. This reference module covers the operational spine of a social commerce business: getting paid without chargebacks or fraud, packing goods so they arrive intact, pricing delivery correctly, and keeping the customer informed when things go wrong. The examples below draw on African market realities â mobile money dominance, cash-on-delivery norms, informal courier networks, and cross-border trade corridors.

1. Accepting Payments Securely (PayPal, Venmo, Mobile Money)

Payment is where trust is won or lost. In most African markets, mobile money (M-Pesa, MTN MoMo, Airtel Money, Orange Money) is the default for local buyers, while PayPal and Payoneer dominate cross-border sales. Venmo is largely US-only and should not be offered to African buyers. The rule is simple: never dispatch goods until funds have cleared and are withdrawable, not just "pending".

For high-value items, take a 50% deposit via mobile money and the balance on delivery. For cross-border PayPal sales, insist on "Goods and Services" rather than "Friends and Family" â the latter removes seller protection and is a common scam vector. Always keep a written record of the order in WhatsApp or email, because PayPal disputes are decided on documentation.

2. Packaging Products for Shipping

Packaging is not cosmetic â it is loss prevention. A damaged parcel in Lagos or Nairobi costs you the product, the shipping fee, and the customer. Use a three-layer approach: a primary protective layer (bubble wrap, foam, or tissue), a rigid secondary layer (corrugated box or padded mailer), and a sealed outer layer with your branding and return address. For cosmetics, liquids, and food items, double-bag with a leak-proof liner and mark the parcel "This Way Up".

Photograph every parcel before sealing, with the buyer's name and order number visible. This photo is your evidence in any dispute and doubles as a marketing asset on your social feed. For apparel, use poly mailers to cut weight; for electronics, use anti-static bags plus rigid boxes. Always include a small thank-you card with your WhatsApp number â repeat orders are cheaper than new customer acquisition.

4. Using Shipping Labels and Discounts

Never handwrite addresses. Printed labels reduce mis-delivery and look professional. Free tools like Pirate Ship (US-focused), Shippo, and Stamps.com offer discounted rates, but for Africa the practical route is a courier aggregator account with G4S, Sendy, or a local postal API. Many African couriers offer 10â30% discounts for volume accounts; negotiate once you exceed 50 parcels per month.

For cross-border sales, use a commercial invoice inside the parcel and a CN22/CN23 customs declaration form on the outside. Under-declaring value to save duty is fraud and voids insurance. Keep a digital log of every tracking number in a spreadsheet or Google Sheet â this is your single source of truth when a buyer claims non-delivery.

5. Local Pickup vs. Shipping

Local pickup eliminates courier cost and COD risk, but it consumes your time and creates security concerns. Offer pickup only at a safe, public location (a mall, a cafÃ©, or a pickup locker). For shipping, you trade cost for reach. A hybrid model works best: free pickup for orders above a threshold, subsidised shipping for orders below it. In cities with high traffic like Lagos or Nairobi, pickup points reduce failed deliveries significantly.

6. Tracking Orders and Managing Delays

Send the tracking number within 24 hours of dispatch â this single action cuts "where is my order?" messages by more than half. Set a delay protocol: if tracking shows no movement for 48 hours, contact the courier; if no resolution in 72 hours, inform the buyer proactively with a new ETA and a small goodwill gesture (discount code or free shipping on next order). Proactive communication prevents chargebacks and negative reviews.
