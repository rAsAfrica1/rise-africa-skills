11. Advanced Breeding and Genetics

The best breeders are students of genetics. Here is how to improve your kennel every generation.

Module 11 of 12 · Dog Breeding for Profit

Why genetics matters

Genetics determine 30-60% of a dog''s traits. Health, temperament, conformation, and working ability are all inherited. Good genetics produce healthy, sound, easy-to-live-with dogs that sell easily. Bad genetics produce sick, aggressive, or defective dogs that ruin reputations.

Every generation you breed with attention to genetics improves your kennel. Every generation you breed carelessly degrades it.

The key genetic concepts

ConceptWhat it means
HeritabilityHow much of a trait is genetic vs environment. High heritability = easier to select
Dominant genesOne copy is enough to show the trait
Recessive genesTwo copies needed to show the trait
CarriersHave a recessive gene but do not show it
InbreedingMating related dogs. Increases genetic defects
Line breedingMating dogs from the same line, but not closely related. Common in breeders
OutcrossingMating unrelated dogs. Increases vigour but can reduce consistency
Hybrid vigourCrossbred puppies are often healthier and stronger

The three breeding strategies

StrategyMethodProsCons
InbreedingFather-daughter, brother-sister, close relativesFixed type quicklyGenetic defects, low fertility, weakened immunity
Line breedingGrandparent-grandchild, cousinsConsistent type without extremesRisk of concentrating defects if not managed
OutcrossingTwo unrelated dogsHybrid vigour, genetic diversityLess predictable offspring

Best practice for small breeders: line breeding with regular outcrosses every 2-3 generations. This gives consistency without accumulating problems.

The coefficient of inbreeding (COI)

COI measures how related a dog''s parents are. Lower is better.

COIMeaning
Below 5%Very low - healthy genetic diversity
5-10%Low-moderate - acceptable
10-20%Moderate - watch for problems
20-30%High - likely to see defects
Over 30%Very high - avoid

Tools like the Kennel Club mating checker or the ICB calculator estimate COI from pedigrees. Aim for below 10%.

Breeding program structure

ElementWhat to do
SelectionOnly breed from dogs that meet breed standard, are healthy, and have good temperament
Health testingAll breeding dogs tested for breed-relevant conditions
Mating planCheck pedigrees and COI before every mating
Record keepingTrack every breeding, whelping, puppy outcome
EvaluationScore each litter: health, size, growth, temperament
Culling from breedingRetire or remove dogs that produce problems
RetentionKeep the best puppy from each litter for the future

Genetic diversity - the long game

Small closed breeding populations lose genetic diversity over time. Prevent with:

  • Import a stud from a distant line every 2-3 generations
  • Use multiple stud males instead of one male for the whole kennel
  • Do not overuse one popular male - even if he is excellent
  • Track COI on every mating plan
  • Test for carriers and mate carrier-to-clear, never carrier-to-carrier
  • Consider crossbreeding programs (breed-specific) to introduce vigour

The 5-year genetic improvement plan

YearActionExpected gain
Year 1Health test all breeding stock. Record every puppy.Baseline
Year 2Choose best female from first litter as replacement. Stud from tested line.Better conformation
Year 3Import or lease a stud from a different line. Reduce COI.Hybrid vigour, health improvements
Year 4Retain the best puppy from each litter. Continue health testing.Consistency and health
Year 5Your kennel has a reputation for health and temperament. Puppy prices rise 30-100%.Market premium

Breeding for specific markets

MarketBreeding priority
Family petsTemperament, health, moderate energy
Working (security, herding)Drive, trainability, working ability, soundness
Show dogsConformation to breed standard, movement, coat
Sports (agility, obedience)Athleticism, trainability, speed
Service dogs (guide, assistance)Temperament, confidence, trainability, health
Companion (small, apartment)Size, low exercise needs, temperament
The genetics rule: Health-test every breeding dog. Never breed an untested dog. Never breed a dog with a known genetic problem. Never breed two carriers of the same recessive gene. Genetics is the long game - play it right.
Practical exercise 11: Research the health tests required for your breed. Contact a vet or genetic testing lab and price the tests. Plan to test your current breeding stock over the next 6 months.
