Insulated Block (Foam Insert)

8 lessons · 30 quiz questions · Easy difficulty

1 Why insulate walls

In hot African climates, walls are the biggest source of heat gain. An uninsulated concrete wall can reach 40°C+ on a sunny afternoon, radiating heat into the room all evening.

Benefits of insulated walls:

  • 4–8°C cooler indoors in summer
  • Lower air conditioning costs (up to 40%)
  • Warm in winter (helps in highland areas)
  • Less noise transmission from outside
  • Fewer thermal cracks in the wall

Insulated blocks have an expanded polystyrene (EPS) foam insert placed in the hollow cores. The foam blocks heat flow through the wall — same principle as a cooler box.

💡 Insulated block is a growing market as energy costs rise and customers look for comfort.

2 Expanded polystyrene basics

Expanded polystyrene (EPS) is the same material used in packaging and coffee cups. It's 98% air, 2% plastic, and it's an excellent insulator.

Key properties:

  • Density: 15–30 kg/m³
  • Thermal conductivity (λ): 0.03–0.04 W/m·K — one of the best insulators available
  • Lightweight, cheap ($2–$5 per kg)
  • Chemically inert — doesn't react with cement
  • Rot-proof, moisture-resistant, insect-proof
  • Fire behaviour: melts and releases toxic smoke, but doesn't easily catch fire

EPS inserts are pre-shaped to fit the hollow cores of your blocks. Buy from packaging suppliers or have them custom-cut.

3 Insert sizes for hollow blocks

Inserts must match your hollow block cores.

For a 2-hole hollow block (440 × 215 × 150 mm):

  • Core size: 130 × 100 mm
  • Insert size: 128 × 98 mm
  • Insert thickness: 25–50 mm

For a 3-hole block: three smaller inserts per block.

Inserts are typically 25 mm (basic insulation) or 50 mm (premium). Thicker inserts = better insulation but tighter fit and higher cost.

Design option: have a custom EPS mould made to match your specific block. Cost: $500–$2,000 for the mould, then $0.20–$0.50 per insert.

4 Placing inserts during casting

Inserts are placed inside the mould before pouring the mix. Two methods:

Method A — Insert as core former: The EPS insert replaces the standard steel core former. The mix fills around it.

  • Simplest method
  • Requires custom-shaped inserts
  • Slower production (one insert per block)

Method B — Insert in hollow core: Standard hollow block is cast first. Insert is pushed into the hollow core afterwards.

  • Faster production
  • Uses standard hollow block moulds
  • Inserts can be added later, off-line

Method B is standard for small producers. Cast hollow blocks, cure them, and push in the inserts before delivery.

5 Adhesion between foam and concrete

Inserts should stay in place — not slide out during transport or in the wall.

How to secure inserts:

  • Snug fit — insert should require light pressure to push in
  • Adhesive dabs — small spots of construction adhesive on the insert sides
  • Mechanical keys — some inserts have ribs that lock into the core
  • End caps — a small plastic cap on the insert ends prevents slide

Most inserts just need a snug fit. If inserts are sliding out during transport, your moulds are slightly oversized or the inserts are undersized. Adjust by ordering a slightly larger insert.

6 Thermal performance

The insulation value is measured by R-value (thermal resistance) or U-value (thermal transmittance).

Comparison of 200 mm thick walls:

  • Solid concrete wall: U-value 6.0 W/m²K (poor)
  • Hollow block (uninsulated): U-value 3.0 W/m²K
  • Hollow block with 50 mm EPS insert: U-value 0.8 W/m²K (4× better)
  • Hollow block with 100 mm EPS: U-value 0.5 W/m²K

Lower U-value = better insulation. A 50 mm insert improves insulation by 4× — huge comfort impact.

Example: an office with insulated walls can cut AC cost by 30–40% compared to uninsulated walls.

7 Cost vs savings

Insulated blocks cost more to make. But the customer saves on energy over time.

Added cost per block:

  • EPS insert (25 mm): $0.20–$0.40
  • EPS insert (50 mm): $0.40–$0.70
  • Labour to insert: $0.05–$0.10

Added selling price: $0.80–$1.50 per block (2–3× the added cost).

Customer savings: Over 10 years, insulated walls save $2,000–$5,000 in air conditioning for a typical 3-bedroom house.

Pitch: "Pay $500 more on blocks, save $3,000 on electricity over 10 years." ROI is 3–4 years.

8 Selling energy efficiency

Insulated blocks sell to a specific customer profile — one who thinks long-term.

Who buys:

  • Homeowners building new houses (they're in it for 20+ years)
  • Commercial buildings with high AC costs
  • Hotels and lodges wanting guest comfort
  • Hospitals and clinics needing temperature stability
  • Eco-conscious clients
  • Green-building certification projects

Pricing (Africa, 2026):

  • Insulated block (25 mm insert): $1.80–$3.00 per block
  • Insulated block (50 mm insert): $2.20–$3.80 per block
  • Premium insulated (100 mm insert): $3.00–$5.00 per block

Cost to make: $0.80–$1.50 per block. Margin: 55–70%.

Selling tools:

  • Thermal demonstration — a heated box with uninsulated vs insulated wall showing temperature difference
  • Payback calculator showing 10-year savings
  • Photos of completed projects
  • Testimonials from customers reporting lower electricity bills
