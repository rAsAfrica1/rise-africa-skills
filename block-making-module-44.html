Mould Changing and Custom Shapes

8 lessons · 30 quiz questions · Business

1 Why moulds wear out

Every block wears the mould a little. Abrasive aggregate, high pressure, and constant cycling add up.

Main wear points:

  • Internal faces — abrasive aggregate polishes and rounds them
  • Edges — sharp corners become rounded
  • Bolts and fittings — vibration loosens them
  • Core formers (hollow blocks) — taper erodes
  • Pallet contact surfaces — scratch and warp

Wear speed factors:

  • Aggregate type — crushed granite wears more than rounded sand
  • Vibration time — longer vibration = more wear
  • Pressure — hydraulic press wears faster than vibration
  • Cycles per day — more production = faster wear
  • Cleaning habits — poor cleaning leaves dried concrete that scratches

Track your block count. When it hits 20,000 (standard) or 25,000 (premium), plan replacement.

2 Quick-release mould design

Modern moulds have quick-release features that make changing faster.

Features:

  • Bolted sides: 4 bolts per side instead of 12. Change in 15 minutes instead of 60.
  • Alignment pins: Automatic alignment — no re-levelling needed.
  • Quick-fit couplings: For hydraulic connections.
  • Colour-coded parts: Match mould to machine faster.
  • Lifting eyes: For crane or hoist lifting of heavy moulds.

Cost: Quick-release moulds cost 20–40% more than standard. Worth it if you change moulds more than once a week.

If you change moulds monthly, standard moulds are fine. If you offer 5+ block types, quick-release pays back in weeks.

3 Changing moulds safely

Moulds are heavy (50–300 kg). Safety is critical.

Safe changing procedure:

  1. Lock out the machine: Switch off power, put lock and tag
  2. Clear the area: No one else nearby
  3. Support the mould: Use a hoist, crane, or pallet jack for heavy moulds — never lift alone
  4. Disconnect fittings: Hydraulic, electrical, or mechanical
  5. Release bolts evenly: Loosen in a cross pattern, not one side at a time
  6. Remove old mould: Use proper lifting technique or hoist
  7. Clean the machine mount: Remove any debris before fitting new mould
  8. Fit new mould: Align pins first, then tighten bolts evenly
  9. Check alignment: Spirit level, and dry cycle test
  10. Restore power and test.

Never change a mould with the machine powered. LOTO (Lock Out, Tag Out) saves fingers and lives.

⚠ Two workers minimum for moulds over 80 kg. No exceptions.

4 Calibrating shape after change

After changing a mould, verify the blocks come out correct.

Calibration steps:

  1. Run 5 test blocks with the new mould
  2. Cool and cure 24 hours (or speed-cure)
  3. Measure each block: length, width, height, diagonal
  4. Compare to specification — within ±2 mm tolerance
  5. If out of tolerance, adjust mould position (shim) or bolt tightness
  6. Re-test after adjustment
  7. Log results in QC records

Common issues:

  • Blocks slightly larger: mould fill too high — reduce fill
  • Blocks slightly smaller: fill too low — increase fill
  • Uneven thickness: mould not level — adjust mounting bolts
  • Warped blocks: uneven vibration or worn mould — replace if needed

5 Custom moulds for clients

Some customers need blocks that aren't standard. Custom moulds let you serve them.

Common custom requests:

  • Special sizes (e.g. 500 × 250 × 150 mm for a specific wall)
  • Coloured edging blocks for a landscaper's project
  • Interlocking shapes for fast assembly
  • Logo embossed on blocks (church, project)
  • Lightweight block sizes for upper floors
  • Rounded corner blocks for feature walls

Cost of custom mould: $1,500–$6,000 depending on complexity. Only worth it for a large order (10,000+ blocks) or ongoing customer.

How to price: Add 30–50% premium to standard block price to cover mould cost amortised over the order.

6 Cost of new moulds

Moulds are a capital expense — plan and budget for them.

Standard mould costs:

  • Single-cavity block mould: $200–$800
  • 6-cavity block mould: $600–$2,000
  • 12-cavity block mould: $1,200–$3,500
  • Paver mould (6 cavity): $600–$1,800
  • Kerb mould (3 cavity): $400–$1,500
  • Split-face mould: $800–$2,500
  • Custom mould: $1,500–$6,000

Budget: 5–8% of annual revenue for moulds. If you produce 100,000 blocks/year, budget $5,000–$10,000 for mould replacement and additions.

Moulds shipped from China: 6–10 weeks. Order early to avoid stockouts.

7 Storage of unused moulds

Moulds sitting idle must be stored correctly or they rust and warp.

Storage rules:

  • Clean thoroughly: Remove all concrete — dried concrete holds moisture and rusts steel
  • Oil coat: Light coat of oil on all steel surfaces
  • Flat surface: Store on flat pallets or racking, not on the ground
  • Cover: Plastic sheet or tarpaulin — keeps dust and moisture off
  • Label: Mark each mould with its type and last use date
  • Rotate: Use stored moulds periodically so they don't sit indefinitely

Well-stored moulds last years between uses. Poorly stored moulds rust in months.

⚠ Never store moulds on bare dirt. The base plate warps from moisture and uneven ground.

8 Selling custom shapes

Custom shapes command premium prices and build customer loyalty.

Who buys custom shapes:

  • Architects with specific design requirements
  • High-end residential projects
  • Church and community buildings (often want logos)
  • Landscape designers (specific edging profiles)
  • Government projects (specific spec)
  • Industrial clients (specific dimensions)

Pricing custom shapes:

  • Small order (500–2,000 blocks): 50–80% premium
  • Medium (2,000–10,000): 30–50% premium
  • Large (10,000+): 20–30% premium

Selling tools:

  • Sample panel of custom shapes you've made before
  • Photos of custom installations
  • Clear pricing with mould cost broken out
  • Turnaround time commitment

Custom shapes are hard for competitors to copy quickly. This is a moat.

💰 Custom shapes = premium prices + loyal customers + competitive moat. Every custom mould adds to your capability. Build up over years.
