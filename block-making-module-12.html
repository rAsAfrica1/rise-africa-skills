Edging Block

8 lessons · 30 quiz questions · Easy difficulty

1 Edging vs kerb

Edging blocks are small, decorative borders. Kerbs are large, heavy road edging.

FeatureEdgingKerb
Length200-600 mm1 m
Weight5-15 kg45 kg
UseGarden borders, path edgesRoads, pavements
CustomerDIY, homeowners, landscapersContractors, councils

Edging is a garden product, kerb is a civil engineering product. Different markets, different pricing.

2 Edging mould sizes

Standard edging profiles:

  • Flat top: 600 × 150 × 50 mm — cheap, simple, common
  • Rounded (bullnose): 600 × 150 × 50 mm — premium look, more expensive mould
  • Sawtooth (serrated): 600 × 150 × 50 mm — decorative for flower beds
  • Palisade (vertical): Individual palisade blocks for border fencing

Multi-cavity moulds with 6–10 cavities per mould are standard. Fibreglass moulds work for edging (unlike kerbs). Cost: £80–£250 per mould.

Because edging is small and light, production is fast — 1,000+ per day on a simple vibrating table.

3 Simple edging mix

Edging doesn't need high strength — it never sees traffic. Standard mix:

  • Cement: 1 part
  • Sharp sand: 2.5 parts
  • Crushed stone (4–6 mm): 2.5 parts

Or even simpler for a low-cost edging:

  • Cement: 1 part
  • Sharp sand: 4 parts
  • No stone

Cement is about 10–12% of total weight. A 50 kg bag makes 35–45 edging blocks.

Coloured edging sells at premium — add 3–5% iron oxide pigment for red, terracotta, or charcoal edges.

💡 Because edging is small, small amounts of pigment go a long way. A £20 bag of pigment colours a week's production.

4 Batch production

Edging is a volume business. Efficiency matters:

  1. Mix a big batch in a pan mixer (batch of 200 kg = 80 blocks)
  2. Pour into multi-cavity moulds on a vibrating table
  3. Vibrate 3–5 seconds
  4. Level top with a straight edge
  5. Demould immediately after casting (edging is small — sets fast)
  6. Move pallets of green edging to curing area

Production rate: 3 people can make 800–1,200 edging blocks/day with a 6-cavity mould.

Because they're small, edge blocks can be stacked 20+ high on the pallet after 24 hours.

5 Handling fragile edges

Edging profiles are often thin and delicate (especially palisade and sawtooth).

Protection rules:

  • Cast on flat pallets that don't warp
  • Never stack green edging more than 4 high
  • Support the full length when lifting (never by the ends)
  • After 24 hours, stack higher but with wooden battens between layers
  • Wrap pallets in plastic for delivery — sliding edges chip

Every broken edging block is £1 lost. Train workers to handle them properly.

6 Curing quickly

Because they're thin, edging cures faster than blocks or kerbs.

  • 24 hours: initial set — move onto curing pallets
  • 3 days: 50% strength — can be stacked
  • 7 days: 80% strength — ready to sell
  • 28 days: 100% strength

Cover with plastic to keep damp. Spray 2× a day for 3 days. Water is cheap; cracked edging is expensive.

7 Retail packaging

Edging sells in retail packaging at hardware stores and garden centres.

Standard packs:

  • Single blocks — sold loose at hardware stores
  • 5-packs — small garden job
  • 10-packs — medium garden
  • 20-packs — large garden
  • 50-packs — trade/landscaper

Shrink-wrap or bundle with wire. Add a printed label with: length, colour, quantity, price, barcode.

Retail price: $1.50–$4 per 600 mm edging block, or $0.30–$0.80 per small palisade.

8 Garden centre sales

Garden centres and hardware stores are the primary retail channel.

How to get stocked:

  • Approach buyers with samples and pricing
  • Offer a starter pack deal (buy 50, get 10 free)
  • Provide attractive display stands (small investment, big lift)
  • Offer consignment for the first month (they pay for what sells)

Other channels:

  • Farmers' markets
  • Landscaper direct supply
  • Online (website, Facebook Marketplace)
  • Local hardware chains

Garden centres take 40–50% margin. Charge them $1.50–$2 per block; they sell at $3–$4.

💰 Edging is a small-margin, high-volume product. Best for building a steady cash flow. Perfect alongside larger products (pavers, kerbs).
