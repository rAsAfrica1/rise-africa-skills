Water Management - Getting Water to Where It Matters

🧠 Brain Opener

Seed crops need water at specific times. Too much and the seed rots. Too little and it never forms. Watering correctly is the difference between 30 kilograms per hectare and 70 kilograms per hectare.

  • How much water does a seed crop need?
  • What is the cheapest irrigation method?
  • How do you avoid watering during the wrong stage?
🎁 Reveal a strong answer

A vegetable seed crop needs 25 to 40 millimetres of water per week, adjusted for rainfall. The cheapest irrigation method for small plots is a watering can or a drip system made from plastic bottles. You must reduce watering when the seed is maturing - too much water then causes seeds to rot or sprout in the pod.

📖 Tendai's Drip System

Tendai was twenty-one and had half an acre of organic kale seed. He watered every day with a bucket. It took him three hours each morning. He was exhausted.

His neighbour had a drip system. It watered automatically. It cost 400 dollars to install.

Tendai could not afford 400 dollars. So he invented something.

He collected fifty plastic bottles. He punched three small holes in the cap of each. He buried the bottle neck-down next to each plant, leaving the base above ground.

Every morning he filled each bottle. The water dripped slowly into the soil all day. The plants got steady moisture. He spent thirty minutes filling instead of three hours pouring.

His total cost: 5 dollars for the bottles and caps.

But the real benefit came during seed maturation. Drip systems let him reduce water slowly, without shock. His kale seed matured evenly, with 92 percent germination rate.

His neighbour with the 400-dollar drip system got the same result. Tendai had spent 5 dollars.

The next season, Tendai added a second row of bottle drips. The season after, he had a full half-acre under bottle drip irrigation. Total cost: 15 dollars.

He now teaches the method to other farmers for 5 dollars per class.

💡 The Big Idea

Water right and the seed rights itself

Different growth stages need different amounts of water. Seedlings need a little, often. Flowering plants need a lot, regularly. Maturing seeds need less, gradually. Understanding these three stages and matching your watering to them is the whole art of irrigation.

📚 Three Deep Lessons

1

Water needs by growth stage

Germination and seedling. Light watering 2 to 3 times a day. The soil surface should stay moist, not wet. Too much water at this stage rots the seed.

Vegetative growth and flowering. Heavy watering 2 to 3 times per week. This is when the plant needs the most water. Flowers are 90 percent water and cannot form properly if water is short.

Seed maturation. Reduce watering gradually over 3 weeks. Stop watering 5 to 7 days before harvest. This is the most important stage and the one most often done wrong.

2

Irrigation methods, from cheapest to most expensive

Watering can. 5 dollars. Works for plots under 100 square metres. Three hours per day for 1,000 square metres.

Bottle drip. 5 dollars for 50 bottles. Works for plots under 500 square metres. Thirty minutes per day.

Bucket drip system. 50 to 100 dollars. Bucket with holes plus tubing. Covers 200 to 500 square metres. Fifteen minutes per day.

Drip irrigation kit. 300 to 800 dollars. Covers 1,000 to 2,000 square metres. Five minutes per day. Lasts 5 to 8 years.

Sprinkler system. 500 to 1,500 dollars. Covers 1,000 to 5,000 square metres. Wastes more water than drip.

Solar-powered pump. 800 to 2,000 dollars. Covers 5,000 to 20,000 square metres. No fuel cost.

3

Rainwater harvesting

If you have a roof, you have a water source. A 20 square metre roof collects 20 litres of water for every millimetre of rain. In a season with 500 millimetres of rain, that is 10,000 litres.

A single 5,000-litre tank costs 200 to 400 dollars. Two tanks give you enough water to irrigate 500 square metres through a dry spell.

Gutters, first-flush diverters, and a simple mesh filter complete the system. Total setup: 300 to 600 dollars for a basic 10,000-litre system that lasts 15 years.

🛠 Hands-On - Build a Bottle Drip System

You need

  • 10 plastic bottles with caps
  • A needle or small nail
  • A knife
  • Your plot with a row of plants
  1. Punch three small holes in each bottle cap.
  2. Cut the base off each bottle (or leave it and use the cap as a watering point).
  3. Bury the bottle neck-down next to each plant, leaving the base above ground.
  4. Fill each bottle with water in the morning.
  5. Watch how fast the water drips out. Adjust the number of holes if needed.
  6. Track how many days a full bottle lasts.
  7. Compare plant growth to plants watered by hand.
Why it works: Bottle drip irrigation triples the efficiency of your watering and costs almost nothing.

🏆 Project Challenge

Set Up a Bottle Drip Test

Compare bottle drip to hand watering for one month.

  1. Pick two rows of similar plants - Row A and Row B.
  2. Water Row A by hand as usual.
  3. Set up bottle drip on Row B.
  4. Water both rows the same total amount, but deliver differently.
  5. Measure plant height every week.
  6. After 4 weeks, compare the two rows.
  7. Write down which was taller and how much water each used.
  8. Decide if the bottle drip is worth continuing.
