6. Harvest, Curing and Storage

Curing is the step almost everyone skips. Do it right and your onions store for 6 months.

Module 6 of 6 · Onion Farming for Profit

When to harvest

  • 50-70% of tops have fallen over
  • Necks are soft, not stiff
  • Outer skin is dry and papery
  • Bulbs are firm and full-size
  • 120-150 days after transplant

Harvest when 50-70% of tops have fallen - do not wait for 100%. Late harvest reduces storage life.

Harvesting technique

  1. Loosen the soil with a fork - do not pull.
  2. Lift bulbs by hand or with a fork, leaving tops attached.
  3. Do not bruise or cut the bulbs.
  4. Leave bulbs in the field on top of the soil, tops covering the bulbs, for 7-14 days.
  5. Turn once after 3-4 days to dry the other side.
  6. If rain threatens, dry under cover instead.

The two-stage curing process

Curing is the drying process that develops the papery skin and seals the neck. Without it, onions rot in storage.

StageDurationWhere
Field drying7-14 daysIn the field, on top of soil
Shade curing2-3 weeksIn a shaded, well-ventilated store

After curing:

  1. Trim tops to 2 cm above the bulb.
  2. Trim roots close to the bulb.
  3. Do not cut into the bulb - that opens a door for rot.
  4. Remove any loose skin or dirt.
  5. Discard bulbs with thick necks or soft spots.

Storage

Never store onion in sealed bags or plastic. They need airflow. Sealed bags = rot within weeks.

Price timing

Onion prices are lowest at harvest (gluts) and highest 3-5 months later (lean season). The price difference can be 2-3x.

MonthPrice index

The storage rule: Onion is the only vegetable where storage properly doubles or triples your income. Cure it and sell into the lean season.
