1. Promoting Listings on Social Media

Social media is your free billboard. In Africa, Facebook, Instagram, and WhatsApp are where buyers spend time. Start by creating a business page on Facebook and Instagram. Use your phone to take clear photos of your products on a plain background (a white wall works). Post at least 3 times a week. Mix product photos, short videos (15-30 seconds) showing the item in use, and customer testimonials. Use hashtags like #ShopNigeria, #JumiaFinds, or #NairobiFashion to reach local buyers. Join Facebook groups for buying and selling in your city. For example, 'Lagos Buy and Sell' or 'Accra Online Market'. Post your items there with a price and 'DM to order'. Respond to comments and messages within 1 hour. Use WhatsApp Business to set up a catalog and auto-replies. Share your listings in WhatsApp status and groups. Offer a small discount for customers who share your post. Track which posts get the most engagement using Facebook Insights. Double down on what works. Remember, consistency beats perfection. Even a simple post with a clear price and a call to action can bring sales.

2. Using eBay and Amazon Ads

If you sell on eBay or Amazon, paid ads can boost your visibility. Start with eBay Promoted Listings. You set an ad rate (percentage of sale price) â typically 2-5% for most categories. eBay only charges when your item sells, so it's low risk. For Amazon, use Sponsored Products. You bid on keywords buyers search for. Start with a daily budget of $5 (about 4,000 Naira or 60 Rand). Use automatic targeting first to let Amazon find keywords, then switch to manual after 2 weeks. Keep your ad rate competitive: if you're not getting impressions, increase your bid by 10% every few days. Monitor your ACoS (Advertising Cost of Sale). Aim for ACoS below 30% â meaning you spend 30 cents for every dollar of sales. If ACoS is higher, lower your bid or improve your listing. Use negative keywords to avoid wasted spend. For example, if you sell phone cases, add 'free' as a negative keyword. Always ensure your product images are high quality and your title includes key search terms. Ads work best when your organic listing is already optimized. Test with a small budget, then scale what works.

3. Email Marketing for Resellers

Email marketing is not dead â it's a direct line to repeat buyers. Start by collecting emails. Offer a 10% discount code for signing up. Use a free tool like Mailchimp (up to 500 subscribers) or Brevo (formerly Sendinblue). Add a sign-up form to your social media bio and your online store. When a customer buys, ask for their email. Send a welcome email immediately with your best-selling items and the discount code. Then, send a newsletter every 2 weeks. Include new arrivals, special offers, and useful tips (e.g., 'How to care for your leather bag'). Personalize with the customer's name. Segment your list: buyers vs. window shoppers. Send buyers a 'thank you' email 3 days after delivery and ask for a review. For window shoppers, send a cart abandonment email after 24 hours. In Africa, mobile is key â make sure your emails are mobile-friendly. Use clear, large buttons. Test subject lines: 'Your 10% off expires tonight' works better than 'Newsletter'. Track open rates and click-through rates. Aim for 20% open rate. Clean your list every 3 months by removing people who haven't opened in 6 months. Consistency builds trust and sales.

4. Cross-Promoting Across Platforms

Don't put all your eggs in one basket. Cross-promotion means using one platform to drive traffic to another. For example, post a TikTok video of you packing an order, then say 'Link in bio to shop'. On Instagram, share a photo of a product and tell followers to 'Swipe up' to your WhatsApp catalog. On Facebook, create a post that links to your eBay listing. Use the same handle across all platforms for easy recognition. When you make a sale on Jumia, include a thank-you card with your Instagram handle and a 5% discount code for their next purchase on your own website. Run a contest: 'Follow us on Instagram and tag a friend to win a free phone case'. Use WhatsApp status to announce a flash sale on your Facebook page. Create a Linktree (free) to house all your links in one place. Share your YouTube videos on LinkedIn if you sell B2B. Cross-promotion works because different platforms attract different audiences. A buyer on Facebook might not know you're on TikTok. By cross-promoting, you increase touchpoints and build brand recall. Track which platform sends the most traffic using UTM links (use Google's Campaign URL Builder). Double down on the best performers.

5. Running Sales and Discounts

Discounts can boost sales, but use them wisely. A common mistake is to discount too often, which trains customers to wait for sales. Instead, plan your promotions around events: Black Friday, Christmas, Ramadan, or back-to-school. Announce your sale 3 days in advance on social media and email. Use a countdown timer on your posts. For example, '24 hours only: 20% off all shoes'. Set a clear start and end time. Use a discount code like 'AFRICA20' to track sales. On eBay, you can run a 'Markdown Sale' event â eBay will promote it. On Amazon, use 'Coupons' or 'Lightning Deals'. For your own website, use WooCommerce or Shopify's discount features. Don't discount your best-sellers too much â you'll lose profit. Instead, bundle slow-moving items with popular ones: 'Buy a phone case, get a screen protector 50% off'. Offer free shipping instead of a discount â it often feels more valuable. After the sale, send a 'thank you' email to buyers and a 'sorry you missed it' email to non-buyers with a smaller offer. Track your profit margin before and after. A sale that increases revenue but kills profit is not a win. Test small discounts (10%) first, then increase if needed.

6. Leveraging Influencers and Affiliates

Influencers can put your products in front of thousands of potential buyers. Start with micro-influencers (1,000 to 10,000 followers) â they are cheaper and often have higher engagement. In Africa, many micro-influencers charge $20 to $50 (about 15,000 to 40,000 Naira) per post. Look for influencers in your niche: fashion, tech, home goods. Send them a free product in exchange for an honest review. Use a simple contract: they post 1 photo and 1 story, tag you, and share a discount code. Track sales using their unique code. For affiliates, use a platform like ShareASale or Refersion. You can also run a simple affiliate program manually: give your best customers a 10% commission for every sale they refer. Use a Google Form to sign them up. Provide them with ready-made posts and images. Pay them via mobile money (M-Pesa, MTN MoMo) or PayPal. Set a cookie duration of 30 days so you get credit for delayed purchases. Always disclose paid partnerships â it's required by law in many countries and builds trust. Start with 3-5 influencers, measure results, and scale the ones that bring sales. Don't just look at likes â look at clicks and conversions.

Practical Reference: Marketing and Sales Strategies for African Resellers

This practical reference is for African resellers who source products (locally or via import) and sell on social platforms, eBay, Amazon, Jumia, Konga, or their own store. The goal is simple: get more qualified buyers to your listings, convert them profitably, and keep repeat customers. Below are the tools, benchmarks, and checklists you can apply immediately.

1. Promoting Listings on Social Media (Africa-First)

Social commerce is the dominant discovery channel in Africa. WhatsApp, Facebook, Instagram, TikTok, and increasingly Telegram drive direct sales. Key rules:

  • Lead with video, not static photos. Short vertical videos (15â30s) showing the product in use outperform catalogs.
  • Use local language and pricing. Quote prices in local currency and mention delivery terms (e.g., "Nairobi CBD pickup" or "Lagos mainland delivery").
  • Post consistently. 3â5 posts per week per platform; repurpose one video across TikTok, Reels, and Status.
  • Use WhatsApp Business. Catalog, quick replies, and labels for leads, paid, and shipped.
  • Add payment trust signals. Show M-Pesa, Paystack, Flutterwave, or bank transfer options.

3. Email Marketing for Resellers

Email is underused by African resellers but delivers the highest ROI when done right. Build a list from checkout, WhatsApp opt-ins, and lead magnets (e.g., "10 Import Mistakes to Avoid").

  • Use free tools: Mailchimp (free up to 500 contacts), Brevo, or MailerLite.
  • Send 2â4 emails per month: new arrivals, restock alerts, discount codes, and buyer education.
  • Segment by country, product interest, and past purchase.
  • Always include a clear unsubscribe link (legal requirement under GDPR, POPIA, and Nigeria's NDPR).

4. Cross-Promoting Across Platforms

Cross-promotion multiplies reach without extra ad spend. Practical tactics:

  • Pin your eBay/Amazon store link in TikTok and Instagram bios.
  • Add WhatsApp click-to-chat links in every social post.
  • Include a "Find us on Jumia/Konga" card in every shipped order.
  • Repurpose eBay listings into WhatsApp catalog items.
  • Use link-in-bio tools (Linktree, Beacons) to host all store links.
