5. Quality Control

Your reputation is built on every block you sell. One bad batch damages it forever in a small market. This module shows you how to test, standardise, and defend the quality of every block that leaves your yard.

Module 5 of 7 · 6 lessons · ~50 minutes · 6 practical exercises · quiz at the end

Lesson 5.1 — Visual inspection checklist

Every block should pass a visual check before it leaves the curing area. This takes 5 seconds per block and catches most quality problems.

CheckPassFail
CracksNoneAny visible crack, especially on edges
ChipsMinor surface onlyChips on any load-bearing face
WarpingFlat and trueBent, twisted, or curved
ColourUniformPatches or uneven tones
CornersSharp, squareRounded or broken
Weight feelConsistentMuch lighter than normal (weak mix)
Surface textureSmooth or evenPitted, honeycombed, or crumbly
Reject immediately: any block with cracks on a load-bearing face, warping, or visibly crumbly corners. These will fail on the customer's site and destroy your reputation.
Practical exercise 5.1: Set up a QC table at the end of your curing line. Every block passes over it. Reject pile to the left, good pile to the right. Count rejects daily.

Lesson 5.2 — Dimension consistency

Blocks that vary in size cause problems for builders. Mortar joints become uneven, walls go crooked, wastage rises.

Standard tolerance: ±2 mm on length, width, height. Some markets accept ±3 mm; high-end construction demands ±1 mm.

How to test:

  1. Take 10 blocks from each batch.
  2. Measure each with a steel tape.
  3. Record length, width, height.
  4. Calculate maximum deviation from the standard.

If any block exceeds ±2 mm, the batch needs checking. Common causes:

  • Worn mould (replace or refurbish)
  • Inconsistent mix (dry or wet spots)
  • Uneven filling of mould
  • Improper compression pressure
The dimension rule: Consistency matters more than exact size. A slightly off-standard block that is identical to all the others is more useful than a standard-size block that varies 5 mm from one to the next.
Practical exercise 5.2: Measure 20 blocks from your last batch. Note the max deviation. If it exceeds ±2 mm, find the cause before making more.

Lesson 5.3 — Strength testing

Strength is the single most important quality measure. Under-loaded blocks crack and fall. Overly strong blocks waste cement.

TestHowWhat it tells you
Drop testDrop from 1 m onto concreteImpact strength, rough indication
Compression test (lab)Send to local university or labMPa — definitive strength
Break test (field)Support at both ends, load in centre until breakRough flexural strength
Water absorptionWeigh dry, soak 24h, weigh wetPorosity and durability

Target compressive strength:

ApplicationMinimum MPaStandard block at 1:6
Non-load-bearing partition2.5 MPaComfortable
Standard walls3.5 MPaTarget zone
Load-bearing walls5.0+ MPaRequires 1:5 or better
Foundation / structural7.0+ MPaRequires 1:4 or 1:5

How to use lab testing: send 3 samples from every 5,000 blocks to a lab. Cost is $5–$20 per sample. This confirms your production is consistent. If any batch fails, quarantine and re-test.

The strength rule: For anything customer-facing, target 3.5 MPa minimum. For load-bearing, 5.0 MPa. Never sell below 2.5 MPa, even for partition walls.
Practical exercise 5.3: Find a lab near you that tests blocks. Send 3 samples. Keep the report. Do this every month for the first 6 months.

Lesson 5.4 — Water absorption and durability

Porosity determines how blocks behave in weather. High absorption means weaker, less durable blocks that can crumble in heavy rain or crack in frost.

Standard target: under 12% by weight for cement blocks. Some international standards require under 10%.

Simple test:

  1. Weigh a dry block. Record weight.
  2. Soak in water for 24 hours.
  3. Remove, pat dry, weigh again.
  4. (Wet weight − dry weight) / dry weight × 100 = % absorption.

Common causes of high absorption:

  • Lean mix (too much sand, too little cement)
  • Inadequate curing (blocks dried out too fast)
  • Poor compaction (moulded with too little pressure)
  • Dirty sand (clay content increases porosity)
Practical exercise 5.4: Run the water absorption test on 3 blocks from your current batch. If it exceeds 12%, adjust your mix or curing. Re-test after changes.

Lesson 5.5 — Handling customer complaints

Every business will have complaints. How you handle them determines whether the customer stays or leaves forever — and whether others hear about it.

The three-rule response:

  1. Acknowledge first, investigate second. "I hear you. Let me look into it." Do not defend yourself before you have seen the problem.
  2. Replace or refund without argument. If the customer is unhappy with a batch, replace it. Even if you think they mishandled it. One replacement costs you $50 — losing the customer and their network costs $5,000.
  3. Fix the cause. After resolving the customer's issue, find out why it happened. Was it a bad mix? A new worker? A changed supplier? Fix the root cause so it does not happen again.
Complaint typeResponse
Broken blocks on deliveryReplace the broken units immediately, no charge
Wrong size deliveredSwap for correct size, cover transport both ways
Blocks failing on siteVisit the site, assess, replace if your batch is at fault
Late deliveryApologise, compensate with a small discount on next order
Colour mismatchFor decorative blocks — remanufacture or offer discount
The complaint rule: A complaint handled well creates a more loyal customer than one who never had a problem. Make it easy for customers to complain — and resolve fast.
Practical exercise 5.5: Write a simple complaint response script. "Thank you for telling us. We will [action] by [date]. I am sorry for the inconvenience." Memorise it. Use it every time.

Lesson 5.6 — Building a quality brand

In the brick market, "quality" is what you say about yourself — but "reliability" is what your customers say about you. Reliability beats quality claims every time.

Six things that build a reputation for reliability:

  • Consistent dimension. Every block the same size.
  • Consistent strength. Every batch passes the drop test.
  • Consistent delivery. You arrive when you promised.
  • Consistent price. Same customer, same price, every time.
  • Fast response. Enquiries answered within 5 minutes.
  • Honest problems. If something goes wrong, you say so first.

Signs of a reputation building:

  • Contractors ask for "the blocks from [your yard]" by name
  • Referrals arrive without you asking
  • Repeat orders from the same contractors
  • You start getting orders from neighbouring districts
  • You can charge 5–10% more than competitors and still sell
The brand premium: Once you have a reputation for reliability, you can charge 5–15% more than competitors. For a 10,000-block/month business, that premium is $500–$1,200 extra profit — every month.
Practical exercise 5.6: List 3 things you will commit to always doing (dimension, delivery, price) and 1 thing you will never compromise on (cement ratio). Write them on the wall of your yard.
