11. Record Keeping and Business Management

You cannot manage what you do not measure. This is how to know if your farm is making money.

Module 11 of 12 · Guinea Fowl and Quail Farming

Why records matter

Most small poultry farmers cannot say what their birds actually cost to raise. Without records, you guess. With records, you know - and you make decisions based on numbers, not feelings.

The six records you must keep

RecordWhat to writeHow often
Stock registerDate, source, number, cost per chickEvery batch
Feed registerDate, type, kg, costDaily
Health registerVaccines, treatments, deaths, symptomsEvery event
Egg productionEggs collected per day, cracked, soldDaily
Sales registerBuyer, kg or number, price, total, paid?Every sale
Expense registerLabour, fuel, tools, repairs, miscEvery purchase

Simple monthly report

Once a month, calculate:

MetricHow to calculateTarget
Mortality %Deaths ÷ starting birds × 100Under 5%
Feed per bird per dayTotal feed ÷ birds ÷ daysSpecies-specific
Cost per birdTotal cost ÷ birds soldUnder $16 (guinea)
Egg production %Eggs ÷ hens ÷ days × 10060-70% (guinea), 65-75% (quail)
Cost per eggTotal feed cost ÷ eggsUnder $0.15
Revenue per birdTotal sales ÷ birds soldMatch market
Net profitRevenue - expensesPositive every month

Guinea fowl has a 6-7 month wait for first income. Quail has income in 6-8 weeks. If cash flow is tight, start with quail while guinea fowl grows.

Tax and compliance

  • Register the business once you exceed household needs. Small-holder registration is usually free or low-cost.
  • VAT - only when you exceed the threshold in your country.
  • Food safety - for selling to restaurants and shops, food handling certificates are needed.
  • Animal movement permits for cross-district sales.
  • Keep receipts of all input purchases - they are deductible.
The records rule: A farm without records is a hobby. Ten minutes a day of bookkeeping tells you which birds make money, which buyers pay best, and which costs you can cut.
Practical exercise 11: Start a monthly P&L notebook. Record all revenue and expenses for the next 30 days. Calculate net profit at month-end. Do this for 3 months to see trends.
