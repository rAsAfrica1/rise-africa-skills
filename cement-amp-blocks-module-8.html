Lessons

1. Identifying who buys blocks: builders, NGOs, and homeowners

Before you make a single block for sale, you must know who will buy it. In most African towns, there are three main groups: builders (contractors and fundis), NGOs, and homeowners. Each buys differently.

Builders are your biggest customers. They buy in bulk500, 1000, or 5000 blocks at a time. They care about price per block, consistent quality, and delivery speed. A builder working on a school or church project needs blocks on site every morning. If you can supply 500 blocks daily at a fair price, you become their go-to. Builders often pay cash or a 50% deposit. They will inspect your blocks for cracks and correct curing.

NGOs and government projects buy through tenders or quotations. They need receipts, sometimes a registered business, and they pay by cheque or bank transfer after delivery. A single NGO order can be 2000 blocks for a health centre. To win them, you must provide a written quotation with your price per block, delivery cost, and timeline. NGOs value reliability and often ask for samples first. They may also require blocks that meet specific strength standards.

Homeowners buy smaller numbers50 to 300 blocksfor a house, a boundary wall, or a pit latrine. They pay cash and often carry blocks themselves or hire a pickup. Homeowners care about appearance (smooth edges, uniform colour) and personal service. They will tell neighbours about you if you treat them well. A homeowner who buys 100 blocks today may buy 500 next year.

To find these buyers, visit building sites, talk to fundis at hardware stores, and leave your number with local chiefs and church leaders. Keep a notebook with names, phone numbers, and what they bought. Your first 10 customers will come from people you already know. Ask each one: Who else do you know who needs blocks? That question alone can double your sales.

2. Creating simple flyers and WhatsApp messages to advertise

You do not need a computer or a professional designer to make a flyer. A flyer is just a piece of paper that tells people what you sell, where you are, and how to reach you. Use a pen and a ruler to make a neat template, then photocopy it at a local shop for 100 shillings per page. Print 100 copiesthat is enough to start.

Your flyer must have five things: (1) Your business name, e.g., Kato Blocks & Cement. (2) What you sell: 6-inch and 4-inch blocks, cured for 14 days. (3) Your price: 6-inch block = 2,500 UGX. (4) Your location: Near Kalerwe Market, after the mosque. (5) Your phone number and WhatsApp. Add a simple drawing of a block with a checkmark. Keep it black and white to save money.

WhatsApp is your most powerful free tool. Create a broadcast list of every builder, fundi, and homeowner you meet. Send one message per week, not more. A good message is short: Hello, this is Kato Blocks. We have 2000 fresh 6-inch blocks ready. Price 2,500 each. Free delivery within 5 km. Call 0777-123456. Add a photo of your blocks stacked neatly. Photos sell better than words.

Use WhatsApp Status to show your work. Post a 10-second video of your block-making machine in action, or a customer loading blocks onto a truck. Post at 7 AM and 6 PM when people check their phones. Add a caption: Order today, get blocks tomorrow.

Do not spam people. If someone says stop, remove them. Instead, ask happy customers to forward your message to their friends. Offer a small reward: If you bring a friend who buys 100 blocks, you get 10 blocks free. This turns your customers into your advertising team. A single flyer or WhatsApp message can bring you 5 new orders in a week if it is clear and honest.

3. Showing samples and inviting customers to your yard

People buy what they can see and touch. Your yard is your showroom. Even if it is small, make it look professional. Clear the grass, stack blocks neatly by size, and keep a few display blocks on a wooden table. Display blocks should be clean, smooth, and free of cracks. Paint one block half-red and half-white to show your colour options if you offer coloured blocks.

When a customer arrives, greet them and offer a seat. Give them a sample block to hold. Say: Feel the weight. Tap itit rings like a bell. That means it is well-cured and strong. Show them how a block fits together with another block. If you have a hammer, break a reject block to show the inside is solid, not hollow. This builds trust.

Invite customers to your yard through WhatsApp and flyers. Write: Visit our yard at [location] any day between 8 AM and 5 PM. See our blocks, touch them, and get a free sample piece. For builders, offer to deliver a free sample of 5 blocks to their site. For homeowners, offer a 10% discount if they come to the yard themselves.

Keep a visitor book. Write down every persons name, phone number, and what they asked about. Follow up within two days: Hello, this is Kato. You visited my yard on Tuesday. Did you like the blocks? I can deliver 200 blocks tomorrow. This follow-up turns a visitor into a buyer.

Make your yard easy to find. Put a signboard at the nearest main road with an arrow: Blocks 200m. If you are near a market, tell the market chairman to announce your yard. A clean, well-organised yard with samples ready will sell more blocks than any radio advert. Customers trust what they see with their own eyes.

4. Building relationships with local hardware stores and contractors

Hardware stores and contractors are your best salespeople. They already have customers who need blocks. Your job is to make it easy for them to sell your blocks and make a profit.

Start with hardware stores. Visit every hardware store within 5 km of your yard. Ask to speak to the owner. Say: I make quality blocks. I will give you a 10% commission on every block you sell for me. You do not pay me until the customer pays you. Leave 5 sample blocks with a price list. The store owner will display your blocks next to their cement and iron sheets. When a customer buys cement, the owner says, You also need blocks? I have Katos blocks2,500 each. You only pay commission after the sale. This costs you nothing upfront.

Contractors are different. They buy directly from you, but they need credit and reliability. Offer a contractor a special price: If you buy 1000 blocks, I will give you 2,300 per block and free delivery. Ask for a 50% deposit. Deliver on time, every time. If you promise blocks at 8 AM, be there at 7:30 AM. One late delivery can lose a contractor forever. Build a relationship by visiting their site, asking about their project, and solving problems. If they need 200 blocks urgently, find a way to supply them even if you have to borrow from another maker.

Keep a list of all hardware stores and contractors. Call them every two weeks. Ask: Do you have any customers who need blocks this week? Send them a WhatsApp message with a photo of your latest stack. Remember their birthdays or important days. Small giftsa soda, a packet of biscuitsbuild loyalty.

Never bypass a hardware store to sell directly to their customer. That destroys trust. Instead, thank the store owner and give them their commission quickly. A good relationship with three hardware stores and two contractors can keep you busy full-time. Treat them as partners, not just buyers.

5. Using word-of-mouth and testimonials to get more orders

Word-of-mouth is the cheapest and most powerful marketing. In Africa, people trust their neighbours more than adverts. One happy customer can bring you ten new ones. Your job is to make every customer happy and then ask them to tell others.

After you deliver blocks, wait two days and call the customer. Ask: Did the blocks work well? Are you satisfied? If they say yes, ask for a testimonial. Say: Can I write down what you said and use it to show other customers? Write their exact words: Katos blocks are strong. My wall has no cracks. Add their name and village. Keep a notebook of testimonials. When a new customer visits your yard, show them the notebook. Say: Look, Mr. Ochieng from Bweyogerere bought 500 blocks. This is what he said. That is more convincing than any flyer.

Ask for referrals directly. After a happy customer pays, say: If you know one person who needs blocks, give them my number. If they buy 100 blocks, I will give you 10 blocks free. This reward system works. Most people will tell at least one friend. Give the reward only after the new customer pays. Keep a record of who referred whom.

Use WhatsApp groups. Join local community groups, church groups, and savings groups. Do not spam. Instead, post a photo of a finished wall built with your blocks and write: This wall was built with my blocks. The owner is happy. Call me for your blocks. When someone comments, reply politely and ask them to visit your yard.

Create a simple customer of the month photo. Take a picture of a happy customer standing next to their new wall. Post it on your WhatsApp Status and on a notice board at the market. Ask the customer for permission first. A real face and a real wall sell more than a thousand words. Word-of-mouth grows slowly at first, then suddenly. Keep every customer happy, and your order book will fill itself.

6. Handling customer complaints and returns professionally

Complaints are not attacksthey are chances to keep a customer for life. How you handle a complaint matters more than the original mistake. Always stay calm, listen, and never argue.

When a customer complains, first say: Thank you for telling me. I am sorry you are not happy. Let me fix it. Do not blame the customer or the weather. Ask questions: Which blocks are broken? How many? Can I see them? If the customer brings back broken blocks, count them. If it is your faultfor example, you sold under-cured blocksreplace them free of charge. Give new blocks immediately, even if you have to take them from another order. A customer who gets a free replacement will tell everyone you are honest.

Set a clear return policy and write it on your receipt. For example: We replace any block that breaks within 7 days of delivery if it was not damaged by the customer. Bring the broken block to our yard. This protects you from people who break blocks themselves and want free ones. If a customer claims a block broke during normal use, ask to see the wall. If the block was laid properly and still broke, replace it. If the customer dropped it or used it as a hammer, politely explain that is not covered.

For large orders, offer a discount on the next order instead of a cash refund. Say: I cannot refund cash, but I will give you 50 free blocks on your next order of 500. This keeps the money in your business and keeps the customer coming back. Always follow up after solving a complaint: Hello, did the new blocks work well? Are you happy now? If they say yes, ask them to tell others.

Keep a complaint book. Write down the date, customer name, problem, and how you solved it. Look for patterns. If three customers complain about the same size block, fix your mould. Complaints are free lessons. Handle them professionally, and your reputation will grow stronger than any advert.

<h2>Practical Reference: Marketing and Selling Your Blocks</h2>

<p>In African cement and block enterprises, production capacity is rarely the main constraint on growth. Demand generation is. A block yard with 2,000 units in stock and no order book is a cash-flow crisis waiting to happen, while a yard that sells out weekly can reinvest in moulds, curing space, and transport. This practical reference consolidates the six lessons in this module into a working system you can apply from the first day of operation. It assumes you are producing solid or hollow cement blocks, whether manually with a vibrating table or semi-automatically, and selling into a local or regional market.</p>

<p>Marketing blocks is not the same as marketing a branded consumer product. Your buyers are practical, price-sensitive, and risk-averse. They care about block strength, dimensional consistency, delivery reliability, and whether you will still be in business when they need a replacement. The system below is built around those realities: identify the buyer, communicate clearly, prove quality with samples, build a repeat network, and handle problems without losing the relationship.</p>

<h2>1. Who Buys Your Blocks and What They Actually Want</h2>

<p>Your market is not "everyone who needs blocks." It is three distinct segments, each with different buying triggers, payment behaviour, and quality expectations. Builders and contractors buy in volume, negotiate hard, and care about delivery timing and consistent strength. NGOs, churches, and community projects buy against a budget line, require quotations and sometimes receipts for accountability, and often pay on delivery or after milestone approval. Homeowners buy in smaller quantities, are more sensitive to unit price and appearance, and often decide based on a neighbour's recommendation or a WhatsApp photo.</p>

<h2>2. Simple Flyers and WhatsApp Messages That Get Replies</h2>

<p>You do not need a graphic designer. A flyer that works in an African block market has five elements: business name and location, block types and sizes, price per unit (or "from" price), a phone number that is answered, and one proof point such as "over 50,000 blocks delivered since 2021." Print A5 size, double-sided if you can afford it, and distribute at hardware stores, building sites, churches, and market days.</p>

<p>WhatsApp is your highest-return channel because it is free, visual, and already used by nearly every buyer. A working format is: one clear photo of stacked blocks, a short caption with price and location, and a call to action. For example: "Solid 6-inch blocks, KES 55 each. Yard at Kiambu Road. Free delivery over 500 blocks. Call 07XX XXX XXX." Post the same message to your status every two to three days, and save every customer's number under a tag like "Block Buyer Kiambu."</p>

<div style="border:2px solid #b00020; background:#fff5f5; padding:14px; margin:18px 0; font-family:Arial, sans-serif;">
<strong style="color:#b00020;"> SAFETY AND COMPLIANCE WARNING</strong>
<p style="margin:8px 0 0 0;">Never advertise a block strength or load-bearing capacity you have not actually tested. In many African markets, building codes and local authorities require blocks to meet a minimum compressive strength (often 3.57 N/mm for hollow blocks and higher for load-bearing walls). Selling under-strength blocks for structural walls has caused wall collapses, injuries, and deaths, and exposes you to criminal liability and business closure. If you claim "high strength," keep test records from a recognised laboratory or a documented cube-crush test. Also confirm your local council's registration, environmental, and workplace safety requirements before scaling up.</p>
</div>

<h2>3. Samples, Yard Visits, and the Power of Touch</h2>

<p>Blocks are a tactile product. Buyers want to lift one, check the corners, and see whether it rings when tapped. Invite every serious enquirer to your yard. Keep a "sample stack" of your best blocks near the entrance, with a small sign showing the size, price, and curing date. Offer a free single block to contractors and hardware stores as a trial the cost is trivial compared with the value of a repeat order.</p>

<p>During a yard visit, walk the customer through your curing process. Explaining that blocks are water-cured for at least 714 days builds confidence and justifies your price against cheaper, uncured competitors. Give them a written quotation on the spot, and follow up on WhatsApp within 24 hours.</p>

<h2>4. Building Relationships with Hardware Stores and Contractors</h2>

<p>Hardware stores are a distribution channel, not just a customer. A store that stocks your blocks gives you shelf space, credibility, and a steady trickle of homeowner buyers. Offer a trade price that leaves the retailer a 1525% margin, agree a restock rhythm, and provide a small branded sign or price tag for their display. Visit at least monthly, check stock levels, and replace damaged units.</p>

<p>Contractors are your highest-value repeat buyers. Keep a simple contact list with their project pipeline, preferred block size, and payment terms. A contractor who trusts you will call you first for the next site. Small gestures a quick delivery, a replacement for a broken block, a flexible payment window on a large order build loyalty far more effectively than discounts alone.</p>

<h2>5. Word-of-Mouth and Testimonials</h2>

<p>In most African towns, reputation travels faster than advertising. After every completed order, ask the customer for a short testimonial a sentence and a photo is enough. Post it on WhatsApp status and on a simple Facebook page. Keep a notebook or phone note of every referral source so you know which relationships are generating business.</p>

<h2>6. Handling Complaints and Returns Professionally</h2>

<p>Complaints are inevitable. The way you handle them determines whether you keep the customer and protect your reputation. Follow a simple four-step response: listen without interrupting, acknowledge the problem, agree a specific remedy and timeline, and follow up to confirm the customer is satisfied. Common complaints in block sales include broken units on delivery, wrong size, late delivery, and inconsistent strength. For broken units, replace them on the next delivery or refund the value do not argue over a few blocks. For quality disputes, invite the customer to the yard, inspect the batch together, and if the fault is yours, replace or refund without delay.</p>

<p>Keep a written record of every complaint, the cause, and the resolution. Patterns will reveal production or delivery problems you can fix for example, poor stacking during transport causing breakages, or insufficient curing causing weak blocks. A customer whose complaint is resolved well often becomes your most loyal advocate.</p>

<ul>
<li>Identify your three main buyer segments and keep a contact list for each.</li>
<li>Print A5 flyers with name, location, block sizes, price, and a phone number that is always answered.</li>
<li>Post a clear block photo and price on WhatsApp status at least three times a week.</li>
<li>Keep a clean sample stack at the yard entrance and invite every serious enquirer to visit.</li>
<li>Sign up at least two hardware stores as resellers and restock them monthly.</li>
<li>Collect one testimonial or photo after every completed order.</li>
<li>Respond to every complaint within 24 hours and record the cause and resolution.</li>
<li>Never claim a strength or certification you cannot prove with a test record.</li>
</ul>
