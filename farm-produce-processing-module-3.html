Sweet Potato Processing

8 lessons · 30 quiz questions · Tubers & Roots

1 Sweet potato varieties

Sweet potato comes in hundreds of varieties, but for processing the key split is:

Orange-fleshed (OFSP): Rich in beta-carotene (Vitamin A). High in sugar, so it browns quickly when dried. Used for puree, juice blends, and biofortified baby food. Premium market.

White/cream-fleshed: Higher starch, lower sugar. Best for flour, chips and crisps. The main processing variety in Africa.

Purple-fleshed: Rich in anthocyanins (antioxidants). Growing health market. Sells at premium for juices and powders.

Pick based on your product: for flour → white. For baby food → orange. For health powder → purple.

2 Washing and sorting

Sweet potato comes in from the field covered in soil and can have damaged areas.

  1. Soak in a tub of clean water for 10 minutes — softens the soil
  2. Brush gently with a soft brush to remove dirt (don't scrub, it damages skin)
  3. Rinse in clean water — change every 30 minutes
  4. Sort — remove any with rot, insect holes, or soft spots
  5. Grade by size — chips and crisps need consistent sizes; flour can use mixed

After washing, use within 4–6 hours. Sweet potato spoils fast once wet.

3 Sweet potato flour

Sweet potato flour is gluten-free and used in baking (mixed with wheat), porridge and as a thickener.

Process:

  1. Peel if desired (peeling gives lighter colour; unpeeled gives more fibre and darker flour)
  2. Slice into 3–5 mm chips
  3. Blanch in hot water for 2 minutes — stops enzyme browning
  4. Cool in cold water
  5. Dip in 0.5% citric acid solution for 2 minutes (prevents browning)
  6. Dry in sun (3–5 days) or solar dryer (1–2 days) until brittle
  7. Mill to fine flour
  8. Sieve through 250 micron

Yield: 100 kg fresh sweet potato → 28–32 kg flour.

💡 Citric acid dip is the secret — without it the flour goes brown and looks old.

4 Chips and crisps production

Sweet potato chips are a growing snack market. They're sweeter than potato chips and higher in Vitamin A.

Steps:

  1. Peel and slice 2 mm thick (mandolin or mechanical slicer)
  2. Soak in cold water 20 minutes to remove surface starch
  3. Blanch for 90 seconds (improves colour and texture)
  4. Dry surface with a cloth or air blower
  5. Fry at 170°C for 3–4 minutes until golden
  6. Drain, salt while hot
  7. Cool and pack in foil bags

Yield: 100 kg fresh → 22–28 kg chips.

5 Puree and baby food

Orange-fleshed sweet potato puree is used as a baby food and as a base for juices, soups and sauces. It's endorsed by nutrition agencies for combating Vitamin A deficiency.

Process:

  1. Peel and steam or boil until soft
  2. Mash or blend into a smooth puree
  3. Add water to target consistency
  4. Heat to 90°C and hot-fill into jars or pouches
  5. Seal and cool
  6. Store refrigerated (2–3 weeks) or freeze (3 months)

For shelf-stable puree: pasteurise in retort pouches (about $1.50–$3 per pouch retail).

6 Drying methods

Three drying options depending on scale:

Sun drying (free): 3–5 days. Cheap but weather-dependent and risks dust/insects. Use raised racks with mesh covers.

Solar dryer ($200–$800): 1–2 days. Faster, cleaner, protected from rain. Best for small operations.

Electric cabinet dryer ($500–$3,000): 8–12 hours. Always available, but power cost adds up. Best in urban areas.

Whichever you choose, aim for under 12% moisture. Test by snapping a chip — it should break cleanly, not bend.

7 Value-added snacks

Higher-margin products from sweet potato:

  • Sweet potato bread and buns — 30% sweet potato flour in wheat-flour recipes
  • Sweet potato chin-chin (Nigerian snack) — fried sweetened dough
  • Sweet potato cookies — gluten-free option
  • Sweet potato juice blends — with pineapple, orange or ginger
  • Sweet potato flour porridge — precooked, instant
  • Sweet potato jam — with cinnamon and sugar

Each of these sells for 3–10× the price of raw sweet potato per kg.

8 Packaging and shelf life

Packaging by product:

  • Flour — 1 kg and 5 kg laminated bags, 25 kg bulk sacks
  • Chips — foil-laminated pouches (nitrogen flush optional)
  • Puree — glass jars or retort pouches
  • Baby food — small (100–200 g) glass jars with vacuum lid

Shelf life:

  • Flour — 12 months in cool dry storage
  • Chips — 4–6 months in sealed foil
  • Puree (unopened jar, pasteurised) — 12 months
  • Puree (opened) — 3–5 days refrigerated
💰 Sweet potato flour for baby food sells for $6–$12 per kg to nutrition programs and NGOs — the highest-margin product from this crop.
