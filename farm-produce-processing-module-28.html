Green Beans & Peas Processing

8 lessons · 30 quiz questions · Fruit Vegetables

1 Variety selection

Green beans and peas are legumes harvested at the immature stage. Different uses require different varieties.

Green beans (Phaseolus vulgaris): Bush beans, pole beans, French beans. Slender pods 10–15 cm. Best for freezing, canning and drying.

Snap peas (Pisum sativum): Flat pods, edible whole. Best for freezing and stir-fry.

Snow peas: Very flat, sweet pods. Premium fresh market.

Garden peas (shelling): Round peas inside pods. Best for freezing and canning peas.

Dried beans: Kidney, black, pinto. Harvested mature and dried.

For commercial processing: green beans (bush type) and garden peas are the workhorses.

2 Washing, topping and tailing

Green beans have tough ends that must be removed.

  1. Wash beans thoroughly in cold water
  2. Snap or cut off both ends (top and tail)
  3. Remove any strings (some varieties have tough fibres along the seam)
  4. Sort — remove any with rust spots, holes or damage
  5. Grade by size for even processing

Peas: shell from pods if not using snap/snow peas.

⚠ Handle gently — bruised beans go black when frozen.

3 Blanching and freezing

Frozen green beans and peas are the largest processed legume market worldwide.

  1. Bring large pot of water to rolling boil
  2. Add beans — 500 g per 4 L water
  3. Blanch 2 minutes for whole beans; 90 seconds for cut
  4. Ice water bath 2 minutes (stops cooking)
  5. Drain thoroughly
  6. IQF freeze on trays, then bag

Yield: 100 kg fresh beans → 95 kg frozen (minimal loss).

Wholesale to food service: $2–$4 per kg. Retail: $3–$6 per 500 g bag.

💡 Frozen peas yield slightly less (90 kg per 100 kg) due to shelling loss.

4 Canning green beans

Canned green beans are a global staple. Low-acid food — requires pressure canning.

  1. Prepare beans as above
  2. Pack into clean jars or cans with 1 tsp salt per 500 ml
  3. Fill with boiling water, leave 2 cm headspace
  4. Seal and process in pressure canner:
    • Pint jars: 20 minutes at 11 PSI
    • Quart jars: 25 minutes at 11 PSI
  5. Cool and test seals

Shelf life: 2 years unopened.

Retail: $1.50–$3 per 400 g can. Big volume, lower margin.

⚠ Never water-bath can green beans — botulism risk. Pressure canner only.

5 Dried beans and peas

Dried legumes are a huge global commodity — dried beans and peas store for years.

  1. Harvest when pods are dry and brown
  2. Thresh — beat pods to release seeds
  3. Winnow — separate seeds from chaff
  4. Sun-dry 2–3 days until moisture is 12–14%
  5. Sort — remove broken, discoloured or insect-damaged
  6. Pack in poly-lined sacks or vacuum bags

Yield: 100 kg fresh pods → 40–50 kg dried beans.

Bulk: $0.80–$2 per kg. Retail: $1.50–$4 per kg.

6 Pea flour and protein

Pea protein is a fast-growing ingredient in plant-based foods.

  1. Mill dried peas (yellow or green) in a hammer mill
  2. Sieve through 250 micron for pea flour
  3. For pea protein isolate: requires wet extraction (advanced)

Pea flour yield: 100 kg dried peas → 90–95 kg flour.

Retail: $3–$8 per kg. Pea protein isolate: $15–$40 per kg (advanced processing).

7 Packaging

Packaging by product:

  • Frozen beans — poly bags (500 g, 1 kg), IQF
  • Canned — cans or jars (400–500 g)
  • Dried — poly-lined sacks (1 kg, 5 kg, 25 kg)
  • Pea flour — foil bags or dark glass (500 g–5 kg)

Shelf life:

  • Frozen: 12 months at −18°C
  • Canned: 2 years unopened
  • Dried: 2–3 years in dry storage
  • Pea flour: 12–18 months

8 Retail and food service

Retail: Supermarkets buy in bulk; brand your own packaging.

Food service: Restaurants, hotels, hospitals buy frozen or canned in bulk — 5 kg and 10 kg bags.

Export: Frozen beans and canned beans exported worldwide. HACCP certification required for major buyers.

Dried beans for ethnic markets: West African, Indian and Latin American diaspora markets pay premium for specific varieties (black-eyed peas, kidney beans, pigeon peas).

💰 Frozen beans are the highest-volume product. Dried beans are highest margin per unit storage. Canned beans sell in huge volumes with low margin — best at scale.
