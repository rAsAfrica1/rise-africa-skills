🧠 Brain Opener

A bad site ruins good seed. A good site forgives many mistakes. Before you plant anything, choose the land like your business depends on it - because it does.

  • What makes a site suitable for organic seed production?
  • Why do seed crops need isolation from other farms?
  • How much does it cost to prepare one hectare for organic seed?
🎁 Reveal a strong answer

A good seed site has fertile soil, reliable water, previous chemical-free history, and isolation from other crops of the same species. Isolation prevents cross-pollination - for example, if your neighbour grows a different tomato variety within 100 metres, your seed may not be pure. Land preparation costs 200 to 400 dollars per hectare in most of Africa.

📖 Simba Loses a Crop

Simba was twenty-two and had saved for two years to buy his first bag of foundation tomato seed. He paid 60 dollars for 200 grams. It was the most expensive seed he had ever held.

He chose a piece of land near his home. It was flat, close to the borehole, easy to reach. He planted the seed. It grew.

Fifty metres away, his neighbour Mr Mukoma was growing a different tomato variety. Simba did not think about it.

The tomatoes flowered. Bees moved between the two fields. Simba harvested his seed at the end of the season. He sent a sample to the lab.

The lab report came back: genetic purity 82 percent. The minimum standard for certified tomato seed was 99 percent.

His entire crop was rejected. Every gram. Sixty dollars of foundation seed, four months of work, all wasted.

Simba sat on the ground for a long time.

A friend who worked at the agricultural extension office found him. She explained the isolation rules. Tomato needed at least 100 metres of isolation from any other tomato variety. Pepper needed 400 metres. Maize needed 200 metres.

"It is not your fault," she said. "Nobody tells first-timers this. Now you know."

The next season, Simba chose a different plot - a piece of land surrounded by maize on two sides and a road on one side. The nearest tomato field was 300 metres away.

He planted. He harvested. He tested. Genetic purity: 99.6 percent.

He sold his entire crop for 450 dollars. And the year after that, he bought a larger piece of land - 300 metres from any other tomato, because now he knew.

💡 The Big Idea

Isolation is the first rule of seed

Seed crops need physical distance from other crops of the same species, or a physical barrier like a tall hedge or a polytunnel. Without isolation, cross-pollination destroys the genetic purity of your seed and the value of your business. Learn the isolation distances for your crop before you plant.

📚 Three Deep Lessons

1

The five criteria for a seed site

Soil. Well-drained, fertile, with a pH between 6 and 7 for most vegetables. Avoid waterlogged land and heavy clay.

Water. Reliable irrigation from a borehole, river, or tank. Seed crops must not suffer drought during flowering or seed set.

Isolation. Distance from other varieties of the same species. Tomato 100 metres, pepper 400 metres, maize 200 metres, cucurbits 800 metres.

History. No banned chemicals used in the last 2 to 3 years for organic certification. Ask the previous farmer for records.

Access. A road or path that allows you to bring in compost, water, and workers, and to move out your harvest.

2

Isolation distance by crop

Different crops need different isolation distances. This is because of how they pollinate.

Self-pollinating crops like tomato, pepper, and beans need less distance - 50 to 400 metres depending on the variety.

Cross-pollinating crops like maize, sorghum, cucurbits (pumpkin, melon, cucumber), and brassicas (cabbage, kale, broccoli) need more - 200 to 1,600 metres.

You can reduce distance requirements by using physical barriers: a tall hedge, a dense crop of a different species, or a polytunnel. Any of these can cut isolation distance by 50 to 80 percent.

3

Land preparation costs in Africa

For one hectare of seed production, typical costs:

Land clearing and ploughing: 100 to 150 dollars.

Compost (10 to 20 tonnes): 100 to 200 dollars if bought, free if made.

Soil testing: 30 to 50 dollars.

Fencing (if needed): 150 to 400 dollars.

Irrigation setup (if not existing): 200 to 600 dollars.

Total first-time cost: 400 to 1,200 dollars per hectare. Ongoing annual cost: 200 to 400 dollars per hectare.

🛠 Hands-On - Score Three Potential Sites

You need

  • A notebook
  • A pen
  • A phone (for GPS and photos)
  1. Pick three potential plots you could use.
  2. Score each on five criteria, one point for poor, five for excellent: soil, water, isolation, history, access.
  3. Add up the scores. Highest total wins.
  4. For the best site, draw a map. Mark nearby crops of the same species.
  5. Measure the distance to each of those crops.
  6. Write down whether the site meets isolation rules for your chosen crop.
  7. If not, look at how to reduce distance with a barrier.
Why it works: This exercise takes two hours. It prevents the kind of mistake that costs a whole season.

🌍 Real Stories from Africa

Zimbabwe A1 farm seed isolation

Zimbabwe A1 resettlement farms often have small plots close together. Seed producers there use tall hedges of napier grass or a fast-growing legume to reduce isolation distance. The barrier technique is essential on small farms.

Kenya pepper seed failure

A Kenyan pepper seed producer lost a full season because a neighbouring farmer planted a different pepper variety 200 metres away. The isolation distance for pepper is 400 metres. One crop lost, one lesson learned.

🏆 Project Challenge

Map Your Isolation Zones

Draw a map of every farm within 500 metres of your potential plot.

  1. Draw a large circle representing 500 metres around your plot.
  2. Mark every farm inside that circle.
  3. Write down what each farm grows.
  4. Draw lines from your plot to every farm growing the same crop or a related crop.
  5. Measure the distances.
  6. Mark any that are inside the isolation distance for your crop.
  7. Decide: can you plant safely, or do you need a barrier or a different plot?
