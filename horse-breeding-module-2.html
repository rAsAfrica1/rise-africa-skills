Choosing a breeding mare

  • Conformation: balanced, correct legs, good feet, deep chest, strong hindquarters
  • Movement: straight, free, ground-covering stride
  • Temperament: calm, easy to handle - temperament is heritable
  • Age: 4-15 years (peaked production)
  • Health history: no laminitis, no colic surgery, no chronic lameness
  • Reproductive record: has she foaled successfully? Any complications?
  • Pedigree: proven bloodlines for your market
  • Soundness: no hereditary defects (navicular, OCD, roaring)
  • Vices: no windsucking, cribbing, weaving, biting, kicking

Choosing a breeding stallion

  • Performance record: proven in sport, work, or racing
  • Progeny record: if proven, his foals' performance matters most
  • Conformation: excellent, because he passes 50% to every foal
  • Temperament: calm, trainable, not aggressive
  • Veterinary check: fertility test, semen evaluation, X-rays of joints
  • Genetic testing: for known hereditary diseases in the breed
  • Age: 4-20 years (peaked fertility)

One stallion can serve 30-60 mares per year naturally, or 200+ via artificial insemination. A good stallion is worth 10-100x a mare in stud value.

Never buy a horse without a pre-purchase veterinary exam. X-rays of the legs, flexion tests, and a full soundness check. A lame horse is worth 10% of its purchase price.

Selection for future productivity

When you keep foals as replacements, choose:

  • Correct conformation - straight legs, good feet
  • Good movement at all three gaits
  • Calm, curious temperament
  • Good growth rate (weight for age)
  • Clean veterinary record (no OCD, no roaring)
  • From mares that foal easily and produce good milk
The stallion rule: Buy the best stallion you can access. He influences 50% of every foal for years. A cheap stallion costs you in every foal you produce. If you cannot afford a good stallion, use AI or pay a stud fee to a proven one.
Practical exercise 2: Visit 2 registered studs. Look at their mares and stallions. Ask about pedigrees, foaling records, and stud fees. Note which bloodlines are sought after in your market.
