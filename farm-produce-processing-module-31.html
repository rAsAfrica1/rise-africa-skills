Groundnut (Peanut) Processing

8 lessons · 30 quiz questions · Nuts & Seeds

1 Groundnut varieties

Groundnut (Arachis hypogaea), also called peanut, is one of Africa's most important crops. It grows underground and is harvested by pulling the whole plant.

Runner: Long-season, high yield. Best for oil extraction and peanut butter.

Virginia: Large kernels, best for roasting and snacking.

Spanish: Small kernels, high oil content. Best for oil and confectionery.

Valencia: 3–5 kernels per pod, sweet. Premium snacking and boiling.

For peanut butter, choose Virginia or Runner. For oil, Spanish or Runner.

⚠ Groundnuts are prone to aflatoxin (a toxic mould) if stored badly. This is a serious food safety issue. Always dry to under 10% moisture and store dry.

2 Cleaning and shelling

Groundnuts come in pods. First step is removing the pods from the plant, then shelling (removing kernels from pods).

  1. Dry pods in sun 3–5 days until rattling sound when shaken
  2. Clean pods — remove stones, soil, plant material
  3. Shell — by hand (slow) or with a mechanical sheller (200–500 kg/hour)
  4. Winnow to separate kernels from husks
  5. Sort kernels — remove shrivelled, mouldy or discoloured
  6. Grade by size — large (snacking), medium (butter), small (oil)

Shelling yield: 100 kg pods → 65–75 kg kernels.

3 Roasting profiles

Roasting develops flavour and makes peanuts easier to grind. The profile matters enormously.

Light roast (140°C, 20 min): Mild flavour. Best for boiled peanut products.

Medium roast (160°C, 25 min): Balanced flavour. Best for peanut butter and snacking.

Dark roast (180°C, 20 min): Strong, deep flavour. Best for oil extraction and Asian sauces.

Roast in a drum roaster or on a wide pan, stirring constantly. Cool immediately after roasting — they continue cooking if left hot.

💡 Test by smell and colour, not just time. Every batch of nuts is slightly different.

4 Peanut butter production

Peanut butter is the highest-value groundnut product for small processors.

  1. Roast peanuts (medium roast)
  2. Cool completely
  3. Remove skins (rub between hands or use a skin-remover machine)
  4. Grind in a colloid mill or heavy-duty food processor
  5. Add salt (1%), sugar (2%), and oil (2–3%) as needed
  6. For crunchy: add chopped peanuts at the end
  7. Fill into jars at 65°C and cap

Yield: 100 kg kernels → 95–100 kg peanut butter (with added oil and salt).

Retail: $5–$12 per 500 g jar. Premium natural: $8–$15.

5 Groundnut oil extraction

Groundnut oil is a premium cooking oil, especially in Africa and Asia.

Cold press: Press kernels at low temperature (below 50°C). Retains nutrients. 40–45% oil yield. Premium price.

Expeller press: Higher temperature (80–100°C). 45–50% oil yield. Standard quality.

Solvent extraction: Large scale only — $500K+ plant.

  1. Clean and shell kernels
  2. Optional: roast lightly
  3. Press in an oil press
  4. Filter through cloth or filter press
  5. Bottle in dark glass or PET

Yield: 100 kg kernels → 40–48 kg oil + 50 kg cake.

Retail: $4–$10 per litre. Cold-pressed premium: $8–$15 per litre.

6 Salted peanuts (snack)

Roasted salted peanuts are a simple, high-margin snack product.

  1. Roast kernels (medium roast)
  2. Remove skins
  3. Toss with oil (1%) and salt (1.5%)
  4. Cool completely
  5. Pack in foil pouches or jars

Yield: 100 kg kernels → 95 kg salted peanuts.

Retail: $8–$15 per kg. Premium flavours (chilli, honey, smoked): $12–$20 per kg.

7 Groundnut flour

Groundnut flour is used in gluten-free baking, porridges and soups.

  1. Roast and skin kernels
  2. Grind in a hammer mill or spice grinder
  3. Sieve through 250 micron
  4. Pack in dark airtight bags (oils go rancid)

Yield: 100 kg kernels → 90–95 kg flour.

Retail: $3–$7 per kg. Higher for organic.

8 Packaging and allergens

Groundnuts are one of the top 8 allergens. This creates both opportunity and responsibility.

Packaging by product:

  • Roasted/salted kernels — foil pouches, jars (100 g–1 kg)
  • Peanut butter — glass jars (250 g, 500 g)
  • Oil — dark glass or PET (500 ml, 1 L, 5 L)
  • Flour — foil bags (500 g, 1 kg)

Mandatory labelling: "Contains peanuts" — required by law in most countries. Cross-contamination warnings if processed in a shared facility.

💰 Peanut butter is the best first product — simple equipment, high margin, big market. Groundnut oil is also high margin but needs a press ($500–$3,000).
