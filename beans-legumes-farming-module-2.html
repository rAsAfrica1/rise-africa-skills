Lesson 1

Seed Selection and Quality

~10 min
In this lesson
  • Choose quality seed that germinates and grows
  • Understand the difference between certified and saved seed
  • Test germination before planting

Certified vs saved seed

Certified seed from a licensed producer is tested for germination, purity and disease. It costs 2–3x saved seed but gives 20–40% higher yields and reliable stand. For a first-year legume crop, always start certified.

Saved seed from your own crop works from year 2 onward, but with two conditions:

  • Select seed from disease-free plants at harvest
  • Store it separately in hermetic containers to protect from weevils
  • Replace with certified seed every 3 years

Germination test

Before planting, test your seed. Count 100 seeds, place them on damp paper or cloth, keep moist and warm for 5–7 days. Count how many sprout. A germination rate above 85% is acceptable; below 70%, buy new seed.

Seed treatment

Legume seed can carry fungal and bacterial diseases. Many certified seeds come pre-treated. If not, dress with a recommended fungicide (thiram or similar) following the label rate. Do not mix fungicide and inoculant at the same time — the fungicide kills the rhizobium. Treat first, let dry, then inoculate just before planting.

Lesson 2

Inoculation Done Right

~10 min
In this lesson
  • Use the correct inoculant for each crop
  • Apply inoculant correctly at planting
  • Avoid the common mistakes that waste inoculant

Match inoculant to crop

Each legume has its own rhizobium species. Do not substitute. Labels show which crops each inoculant is for:

  • Bean inoculant (Rhizobium phaseoli) — for common beans
  • Cowpea inoculant (Bradyrhizobium spp.) — for cowpea, green gram, pigeon pea
  • Soybean inoculant (Bradyrhizobium japonicum) — for soybean only
  • Groundnut inoculant (Bradyrhizobium spp.) — for groundnut

Correct application

  1. Store inoculant cool until the day of planting
  2. Moisten seed slightly with clean water or a weak sugar solution
  3. Add inoculant at packet rate — usually 5–10 g per kg of seed
  4. Mix until every seed is coated with a thin dark film
  5. Shade the seed while you work — direct sun kills rhizobium
  6. Plant within 2–4 hours

Common mistakes

  • Mixing inoculant with fertiliser or fungicide in the same container
  • Storing mixed seed overnight before planting
  • Using old, expired inoculant
  • Leaving inoculated seed in the sun

Lesson 3

Land Preparation and Spacing

~10 min
In this lesson
  • Prepare land for good legume germination and root nodulation
  • Space crops correctly for the variety and system
  • Avoid the mistake of dense planting for a cash crop

Land preparation

Legume seed is often larger than cereal seed. It needs firm, fine, well-drained soil. Plough or hoe 15–20 cm deep, then harrow to a fine tilth. Do not leave clods — legume seed must have good soil contact.

Spacing

Do not plant legumes too densely. Dense planting produces tall, thin plants with few pods and more disease. The correct spacing for a market crop is the one that produces well-filled pods and lets air move through the canopy.

Lesson 4

The P-Not-N Rule — Phosphorus for Legumes

~11 min
In this lesson
  • Understand why legumes need phosphorus, not nitrogen
  • Apply the correct phosphorus rate at planting
  • Recognise the signs of phosphorus deficiency

This is the single most important rule for legume farming: do not apply nitrogen fertiliser to a legume. Apply phosphorus.

Nitrogen fertiliser suppresses nodulation. The plant gets its nitrogen cheaply from the fertiliser bag, so it stops investing in the rhizobium partnership. You have paid for nitrogen the legume would have made for free, and you have reduced the nitrogen it would have returned to the soil. It is a double loss.

Apply phosphorus instead

Phosphorus drives root development, nodulation and pod fill. Apply DAP (18-46-0) or TSP at planting:

  • Beans and cowpea: 100–150 kg/ha DAP
  • Groundnut: 100–150 kg/ha DAP + 100–200 kg/ha gypsum (calcium for pod fill)
  • Soybean: 150–200 kg/ha DAP
  • Pigeon pea: 100 kg/ha DAP

Place fertiliser in a band 5 cm to the side and 5 cm below the seed — not in direct contact (which burns seed), but close enough for roots to reach it.

Signs of phosphorus deficiency

  • Stunted, dark green plants with a purple tinge on leaves
  • Poor nodulation — few pink nodules on roots
  • Few flowers, poor pod fill
  • Reduced root development

Organic alternative

Manure and compost provide phosphorus, though in lower concentration. 5–10 t/ha of well-rotted manure supplies enough phosphorus for a decent legume crop. Bone meal is an excellent phosphorus source if available.

Lesson 5

Planting Depth and Timing

~9 min
In this lesson
  • Plant at the correct depth for each legume
  • Time planting to reliable rains
  • Handle the double-crop option

Planting depth

  • Common bean: 3–5 cm
  • Cowpea: 3–5 cm
  • Groundnut: 4–6 cm (deeper for dry soil)
  • Soybean: 3–4 cm
  • Pigeon pea: 3–5 cm

Too shallow and the seed dries out. Too deep and the seedling runs out of energy before reaching the surface.

Timing

Plant at the start of reliable rains. For most of Africa:

  • East Africa: with the long rains (March–April) or short rains (October–November)
  • Southern Africa: with the main rains (November–December)
  • West Africa: with the main rains (June–July)
  • Sahel: with the single rainy season (July)

Do not plant on the very first rain of the season — wait for the second or third rain when soil moisture is reliable to 20 cm.

Double-crop systems

In areas with 6+ months of rain, plant a short-cycle legume (green gram, cowpea, bush bean) after the cereal harvest. The legume uses residual moisture, fixes nitrogen, and provides a second crop. Requires early cereal harvest and reliable late-season rains.

Knowledge check — Module 2

1. What fertiliser should you apply to legumes?

Correct: DAP or TSP. Nitrogen suppresses nodulation; phosphorus drives it.

2. When should you plant inoculated seed?

Correct: within 2–4 hours. Bacteria die on dry seed quickly.

3. What is the germination rate for acceptable seed?

Correct: above 85%. Below 70% and you need to buy new seed.

4. What does groundnut need extra of at planting?

Correct: gypsum. 100–200 kg/ha provides calcium for pod development.

5. What is the correct bean spacing for a bush variety?

Correct: 45–60 × 10–15 cm. Correct spacing produces well-filled pods and lets air through the canopy.

Module 2 capstone

Plan your legume planting. Include: (1) seed source and germination test result; (2) which inoculant you will use; (3) land preparation method; (4) spacing for your chosen legume; (5) phosphorus rate and product; (6) planting date.
