Banana & Plantain Processing

8 lessons · 30 quiz questions · Fruits

1 Banana vs plantain

Banana and plantain are the same genus (Musa) but different uses.

Dessert banana: Sweet, eaten raw. Varieties: Cavendish, Gros Michel, Apple banana. Best for eating fresh, baby food, banana flour from over-ripe fruit.

Plantain: Starchy, cooked before eating. Varieties: French, Horn, False Horn. Best for chips, frying, flour.

Cooking banana (East African): Matoke, Gonja. Steamed and eaten as staple. Best for local markets and flour.

For chips and crisps, plantain is the standard. For baby food and smoothies, dessert banana.

2 Peeling and slicing

Banana and plantain skins are tough — peeling method depends on ripeness.

Ripe banana: Peel by hand (skin comes off easily).

Plantain: Cut off both ends, score the skin lengthwise with a knife, then peel. Use a spoon to lift under the skin for green plantain.

After peeling:

  • Slice 2–3 mm for chips
  • Chop into chunks for flour
  • Blend for baby food
⚠ Peeled banana browns within minutes. Use lemon water dip (1 tbsp lemon per litre) if holding.

3 Banana chips (fried)

Banana chips are a global snack — over $1 billion market.

  1. Use slightly under-ripe plantain (firmer, less sugar)
  2. Peel and slice 2 mm thick (mandolin for consistency)
  3. Soak in cold water 15 minutes (removes surface starch)
  4. Pat dry thoroughly
  5. Fry at 170°C for 3–4 minutes until golden
  6. Drain on paper, salt immediately
  7. Cool completely
  8. Pack in foil pouches

Yield: 100 kg fresh plantain → 25–30 kg chips.

Retail: $8–$15 per kg.

4 Banana flour

Banana flour (from green plantain or banana) is gluten-free and nutrient-rich.

  1. Peel green bananas/plantain
  2. Slice into 3 mm chips
  3. Blanch 2 minutes (prevents browning)
  4. Dry at 60°C for 10–14 hours
  5. Target moisture: 8–10%
  6. Mill in hammer mill
  7. Sieve through 250 micron
  8. Pack in dark airtight bags

Yield: 100 kg fresh → 25–30 kg flour.

Retail: $4–$8 per kg. Higher for organic.

5 Dried banana

Dried banana slices are a chewy sweet snack.

  1. Use ripe bananas
  2. Peel and slice 5 mm thick
  3. Dip in 1% citric acid solution (prevents browning)
  4. Dry at 55°C for 8–12 hours
  5. Target moisture: 15–18%
  6. Cool and pack

Yield: 100 kg fresh → 20–25 kg dried.

Retail: $20–$35 per kg.

6 Plantain chips (African style)

Plantain chips are a $2 billion industry in Nigeria alone. Also big in Ghana, Cameroon and the diaspora.

  1. Use green or slightly yellow plantain
  2. Peel and slice 2 mm thick
  3. Soak in salted water 10 minutes (or plain water for sweet version)
  4. Fry at 170°C for 3–4 minutes
  5. Drain and cool
  6. Pack in foil pouches

Yield: 100 kg fresh → 28–32 kg chips.

Retail: $10–$18 per kg. Premium flavoured: $15–$25 per kg.

💰 Plantain chips are one of Africa's biggest snack businesses. Low equipment cost, huge market.

7 Banana puree for baby food

Banana puree is a classic first baby food — sweet, smooth, easy to digest.

  1. Use very ripe bananas
  2. Peel and mash or blend
  3. Add 0.5% citric acid and 0.05% ascorbic acid
  4. Heat to 90°C briefly
  5. Hot-fill into small jars or retort pouches
  6. Seal and cool

Retail: $2–$4 per 100 g jar. Big market with baby food brands.

8 Packaging and shelf life

Packaging by product:

  • Chips — foil pouches (50 g, 100 g, 250 g)
  • Flour — dark airtight bags (500 g–5 kg)
  • Dried slices — foil pouches (100 g–1 kg)
  • Puree — small jars (100 g) or retort pouches

Shelf life:

  • Banana chips: 6–9 months in sealed foil
  • Banana flour: 12 months
  • Dried banana: 6–12 months
  • Puree (pasteurised, sealed): 12 months
💰 Highest-margin: banana chips and plantain chips. Highest-volume: banana flour (sold to food manufacturers).
