Adobe (Sun-Dried Mud Brick)

8 lessons · 30 quiz questions · Easy difficulty

1 Traditional adobe basics

Adobe is one of the oldest building materials on Earth. Sun-dried mud bricks have been used in Africa, the Middle East, and the Americas for over 8,000 years.

The basic formula:

  • Clay soil (60–70%)
  • Sand (20–30%)
  • Straw, dung, or plant fibre (5–10%)
  • Water (as needed)

The clay binds, the sand reduces shrinkage and cracking, and the straw acts like reinforcing steel — it holds the brick together as it dries.

No cement, no kiln, no machinery. Adobe is the cheapest possible building block. Rural builders can make it on-site with what's under their feet.

💡 Adobe is the simplest entry into block-making. Total equipment cost: a wooden mould and a shovel.

2 Clay-sand-straw ratio

Get the ratio wrong and bricks crack as they dry.

Standard mix by volume:

  • Clay soil: 3 parts
  • Sand: 1 part
  • Straw (chopped 5–10 cm): 0.5 parts
  • Water: enough for a stiff, moldable consistency

Test the mix: roll a ball the size of a golf ball. Press it flat between your palms. If it cracks at the edges, too much clay — add sand. If it crumbles, too much sand — add clay.

For a stronger brick: replace 10% of the clay soil with cement (this becomes "stabilised adobe" — closer to CSEB, Module 14).

3 Mixing adobe by foot

Traditional adobe is mixed by foot on a tarp or in a pit.

  1. Spread dry soil on the tarp.
  2. Add chopped straw and mix dry.
  3. Add water slowly and stomp with bare feet (or boots) until the mix is uniform.
  4. Knead with a hoe or spade until the mixture holds together in a stiff ball.
  5. Cover the pile with a tarp and let it rest 12–24 hours (the clay absorbs water evenly).

Kneading time: 20–40 minutes for a 500 kg batch. If mixing for a full crew, use a mortar mixer or a converted cement mixer — much faster.

4 Wooden moulds

Traditional adobe moulds are simple wooden boxes.

Standard dimensions:

  • Length: 300–400 mm
  • Width: 150–200 mm
  • Height: 80–120 mm

The mould is a bottomless box — it sits on wet ground or a board, is filled with adobe mix, and lifted straight up.

Making a mould:

  • Use hardwood or plywood (moisture-resistant)
  • Nail or screw the four sides into a rigid rectangle
  • Add a removable base plate if you want to move bricks easily
  • Sand the inside — rough wood traps the wet mix

Cost: $5–$20 per mould. A family can make 100–200 bricks per day with 2–3 moulds.

5 Sun-drying schedule

Adobe bricks dry in the sun, not in a kiln. Drying is a two-stage process:

Stage 1 — Initial set (24–48 hours):

  • Leave bricks in the moulds on flat ground for 24 hours.
  • They become firm enough to handle but are still soft.

Stage 2 — Stack drying (2–4 weeks):

  • Remove from the mould and stand the bricks on edge.
  • Leave for 2 days.
  • Then stack in open-air piles with air gaps between bricks.
  • Turn every 3 days so both sides dry evenly.
  • Fully dry in 2–4 weeks depending on weather.

Do not use bricks until completely dry — a wet brick will shrink inside the wall and crack the plaster.

⚠ Cover the drying bricks with a tarp in heavy rain. One soaked batch can ruin a week's work.

6 Turning bricks to dry evenly

Bricks dry from the outside in. If one face dries faster, it shrinks and cracks the brick.

Turning rule: Turn every brick every 3 days during drying.

Why turning matters:

  • Bottom face is usually dampest.
  • Sun-facing face dries fastest.
  • Turning equalises moisture loss and prevents warping.

After 10–14 days, the bricks should be hard and dry. Drop one from knee height — it should ring like a bell, not thud. A ringing brick is dry; a thudding brick still has moisture inside.

7 Firing vs sun-drying

Adobe is not fired. But many producers confuse adobe with fired clay brick (Module 18).

FeatureAdobeFired Brick
MethodSun-driedKiln-fired at 900-1,100°C
StrengthLow (1-2 N/mm²)High (10-20 N/mm²)
Water resistancePoor — needs renderGood
CostVery cheapMedium

Adobe is for rural, dry climates. Fired brick is for wetter climates and permanent structures.

8 Selling to rural builders

Adobe sells to a specific market — cost-driven rural builders.

Who buys:

  • Rural homeowners (self-build)
  • NGOs building schools, clinics, community centres
  • Traditional house restorations
  • Farm outbuildings and animal shelters
  • Eco-tourism camps wanting local materials

Pricing (Africa, 2026):

  • Adobe brick: $0.05–$0.15 per brick
  • Stabilised adobe (with cement): $0.20–$0.40 per brick

Cost to make: $0.02–$0.06 per brick (mostly labour). Margin: 50–70%.

Selling points:

  • Cheapest wall material available
  • Made from local materials (soil, straw, water)
  • Very low carbon footprint
  • Good thermal mass (cool in hot climates)
  • Traditional look, fits rural architecture

Limitations to warn buyers about:

  • Needs render (plaster) to protect from rain
  • Not for foundations or wet areas
  • Lower strength than concrete or fired brick
  • Not suitable for multi-storey buildings
💰 Adobe is the simplest block-making business. Near-zero startup cost. Perfect for rural areas where cement and fuel are expensive. Build on-site with local labour.
