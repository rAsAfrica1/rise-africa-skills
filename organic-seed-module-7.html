🧠 Brain Opener

Conventional farming feeds the plant. Organic farming feeds the soil. It sounds like the same thing. It is not. Fed plants grow this season. Fed soil grows every season.

  • Why is nitrogen the biggest challenge in organic farming?
  • How much compost does one hectare need?
  • What does it cost to make compost vs buy fertiliser?
🎁 Reveal a strong answer

Nitrogen is the most limiting nutrient in organic systems because it comes from decomposed organic matter, which takes time. One hectare of vegetable seed production needs 10 to 20 tonnes of compost per year. Making compost costs almost nothing except labour - about 20 to 40 dollars per tonne. Buying synthetic nitrogen costs 60 to 90 dollars per 50-kilogram bag.

📖 Tendai Builds a Compost Mountain

Tendai was nineteen and had just been given a quarter hectare by his grandfather. He wanted to grow organic kale seed. He had no money for fertiliser.

His grandfather laughed. "You do not need money. You need a compost pile."

"How big?"

"For a quarter hectare? Three tonnes."

Tendai had no idea how to make three tonnes of compost. His grandfather showed him.

First layer: dry material. Maize stalks, dry grass, chopped into short pieces. Twenty centimetres deep.

Second layer: green material. Fresh weeds, vegetable scraps, kitchen waste. Ten centimetres deep.

Third layer: animal manure. Goat, chicken, or cattle. Five centimetres deep.

Fourth layer: a thin dusting of wood ash and soil.

Repeat the layers until the pile is one and a half metres high. Water between each layer so it is damp like a wrung-out cloth.

Turn the pile every two weeks. After eight weeks, it is ready - dark, crumbly, and sweet-smelling.

Tendai made three piles over three months. Each one produced about one tonne of finished compost.

He spread the compost before planting. He planted his kale seed. He watered. He waited.

The kale was strong. The leaves were dark green. The seeds matured fast and heavy.

He harvested 20 kilograms of kale seed from the quarter hectare and sold it for 500 dollars. His entire input cost - for seed, water, and labour - was under 80 dollars.

His grandfather walked through the plot at the end. "You see?" he said. "The soil did the work."

Tendai now makes 30 tonnes of compost a year. He sells the surplus to neighbours at 30 dollars per tonne.

💡 The Big Idea

Compost is the fuel of organic agriculture

Organic farming lives or dies by compost. A good compost pile turns free waste into a rich fertiliser that costs nothing except labour. Farmers who master compost can grow strong seed without buying a single bag of synthetic fertiliser. Farmers who do not master it struggle forever.

📚 Three Deep Lessons

1

How to make proper compost

Layers. Alternate dry material (carbon) with green material (nitrogen) with animal manure. The ratio should be roughly 3 parts dry, 2 parts green, 1 part manure by volume.

Size. A pile should be at least 1 metre by 1 metre by 1 metre. Smaller piles do not heat up enough to break down properly.

Moisture. The pile should be as damp as a wrung-out cloth. Too dry and it will not decompose. Too wet and it will smell bad and rot.

Turning. Turn the pile every two weeks to add oxygen. This accelerates decomposition and prevents anaerobic rot.

Timing. Compost is ready when it is dark, crumbly, and smells like forest soil. Usually 8 to 12 weeks.

2

The other organic inputs

Animal manure. Goat, chicken, and cattle manure are all excellent. Chicken manure is highest in nitrogen. Let manure age for 4 to 6 weeks before using - fresh manure can burn plants.

Green manures. Grow crops specifically to be cut and dug into the soil. Cowpea, lablab, sunhemp, and jack bean are excellent nitrogen fixers. They add nitrogen and organic matter.

Wood ash. Rich in potassium and calcium. Use sparingly - a thin dusting on compost. Avoid ash from treated wood or coal.

Bone meal. Slow-release phosphorus. Available from butcheries and slaughterhouses. Grind and add to compost.

Rock phosphate. Mined mineral phosphorus approved for organic use. Bought from agricultural suppliers.

3

The economics of compost vs fertiliser

One hectare of vegetable seed production needs the equivalent of 60 kilograms of nitrogen, 30 kilograms of phosphorus, and 40 kilograms of potassium.

Buying synthetic fertiliser to supply this: 200 to 300 dollars per hectare per season.

Making compost to supply this: 20 to 40 dollars per tonne for labour, 10 to 20 tonnes per hectare = 200 to 800 dollars, but the compost also improves soil structure, water retention, and microbial life - benefits synthetic fertiliser cannot provide.

After three years of compost, the soil itself starts producing nitrogen and phosphorus naturally. Annual input costs drop by 30 to 50 percent.

🛠 Hands-On - Build a Real Compost Pile

You need

  • Dry plant material - maize stalks, dry grass, straw
  • Green plant material - weeds, kitchen scraps, fresh leaves
  • Animal manure - goat, chicken, or cattle
  • Water
  • A fork or shovel
  1. Find a shaded spot near your plot.
  2. Spread a 20 centimetre base layer of coarse dry material.
  3. Add a 10 centimetre layer of green material.
  4. Sprinkle a 5 centimetre layer of animal manure.
  5. Dust with wood ash and a little soil.
  6. Water until damp.
  7. Repeat layers until the pile is 1.5 metres high.
  8. Turn every two weeks. Compost is ready in 8 to 12 weeks.
Why it works: The first compost pile is always the hardest. After the first one, it becomes routine. Most organic farmers make 10 to 20 piles a year.

🏆 Project Challenge

Make Three Compost Piles

Build compost for your first season and track it.

  1. Choose a shaded spot near your intended plot.
  2. Build the first pile following the layer method.
  3. Mark the date. Turn every two weeks.
  4. Build a second pile two weeks later.
  5. Build a third pile four weeks later.
  6. Track each pile: temperature, smell, colour.
  7. When the first pile is ready, use it on a small test plot.
  8. Compare the test plot to an untreated plot after four weeks.
