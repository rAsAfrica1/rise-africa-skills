1. Future Tense: Simple and Compound

In Russian, the future tense has two forms: simple and compound. The simple future is used with perfective verbs. Perfective verbs describe actions that will be completed. To form the simple future, conjugate the perfective verb like a present tense verb. For example, ' ' means 'I will read (and finish)'. The compound future uses the verb '' (to be) in its future form plus the infinitive of an imperfective verb. The future forms of '' are: , , / , , , . Then add the imperfective infinitive. Practical Reference: Future Tense and Plans in Russian for African Agribusiness

This practical reference supports the Rise AFRICA Skills module Future Tense and Plans within the Russian Language course. It is designed for African agribusiness professionals, exporters, cooperative managers, and trade officers who need to discuss future activities with Russian-speaking buyers, logistics partners, input suppliers, and government agencies. The focus is on using Russian future forms to negotiate contracts, confirm shipment schedules, discuss seasonal production, issue invitations, and make conditional agreements. All examples keep business outcomes at the centre: payments, delivery windows, quality certificates, and partnership commitments.

Russian has two future forms. The simple future uses perfective verbs and often expresses a single completed action or a firm commitment. The compound future uses (to be) plus an imperfective infinitive and describes ongoing, repeated, or general future activity. For agribusiness, this distinction matters. A simple future can signal a guaranteed delivery, while a compound future can describe a routine export cycle or an expected seasonal pattern. Mastering both forms helps you avoid ambiguity when timelines and obligations are critical.

Simple Future for Firm Business Commitments

The simple future is formed from perfective verbs. It often translates as will do and implies completion. In agribusiness, use it when you promise a specific action: sending a certificate, paying an invoice, loading a container, or signing an annex. Examples:

  • . I will send the quality certificate tomorrow.
  • . We will sign the contract on Friday.
  • 15 . They will pay the invoice by 15 March.

For African exporters dealing with Russian importers, simple future statements can be used in emails to confirm actions. However, avoid overusing them for uncertain events. If a shipment depends on port clearance or weather, the compound future is safer.

Compound Future for Ongoing and Seasonal Plans

The compound future uses the future form of (, , , , , ) plus an imperfective infinitive. It describes actions that will happen repeatedly, continuously, or without a focus on completion. This is useful for seasonal production and long-term trade relationships:

  • . We will export coffee to Russia every season.
  • . Farmers will harvest in November.
  • . I will check the documents every week.

In negotiations, compound future can soften a commitment or describe a process. For example, means We will work on quality improvement and signals ongoing effort rather than a single completed task.

Talking About Plans and Intentions

Plans and intentions often use (to intend), (to plan), or (to want) plus an infinitive. These structures are common in business correspondence and meetings:

  • . We plan to increase cocoa supplies.
  • . I intend to visit the exhibition in Moscow.
  • . They want to open a representative office in Nairobi.

When discussing intentions, pair them with time expressions: (next year), (in a month), (by the end of the quarter). This makes your Russian more precise and credible.

Weather and Seasons in Agribusiness Planning

Weather affects planting, harvesting, storage, and transport. Russian future forms are used in forecasts and planning. Common expressions:

  • . Tomorrow there will be rain.
  • . In summer it will be hot.
  • . In winter there will be snow.
  • . In the rainy season we will use covered warehouses.

For African suppliers, understanding Russian seasons helps with delivery scheduling. Russian winter can delay port operations, while summer increases demand for certain products. Use future forms to propose contingency plans.

Making Invitations and Suggestions

Invitations and suggestions are essential for trade fairs, farm visits, and partnership meetings. Useful phrases:

  • . Lets discuss the delivery terms.
  • . I invite you to the agro exhibition.
  • , ? Maybe we will meet on Thursday?
  • . I suggest sending samples.

These forms build relationships. In Russian business culture, direct invitations are acceptable, but politeness markers such as and improve tone.

Conditional Sentences with Future Meaning

Conditional sentences with (if) often use future forms. The structure is: + future, ... future. Examples:

  • , . If you sign the contract, we will start deliveries.
  • , . If there is a good harvest, we will export more.
  • , . If prices rise, we will revise the budget.

These sentences are powerful in risk management. They allow you to link commitments to conditions such as quality, price, or logistics.

Safety Warning: Do not use the simple future to guarantee delivery when your shipment depends on customs clearance, weather, or third-party logistics. A false promise in Russian can damage trust and create legal risk. Use compound future or conditional forms: (We will try to send) or , (If everything goes well, we will send). Always confirm timelines in writing and check sanctions and import regulations before committing.
