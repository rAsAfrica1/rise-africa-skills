5. Feeding Systems and FCR

Even a perfect ration gives poor results if the feeding system is wrong. This module covers feeding frequency, trough design, and how to hit target growth rates.

Lesson 5.3 - Trough design and management

  • Trough length: 30-35 cm per pig for dry feed, 25 cm for wet feed.
  • Trough height: Adjust so pigs cannot stand in the trough.
  • Dividers: Prevent dominant pigs from hogging the feed.
  • Sloped bottom: Easier to clean and reduces waste.
  • Drainage: Wet troughs must drain fully.
  • Clean daily - leftover feed breeds bacteria.
  • Mount on a concrete base - no mud, no waste.

  • Use nipple drinkers - cleaner water, less waste.
  • One nipple per 10 pigs, or two bowl drinkers per pen.
  • Water must always be available - 24 hours a day.
  • Check water flow every day. A blocked nipple is a pig losing weight.

Lesson 5.5 - Hitting your FCR target

Common causes of poor FCR:

  • Unbalanced ration - a pig eats more to get the protein it needs, but still grows slowly.
  • Overfeeding - pigs fed above their requirement lay down fat, which costs more per kg of gain.
  • Underfeeding - pigs fed below requirement grow slowly, but eat the same for maintenance.
  • Cold housing - pigs burn calories to stay warm. Below 18°C, FCR rises 10%.
  • Disease - a sick pig eats but does not gain.
  • Worm burden - internal parasites steal nutrients. Deworm every 3 months.
  • Feed waste - trough design, spillage, rats.
The 5% rule: A 5% improvement in FCR is worth $5-6 per pig. On 50 pigs/year that is $250-300. Always look for the small gains.
Practical exercise 5.1: Weigh 5 pigs today. Weigh 5 kg of feed into the trough. Record how long it takes them to eat it. Repeat tomorrow. Track daily intake per pig.
Practical exercise 5.2: Weigh the same 5 pigs at 2-week intervals. Plot their growth on paper. Compare to the target growth table.
