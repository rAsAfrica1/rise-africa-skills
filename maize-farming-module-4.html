4. Fertiliser and Top Dressing

Top dressing is where yield is made. Skip it and you cap at 3 tonnes.

Module 4 of 6 · Maize Farming for Profit

The two-stage feeding plan

StageProductRate
At plantingDAP or compound100-150 kg/ha
4-6 weeks (knee height)Urea or CAN100-150 kg/ha
8-10 weeks (tasseling)Urea or CAN50-100 kg/ha

Total nitrogen: 100-180 kg N/ha for high yields.

Timing top dressing

  • First top dress: when plants are 30-45 cm tall (knee height).
  • Second top dress: just before tasseling - the single biggest yield response.
  • Apply when soil is moist. Dry application followed by no rain loses nitrogen to the air.
  • Band 10-15 cm from the plant, then cover with soil.

Signs of nitrogen deficiency

  • Pale yellow-green leaves
  • Older leaves yellow first, tip to base
  • V-shaped yellowing from the tip of the leaf
  • Stunted growth, thin stalks

Micronutrients

NutrientDeficiency symptomFix
ZincWhite/yellow bands on leaves, stuntedZinc sulphate foliar, 15 g/15 L
BoronPoor cob fill, hollow stemsBorax foliar (5 g/15 L) - careful, toxic in excess
SulphurPale leaves, poor proteinSulphate fertilisers

Manure and compost

5-10 tonnes/ha of well-rotted manure, applied before planting, is worth 50-100 kg of compound fertiliser. It also improves soil structure and water holding.

Green manure rotation

Plant a legume cover crop (cowpea, lablab) after maize harvest. It fixes 60-150 kg N/ha, feeds next season's maize for free.

The top-dress rule: Split nitrogen into two or three applications. One big dose at planting is lost to rain.
Practical exercise 4: Split a plot in two. Top dress one at knee height and tasseling. Leave the other untreated. Compare cob size and final yield.
