Lessons

1. Finding Local Customers: Markets, Shops, and Schools

Start by mapping your immediate area. In most African towns, the best first customers are within 5 km of your kitchen. Visit your local open-air market on a busy day (e.g., Saturday morning) and talk to at least 10 stallholders who already sell snacks, nuts, or dried goods. Ask what they pay for similar products and whether they would stock yours on consignmentyou give them 10 packs, they pay you after selling, and you keep 70% of the retail price. For example, if you sell a 100g pack of dried mango for 5,000 UGX, the stallholder keeps 1,500 UGX.

Next, target small shops and kiosks. Bring a sample pack and a simple price list. Offer a wholesale price that gives the shopkeeper a 3040% margin. For instance, if your retail price is 5,000 UGX, your wholesale price should be 3,0003,500 UGX. Start with 5 shops. Restock weekly and replace any unsold packs with fresh onesthis builds trust.

Schools are a goldmine. Boarding schools, especially, buy snacks in bulk. Approach the school bursar or matron with a proposal: 200 packs of 50g dried fruit per term at 2,000 UGX each, delivered every two weeks. Offer a free taste test for the staff first. Also try health centres, offices, and bus parks where travellers want quick, healthy snacks.

Keep a simple notebook: customer name, phone number, amount sold, and payment date. Follow up every 3 days. In one month, you can realistically get 10 regular customers buying 50 packs weeklythats 500 packs, or 2,500,000 UGX in revenue if you sell at 5,000 UGX each. Start small, deliver on time, and always smile.

2. Selling Online via WhatsApp, Facebook, and Instagram

You dont need a website. A smartphone and a data bundle are enough. Start with WhatsApp Business (free download). Set your profile name to your business name, e.g., Mama Ngozi Dried Fruits. Write a short status: Sweet dried mango, pineapple, and banana. 100g for 5,000 UGX. Delivery in Kampala. Add a catalogue with 510 products: take clear photos in natural light, show the pack size, and list the price. Use WhatsApp Status to post daily: a photo of your drying trays, a happy customer, or a short video of you packing orders. Reply to every message within 1 hour.

On Facebook, create a Page (not a personal profile) for your business. Post 3 times a week: Monday (new stock), Wednesday (health tip, e.g., Dried fruit is a natural energy boost for school children), Friday (customer testimonial or special offer). Join 5 local buy-and-sell groups (e.g., Kampala Market Online) and post your products there, following group rules. Use Facebook Marketplace to list each product with a price and your phone number.

Instagram works best with good photos. Use hashtags like #DriedFruitUganda #HealthySnacksKampala #MadeInAfrica. Post 4 times a week: a flat lay of your packs, a short reel of you making the product, and a customer holding your pack. Offer a 10% discount for the first 20 online orders to build momentum.

Always include a call to action: Send a WhatsApp message to 07XX-XXXXXX to order. For delivery, use boda boda within your city (cost 3,0005,000 UGX) and add it to the order. For bulk orders, use a bus courier (e.g., Link Bus) and ask the customer to pay the transport. Track orders in a notebook: name, phone, address, amount, and delivery date. In one month, online sales can bring 100200 packs, adding 500,0001,000,000 UGX to your income.

3. Attending Trade Fairs and Food Exhibitions

Trade fairs put your product in front of hundreds of buyers in one day. In East Africa, look for events like the Uganda International Trade Fair (October, Kampala), Nairobi Trade Fair (September), or local district agricultural shows. A basic stall costs 50,000200,000 UGX for 35 days. Book early. Your goal is not just to sellits to collect contacts and find bulk buyers.

Prepare 200300 small sample packs (20g each) and 100 full-size packs for sale. Bring a banner with your business name, phone number, and Dried Fruit & Vegetables Wholesale & Retail. Display your products on clean trays with labels showing price and weight. Have a notebook and pen to record every visitors name, phone, and interest (e.g., wants 50 packs weekly).

During the fair, stand up, smile, and offer a free sample. Say: Try our sweet dried mangono sugar added. We supply shops and schools. Ask: Do you own a shop or buy for an institution? If they say yes, write down their details and promise to call within 2 days. Follow up immediately after the fair with a WhatsApp message: Thank you for visiting our stall. Here is our price list. Can we deliver 20 packs on Monday?

At the end of the fair, count your contacts. A good fair yields 50100 leads, 1020 actual orders, and 23 wholesale clients. For example, if you sell 100 packs at 5,000 UGX each, thats 500,000 UGX, covering your stall fee and profit. More importantly, you now have a list of buyers who already know your product. Always wear clean clothes, speak clearly, and never sit down while customers are passing.

4. Creating Free Samples and Taste Tests

People buy food they have tasted. Free samples are the cheapest marketing you can do. Make small 20g packsabout 56 pieces of dried mango or banana. Cost per sample: 200300 UGX. If you give out 100 samples, you spend 20,00030,000 UGX. One new regular customer who buys 10 packs weekly at 5,000 UGX brings 50,000 UGX per weekso you recover the cost in less than a week.

Where to give samples: outside schools at closing time, at busy bus parks, outside supermarkets, at church after service, and at local markets. Stand near the entrance, not inside. Wear a clean apron and a badge with your business name. Smile and say: Good morning! Would you like to taste our natural dried pineapple? No sugar, just fruit. Hand them one piece. If they like it, show the full pack and say: This 100g pack is 5,000 UGX. If you buy two, Ill give you one free sample pack for your child.

For taste tests at shops, ask the shopkeeper for permission to set up a small table outside for 2 hours. Bring 50 samples and a few full packs. When someone tastes, ask: Would you buy this if I delivered to your home? If yes, take their phone number. Follow up the next day with a WhatsApp message offering free delivery on their first order.

Keep records: date, location, number of samples given, number of packs sold, and new contacts. After one month, you will know which locations work best. For example, a school gate on Friday at 4 PM might give 20 samples and 8 sales, while a market on Monday morning gives 10 samples and 2 sales. Do more of what works. Never give samples without also asking for a sale or a contactfree food alone doesnt build a business.

5. Building Relationships with Retailers and Wholesalers

Retailers and wholesalers can buy 1001,000 packs at a time. But they only buy from people they trust. Start by identifying 10 small shops, 5 supermarkets, and 2 wholesalers in your area. Visit them in person, not just by phone. Bring a sample pack, a price list, and your contact card. Dress neatly and arrive during quiet hours (e.g., mid-morning on a Tuesday).

When you meet the owner or manager, introduce yourself: My name is [Your Name] from [Business Name]. We make dried fruit and vegetables in [Town]. Id like you to stock our products. Hand them the sample. Explain your wholesale price: for a 100g pack that retails at 5,000 UGX, your wholesale price is 3,500 UGX. They make 1,500 UGX per pack. Offer a first order of 20 packs on consignment: they pay you after selling. This removes their risk.

After they agree, deliver on time. If you promise Monday 9 AM, be there at 8:45 AM. Replace any packs that are near expiry (dried fruit lasts 6 months, but shops prefer fresh stock). Check in every 2 weeks: How are sales? Do you need more? If they sell well, ask for a larger order and a better shelf position. Offer a small incentive: buy 100 packs, get 10 free.

For wholesalers, they want volume and consistency. Offer a price of 3,000 UGX per pack for orders of 500+ packs, and agree on a delivery schedule (e.g., every 2 weeks). Always provide a receipt or invoice. Keep a ledger: date, shop name, packs delivered, packs paid, balance. If a shop pays late, remind them politely after 7 days. Good relationships lead to repeat orders. One good wholesaler can buy 2,000 packs per monththats 6,000,000 UGX in revenue for you. Treat every retailer like a partner, not just a customer.

6. Writing a Simple Sales Pitch for Your Products

A sales pitch is what you say when someone asks, What do you do? or when you meet a potential buyer. It should be short30 secondsand end with a question. Write it down and practise until it feels natural. Use this formula: Who you are + What you make + Why its special + What you want.

Example pitch for a shopkeeper: Hello, my name is Amina. I make dried mango, pineapple, and banana in Jinja. Our fruit is sun-dried with no added sugar or preservativesjust natural sweetness. Shops like yours sell our 100g packs for 5,000 UGX and make 1,500 UGX profit per pack. Can I leave 20 packs with you on consignment to try?

For a school matron: Good morning, Madam. Im Peter from Fresh Dry Fruits. We supply healthy snacks to schools. Our 50g dried fruit packs cost 2,000 UGX wholesaleperfect for break time. They have no sugar and no chemicals. Could we deliver 200 packs next Monday for the students to try?

For a WhatsApp message: Hi [Name], this is [Your Name] from [Business]. We make dried fruit snacks. Our 100g pack is 5,000 UGX, and we deliver free within Kampala for orders above 10 packs. Would you like to try 2 packs this week?

Always include a clear next step: Can I bring a sample tomorrow at 10 AM? or Shall I send you our price list? Never end with Let me know. End with a yes-or-no question. Practise in front of a mirror or with a friend. Record yourself on your phone and listen. Speak slowly and smile. After 10 practises, you will sound confident. A good pitch turns a stranger into a customer in under a minute.

7. Practical Reference

Practical Reference: Marketing and Selling Your Dried Products

Dried fruit and vegetable processing is one of the most accessible value-addition businesses in Africa. But processing is only half the story. Without a clear route to market, even the best-dried mango, tomato, or onion will sit in a bucket while your capital sleeps. This practical reference gives you the tools, numbers, and scripts you need to find buyers, price your products, and build repeat sales whether you are selling in a local market, a WhatsApp group, or a trade fair in Nairobi, Lagos, or Accra.

Across sub-Saharan Africa, the dried fruit and vegetable market is growing. Urbanisation, busy working households, and demand for longer-shelf-life foods are driving sales of dried mango, pineapple, tomato, onion, and leafy greens. At the same time, informal markets and small shops (dukas, kiosks, spaza shops) remain the dominant retail channel. Your job is to link your production to these channels with consistent quality, clear pricing, and simple relationship-building.

Finding Local Customers: Markets, Shops, and Schools

Start where you live. Local markets are the fastest way to test your product and get feedback. Walk the market on a busy day, talk to vendors who already sell snacks, nuts, or dried goods, and offer them a small consignment on a sale-or-return basis. Schools are an underrated channel: many African schools buy snacks for break time, and dried fruit is a healthier alternative to fried snacks. Approach the school bursar or head teacher with a sample pack and a simple price list. Dukas and kiosks in residential areas are also strong: they buy small quantities frequently, so you can build steady volume.

Selling Online via WhatsApp, Facebook, and Instagram

WhatsApp is the most powerful sales tool in most African markets. Set up WhatsApp Business, add a catalogue with photos, prices, and pack sizes, and use a simple greeting message. Post in community groups, church groups, and neighbourhood groups but always ask the admin first. Facebook Marketplace and Instagram work well for urban customers who want home delivery. Use clear photos taken in natural light, show the product in a bowl or on a plate, and always include the price and delivery options. Consistency matters: post at least three times a week.

Attending Trade Fairs and Food Exhibitions

Trade fairs connect you to wholesalers, supermarkets, and institutional buyers. In Africa, key events include the Kenya International Trade Exhibition, the Lagos International Trade Fair, the Ghana Trade Fair, and the SIAL Africa pavilions. Prepare a one-page flyer, a price list, and 50100 sample packs. Collect business cards and follow up within 48 hours. A single fair can generate more leads than months of door-to-door selling.

Creating Free Samples and Taste Tests

People buy what they taste. Offer small samples at markets, in shops, and at fairs. Use clean, sealed sample packs with your label and phone number. Track how many samples you give and how many convert to sales. A conversion rate of 2030% is realistic. Always keep a notebook or phone list of interested buyers.

Building Relationships with Retailers and Wholesalers

Retailers care about three things: reliable supply, consistent quality, and a price that lets them make a margin. Visit shops weekly, check stock, and replace slow-moving products. Offer a simple trade discount: for example, 10% off for orders above 10 kg. Wholesalers buy in bulk, so offer a tiered price list. Always deliver on time and keep your packaging clean and labelled.

Writing a Simple Sales Pitch for Your Products

Your pitch should be short, clear, and focused on the buyer's benefit. Example: "Good morning. I supply dried mango, pineapple, and tomato made in [your town]. Our products are 100% natural, no added sugar, and last up to 12 months. A 100 g pack sells for [price], and I offer a 10% discount on orders above 10 kg. Can I leave a free sample for you to try?" Practise this until it feels natural. Always end with a question that invites a response.

SAFETY WARNING Food Safety and Legal Compliance

Dried products must be safe for human consumption. Always dry produce to a moisture content below 15% (ideally 1012% for fruit) to prevent mould and aflatoxin growth. Use clean, food-grade packaging and label your products with the product name, net weight, ingredients, production date, and your contact details. In many African countries, selling processed food requires registration with the national food safety authority for example, NAFDAC in Nigeria, KEBS in Kenya, or the Ghana Standards Authority. Selling unregistered or contaminated food can lead to fines, product seizure, or harm to consumers. Never sell mouldy, discoloured, or off-smelling products. When in doubt, throw it out.

Quick Reference Checklist

  1. Identify at least 5 local buyers: market vendors, dukas, schools, restaurants, and health shops.
  2. Set up a WhatsApp Business account with a catalogue, clear photos, and prices.
  3. Print a one-page price list and a simple flyer with your contact details.
  4. Prepare 50100 free sample packs with your label and phone number.
  5. Attend at least one trade fair or food exhibition per year.
  6. Offer a trade discount (e.g., 10% above 10 kg) to retailers and wholesalers.
  7. Follow up with every lead within 48 hours and record their details.
  8. Check moisture content and food safety compliance before every sale.
