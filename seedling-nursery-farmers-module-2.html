2. Nursery Setup and Infrastructure

The physical nursery determines how well your seedlings grow. This module covers site selection, shade structures, trays, media, and water systems — the foundation on which your seedling business stands.

Module 2 of 7 · 6 lessons · ~50 minutes · 6 practical exercises · quiz at the end

Lesson 2.1 — Site selection

Your nursery's location determines both plant health and business logistics.

What to look for:

  • Water access. The #1 requirement. Seedlings need daily watering. A borehole, well, river, or municipal connection on-site saves time and money.
  • Level ground. Or slight slope. Not a valley where water pools. Not a steep hill where trays slide.
  • Partial shade. Seedlings need 50–70% sunlight, not full sun. Under a tree, or with shade netting.
  • Wind protection. Strong wind dries trays and topples seedlings.
  • Road access. Customers come to you, or you deliver. Either way, a decent road matters.
  • Security. Seedlings are valuable. Fence or guard.
  • Room to expand. Your nursery will grow. Leave space.
The water rule: If water access is uncertain, do not buy the site. Every other issue can be fixed. Water cannot. This single factor decides whether your nursery works or fails.
Practical exercise 2.1: Identify 3 potential sites. Score each: water access (1–5), ground (1–5), shade (1–5), road (1–5), security (1–5). Highest total wins.

Lesson 2.2 — Shade nets and greenhouses

Full sun kills seedlings. Young plants need filtered light — like being under a tree canopy in nature. Three options:

StructureCost per m²Best forEffectiveness
Natural tree shade$0Very small nurseriesVaries — uneven
Shade net (50–70%)$3–$10Most nurseriesExcellent
Greenhouse (plastic)$15–$40Commercial, all-weatherExcellent, higher investment
Simple shade frame + netting$5–$15Small-mediumGood, low cost

Shade net percentage:

  • Young seedlings (week 1–2): 70% shade
  • Growing seedlings (week 3–5): 50% shade
  • Hardening off (week 6–7): 30% shade or full sun

You can use a single structure with adjustable shade (roll-up sides), or build separate zones. Simpler: use 50% shade throughout, and harden off outdoors.

Practical exercise 2.2: Buy 20 m² of 50% shade netting and poles. Build a 4×5 m shade structure. Test with 20 seedlings — if they grow well, you have the right design.

  • Vegetables: 128-cell plastic trays. Reusable, easy to transport, standard.

Lesson 2.4 — Growing media — soil, compost, coir, sand

Seedlings do not grow well in garden soil alone. They need a light, well-drained, nutrient-rich mix.

Standard mix for African nurseries:

IngredientProportionPurpose
Loamy soil (sieved)40%Base, structure
Well-rotted compost or manure40%Nutrition, water retention
Coarse river sand15%Drainage
Wood ash or agricultural lime5%pH balance, calcium

Alternative media:

  • Coir (coconut fibre): Excellent drainage and water retention. Buy from suppliers. Expensive but effective. Good for commercial.
  • Rice husk: Cheap, locally available. Mix 20% with compost and soil.
  • Commercial seed-starting mix: Ready-made. Best quality but expensive. Use for high-value crops.

Preparation:

  1. Sieve soil to remove stones and clods.
  2. Mix ingredients thoroughly on a clean surface.
  3. Dampen slightly — not wet.
  4. Fill trays loosely. Do not compact.
  5. Sow seeds at correct depth.
The media rule: Do not fill trays with garden soil alone. It compacts, drains poorly, and carries disease. Always use a proper mix.
Practical exercise 2.4: Make 100 litres of growing media using the recipe above. Fill 10 trays. Compare germination and growth to a trial tray filled with plain garden soil.

Lesson 2.5 — Watering systems

Misting system$300–$1,000Very lowCommercial, high-quality control

For a start-up: hand-water with a fine-rose watering can. As you scale, install overhead sprinklers. Eventually, drip irrigation on benches.

Watering rules:

  • Water in the morning. Evening watering risks fungal disease overnight.
  • Water deeply but not often. Shallow daily watering makes weak roots.
  • Use fine spray. Do not knock over seedlings with a heavy stream.
  • Check moisture before watering. Push a finger into the media — if damp, skip.
  • Never over-water. Damping-off kills more seedlings than drought.
Practical exercise 2.5: Buy a watering can with a fine rose ($10–$20). Water your seedlings by hand for 2 weeks. Log water usage and seedling response.

Lesson 2.6 — Bench and layout design

Benches (raised platforms) hold trays at working height. They have three benefits: easier work, better drainage, and fewer ground pests.

Bench specifications:

  • Height: 80–100 cm for easy access.
  • Width: 100–120 cm so you can reach the middle from both sides.
  • Length: As long as your space allows.
  • Material: Treated wood, metal mesh, or concrete. Mesh is best — good drainage and airflow.
  • Spacing: 60 cm between benches for walking.

Nursery layout:

  1. Entrance. Deliveries in, seedlings out.
  2. Storage. Seed, media, tools.
  3. Media prep area. Where you mix and fill trays.
  4. Sowing area. Under shade, near prep.
  5. Germination area. Warm, dark or dim, high humidity.
  6. Growing area. Main shaded bench area.
  7. Hardening area. Partial shade, outdoors.
  8. Dispatch area. Packing and loading.
The workflow rule: Seedlings should flow through the nursery in one direction: sowing → germination → growing → hardening → dispatch. Never have a path crossing back. This saves time and prevents confusion.
Practical exercise 2.6: Sketch your nursery layout on paper. Mark every zone. Build the first bench this week. Expand bench by bench as you scale.
