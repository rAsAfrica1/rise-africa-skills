5. Breeding and Queening

From mating to weaning in 14 weeks. This module covers the reproductive cycle, queening, and kitten care.

Module 5 of 12 · Cat Breeding for Profit

Reproductive cycle of the queen

StageDurationSigns
Proestrus (heat start)1-2 daysRestlessness, affectionate, not yet receptive
Oestrus (mating time)4-7 days (spring-summer)Calling, rolling, receptive to male, lordosis
Interestrus (between heats)7-14 daysReturn to normal behaviour, then heat repeats
Dioestrus (after ovulation)60-70 daysHeat ends, pregnancy begins
Anoestrus (rest)VariesSexually inactive (winter months in seasonal breeders)

Cats are seasonally polyoestrous - they cycle repeatedly during long daylight hours. Indoor cats with artificial light may cycle year-round.

Breeding cycle - a 12-month calendar

MonthEvent
Month 1Health checks, FeLV/FIV test, HCM screening, vaccines up to date
Month 2-3Heat cycle - monitor for signs
Month 3Mating days (usually 2-3 days of standing heat)
Month 4-5Pregnancy - whelping box ready, feeding adjusted
Month 5.5Queening
Month 6-8Kitten care, weaning, vaccination
Month 8-9Kittens sold or placed
Month 9+Rest - no breeding for 2-3 months minimum

Signs of heat (oestrus)

  • Loud, persistent calling (especially at night)
  • Rolling on the ground
  • Restlessness, increased affection
  • Rubbing against objects and people
  • Lordosis: raising hindquarters when touched on the back
  • Treading back feet
  • Receptive to male - stands still for mating
  • Reduced appetite
  • Vulva slightly swollen (less obvious than in dogs)

Determining the right day to mate

MethodAccuracyCost
Observing standing heatGoodFree
Vaginal cytologyHighLow
Progesterone blood testHighModerate
LH surge testVery highHigher

Best practice: mate 2-3 times on consecutive days during standing heat. If using progesterone testing, mate 2 days after ovulation.

Mating rules

  1. Both cats must be willing. Never force a mating.
  2. Bring the queen to the stud''s territory (cats usually mate in the male''s space).
  3. Supervise all matings. Cats can hurt each other if mismatched.
  4. Multiple matings over 2-3 days improve conception rate.
  5. Ovulation in cats is induced by mating - multiple matings increase ovulation.
  6. Record the date, time, and male.
  7. Separate the cats after mating - males can be aggressive.

Pregnancy diagnosis

MethodDays post-matingAccuracy
Palpation (skilled vet)20-30 daysGood if trained

X-ray (count kittens)45-55 daysVery high
External signs (size, milk, nesting)50+ daysModerate

X-rays at day 55 give an exact kitten count - critical for queening planning.

Pregnancy care by week

WeekCare
Week 1-3Normal routine, no stress, no drugs, no vaccines
Week 4-6Gradual increase in feed, moderate exercise, deworm at week 6 (safely)
Week 6-7Prepare queening box, watch for nesting signs
Week 7-8Reduce handling, watch for signs of queening
Week 8-9Temperature monitoring (drops below 37 C within 24 hours of queening)

Queening - three stages

Stage 1 (2-12 hours)

  • Restless, panting, nesting
  • Licking the vulva
  • May refuse food
  • Seeking isolation

Stage 2 (delivery of kittens)

  • Visible contractions
  • First kitten usually within 30 minutes
  • Subsequent kittens every 10-60 minutes
  • Total queening usually 2-6 hours (can be up to 24 hours)

Stage 3 (after each kitten)

  • Placenta passes after each kitten
  • Count placentas - should equal kitten count
  • Queen eats placentas (normal)
  • Queen cleans and nurses each kitten immediately

When to call the vet during queening

  • Strong straining for over 20-30 minutes with no kitten
  • More than 1-2 hours between kittens with continuing contractions
  • Green or black discharge before first kitten
  • Queen in obvious pain, exhausted
  • Kitten stuck in the birth canal
  • More than 4 hours total without producing a kitten after contractions begin
  • Queen''s temperature rises above 39.5 C
  • Any retained kittens - detectable by X-ray count vs delivered count

Assisting with a queening

  1. Wash hands, use clean gloves.
  2. Break the sac if the kitten is born still inside.
  3. Clear the airway - wipe the mouth and nose.
  4. Rub the kitten vigorously with a warm towel to stimulate breathing.
  5. If no breathing within 30 seconds - use a bulb syringe or hold head-down and swing gently.
  6. Clamp and cut the cord 2 cm from the body, dip in iodine (if the queen has not done it herself).
  7. Place the kitten on the queen''s teat - colostrum is critical in the first hour.
  8. Weigh and record the kitten (typical 80-140 g depending on breed).
  9. Keep the queening box warm (28-32 C).

Care of the queen after queening

  • Fresh water and food available.
  • Warm, quiet, dark queening area.
  • Check for retained placentas - count them. Retained = vet.
  • Check discharge - should be red-brown, becoming clear within 7 days.
  • Check for metritis - smelly discharge, fever, off-feed = vet.
  • Monitor milk production - swollen, hot udders = mastitis.
  • Continue feeding free-choice for 6-8 weeks.
  • Do not bathe for 7-10 days.
  • Minimal visitors for 2 weeks.

Weighing and monitoring

AgeExpected daily gain
Day 0-75-15 g per day
Week 2Doubled birth weight
Week 44x birth weight
Week 88-10x birth weight
Week 1212-14x birth weight

Weigh kittens daily for the first 2 weeks and weekly after. A kitten not gaining weight is sick - check for:

  • Insufficient milk (queen problem)
  • Rejection by mother
  • Infection (URI, panleukopenia, parasites)
  • Too cold (kittens cannot digest when cold)
  • Congenital defect
  • Fading kitten syndrome

Hand-rearing rules:

  • Never feed cow milk - use kitten milk replacer only.
  • Feed warm (37 C) - test on your wrist.
  • Stimulate toileting after each feed with a warm damp cloth.
  • Keep warm - 28-30 C for newborns.
  • Weigh daily.
  • Consult a vet early - hand-reared kittens need close monitoring.

Common queening emergencies

ProblemAction
Dystocia (difficult birth)Vet immediately - may need C-section
Retained placentaVet within 12-24 hours
Stillborn kittensContinue queening, call vet if labor stalls
Queen with no milkBottle-feed or find surrogate, vet for hormones
Eclampsia (milk fever)Vet immediately - calcium deficiency, can kill
MastitisVet for antibiotics, keep kittens feeding or bottle-feed
MetritisVet for antibiotics - infection of the uterus
HaemorrhageVet immediately - life-threatening
The vet rule: During queening, if anything seems wrong, call the vet. Waiting 20 minutes can be the difference between a live litter and a dead one.
