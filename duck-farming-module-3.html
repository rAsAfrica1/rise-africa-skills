1. Nutritional Requirements by Age and Purpose

Ducks need different nutrients at different stages: starter (0-2 weeks), grower (3-8 weeks), finisher (9 weeks to market), and layer (for egg production). Starter feed should have 20-22% protein, 0.9% calcium, and 0.4% available phosphorus. A good starter mash in Africa can be made from maize meal, soybean meal, fishmeal, and a vitamin-mineral premix. For example, mix 55% maize, 25% soybean meal, 10% fishmeal, 8% wheat bran, and 2% premix. This gives about 21% protein. Grower feed: 16-18% protein, 0.8% calcium. Finisher: 14-16% protein, 0.8% calcium. Layer feed: 16-18% protein, 3.5-4% calcium for strong eggshells. If you keep ducks for meat, focus on energy and protein for rapid growth. For eggs, calcium and protein are critical. Local ingredients like cassava, sweet potato, and moringa can replace some maize and soybean, but adjust to avoid nutrient gaps. Always provide clean waterducks drink up to 1 liter per day per adult. Without proper nutrition, ducks grow slowly, lay fewer eggs, and get sick easily. Weigh your ducks weekly: a healthy Pekin should reach 2.5-3 kg in 8-10 weeks. If they are underweight, increase protein and energy. Use a simple kitchen scale or a hanging scale for accuracy. In Africa, many farmers use commercial feeds from companies like Vital, Ugachick, or local millers. If you mix your own, use a feed formulation table or ask an extension officer. Never guesswrong ratios waste money and harm birds. Adjust feed as seasons change: in cold weather, ducks need more energy; in hot weather, they eat less, so increase nutrient density. Always store feed in dry, cool place to prevent mold.

2. Commercial Feed Types and Formulations

Commercial duck feeds come as mash, pellets, or crumbles. In Africa, common brands include Vital, Ugachick, Nutri, and local miller mixes. For ducks, choose feeds labeled for ducks or poultry with similar protein levels. Starter crumbles (0-2 weeks) are easy for ducklings to eat. Grower pellets (3-8 weeks) reduce waste. Finisher pellets (9+ weeks) for meat. Layer mash or pellets for egg-laying ducks. If you cannot find duck-specific feed, use chicken feed but adjust: ducks need more niacin (vitamin B3). Add 10-15 mg niacin per kg of feed, or supplement with brewer's yeast. A typical commercial formulation: Starter: 20% protein, 2,900 kcal ME/kg. Grower: 17% protein, 3,000 kcal ME/kg. Finisher: 15% protein, 3,100 kcal ME/kg. Layer: 17% protein, 3.5% calcium, 2,800 kcal ME/kg. If you mix your own, use a digital scale for accuracy. A simple 100 kg batch of grower feed: 50 kg maize, 20 kg soybean meal, 10 kg fishmeal, 10 kg wheat bran, 5 kg limestone, 3 kg bone meal, 1 kg salt, 1 kg premix. Mix thoroughly on a clean concrete floor or in a drum mixer. Store in airtight bags away from moisture. Commercial feeds are convenient but costly. Many African farmers blend commercial feed with local ingredients to cut costs. For example, replace 20% of commercial feed with chopped greens or kitchen waste, but ensure the total protein remains adequate. Always check expiration dates and avoid moldy feed. Mold produces aflatoxins that can kill ducks. If feed smells musty or has clumps, discard it. Buy from reputable suppliers and ask for a feed analysis certificate. For small flocks, buy small quantities to avoid spoilage. For large flocks, order in bulk and store in a dry, ventilated room on pallets. Never feed ducks moldy or wet feed.

3. Supplementary Feeding with Greens and Insects

Ducks love greens and insects, and these can cut feed costs by 20-30% while improving health. Safe greens include: amaranth leaves, sweet potato vines, cabbage, kale, spinach, water hyacinth (chopped), duckweed, and azolla. Duckweed is a superfood for ducksit contains 25-35% protein and grows in ponds. You can grow duckweed in a shallow pit with animal manure. Harvest daily and feed fresh. Azolla is another aquatic fern with 20-25% protein. Insects: termites, black soldier fly larvae (BSFL), earthworms, and crickets. BSFL are excellentthey contain 40-45% protein and 30% fat. You can breed BSFL in a bin with kitchen waste. Termites can be trapped using a plastic container filled with dry grass and a little water, buried upside down. Ducks will eat them eagerly. Feed greens and insects as supplements, not main diet. For growing ducks, give 50-100 g of greens per duck per day, and 20-50 g of insects. For layers, give 100-150 g greens and 30-60 g insects. Chop greens finely to prevent choking. Never feed moldy, wilted, or sprayed greens. Avoid toxic plants like cassava leaves (raw), tomato leaves, and potato leaves. Introduce new feeds gradually over 5-7 days to avoid digestive upset. Ducks also eat snails, slugs, and small fishthese are good protein sources. In Africa, many farmers let ducks scavenge in rice paddies, gardens, and ponds. This reduces feed costs but can expose ducks to predators and diseases. If you free-range, provide supplemental feed twice a day. A simple supplement mix: 1 part chopped greens, 1 part cooked cassava, 1 part insects, mixed with a little commercial feed. This gives balanced nutrition. Always provide clean water for drinking and foraging. Ducks need water to swallownever feed dry feed without water. Greens and insects also provide water. Monitor ducks' weight and egg production; if they drop, increase commercial feed.

4. Feeding Schedules and Ration Calculation

Ducks need a consistent feeding schedule. Feed adults twice a day: morning (7-8 am) and evening (4-5 pm). Ducklings (0-2 weeks) need feed available all dayuse shallow feeders. From 3 weeks, feed three times a day. Layers: feed twice a day, but provide calcium (oyster shell) free-choice. Meat ducks: feed twice a day, but restrict to avoid overfat. A simple rule: ducks eat about 100-150 g of feed per day for growing, 150-200 g for layers. But calculate based on body weight and purpose. For meat ducks, feed 3-4% of body weight daily. For layers, 150 g per day plus greens. To calculate ration: Determine total feed needed per day = number of ducks daily feed per duck. For 10 growing ducks at 120 g each = 1.2 kg per day. For 10 layers at 160 g = 1.6 kg per day. Mix your feed in a wheelbarrow or basin. Use a weighing scalekitchen scale for small amounts, hanging scale for large. If you don't have a scale, use a known container: a 1-liter cup holds about 500 g of feed. But weighing is more accurate. Feed in clean, shallow troughs or trays. Ducks are messythey will spill and waste feed. Use feeders with lips to reduce waste. Never let feed sit in feeders for more than a few hours, especially in humid climatesit gets moldy. Remove leftover feed and clean feeders daily. Provide water in deep containers so ducks can dip their heads and clean their nostrils. Ducks need water to swallow. Always have water available. Adjust feed based on season: in hot weather, feed early morning and late evening when it's cool. In cold weather, feed more energy-rich feeds like maize. Monitor feed consumptionif ducks suddenly eat less, they may be sick. Keep records: date, number of ducks, feed type, amount, and notes. This helps you track costs and productivity. For 100 layers, you need about 16 kg feed per day. At $0.50 per kg, that's $8 per day. Plan your budget accordingly.

5. Waterfowl-Specific Feed Supplements

Ducks have specific nutritional needs that differ from chickens. The most critical is niacin (vitamin B3). Ducklings fed chicken feed without added niacin can develop leg deformities, weak legs, and difficulty walking. Add niacin at 10-15 mg per kg of feed, or supplement with brewer's yeast (1-2% of feed). If you see ducklings with bowed legs, add niacin immediately. Another key nutrient is vitamin E and selenium for immune function and fertility. Add 20-30 IU vitamin E and 0.1 mg selenium per kg feed. Calcium and phosphorus must be balanced: for layers, 3.5-4% calcium, 0.4% available phosphorus. For growers, 0.8% calcium, 0.4% phosphorus. Too much calcium for growing ducks causes kidney damage. Use limestone or oyster shell for calcium, and bone meal or dicalcium phosphate for phosphorus. Ducks also need omega-3 fatty acids for egg quality and health. Add 1-2% fish oil or flaxseed oil to layer feed. In Africa, you can use local sources: moringa leaves (rich in vitamins), papaya leaves, and pumpkin seeds. Moringa leaf meal can replace 5-10% of feed and provides vitamins A, E, and K. Pumpkin seeds are a natural dewormer and provide zinc. For waterfowl, provide grit (small stones) to help digest food. Ducks that free-range pick up grit naturally. For confined ducks, offer a bowl of grit (crushed granite) free-choice. Also provide oyster shell free-choice for layers. Salt: add 0.3-0.5% salt to feed, but never moreexcess salt causes poisoning. A simple supplement mix for 100 kg feed: 100 g niacin, 50 g vitamin E, 50 g selenium premix, 500 g brewer's yeast, 1 kg moringa leaf meal, 500 g pumpkin seed meal. Mix thoroughly. Always follow label instructions for commercial premixes. Overdosing vitamins can be toxic. Store supplements in cool, dark place. If you use commercial duck feed, it usually has these supplements already. But if you mix your own, you must add them. Check with an extension officer or vet for specific recommendations for your region.

6. Avoiding Common Feeding Mistakes

Many duck farmers lose money and birds due to avoidable feeding mistakes. The most common: feeding moldy feed. Mold produces aflatoxins that cause liver damage, poor growth, and death. Always smell feed before givingif musty, discard. Store feed in dry, cool place on pallets, not on floor. Another mistake: not providing enough water. Ducks need water to swallow and digest. Without water, they choke and die. Provide deep water containers (at least 10 cm deep) so ducks can submerge their heads. Change water daily. Third: feeding only maize or kitchen scraps. This causes protein and vitamin deficiencies. Ducks need balanced feed with protein, energy, vitamins, and minerals. Fourth: overfeeding layers. Fat ducks lay fewer eggs. Restrict feed to 150-160 g per day for layers. Fifth: not adjusting feed for age. Feeding starter to adults wastes money; feeding layer feed to ducklings causes kidney damage from excess calcium. Sixth: sudden feed changes. Always transition over 5-7 days by mixing old and new feed. Seventh: using chicken feed without niacin. Ducklings need extra niacin. Eighth: feeding toxic plants: raw cassava leaves, tomato leaves, potato leaves, and avocado leaves. These contain toxins that can kill ducks. Ninth: not providing grit. Ducks need grit to grind food in their gizzard. Tenth: feeding on dirty ground. Ducks pick up feces and bacteria. Use clean feeders and trays. Eleventh: not weighing feed. Guessing leads to under- or overfeeding. Use a scale. Twelfth: ignoring weight loss or drop in egg production. These are signs of illness or poor feed. Act quickly. Thirteenth: feeding too much salt. Salt poisoning causes thirst, diarrhea, and death. Keep salt at 0.3-0.5% of feed. Fourteenth: not storing feed properly. Rats, mice, and insects contaminate feed. Use airtight containers. Fifteenth: feeding expired commercial feed. Vitamins degrade over time. Check dates. By avoiding these mistakes, you will save money and have healthier ducks. Keep records and observe your birds daily. If something seems wrong, consult a vet or experienced farmer.

7. Practical Reference

Practical Reference: Duck Nutrition & Feeding for African Duck Enterprises

Duck farming in Africa is a business, not a hobby. Whether you are raising Muscovy, Pekin, Khaki Campbell, or local indigenous ducks in Kenya, Nigeria, Ghana, Uganda, or South Africa, feed is your single largest cost typically 6075% of total production cost. Get nutrition right and your flock converts feed into eggs and meat profitably. Get it wrong and you burn money on wasted feed, slow growth, poor laying, and disease.

This Practical Reference lesson consolidates the six core lessons of this module into one field-ready document. It is written for the small-to-medium commercial duck farmer who needs to make fast, correct decisions on the ground.

1. Nutritional Requirements by Age and Purpose

Ducks are not chickens. They have higher energy requirements per kg of feed, higher niacin needs, and a faster passage rate through the digestive tract. Feeding chicken mash to ducks is a common and costly mistake. Below is a practical nutrient matrix for duck classes common in African production systems.

2. Commercial Feed Types and Formulations

In most African markets you will find three practical feed routes: (a) buy complete commercial duck/chicken feed, (b) buy concentrates and mix with your own maize and protein meals, or (c) mix entirely on-farm from raw ingredients. Route (c) is cheapest per kg but demands accurate formulation and consistent ingredient quality.

Common locally available ingredients and their typical CP content:

  • Maize / maize bran energy base, 89% CP (maize), 810% CP (bran)
  • Soya bean meal 4448% CP, the gold standard plant protein
  • Sunflower cake 2832% CP, high fibre, use sparingly in starter
  • Cottonseed cake 3240% CP, but limit due to gossypol (max 58% of diet)
  • Fish meal (local tilapia/sardine) 5565% CP, excellent but expensive
  • Groundnut cake 4045% CP, watch for aflatoxin
  • Cassava chips / peel energy source, low CP, use in grower/finisher only
  • Limestone / oyster shell calcium source
  • Dicalcium phosphate / bone meal phosphorus source
  • Premix vitamins, minerals, niacin, methionine

A workable on-farm starter mix for 100 kg (approximately 21% CP): 55 kg maize, 30 kg soya meal, 8 kg fish meal, 3 kg limestone, 2 kg dicalcium phosphate, 1 kg premix, 1 kg salt. Adjust based on your actual ingredient analysis. Always add a duck-specific or poultry premix containing niacin ducks require roughly 23 the niacin of chickens, and deficiency causes leg weakness and poor growth.

3. Supplementary Feeding with Greens and Insects

Ducks are excellent foragers and this is a genuine cost advantage in Africa. Supplementary feeding with greens and insects can cut purchased feed cost by 1530% in free-range and semi-intensive systems.

Greens: Duckweed (from ponds), water spinach, amaranth, sweet potato vines, cassava leaves (limited cyanide risk if not wilted/dried), kale, cabbage, and vegetable market waste. Chop finely for young ducks. Greens should not exceed 2030% of the diet for layers if you want consistent egg size.

Insects: Black soldier fly (BSF) larvae are the most commercially viable insect protein in Africa. Dried BSF larvae contain 3545% CP and 2535% fat. Termites, housefly larvae, and earthworms are also effective. A BSF rearing unit can be built for under USD 100 and supplies protein year-round.

Aquatic protein: Where ponds or rivers are available, ducks self-harvest small fish, snails, and tadpoles. This is free protein but biosecurity risk avoid stagnant water shared with wild waterfowl.

Ration calculation example (100 layers): 100 birds 200 g/day = 20 kg feed/day. If your layer mix costs KES 55/kg, daily feed cost = KES 1,100. If each bird lays 0.75 eggs/day, you get 75 eggs. At KES 20/egg, revenue = KES 1,500. Feed cost is 73% of revenue this is your break-even pressure point. Every 1% reduction in feed waste is direct profit.

Feeding schedule: Feed twice daily early morning (67 am) and late afternoon (45 pm). Ducks eat most actively at dawn and dusk. Never feed mouldy or wet fermented feed left overnight in tropical heat.

5. Waterfowl-Specific Feed Supplements

Ducks have specific needs that chicken feed does not always meet:

  • Niacin (Vitamin B3): Critical. Ducks need 5570 mg/kg feed. Chicken starter often has only 2530 mg/kg. Add brewer's yeast or a niacin supplement in the first 3 weeks.
  • Methionine: First limiting amino acid. Add 0.050.1% DL-methionine to maize-soya diets for optimal growth and feathering.
  • Calcium: Layers need 2.83.5% calcium. Provide oyster shell or limestone free-choice in a separate hopper.
  • Grit: Ducks swallowing whole grains need insoluble grit (small stones) for gizzard function. Offer free-choice.
  • Vitamin E & Selenium: Especially for breeders improves hatchability and immunity.
  • Electrolytes: In heat stress (common in Sahel, northern Nigeria, Kenya lowlands), add electrolytes to drinking water.

SAFETY WARNING Aflatoxin & Mouldy Feed

Aflatoxin is the number one silent killer in African duck production. Groundnut cake, maize, and cottonseed cake stored in humid tropical conditions can develop Aspergillus flavus mould within 4872 hours. Aflatoxin causes liver damage, reduced egg production, poor growth, immunosuppression, and mortality often without obvious symptoms. Never feed mouldy, musty-smelling, or discoloured feed to ducks. Store all feed in dry, raised, ventilated conditions. Use a mould inhibitor (e.g., propionic acid) in humid climates. If you suspect aflatoxin, stop the feed immediately and consult your extension officer or veterinarian. Ducklings are 510 more sensitive than adult birds.

6. Avoiding Common Feeding Mistakes

  1. Feeding chicken mash to ducks. Ducks need higher energy, niacin, and methionine. Use duck-specific or waterfowl-formulated feed.
  2. Overfeeding protein to layers. Excess CP (above 18%) increases feed cost without increasing egg output, and stresses kidneys.
  3. No calcium source for layers. Without supplemental calcium, shells become thin and eggs break in the nest direct revenue loss.
  4. Wet, stale feed left in troughs. Ducks splash water into feed. Use shallow, clean troughs and remove leftovers daily.
  5. Ignoring water quality. Ducks need clean drinking water and water for head-dipping. Dirty water spreads disease.
  6. Sudden feed changes. Transition over 57 days by mixing old and new feed to avoid digestive upset and drop in production.
  7. No record-keeping. You cannot calculate feed conversion ratio (FCR) or cost per egg without records. Track daily feed in, eggs out, and mortality.
  8. Feeding only maize. Maize is energy, not protein. Ducks on pure maize grow slowly and lay poorly. Always balance with protein meals.

Quick Reference Checklist

  • Match feed CP and energy to the correct age/purpose class (see Table 1).
  • Ensure niacin 55 mg/kg in starter feed supplement if using chicken feed.
  • Provide free-choice oyster shell/limestone to all laying ducks.
  • Offer insoluble grit free-choice, especially when feeding whole grains.
  • Feed twice daily (dawn and late afternoon); remove stale feed daily.
  • Supply clean drinking water at 1.52 L/bird/day for layers.
  • Supplement with greens and BSF larvae to cut purchased feed cost by 1530%.
  • Record daily feed intake, egg count, and mortality calculate FCR weekly.
  • Reject any mouldy, musty, or discoloured feed aflatoxin risk is non-negotiable.
  • Transition between feed types over 57 days, never abruptly.
