1
Choosing Your Breed
Start

Africa has diverse goat and sheep breeds. Your choice depends on climate, market (meat/milk/wool), and management intensity.

  • West African Dwarf (WAD) goat: hardy, disease-resistant, dual-purpose meat + milk.
  • Red Sokoto (Maradi): excellent meat quality, popular in Northern Nigeria.
  • Balami sheep: large frame, fast growth, preferred for ram sales during festivals.
Pro Tip: Buy from reputable breeders, not markets. Market animals may carry hidden diseases.

Key Takeaway: Match breed to your environment and market. WAD goats are the safest beginner choice.

Housing & Fencing
Locked

Goats and sheep are browsers, not grazers. They will escape, climb, and destroy crops if your fencing is inadequate.

  • Housing: 1.5-2 sq meters per adult. Raised slatted floors reduce parasite load.
  • Fencing: use chain-link or barbed wire at least 1.5m high. Goats climb wire mesh.
  • Night penning is essential in areas with hyenas, dogs, or thieves.
Pro Tip: Plant Gliricidia or Leucaena around the perimeter. They provide fodder AND living fence posts.

Key Takeaway: Strong fencing is cheaper than replacing stolen or predated stock.

Nutrition & Pasture Management
Locked

Sheep graze; goats browse. Understanding this difference determines your feeding strategy and pasture design.

  • Goats prefer leaves, twigs, and shrubs. Sheep prefer grasses and legumes.
  • Supplement with concentrate feed (maize bran, soybean cake, mineral lick) during dry season.
  • Provide salt licks always. Mineral deficiency causes poor growth and weak offspring.
Pro Tip: Cut and carry fodder during the wet season and store as hay for the dry season.

Key Takeaway: Mixed grazing (sheep + goats) utilizes all pasture layers and increases total farm output.

Breeding & Reproduction
Locked

Goats and sheep are seasonal breeders in the tropics, but management can extend your breeding window.

  • Goats cycle every 18-21 days. Signs: tail wagging, swollen vulva, mounting others.
  • Gestation: goats 150 days, sheep 147 days. Mark breeding dates on a calendar.
  • Flushing (extra feed) 2-3 weeks before mating improves ovulation and twinning rates.
Pro Tip: Keep a breeding record. Knowing exact due dates lets you supervise births and reduce mortality.

Key Takeaway: Record breeding dates, observe heat signs, and flush before mating for maximum offspring.

Kidding & Lambing Care
Locked

Neonatal mortality is the biggest loss in small ruminant farming. Proper birth assistance saves lives.

  • Prepare a clean, dry kidding pen with fresh bedding 1 week before due date.
  • Normal birth: front feet and nose appear first. If not, assist gently or call a vet.
  • Ensure kids/lambs nurse colostrum within 1 hour. Colostrum provides immunity.
Pro Tip: Dip the navel in iodine or strong salt solution immediately after birth to prevent infection.

Key Takeaway: Clean pens, timely assistance, and first colostrum = low kid/lamb mortality.

Health & Parasite Control
Locked

Internal parasites (worms) are the #1 killer of goats and sheep in humid Africa. Control requires strategy, not just dewormers.

  • Rotate pastures: don't graze the same paddock continuously. Rest paddocks for 3+ weeks.
  • Deworm strategically: at weaning, pre-partum, and when FAMACHA eye color shows anemia.
  • Vaccinate against PPR (Peste des Petits Ruminants) — a devastating viral disease.
Pro Tip: Use the FAMACHA chart (eye membrane color) to identify anemic animals. Only deworm those that need it to prevent resistance.

Key Takeaway: Strategic deworming + vaccination + pasture rotation = healthy flock.

Meat & Milk Marketing
Locked

Goat and sheep sales peak during religious festivals (Eid, Christmas, Easter). Timing your production to these dates multiplies profit.

  • Ram sales for Eid-el-Kabir can be 3x normal price. Plan breeding backwards from festival dates.
  • Goat milk is hypoallergenic and commands premium prices in urban areas.
  • Sell live at market, or process into smoked meat, suya, or kilishi for higher margins.
Pro Tip: Build relationships with butchers and festival buyers 3 months before peak season.

Key Takeaway: Religious festivals are your cash crop. Plan breeding and fattening to hit these windows.

Record Keeping & Scaling
Locked

From 5 goats to 50, then 500 — scaling requires the same discipline: records, selection, and reinvestment.

  • Tag every animal. Record birth date, parent, weight at weaning, and health events.
  • Cull poor performers: slow growers, frequent abortions, bad mothers.
  • Reinvest 50% of profit into stock or infrastructure for the first 3 years.
Pro Tip: Join a livestock association. Group marketing gives you bargaining power with buyers and abattoirs.

Key Takeaway: Records enable selection. Selection improves genetics. Improved genetics = exponential growth.
