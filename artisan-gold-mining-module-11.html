1. Introduction and Why It Matters

Selling your gold correctly is the difference between being paid fairly and being cheated. In Africa, artisanal gold miners often sell to the first buyer who comes along, usually a local trader who offers cash on the spot. But that convenience comes at a cost: prices can be 20-30% below the global market rate. For example, if the world gold price is $60 per gram, a local buyer might offer you $45 per gram. On 10 grams, that's a loss of $150 enough to buy a new metal detector or pay school fees. Learning to sell properly means you keep more of your hard-earned money.

Gold is sold by weight and purity. The global standard is the troy ounce (31.1 grams), but in Africa, most transactions use grams. Purity is measured in karats or fineness. Pure gold is 24 karats (99.9% gold). Your raw gold might be 18-22 karats, mixed with silver, copper, or other minerals. Buyers will test purity using acids, electronic testers, or fire assay. If you don't know your gold's purity, you can't negotiate a fair price. A simple acid test kit costs about $30 and can save you hundreds.

Compliance is not just bureaucracy it's protection. Many African countries require artisanal miners to sell through licensed dealers or government buying centres. For instance, in Ghana, the Precious Minerals Marketing Company (PMMC) is the official buyer. In Tanzania, you must sell through licensed mineral traders. Selling to unlicensed buyers might seem easier, but you risk arrest, confiscation, or not getting paid at all. Licensed buyers also provide receipts, which you need if you want to open a bank account or apply for a loan.

This module will teach you how to find reputable buyers, test purity, negotiate using live gold prices, and stay on the right side of the law. You'll learn to calculate the value of your gold before you walk into a deal. By the end, you'll be able to sell with confidence and keep more money in your pocket. Remember: gold is a global commodity you deserve a global price.

2. Tools and Materials

To sell gold professionally, you need a few basic tools and materials. First, a digital scale that measures in grams to at least 0.01g accuracy. A good one costs $20-$50 on Amazon or from local electronics shops. Calibrate it with a 100g calibration weight before each use. Second, a gold purity testing kit. This includes a set of test acids (10k, 14k, 18k, 22k, 24k), a touchstone (a black slate stone), and a set of needles. You can buy a complete kit for about $30-$60. Third, a jeweller's loupe (10x magnification) to inspect gold for impurities. Fourth, a small magnet gold is not magnetic, so if your gold sticks to a magnet, it's fake or mixed with iron.

For compliance, you need identification documents: your national ID, a mining licence or permit if required, and any government-issued seller's card. In many countries, you must register as an artisanal miner with the local minerals commission. Keep photocopies of all documents. Also, a notebook to record every sale: date, buyer's name, weight, purity, price per gram, total amount, and receipt number. This record is crucial for tax purposes and for proving your income if you need a loan.

You also need a safe way to transport gold. A small leather pouch or a locked metal box is fine. Never carry large amounts of gold openly. If you are travelling to a city to sell, consider using a licensed courier or a trusted friend. For large quantities (50g+), some buyers will come to you, but always meet in a public place during daylight.

Finally, access to live gold prices. A smartphone with internet is ideal you can check websites like goldprice.org or Kitco. If you don't have a smartphone, call a friend who does, or visit a local internet caf. The London Bullion Market Association (LBMA) sets the global benchmark price twice daily. Your local price should be within 5-10% of that, minus refining and transport costs. Knowing the current price gives you negotiating power.

3. Step by Step Method

Step 1: Weigh and test your gold. Use your digital scale to get the exact weight in grams. Then test purity with your acid kit. Rub the gold on the touchstone, apply a drop of 18k acid. If the mark disappears, it's less than 18k; if it stays, it's 18k or higher. Test with 22k and 24k acids to narrow it down. Record the weight and estimated purity. For example, 10 grams of 20k gold contains 10 x (20/24) = 8.33 grams of pure gold.

Step 2: Calculate the fair price. Look up the current global gold price per gram (e.g., $60/g for pure gold). Multiply by the pure gold content. For 8.33g pure, that's 8.33 x $60 = $499.80. Then subtract a refining and dealer margin, typically 5-15%. So a fair offer might be $425-$475. If a buyer offers $300, you know you're being underpaid.

Step 3: Find licensed buyers. Contact your country's minerals commission or ministry of mining for a list of licensed buyers. In Ghana, call PMMC at +233 30 266 4141. In Tanzania, check the Ministry of Minerals website. You can also ask other miners in your area they often know who pays well. Avoid buyers who refuse to show a licence or who want to meet in secret locations.

Step 4: Negotiate. Bring your test results and price calculation. Start by asking the buyer what price they offer. If it's below your fair price, counter with your calculation. Be polite but firm. Mention that you know the global price. Many buyers will increase their offer if they see you are informed. If they won't budge, walk away there are other buyers.

Step 5: Complete the sale. Once you agree on a price, weigh the gold in front of the buyer on your own scale (or a mutually trusted scale). Get payment in a secure form: bank transfer, mobile money, or cash in a safe place. Insist on a receipt that includes the buyer's name, licence number, weight, purity, price per gram, total amount, and date. Keep a copy. If the buyer refuses a receipt, do not sell it's likely illegal.

Step 6: Record and bank. Enter the sale in your notebook. Deposit the money into a bank or mobile money account immediately. This creates a financial trail that helps you get loans or prove income. Never keep large amounts of cash at home.

4. Common Mistakes and Fixes

Mistake 1: Selling without testing purity. Many miners accept the buyer's word on purity. Fix: Always test with an acid kit. If you don't have one, ask a trusted fellow miner or a jeweller to test it for a small fee. Knowing your purity can increase your price by 10-20%.

Mistake 2: Not checking the global price. If you don't know the current price, you can't negotiate. Fix: Check goldprice.org or Kitco daily. Write down the price before you go to sell. Even a 5% difference on 20 grams can mean $60.

Mistake 3: Selling to unlicensed buyers. Unlicensed buyers may offer cash quickly but often cheat on weight or purity, or disappear without paying. Fix: Only sell to licensed buyers. Ask to see their licence. In Ghana, licensed buyers have a PMMC certificate. In Tanzania, they have a mineral trading licence.

Mistake 4: Not getting a receipt. Without a receipt, you have no proof of sale. If the buyer later claims you stole the gold, you can't defend yourself. Fix: Always insist on a receipt with full details. If the buyer refuses, walk away.

Mistake 5: Carrying large amounts of gold or cash alone. This makes you a target for robbery. Fix: For large sales, bring a trusted friend. Use mobile money or bank transfer instead of cash. If you must carry cash, split it into different pockets and never show it in public.

Mistake 6: Ignoring taxes. In many countries, gold sales are taxable. If you don't pay, you risk fines or jail. Fix: Ask the licensed buyer about tax. They often deduct it at source and give you a tax receipt. Keep all records.

Mistake 7: Selling everything at once. If you sell all your gold when the price is low, you lose potential profit. Fix: If you can, sell in smaller batches over time. But don't hoard too long prices fluctuate and storage is risky.

Mistake 8: Not negotiating. Many miners accept the first offer. Fix: Always counter-offer. Even a 5% increase is worth it. Practice your negotiation with a friend before you go.

5. Safety and Best Practice

Safety when selling gold is about protecting yourself, your money, and your reputation. First, personal safety. Never meet a buyer alone in a remote area. Choose a public place like a bank, a licensed dealer's office, or a busy market during daylight. Tell a family member or friend where you are going and when you expect to return. If you feel threatened, leave immediately. Carry a whistle or pepper spray if legal in your area.

Second, financial safety. Avoid carrying large sums of cash. Use mobile money (M-Pesa, Airtel Money, MTN Mobile Money) or a bank transfer. If cash is the only option, count it in a secure room, not in the open. Check for counterfeit notes in some countries, fake currency is common. Use a counterfeit detector pen (costs about $5). Deposit money into your bank or mobile money account the same day. Never keep gold or cash under your mattress.

Third, legal safety. Always comply with local mining and selling laws. In many African countries, artisanal miners must sell through government-approved channels. For example, in Ghana, you must sell to PMMC or a licensed agent. In Tanzania, you need a mineral trading licence. In South Africa, you must sell to a licensed precious metals dealer. Selling to illegal buyers can lead to arrest, fines, or confiscation of your gold. Keep your mining licence and ID with you. If a buyer asks for a kickback or bribe, report them to the minerals commission.

Fourth, best practices for pricing. Always get at least two offers before selling. Compare prices from different licensed buyers. Some buyers offer better prices for larger quantities. If you have 50g or more, ask for a premium. Also, consider the buyer's reputation a buyer who pays slightly less but always pays on time is better than one who offers more but delays payment.

Fifth, record-keeping. Keep a log of every sale: date, buyer, weight, purity, price, receipt number. This helps you track your income and prove your earnings if you need a loan. It also helps you spot if a buyer is consistently underpaying you. Review your records monthly. If you see a pattern of low prices, find a new buyer.

Finally, build relationships. A good buyer can become a long-term partner. They may offer advance payments, equipment loans, or help with permits. Treat them professionally and honestly. Never mix gold from different sources without telling the buyer that can ruin trust.

6. Practical Exercise

Now it's time to practice what you've learned. This exercise will walk you through a simulated gold sale. You'll need: a digital scale, a gold purity test kit (or a friend who has one), a notebook, and a phone or computer to check gold prices. If you don't have real gold, use a small piece of brass or copper as a stand-in the steps are the same.

Step 1: Weigh your sample. Record the weight in grams. Let's say it's 5.00 grams. Step 2: Test purity. Rub the sample on the touchstone. Apply a drop of 18k acid. If the mark remains, test with 22k acid. Suppose it remains with 22k but disappears with 24k your sample is approximately 22k (91.7% pure). Step 3: Calculate pure gold content. 5.00g x 0.917 = 4.585g pure gold. Step 4: Check the global price. Go to goldprice.org and find the price per gram of pure gold. Suppose it's $60/g. Your gold's value is 4.585 x $60 = $275.10. Step 5: Calculate a fair offer range. Subtract 5-15% for refining and dealer margin. Fair offer: $233.84 to $261.35. Write this down.

Step 6: Find a licensed buyer. Use your phone to search for 'licensed gold buyer [your country]' or call your local minerals commission. Write down two names and phone numbers. Step 7: Role-play the negotiation. Ask a friend to act as the buyer. Have them offer you $200. Practice countering: 'I have tested this at 22k, 5 grams. The global price is $60/g, so pure gold value is $275. A fair offer is $250. Can you do $250?' See if you can get them to $240. Step 8: Create a receipt. Write a sample receipt with all required details: date, buyer name, licence number, weight, purity, price per gram, total amount, and both signatures. Step 9: Record the sale in your notebook. Step 10: Deposit the money (pretend) into a mobile money account.

After the exercise, answer these questions in your notebook: What was the difference between the first offer and your final negotiated price? How did knowing the global price help you? What would you do differently next time? Repeat this exercise with different weights and purities until you feel confident. The more you practice, the better you'll get at selling your gold for a fair price.

<h2>2. Pricing: How Gold Is Valued at the Point of Sale</h2>
<p>Gold price at the point of sale is calculated from four variables: the international spot price, the assayed purity, the weight, and the buyers discount for risk and processing. The formula is:</p>
<p><strong>Sale value = (Weight in grams Purity as decimal) Spot price per gram (1 Buyer discount)</strong></p>
<p>For example, 50 grams of 85% purity gold at a spot price of USD 65 per gram, sold to a buyer with a 7% discount, yields: 50 0.85 65 0.93 = USD 2,570. The same gold sold to an informal buyer at a 25% discount yields USD 2,070 a difference of USD 500.</p>

<h2>3. Compliance: What Every Seller Must Have</h2>
<p>Compliance is not a bureaucratic add-on; it is the price of access to formal buyers. The minimum compliance package for an artisanal gold seller in most African jurisdictions includes:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Mining licence or artisanal mining permit</strong> issued by the national minerals authority (e.g., Ghana Minerals Commission, Tanzania Mining Commission, Zimbabwe Mining Affairs Board).</li>
<li><strong>Environmental permit or reclamation plan</strong> where required by national law.</li>
<li><strong>Assay certificate</strong> from an accredited laboratory or licensed assay office.</li>
<li><strong>KYC / AML records</strong> identity documents, proof of address, and beneficial ownership declaration.</li>
<li><strong>Tax receipts</strong> royalty, export levy or income tax as applicable.</li>
<li><strong>Traceability records</strong> mine site, production date, weight, purity, and chain of custody.</li>
</ul>
<p>Under the OECD Due Diligence Guidance and the LBMA Responsible Gold Guidance, buyers are required to conduct supply chain due diligence. If the seller cannot provide documentation, the buyer cannot legally purchase the gold without breaching their own compliance obligations. This is why informal buyers exist: they absorb the compliance risk, and they price that risk into a lower offer.</p>

<p style="margin:0 0 6px 0; font-weight:bold; color:#a33;"> SAFETY AND LEGAL WARNING</p>
<p style="margin:0; color:#2b2b2b;">Selling gold without a valid licence, falsifying assay documents, or trading with unlicensed buyers is a criminal offence in most African jurisdictions and can result in arrest, seizure of gold, heavy fines, and permanent disqualification from formal markets. It also exposes you to mercury and cyanide poisoning if processing is done informally, and to armed robbery and extortion at unregulated sale points. Never carry large quantities of gold or cash alone, never meet unknown buyers in isolated locations, and never sign blank documents or accept verbal-only agreements. Always sell through licensed buyers with written contracts, receipts and bank or mobile-money payment trails.</p>
</div>

<h2>4. Documentation and Record-Keeping</h2>
<p>Every sale should generate a paper trail. At minimum, keep a sale register with the following fields: date, buyer name and licence number, weight, purity, assay reference, price per gram, total value, payment method, receipt number, and tax paid. This register is your evidence of legal trading and your negotiating tool with future buyers and financiers.</p>
<p>For exporters, additional documents are required: export permit, certificate of origin, customs declaration, and in some cases a Kimberley Process-style certificate for gold. In Ghana, for example, all gold exports must go through the Precious Minerals Marketing Company (PMMC). In Tanzania, the Bank of Tanzania oversees gold export and buying. In Zimbabwe, the Fidelity Printers and Refiners (FPR) is the sole authorised gold buyer. Knowing your national channel is essential.</p>

<h2>Quick Reference Checklist</h2>
<ul>
<li> Confirm your mining licence or artisanal permit is valid and current.</li>
<li> Get an independent assay certificate before negotiating price.</li>
<li> Weigh gold on a calibrated scale and record the weight in grams.</li>
<li> Verify the buyers licence number with the national minerals authority.</li>
<li> Insist on a written contract stating price, purity, weight and payment terms.</li>
<li> Use bank transfer or mobile money avoid unrecorded cash deals.</li>
<li> Collect and file the receipt, tax payment proof and assay document.</li>
<li> Update your sale register after every transaction.</li>
</ul>

Documentation is not a cost it is the asset that unlocks the formal market, better prices and long-term business growth.</p>
