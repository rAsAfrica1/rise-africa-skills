1 Hazards in block making

Block yards have many hazards. Know them to control them.

  • Dust from cement, sand, aggregate
  • Noise from mixers, vibrating machines
  • Vibration from hand tools
  • Heavy loads — blocks, moulds, pallets
  • Moving machinery — belts, gears, mixers
  • Cement burns and skin irritation
  • Electricity — motors, cables, water
  • Slips, trips, falls

2 Personal protective equipment (PPE)

PPE is the last line of defence. Use it always.

  • Dust mask (N95 or better) — for silica dust
  • Safety glasses — mixing, chipping, maintenance
  • Gloves — cement and rough blocks
  • Ear defenders — near mixers and vibration
  • Safety boots — steel toe, non-slip
  • Hard hat — when working under loads
  • High-vis vest — in vehicle areas

Replace damaged PPE immediately.

3 Machine guarding and lockout/tagout

Never reach into a running machine.

  • Guards must be fitted on belts, gears, mixers
  • Emergency stops must be visible and working
  • LOTO: Lock Out, Tag Out before any maintenance or jam clearing
  • Only trained, authorised staff do LOTO

LOTO saves fingers and lives.

4 Dust and silica safety

Silica dust causes lung disease. Control it.

  • Use water suppression on mixers and conveyors
  • Use local exhaust ventilation where possible
  • Wear dust masks correctly
  • Never dry sweep — use wet cleaning or vacuum
  • Keep dust off surfaces and clothes

5 Manual handling and lifting

Blocks and moulds are heavy. Lift safely.

  • Use trolleys, pallet jacks, hoists
  • Team lift for heavy or awkward loads
  • Bend knees, keep back straight
  • Hold load close to body
  • Never twist while lifting
  • Two workers for moulds over 80 kg

6 Chemical safety

Cement is alkaline and can burn.

  • Wear gloves and safety glasses
  • Wash skin immediately if cement contacts
  • Use eye wash station if in eyes
  • Read Safety Data Sheets (SDS) for admixtures
  • Store chemicals in labelled containers

7 Emergency procedures and first aid

Be ready for accidents.

  • First aid kit stocked and accessible
  • Eye wash station near mixing area
  • Fire extinguishers serviced
  • Emergency contacts posted
  • Incident reporting book
  • Know evacuation route

8 Daily safety culture and toolbox talks

Safety is every day, not once a year.

  • 5-minute toolbox talk each morning
  • Report near misses — they prevent real accidents
  • Keep walkways clear
  • Supervisors lead by example
  • Reward safe behaviour
