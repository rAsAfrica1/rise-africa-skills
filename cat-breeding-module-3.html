3. Housing and Cattery Setup

A cattery must protect health, welfare, and hygiene. Get it right and disease drops dramatically.

Module 3 of 12 · Cat Breeding for Profit

Why catteries matter

Cats are territorial and stress-sensitive. Poor housing causes stress, which leads to illness, poor reproduction, and behavioural problems. A well-designed cattery keeps cats healthy, confident, and productive.

Cattery design principles

  1. Temperature control - 18-22 C ideal. Cats cope with cold better than heat.
  2. Ventilation - airflow without drafts. Ammonia from urine builds up fast.
  3. Natural light - windows or skylights. Cats need light for wellbeing.
  4. Easy-clean surfaces - tile, vinyl, or sealed concrete floors.
  5. Vertical space - shelves, cat trees, platforms at multiple heights.
  6. Hiding places - every cat needs somewhere to retreat.
  7. Separate litter areas - away from food and water.
  8. Isolation unit - separate room for sick or new cats.
  9. Queening room - quiet, warm (22-24 C), away from other cats.
  10. Kitten play area - safe, escape-proof, easy to clean.

Litter box management

  • Rule: one litter box per cat + one spare. 3 cats = 4 boxes.
  • Size: at least 1.5x the length of the adult cat.
  • Location: quiet, away from food and water.
  • Scoop daily. Full change and disinfect weekly.
  • Litter type: unscented clumping clay or pellets. Avoid perfumed litters.
  • Never punish a cat for toileting outside the box - it is a sign of stress or illness.

Quarantine and isolation

Any new cat or returning cat must be isolated for 3-4 weeks:

  • Separate room 10+ m from the main cattery
  • Separate bowls, litter tray, bedding, tools
  • Handle last, after all other cats
  • Test for FeLV/FIV before introduction
  • Vaccinate and deworm during quarantine
  • Observe daily for signs of illness

Climate control

  • Temperature: 18-22 C for adults. 22-26 C for queens with kittens.
  • Humidity: 40-60% - prevents respiratory and skin issues.
  • Airflow: gentle, no drafts. Use vents with baffles.
  • Heating for kittens: newborn kittens need 28-32 C.
  • Cooling: essential in hot climates. Fans, shaded windows, or air conditioning.

Bored cats become stressed cats. Stressed cats get sick and reject kittens.

Noise and stress reduction

  • Locate the cattery away from loud areas.
  • Use soft lighting - no harsh fluorescent lights.
  • Play calm music or leave a radio at low volume.
  • Keep regular routines - same feeding, cleaning, and interaction times.
  • Introduce new cats slowly.
  • Use Feliway diffusers or sprays (synthetic calming pheromones).

The cattery rule: A stressed cattery is a sick cattery. Design for calm, provide vertical space and hiding places, and keep routines consistent. Prevention is 90% of cat health.
