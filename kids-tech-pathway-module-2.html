MODULE 2 · AGES 6–8

Spot the AI 🕵️

This is exactly where school starts in China: finding the thinking machines already hiding in your house, your phone and your street. Four games. Play them in any order.

For the grown-up, before you start. Sit next to the child, not across from them. Let them press the buttons. When they get something wrong, do not say the answer — ask "what made you choose that?" The wrong answers are where the learning is. Twenty minutes is plenty; stop while they still want more.

Now go outside — this is the real homework. Walk to the shops together. Count every thinking machine you can find on the way: the phone that knows a face, the keyboard that guesses the next word, the money app that spots a strange payment, the music that picks the next song. Draw a map of your street and mark each one. That drawing is the thing to photograph and keep.

📋 Print this if you have no screen at home

Every game here works on paper:

  • Robot Commander: chalk a 5×5 grid on the ground. One person is the robot and must obey exactly — including walking into the wall.
  • Patterns: bottle tops, stones and leaves in a row. Ask what comes next.
  • Teach the machine: two baskets and a pile of objects. The child invents the rule, then tests whether you can guess it from their examples alone.
  • Robot brain: point at things in the house. Switch, or decision?

A child who does all four on the ground has learned the same thing as a child who did them on a tablet. That is the whole point of this course.

What they actually learned today. Game 1 is classification — the thing most AI does all day. Game 2 is algorithms and debugging. Game 3 is pattern recognition, which is what a model is doing when it "predicts". Game 4 is training data, and why the examples you choose decide what the machine believes. Those four ideas are the whole foundation. Everything after this is just bigger versions of them.
