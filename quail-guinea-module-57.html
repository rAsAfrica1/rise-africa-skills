Restaurants, Hotels, and Catering Supply

57 Restaurants, hotels, and catering supply

One restaurant contract can equal 50 retail customers. This is where reliable volume lives.

Types of buyers and what they want:

  • Fine dining restaurants - fresh quail, premium presentation, weekly standing order
  • Hotels - consistent supply, specific sizes, invoiced monthly
  • Wedding and event caterers - bulk orders, occasional but large
  • Street food vendors - daily eggs, low price, high volume
  • Canteens and cafeterias - company lunches, school canteens
  • Boutique butchers - processed products, sausages, smoked meats

How to land a restaurant contract:

  1. Research their menu. Do they serve quail? Eggs at breakfast?
  2. Prepare a sample box. Deliver free samples.
  3. Approach the chef, not the manager.
  4. Offer a 4-week trial at slightly reduced price.
  5. Deliver on a fixed schedule. Never late. Never short.
  6. Invoice monthly (Net-30). Build cash reserve.
  7. Get feedback monthly.

Pricing for restaurants: charge 20-30% less than retail (they buy in volume), BUT guaranteed weekly orders mean stable income. Include a delivery fee or minimum order. Never allow credit past 30 days.

Quality expectations: consistent size and grade, clean, cold chain maintained, accurate count.

5 hotel/restaurant accounts with weekly deliveries = steady monthly income. One contract pays for a freezer, incubator, or month of feed.
