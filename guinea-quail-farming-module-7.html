7. Incubation and Hatching

Buying keets is expensive. Hatch your own and cut cost by 70%.

Module 7 of 12 · Guinea Fowl and Quail Farming

Why hatch your own

Day-old keets cost $3-5 each. Day-old quail cost $1-2 each. When you hatch your own, cost drops to under $1 per keet and $0.30 per quail chick. On 500 birds per year, that is $1,000-2,000 saved.

Egg selection for hatching

  • Fresh: 1-7 days old. After 10 days, hatch rate drops sharply.
  • Clean: No cracks, no dirt, no washed eggs.
  • Correct shape: Normal oval. Reject misshapen or double-yolk eggs.
  • Size: Medium, uniform. Too small or too large reduces hatch rate.
  • Storage: 15-18 C, 75% humidity, pointed end down, turned daily.

Natural incubation

A broody hen (any breed) can hatch guinea fowl eggs, but the timing is longer than chickens - 26-28 days vs 21. Many hens abandon the nest before day 26. Best options:

  • Broody chicken hen: Works if she is committed. Mark eggs and check daily.
  • Broody guinea hen: Best. She knows the timing. But she hides nests.
  • Turkey hen: Excellent mother, patient, hatches guinea eggs well.
  • Muscovy duck: Patient, hatches 25-30 eggs at once.

Incubator incubation - step by step

SpeciesIncubation daysTemperatureHumidity
Guinea fowl26-2837.5 C50-55% (days 1-24)
65-70% (days 25-28)
Quail (Japanese)17-1837.5 C55-60% (days 1-14)
70% (days 15-18)
  1. Clean and disinfect the incubator.
  2. Run it empty for 24 hours to stabilise temperature.
  3. Place eggs in trays, pointed end down or on their side.
  4. Days 1-18 (quail) or 1-24 (guinea): turn eggs 4-6 times daily. Some incubators turn automatically.
  5. Days 19-25 stop turning (guinea) or days 15-17 (quail) - the chick is positioning for hatch.
  6. Increase humidity in the final 3 days.
  7. Do not open the incubator during lockdown - you lose humidity.
  8. After hatch, leave chicks in the incubator until dry (6-12 hours).
  9. Move to brooder.

Candling

Candling = shining a light through the egg to see if it is developing.

DayWhat you seeMeaning
Day 5-7Red spider-like veins, dark spotFertile, growing
Day 5-7Clear or slightly cloudyInfertile - remove
Day 10-14Large dark mass, veinsHealthy embryo
Day 14-18Fully dark, no light throughChick nearly ready
Day 18+Air cell visible at blunt endChick ready to pip

Remove infertile eggs early - they rot and reduce humidity.

The incubator rule: Buy a good incubator once. A cheap incubator that loses temperature will cost you more in failed hatches than a good one costs to buy.
