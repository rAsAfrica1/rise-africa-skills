Hollow Concrete Block (2-3 holes)

8 lessons · 30 quiz questions · Easy difficulty

1 Hollow block vs solid block

The hollow block is the workhorse of African construction. It has 2–3 vertical voids (holes) that make it:

  • Lighter — a hollow 200 mm block weighs 15–18 kg vs 25 kg solid. Easier to lift, faster to lay.
  • Cheaper — uses 30–40% less concrete per block, so less cement per wall.
  • Better insulated — the air pockets slow heat transfer, so houses are cooler in summer.
  • Reinforceable — you can pour concrete and steel into the holes for structural strength.

In the UK, hollow blocks are less common than solid. In Africa, the opposite — hollow blocks (4.5", 6", 9" sizes) are the standard.

⚠ A hollow block is NOT weaker than a solid block. Tested correctly, it meets the same strength standards.

2 Mould design for hollow cores

The mould is the machine's tool that shapes the block. A hollow block mould has extra parts inside the main cavity:

  • Core formers — vertical posts that create the holes
  • Top plate — presses the block's top face
  • Base plate — pallet the block sits on
  • Side walls — determine block's outer shape

Typical hollow block sizes:

  • 4.5-inch (115 mm) — internal walls, small houses
  • 6-inch (150 mm) — common walls
  • 9-inch (225 mm) — external walls, load-bearing

Core formers must taper slightly (2–3 degrees) so they release cleanly from the wet block. Straight cores trap the concrete and rip it out.

3 Core-forming tooling

Three options for making hollow cores in a small operation:

Fixed core formers: Welded to the mould. Cannot be changed. Best for high-volume single-product production. £100–£300 per mould.

Removable core formers: Bolt in and out. Change from 2-hole to 3-hole by swapping formers. £200–£600 per set.

Sliding core formers: Slice down through the mould and retract before demoulding. Used in automated machines. Complex and expensive.

For small hand-mould work: use plastic pipe sections as core formers. Cheap, easy to replace. For machine work: invest in proper steel formers.

4 Concrete mix for hollow blocks

The mix for hollow blocks is slightly different from dense blocks. Why? The thin walls (30–40 mm thick) need to flow into tight spaces without honeycomb.

Standard hollow block mix by weight:

  • Cement: 1 part
  • Sharp sand (0–4 mm): 3.5 parts
  • Crushed stone (4–6 mm, never bigger than 10 mm): 4.5 parts
  • Water: for earth-damp consistency

Notice: more sand, less stone than the dense block mix. The higher sand content makes the mix more workable and helps it flow around the core formers.

Cement quantity: about 10% of total weight. A 50 kg bag makes 22–26 hollow blocks of 440 × 215 × 150 mm.

💡 Test with a squeeze — the mix should hold shape and leave your palm just damp, not wet.

5 Filling the mould evenly

Hollow blocks fail more often than solid ones because of filling problems. Get these steps right:

  1. Fill in two layers, not one.
  2. Compact the first layer around the core formers. Use a rod or tamper to poke the mix into the corners.
  3. Fill the second layer.
  4. Vibrate longer than for solid blocks — 6–10 seconds is typical.
  5. Level the top with a straight edge.

Common fault: honeycomb in the corners (mix didn't reach). Cause: mix too dry, or not vibrated enough, or stone too large. Fix: add water, vibrate longer, check stone size.

Second common fault: cores not straight. Cause: core formers bent or worn. Fix: replace formers.

6 Removing the core cleanly

Demoulding a hollow block is the hardest step. The cores must pull out without dragging the walls.

  1. Lift the mould body straight up first. Do not tilt.
  2. The cores should slide up through the block. If they resist, the mix is too wet or the cores are tapered wrong.
  3. If you're using a fixed-core mould: the entire mould lifts up, and the cores lift with it. Same technique — straight up.
  4. After the cores clear, the block stays on its pallet. Do not touch it for 24 hours.

If the block slumps or cracks: mix too wet. Add sand to the next batch.

7 Curing hollow blocks

Curing is identical to dense blocks: 7 days damp, 28 days to full strength. Keep them damp and shaded.

Special handling for hollow blocks:

  • Because they're lighter, they stack higher. Standard: 8–10 layers on a pallet.
  • Keep the top layer covered with plastic or damp cloth — they dry from the top faster than solid blocks.
  • Stack them so the holes line up vertically (natural airflow) but with plastic on top to prevent drying out.

Test: the same drop test as Module 1. A good hollow block chips at the edges instead of shattering.

8 When to use hollow blocks

Hollow blocks are the right choice for:

  • External walls of houses and shops — 6" or 9"
  • Internal partitions — 4.5"
  • Farm buildings — barns, stores, animal housing
  • Boundary walls and security walls
  • Any wall that will be reinforced — the cores take steel and grout

Solid blocks are better for:

  • Below-ground foundations — where core voids would let water in
  • Severe-load structures — engineer-specified
  • Areas requiring "mass" — e.g. some drainage works

In Africa, 90% of the market is hollow blocks. That's where you focus production.
