Data Is Food - Why Bad Data Makes Bad AI

~12 min read20 min gameBeginner
No device needed

🧠 Brain Opener

If you cook with bad maize, the sadza tastes bad. If you build AI with bad data, the AI makes bad decisions. Same rule. Different kitchen.

  • What is data?
  • What makes data good or bad?
  • What happens if you never check your data?
🎁 Reveal a good answer

Data is facts and examples collected together. Good data is complete, correct, and covers many cases. Bad data is missing things, has mistakes, or only shows one type of example. Bad data makes a machine that is wrong even when it tries hard.

📖 Rufaro and the Porridge Test

Rufaro was eleven. Her grandmother made the best porridge in the village. People came from three roads away to buy it.

One day, Rufaro decided she would make the porridge herself. She watched her grandmother every morning, so she knew the steps.

The first day, she made porridge. It was too watery.

"You used too much water," her grandmother said.

The second day, she used less water. It was too thick.

"You used too little," her grandmother said.

The third day, she measured exactly. It was right. But it tasted strange. Her grandmother tasted it.

"The maize is old," she said. "Old maize makes bad porridge, no matter how good the cook."

Rufaro learned two things that day. First, the recipe matters. Second, the ingredients matter even more. You can follow every step and still make bad porridge if your maize is old.

"The same is true with machines," her grandmother said. "They follow the steps. But if the ingredients are bad, the answer is bad. There is no fixing a bad ingredient by following a good recipe."

💡 The Big Idea

Data is the food of AI

AI eats data. Give it good data, it makes good decisions. Give it bad data, it makes bad decisions - even if the AI itself is well built. Fixing bad data after training is very hard. Getting good data before training is the most important job in AI.

📚 Three Deep Lessons

1

What makes data good

Good data has three qualities. Complete: it covers everyone, not just one group. Correct: the answers are right. Balanced: it shows many kinds of examples, not just the common ones.

If a hospital collects patient data only from the capital city, the AI learns city diseases. When it is used in a village, it makes wrong guesses. Complete and balanced data fixes this.

2

How bad data sneaks in

Bad data sneaks in through shortcuts. A form filled in a hurry. A number typed wrong. A photo taken badly. A group of people never asked. Bad data rarely looks bad - it looks like data. You have to check it on purpose.

This is why real AI teams spend 80 percent of their time cleaning data. Only 20 percent of the time is spent on the actual AI. The cleaning is the work.

3

The cost of bad data

Bad data has real consequences. A bank's AI learned that women in a certain region did not repay loans well - so it stopped lending to them. Years later, they discovered the data had only included women who were already struggling. The AI was right about the data and wrong about the world.

Before you build anything with data, ask: who is missing? Whose story is not here? These questions save you from building something that hurts people.

🛠 Hands-On - Test Your Own Data

You need

  • Your notebook
  • A group of friends or classmates
  1. Write a simple question: favourite food, favourite subject, favourite game.
  2. Ask ten people. Write down their answers.
  3. Ask: who did I not ask? Who is missing from my group?
  4. Ask three more people from a different group (different age, class, area).
  5. Compare the answers. Did they change your idea?
Why it works: Data is always incomplete until you ask who is missing. Finding the missing voices makes the data stronger.

🎮 Game - Data Clean-Up

2 to 8 players - 20 min

One person writes ten facts about a made-up village - five true, five with mistakes. The other players have five minutes to find the mistakes. The one who finds the most wins. Discuss: which mistakes were hardest to find? Why?

🤖 AI-Era Skill

AI will use whatever data you give it. Test it with bad data and see what happens.

Try this prompt: "I want to predict which students will do well in maths. My data only includes boys. What is wrong with this data, and how would the AI be wrong when predicting for girls?"

Check the AI answer. The problem is obvious when you name it: the AI has never seen a girl student, so it will guess randomly for them.

🌍 Real Stories from Africa

Kenya medical data gap

A Kenyan hospital collected patient data mainly from Nairobi. When they built an AI to help diagnose disease, it worked well in the city and badly in rural areas. The rural patients had different patterns of illness. The data had missed them.

Nigerian credit scoring

A Nigerian fintech built an AI to approve small loans. Their data was mostly from men. When they launched, women were approved at half the rate. They had to collect two more years of balanced data to fix the AI.
