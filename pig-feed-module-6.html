6. Costing, Records and Scaling

A pig enterprise without records is a hobby. This module gives you the numbers that tell you whether to expand, adjust, or exit.

Lesson 6.2 - Cost per kg gain

The most important single number in pig production:

Cost per kg gain = total feed cost ÷ total kg gained

ScenarioFeed cost per kg gainCost per pig (82 kg gain)
Commercial feed at $0.45/kg, FCR 3.2$1.44$118
Home mix at $0.35/kg, FCR 3.0$1.05$86
Home mix at $0.28/kg, FCR 2.9$0.81$67

The difference between the worst and best scenario is $51 per pig. On 50 pigs/year, that is $2,550 - often the whole profit of the enterprise.

Lesson 6.4 - The margin calculation

Every month, calculate:

  • Feed conversion ratio - kg feed / kg gain. Target under 3.2.
  • Cost per kg gain - feed cost / kg gain. Target under $1.20.
  • Average daily gain - total gain / days / number of pigs. Target 700-800 g/day for growers.
  • Feed cost as % of revenue - target under 55%.
  • Net profit per pig - revenue - all costs. Target $40+.

Lesson 6.5 - When to scale

Scale up your pig enterprise only when:

  • Your FCR has been under 3.2 for 3 consecutive months.
  • Cost per kg gain is under $1.20.
  • Mortality is under 3% per batch.
  • You have a confirmed buyer for the extra pigs.
  • You have the housing and feed storage for the larger numbers.
  • You have the cash flow to feed the extra pigs to market weight.

Do not scale on hope. Scale on numbers.

Lesson 6.6 - Vertical integration

The most profitable pig farms integrate three operations:

  1. Feed mixing - cuts cost by 30-50%.
  2. Breeding - own sows produce piglets at $10-15 cost instead of $35-50 purchase.
  3. Growing to market - captures the full margin per pig.

Start with feed mixing. It is the cheapest, fastest improvement. Add breeding when you have mastered growing to market.

The integration rule: Feed mixing is the single biggest win for any smallholder pig farm. It costs a scale and a grinder, and pays back inside one batch.
Practical exercise 6.1: Calculate the cost per kg gain from your last batch. Compare to the target. Write it on the wall.
Practical exercise 6.2: Source prices for a hammer mill (or access to grinding services). Calculate how many batches to pay it back.
