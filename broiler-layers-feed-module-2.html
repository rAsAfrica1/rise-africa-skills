🐔 Broiler Chicken Nutrition

Lesson 2.1~11 min

Broiler Genetics and Growth Rate

In this lesson
  • Explain why modern broiler genetics demand tighter nutrition than village birds
  • Relate the three Ross feeding phases to what the bird is building at each stage
  • Calculate feed conversion and translate it into money per bird

A modern broiler is not simply a chicken that grows faster. It is a bird whose appetite, skeleton, gut and heart have been selected over decades to convert feed into breast meat at a rate no traditional bird approaches. That selection has a cost: the margin for nutritional error is far smaller. A village cockerel fed maize scraps will survive to a year. A Ross broiler fed the same will be lame by week three.

The practical evidence sits in the specifications themselves. Aviagen's Ross 2022 phases are set at 0-10 days, 11-24 days and 25 days onward - three distinct diets inside about six weeks, because the bird's requirement changes that fast. Energy climbs across the phases from 2,975 to 3,050 to 3,100 kcal/kg while digestible lysine falls from 1.32% to 1.18% to 1.08%. Calcium falls from 0.95% to 0.75% to 0.65% and available phosphorus from 0.50% to 0.42% to 0.36%. Read those two directions together and the biology becomes obvious: the young bird is building skeleton and lean tissue and needs protein and minerals concentrated relative to energy; the older bird is finishing muscle and depositing fat and needs energy concentrated relative to protein.

The efficiency data explain why the phases must not be stretched. Published broiler work shows that at 10-21 days maintenance consumes about 6% of total daily digestible amino acids and utilisation efficiency is around 71%. By 32-43 days maintenance takes about 22% and efficiency has fallen to about 62%. Every extra day you keep a bird past its target weight, you are buying feed at a worse exchange rate. This is the single most important economic fact in broiler production.

NRC 1994 describes the same animal in cruder terms, with crude protein at 23.0% for 0-3 weeks, 20.0% for 3-6 weeks and 18.0% from 6 weeks, and energy held constant at 3,200 kcal AMEn/kg across all phases. If you cannot source or afford three separate diets, the NRC frame is a workable fallback. What does not work is feeding one diet from day one to sale.

Do the money. Take 500 birds sold at 1.9 kg live weight each, giving 950 kg. If your feed conversion is 1.75, you used 950 x 1.75 = 1,662 kg of feed. At $0.55/kg that is $914, or $1.83 per bird in feed. Now suppose poor phase management pushes conversion to 2.05. Feed used becomes 950 x 2.05 = 1,948 kg, costing $1,071 - an extra $157 on the same 500 birds, or $0.31 per bird. If your margin was $1.20 per bird, you have just given away a quarter of it. Feed conversion is not an abstract metric; it is the difference between a farm that grows and one that stalls.

Heat changes what genetics can deliver. A fast-growing broiler generates a lot of metabolic heat and struggles above about 32C. Feed intake falls, growth slows, and mortality climbs in the last two weeks when the bird is heaviest. The nutritional levers are covered later in this module and in Module 1 Lesson 4 - raising dietary electrolyte balance into the 300-350 mEq/kg band for 32-35C conditions, and concentrating the diet so fewer grams still carry the required nutrients.

A farmer in Kaduna, Nigeria running 800 Ross birds in an open-sided house found his birds reaching only 1.6 kg at day 42 while his neighbour reached 2.0 kg. The difference was not the chicks. It was a single all-purpose 'broiler mash' fed from day one, and afternoon house temperatures over 35C with feed still in the troughs. Splitting into a proper starter and finisher, and shifting the main feeding to early morning and evening, lifted his day-42 weight to 1.85 kg in one cycle. Genetics set the ceiling; management decides how close you get to it.

Lesson 2.2~11 min

Starter Feeds (0-10 days)

In this lesson
  • State the Ross 2022 starter specification for energy, amino acids and minerals
  • Explain why starter calcium must be restricted rather than maximised
  • Cost the starter phase per bird and judge whether cheapening it pays

The first ten days decide the cycle. A chick placed on the right starter builds the gut surface, skeleton and immune tissue it will use for the rest of its life. A chick that stumbles in week one never catches up, and no amount of good finisher feed fixes it. Starter is also the smallest quantity of feed you will buy - typically a quarter of a kilogram per bird - which makes it the worst possible place to save money.

The Ross 2022 starter specification, for 0-10 days, is metabolisable energy 2,975 kcal/kg (12.4 MJ), digestible lysine 1.32%, digestible methionine 0.55%, methionine plus cysteine 1.00%, threonine 0.88%, arginine 1.40%. Valine is estimated at 0.90-0.82% and isoleucine at 0.80-0.73%. Total calcium is 0.95% with available phosphorus 0.50%, a ratio of 1.9:1. Sodium and chloride each sit at 0.18-0.23%. Trace minerals are zinc 120 mg/kg, copper 16, manganese 120, selenium 0.30. Vitamins are A 13,000 IU/kg, D3 5,000 IU/kg, E 80 IU/kg. On the NRC 1994 frame the equivalent is 23.0% crude protein for 0-3 weeks.

The calcium figure surprises people. Starter calcium is deliberately restricted to 0.85-0.95%, not raised. Calcium above 1.0% in this window interferes with phosphorus metabolism and predisposes the bird to leg problems - tibial dyschondroplasia and angular limb deformities. The optimal calcium to available phosphorus ratio for growing poultry is about 2:1, and ratios below 1.5:1 or above 2.5:1 increase skeletal problems and nutrient malabsorption. If you mix your own feed and are tempted to add limestone for 'strong bones', this is exactly the wrong phase to do it in.

Phytase earns its place here more than anywhere else. Phytase at 500-1,500 FTU/kg improves calcium and phosphorus digestibility and its effect is most pronounced in young broilers at 2-14 days - precisely the starter window. Roughly, 1,000 FTU/kg is worth about 0.15-0.25 percentage points of available phosphorus, which lets you cut expensive inorganic phosphate.

Physical form matters as much as chemistry in week one. A day-old chick has a small beak and cannot handle coarse mash. Crumb or fine crumble is worth paying for. Feed must be within a metre of every chick for the first three days - spread paper on the litter and scatter feed on it so chicks find it by sight and sound. Chicks that do not find feed and water in the first 24 hours show it in the day-7 weight, and day-7 weight is the single best early predictor of final weight.

Cost it. Take 500 chicks eating about 0.25 kg each over ten days, so 125 kg of starter. A properly specified starter might cost $0.72/kg against a cheap grower-type mash at $0.55/kg. The difference is 125 x $0.17 = $21.25 for the whole batch, or $0.043 per bird. Now compare with the downside: if the cheap starter costs you 4% extra mortality, that is 20 birds at $4.00 sale value, or $80, plus the chick and feed already invested. Even a one-day delay in reaching target weight, across 500 birds eating roughly 0.13 kg each on that day, costs about 65 kg of feed, near $36. The premium starter is cheap insurance.

Mycotoxins hit hardest here too. Chicks of 0-4 weeks are the most sensitive class to aflatoxin, with a regulatory limit of 0.005 ppm - four times stricter than the 0.02 ppm applied to broilers generally. Fumonisin limits are 10 ppm for chicks against 20 ppm for broilers. Never put questionable grain into a starter. Module 10 covers sampling and storage.

A hatchery-linked producer in Mbeya, Tanzania placing 1,200 chicks a month moved from a self-mixed mash to a bought commercial crumb for the first ten days only, then back to his own mix. Day-7 weights rose noticeably and first-week mortality fell. His total extra cost was under $60 a month against a flock worth several thousand dollars at sale.

Lesson 2.3~11 min

Grower Feeds (10-24 days)

In this lesson
  • State the Ross 2022 grower specification and how it differs from starter
  • Manage the feed transition without causing a growth check
  • Calculate the grower phase feed requirement and cost for a given flock

The grower phase, days 11 to 24 on the Ross 2022 schedule, is where the bulk of the frame is laid down and where most of the total feed for the cycle begins to be consumed. It is a less dramatic phase than starter, and for that reason it is where quiet losses accumulate.

The Ross 2022 grower specification is metabolisable energy 3,050 kcal/kg (12.8 MJ), digestible lysine 1.18%, methionine 0.51%, methionine plus cysteine 0.92%, threonine 0.79%, arginine 1.27%. Valine is estimated at 0.82-0.75% and isoleucine at 0.73-0.66%. Total calcium is 0.75% with available phosphorus 0.42%, a ratio of 1.8:1. Sodium and chloride hold at 0.18-0.23%. Vitamins step down from starter: A 11,000 IU/kg, D3 4,500 IU/kg, E 65 IU/kg. Trace minerals stay at zinc 120, copper 16, manganese 120 and selenium 0.30 mg/kg. On the NRC 1994 frame, this window sits inside the 3-6 week band at 20.0% crude protein.

Compare grower with starter and the direction is clear. Energy rises 75 kcal/kg. Lysine falls 0.14 percentage points. Calcium falls 0.20 points and available phosphorus 0.08. The bird is eating more, so the same daily nutrient intake is delivered by a less concentrated feed.

The transition itself is the risk. Birds recognise feed by particle size, colour and smell, and an abrupt change can cause a day or two of reduced intake at precisely the moment appetite should be climbing. Change over three days: on day one give two parts old feed to one part new, on day two half and half, on day three one part old to two parts new, then move fully across. Mix the two properly rather than layering them in the trough. Watch feed disappearance across the change - if it drops, slow down.

Work out what you need. For 500 birds, published intake in this window is roughly 1.0-1.2 kg per bird across fourteen days, so budget about 550 kg of grower for the flock. At $0.58/kg that is $319, or $0.64 per bird. If you can lift the digestible lysine from 1.05% to the Ross figure of 1.18% by adding synthetic L-lysine HCl, the arithmetic is: you need 0.13 percentage points more lysine; L-lysine HCl is about 78% lysine, so you need 0.13 / 0.78 = 0.167% of the mix. On 550 kg that is 0.92 kg. At $2.20/kg that is $2.02 for the whole phase - about $0.004 per bird. This is the cheapest performance improvement available to a small producer, and it is routinely ignored.

Enzymes deserve a place in the grower diet if your grain base is not maize. Non-starch polysaccharidase complexes improve energy by 96-265 kcal/kg depending on ingredient type and give 3-7% feed efficiency improvement in broilers, with typical inclusion at 500-1,500 units/kg. The response is largest in wheat-based diets, because of arabinoxylans, and in barley or oats because of beta-glucans, and modest in maize-based diets. If you are feeding a maize and soybean mix, do not expect the same return.

Heat becomes a factor as the birds grow. From about day 18 the flock generates real metabolic heat and intake falls in the afternoon. Feed early in the morning and again in the evening, keep feed fresh in troughs rather than sitting warm all day, and check dietary electrolyte balance against the temperature band - 250-300 mEq/kg at 28-32C, 300-350 at 32-35C.

A producer near Kampala, Uganda ran 1,000 birds and had good day-10 weights but disappointing day-24 weights. Her grower was a bought mash with an 18% crude protein label - below the NRC 20% for this window - and she was changing feed in a single day. Moving to a 20% grower and phasing the change over three days added roughly 90 g of live weight per bird by day 24, worth around $0.18 per bird at sale, against a feed cost increase of about $0.05 per bird.

Lesson 2.4~11 min

Finisher Feeds (24-42 days)

In this lesson
  • State the Ross 2022 finisher specification and its economic logic
  • Decide when to sell using the falling efficiency of older birds
  • Calculate finisher feed cost per kilogram of live weight gained

The finisher phase, day 25 onward on the Ross 2022 schedule, consumes the largest share of the feed you will buy and returns the least per kilogram. Understanding why is the difference between a profitable cycle and a break-even one.

The Ross 2022 finisher specification is metabolisable energy 3,100 kcal/kg (13.0 MJ), digestible lysine 1.08%, methionine 0.48%, methionine plus cysteine 0.86%, threonine 0.72%, arginine 1.17%. Valine is estimated at 0.75-0.68% and isoleucine at 0.66-0.59%. Total calcium is 0.65% with available phosphorus 0.36%, again about 1.8:1. Sodium and chloride hold at 0.18-0.23%. Vitamins step down again: A 10,000 IU/kg, D3 4,000 IU/kg, E 55 IU/kg. Trace minerals are unchanged. The NRC 1994 equivalent is 18.0% crude protein from six weeks.

This is the highest-energy, lowest-protein diet of the cycle, and that is deliberate. The bird is finishing muscle and laying down fat, and energy is what it needs. But it is also the diet fed to the least efficient animal you will ever own. Published broiler data show maintenance takes about 22% of total daily amino acid intake at 32-43 days against 6% at 10-21 days, with utilisation efficiency down from about 71% to 62%. Almost a quarter of what you feed at this age is simply keeping the bird alive.

That fact drives the most important commercial decision in broiler production: when to sell. Work it through. A bird at day 38 weighing 1.85 kg gaining 60 g a day and eating 155 g a day is converting at 155 / 60 = 2.58 on that day's margin. Five more days adds 300 g of live weight but costs 775 g of feed. At $0.56/kg that feed is $0.43, and the extra 300 g at a live price of $2.10/kg is worth $0.63. You made $0.20 - real but thin, and it disappears entirely if mortality in those five days is above about 1%, or if heat suppresses intake. Compare that with the same feed spent on the first ten days of a new flock and the case for selling on time is strong. Know your marginal conversion, not just your whole-cycle average.

Coccidiostat withdrawal belongs to this phase. Where an in-feed coccidiostat has been used, the published withdrawal is 7 days before slaughter for monensin, lasalocid, salinomycin, nicarbazin and amprolium alike. That means your last feed change is a planning decision, not an afterthought - you must have a withdrawal feed on the farm before day 35 if you intend to sell at day 42. Lesson 5 covers this in more detail, including the fact that permitted products differ between countries.

Heat management peaks here because the birds are heaviest and generate the most metabolic heat. The dietary electrolyte balance table applies directly: 300-350 mEq/kg for 32-35C conditions and 350-400 above 35C, achieved by raising sodium, ideally using sodium bicarbonate, and reducing chloride. Feed intake falls under heat, so a more concentrated finisher partly compensates. Practical measures cost nothing: feed at dawn and dusk, keep water cool and abundant, and avoid handling birds in the afternoon.

Cost the phase honestly. For 500 birds, budget roughly 2.2-2.5 kg per bird across days 25-42, so about 1,175 kg. At $0.56/kg that is $658 - more than starter and grower combined. This is why a 0.05 improvement in feed conversion during finisher is worth more than any change you can make earlier.

A producer in Chinhoyi, Zimbabwe habitually held birds to day 49 believing bigger birds meant more money. Weighing and costing the last week showed his marginal conversion had reached 3.1 while his live price was flat. Selling at day 42 and starting the next batch a week earlier gained him roughly one extra cycle a year on the same house.

Lesson 2.5~12 min

Feed Additives for Broilers

In this lesson
  • Describe how the main coccidiostat classes kill Eimeria parasites
  • Apply the 7-day withdrawal rule and check national legality before use
  • Compare enzymes, organic acids and probiotics on published response ranges

Additives are the part of the feed budget where producers spend most and understand least. Judge every one of them by the same test: what does the published evidence say it does, what does it cost per tonne, and is it legal where you farm.

Start with coccidiostats, because coccidiosis is the single biggest parasitic drain on African broiler flocks. Two families exist. The ionophores - monensin, lasalocid and salinomycin - share a mechanism. They are polyether compounds that transport sodium, potassium and hydrogen ions across the membranes of Eimeria parasites, destroying the osmotic balance so the parasite cell vacuolises and dies. Because they act in the initial phase of the parasite life cycle, they allow the bird to develop some natural immunity while still being protected, which is a real advantage in repeat-use houses.

The synthetics work differently. Nicarbazin is not an ionophore; it interferes with one or more stages of the Eimeria life cycle, destroying parasites during their intracellular development inside the gut epithelial cells. Its acceptable daily intake is 0.2 mg/kg body weight per day, among the highest of the coccidiostats. Amprolium, in use since the early 1960s, is a thiamine antagonist: it blocks the thiamine-dependent metabolic pathways the parasite depends on rather than lysing the cell directly.

The withdrawal rule is the same for all of them. Coccidiostats may be given from day one of the bird's life up to 7 days before slaughter, and 7 days is the published minimum withdrawal for broilers. Maximum residue limits are established for every authorised compound, with published acceptable daily intakes including halofuginone 0.00003, lasalocid 0.02, monensin 0.03 and nicarbazin 0.2 mg/kg body weight per day.

Now the part that matters most for you. Which coccidiostats are legal, at what inclusion, and with what withdrawal, varies by country. The EU authorises eleven coccidiostats as feed additives under Regulation EC 1831/2003 with strict monitoring; the United States operates a separate FDA-approved list that does not match it. Your own country has its own regulator and its own permitted list, and some products sold informally in regional markets are not authorised anywhere. Before you buy or include any coccidiostat, check with your national veterinary or feed regulator, or a registered vet, which products are permitted for the class of bird you keep and what withdrawal period applies locally. Never assume a product is legal because it is on a shop shelf, and never shorten a withdrawal because a buyer is waiting.

Enzymes are lower-risk and often better value. Phytase at 500-1,500 FTU/kg degrades phytic acid and releases bound phosphorus and minerals, improving tibia ash, calcium and phosphorus, with the strongest effect at 2-14 days; about 1,000 FTU/kg is worth roughly 0.15-0.25 points of available phosphorus. Commercial products are typically 10,000 FTU/g. NSP-ase complexes, containing xylanase at 12,500 VU/g or more, beta-glucanase at 8,600 VU/g or more and arabinofuranosidase, add 96-265 kcal/kg of energy and 3-7% feed efficiency, with the biggest wins on wheat, barley and oats. Protease at 500-1,500 units/kg improves villus height and the villus height to crypt depth ratio and raises crude protein and amino acid digestibility, working synergistically with phytase in reduced-density diets.

Organic acids lower gut pH, suppress pathogens, improve mineral availability and strengthen the intestinal barrier. Published ranges are 2-8% feed conversion improvement, 1-3% better feed intake and 3-5% better weight gain, with the largest gains where sanitation or health status is poor - which describes many small African units. Typical inclusions are formic 0.5-1.0%, propionic 0.5-1.0%, butyric 0.3-0.8% and citric 0.5-1.5%.

Probiotics and phytogenics such as oregano, garlic and thyme match antibiotic growth promoter controls on weight gain and feed efficiency over 0-42 days without raising E. coli counts, and garlic and thyme significantly reduced ileal crypt depth. The trade-off is cost: feed cost per kilogram of gain rises 0.49-1.80% against antibiotics.

A broiler unit in Eldoret, Kenya added a phytase and an NSP enzyme to a wheat-offal-based mix and reduced dicalcium phosphate inclusion accordingly. The enzyme cost about $3 per tonne while the phosphate saving was larger, and feed conversion improved. The decision was made on published response ranges, not a salesman's claim - which is the only way to buy an additive.

Lesson 2.6~11 min

Flock Management and Feeding Strategy

In this lesson
  • Design a feeding routine that protects intake in a hot open-sided house
  • Calculate whole-cycle feed budget and feed conversion for your flock
  • Identify the three management faults that most often waste bought feed

You can buy a perfectly formulated feed and still lose money on it. Feed only becomes weight if the bird eats it, and whether the bird eats it is a management question. This lesson is about the gap between the feed you buy and the feed the bird actually converts.

Start with the feed budget, because most small producers do not have one. For 500 broilers to day 42, budget roughly 0.25 kg per bird of starter, 1.0-1.2 kg of grower and 2.2-2.5 kg of finisher, so approximately 125 kg, 550 kg and 1,175 kg - about 1,850 kg in total, or 3.7 kg per bird. Priced at $0.72, $0.58 and $0.56 per kilogram respectively, that is $90 + $319 + $658 = $1,067, or $2.13 per bird. If the birds sell at 1.9 kg live and $2.10/kg, revenue is $3.99 per bird. Take off the chick at about $0.80 and you have $1.06 per bird before labour, litter, medication and electricity. That is the real shape of the business, and it explains why a 5% feed waste rate - $53 on this flock - matters so much.

Feed waste is the first management fault. Troughs filled to the brim are shovelled out by birds; fill to about a third. Troughs set too low let birds stand in them; the lip should sit at roughly the height of the birds' backs and be raised as they grow. Feed left in troughs all day in a hot house goes stale, oxidises and loses vitamin potency. Spilled feed on litter also feeds rodents, and rodents bring disease.

The second fault is feeder and drinker space. Count both against bird numbers and watch at the busiest hour. If there is a queue, subordinate birds are being excluded and your flock uniformity is falling - and uniformity is what determines how many birds hit sale weight on the same day. A flock with wide weight spread forces you either to sell underweight birds or to feed the whole house an extra week for the sake of the tail.

The third fault is ignoring temperature. Feed intake collapses in the afternoon heat of an African open-sided house. The fix costs nothing: feed the main meals at dawn and again in the late evening when the house has cooled, and leave troughs lighter through the middle of the day. Combine this with the dietary electrolyte balance adjustments from Module 1 Lesson 4 - 250-300 mEq/kg at 28-32C, 300-350 at 32-35C, 350-400 above 35C - achieved by raising sodium, preferably as sodium bicarbonate, and cutting chloride. Ensure water is abundant and as cool as you can keep it; a bird that cannot drink will not eat.

Measure what you are managing. Weigh 20 randomly caught birds every seven days - catch them where you happen to be standing, not the ones you can reach easily, or you will bias the sample towards slow birds. Record flock weight, feed delivered and mortality with the reason. At the end, calculate feed conversion as total feed used divided by total live weight sold, including the weight of birds that died, and compare against your plan. Without those three records you cannot tell a feed problem from a management problem.

Biosecurity and feed hygiene sit alongside all of this and are covered fully in Module 10; the point here is only that a feeding routine which leaves stale feed, spillage and open bags is also a disease routine.

A cooperative in Ouagadougou, Burkina Faso running batches of 600 birds moved from all-day trough feeding to two main feeds at 5am and 7pm during the hot season, raised trough lips weekly as the birds grew, and started weighing 20 birds each Sunday. Feed conversion improved from 2.0 to 1.83 across two cycles with no change in the feed itself. On 600 birds at 1.9 kg, that is 1,140 kg live weight; the conversion change saved 1,140 x 0.17 = 194 kg of feed, worth about $109 a cycle for no extra cost at all.

Knowledge check

Questions from all lessons. Click an answer to see whether it is right and why.

1. Across Ross 2022 broiler phases, energy and lysine move in which directions?

Energy goes 2,975 to 3,050 to 3,100 kcal/kg while digestible lysine falls 1.32 to 1.18 to 1.08%, reflecting a shift from frame-building to finishing.

2. Amino acid utilisation efficiency in broilers at 32-43 days is about:

Efficiency falls from about 71% at 10-21 days to about 62% at 32-43 days, and maintenance rises from roughly 6% to 22% of daily amino acid intake.

3. 500 birds at 1.9 kg with FCR 1.75 consume how much feed?

950 kg live weight x 1.75 = 1,662 kg of feed, costing $914 at $0.55/kg, or $1.83 per bird.

4. NRC 1994 broiler crude protein for 3-6 weeks is:

NRC 1994 gives 23.0% for 0-3 weeks, 20.0% for 3-6 weeks and 18.0% from 6 weeks, with energy held at 3,200 kcal AMEn/kg throughout.

5. Feeding a single all-purpose broiler mash from day one to sale mainly causes:

One diet cannot match a requirement that changes every ten days. The bird is over-supplied on some nutrients and under-supplied on others throughout the cycle.

6. Ross 2022 starter (0-10 days) digestible lysine is:

Starter digestible lysine is 1.32%, the highest of the three phases, because the young bird is building lean tissue fastest.

7. Broiler starter calcium should be:

Calcium above 1.0% in the first ten days interferes with phosphorus metabolism and increases leg disorders. Restriction, not maximisation, is correct.

8. Phytase gives its strongest response in broilers aged:

The published peak response period is 2-14 days post-hatch, which makes starter the phase where phytase pays best.

9. The aflatoxin limit for chicks aged 0-4 weeks is:

Chicks are the most sensitive class at 0.005 ppm (5 ppb), against 0.02 ppm for broilers, layers and breeders.

10. 500 chicks eating 0.25 kg of starter each, with a $0.17/kg quality premium, costs an extra:

500 x 0.25 kg = 125 kg; 125 x $0.17 = $21.25, or about $0.043 per bird - trivial against the cost of a poor start.

11. Ross 2022 grower (11-24 days) metabolisable energy is:

Grower sits at 3,050 kcal/kg (12.8 MJ), between starter at 2,975 and finisher at 3,100.

12. The correct way to change from starter to grower is:

Birds recognise feed by particle size, colour and smell. A graded three-day blend avoids the intake dip that an abrupt change causes.

13. NSP-degrading enzymes give the largest energy response in diets based on:

Arabinoxylans in wheat and beta-glucans in barley and oats are the substrates. Maize-based diets show only modest improvements.

14. Ross 2022 grower calcium to available phosphorus ratio is:

0.75% calcium with 0.42% available phosphorus gives 1.8:1, inside the safe 1.5-2.5:1 window for growing birds.

15. To raise digestible lysine by 0.13 points using L-lysine HCl at 78% lysine, you add:

0.13 divided by 0.78 = 0.167% of the mix, which on 550 kg is 0.92 kg costing about $2 at $2.20/kg.

16. Ross 2022 finisher digestible lysine is:

Finisher lysine is 1.08% with energy at its highest, 3,100 kcal/kg, matching a bird that is finishing rather than building frame.

17. Why is the finisher phase the least efficient?

Published data show maintenance rising from about 6% to 22% of daily amino acid intake between 10-21 and 32-43 days, with efficiency falling from about 71% to 62%.

18. The published in-feed coccidiostat withdrawal for broilers is:

Seven days minimum before slaughter is the published withdrawal period across the common compounds, and it must be planned for in advance.

19. A bird gaining 60 g/day while eating 155 g/day has a marginal feed conversion of:

155 divided by 60 = 2.58. That marginal figure, not the cycle average, tells you whether another week of feeding is worth it.

20. Under 32-35C house conditions, target dietary electrolyte balance is:

Moderate heat stress at 32-35C calls for 300-350 mEq/kg, raised further to 350-400 above 35C by increasing sodium and cutting chloride.

21. Monensin, lasalocid and salinomycin kill Eimeria by:

All three are polyether ionophores with the same mechanism: ion transport across the parasite membrane leading to vacuolisation and cell death.

22. Amprolium works as a:

Amprolium, in use since the early 1960s, is a thiamine antagonist that disrupts thiamine-dependent Eimeria metabolism rather than lysing cells directly.

23. Before including any coccidiostat in your feed you must:

Permitted products, inclusion rates and withdrawal periods differ by country. The EU authorises eleven compounds under EC 1831/2003 and the US list differs; only your national regulator governs your farm.

24. NSP-degrading enzymes typically add how much energy?

Published gains are 96-265 kcal/kg with 3-7% feed efficiency improvement, largest on wheat, barley and oat-based diets.

25. Compared with antibiotic growth promoters, probiotics and herbal additives:

Published trials show equivalent body weight gain and feed efficiency with no rise in E. coli, at a modest cost penalty of 0.49-1.80% per kilogram of gain.

26. Roughly how much total feed does a 500-bird broiler flock need to day 42?

About 0.25 kg starter, 1.0-1.2 kg grower and 2.2-2.5 kg finisher per bird gives roughly 3.7 kg each, or 1,850 kg for 500 birds.

27. Troughs should be filled to about:

Brim-full troughs are billed out onto the litter. One third full sharply reduces waste, which at 5% of 1,850 kg is around $53 on a single flock.

28. In a hot open-sided house, the main feeds should be given:

Intake collapses in afternoon heat. Feeding when the house is cool captures intake that would otherwise be lost, and costs nothing.

29. When weighing a sample of birds you should:

Catching only easy birds biases the sample towards slow or weak individuals and gives a false picture of flock weight.

30. Poor flock uniformity forces a producer to:

A wide weight spread means birds do not reach target together, so either light birds are sold at a loss or the entire flock is fed longer at the worst conversion of the cycle.

Module 2 capstone

Run one complete broiler cycle as a costed experiment on your own farm. Before placement, write down your target: bird numbers, expected finish weight, target feed conversion and target mortality. Build a three-phase feeding plan on paper with the Ross 2022 energy, lysine, calcium and available phosphorus figures for starter, grower and finisher, and price each phase per kilogram from this week's local ingredient prices. During the cycle, weigh 20 randomly caught birds every seven days and record the flock average, record feed delivered per phase, record daily mortality with the reason, and note the maximum house temperature each afternoon. At sale, calculate your actual feed conversion as total kilograms of feed divided by total kilograms of live weight sold, your feed cost per kilogram of live weight, and your margin per bird in dollars. Then write half a page identifying the single biggest gap between plan and result, and what it cost you. Bring that number into Module 3 - the same discipline applies to layers.
