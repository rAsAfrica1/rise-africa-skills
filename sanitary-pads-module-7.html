1. How to Wear a Pad Correctly

To wear a sanitary pad correctly, start by washing your hands with soap and water. Then, open the pad wrapper and remove the pad. If you are using a pad with wings, peel off the paper from the back that covers the adhesive. If your pad has no wings, just peel the back paper. Stand with your legs slightly apart or sit on the toilet with your knees apart. Remove the old pad if you are changing, and wrap it in paper or a plastic bag for disposal. Take the new pad and press the adhesive side firmly onto the center of your underwear, making sure it covers the vaginal opening. If the pad has wings, peel the paper from the wings and fold them under your underwear to secure the pad in place. The pad should be comfortable and not shift when you move. Adjust the pad so it is smooth and not wrinkled. Wash your hands again after handling the pad. Change your pad every 4 to 6 hours, or more often if your flow is heavy. Wearing a pad correctly helps prevent leaks and keeps you comfortable. In many African settings, girls may use cloth pads or disposable pads; the method is similar. For cloth pads, ensure they are clean and dry before use. Always check that the pad is not expired or damaged. If you have sensitive skin, choose unscented pads. Practice makes perfect, so don't worry if it takes a few tries. Remember, proper placement is key to avoiding stains and embarrassment.

2. Changing Frequency and Hygiene Practices

Changing your sanitary pad regularly is crucial for hygiene and health. During your period, you should change your pad every 4 to 6 hours, even if the flow is light. If your flow is heavy, change every 3 to 4 hours. Leaving a pad on too long can cause bad odor, skin irritation, and even infections like bacterial vaginosis or toxic shock syndrome (though rare). Always wash your hands before and after changing a pad. When changing, remove the used pad, wrap it in toilet paper or a plastic bag, and dispose of it in a bin. Do not flush pads down the toilet as they can clog pipes. After removing the pad, clean your genital area with water and mild soap if possible, or use a wet wipe. Wipe from front to back to avoid spreading bacteria from the anus to the vagina. Pat dry with toilet paper or a clean cloth. Then put on a fresh pad. If you are at school or work, carry spare pads, wipes, and a small bag for disposal. In many African communities, water may be scarce, so use a small amount of water to rinse. If you use reusable cloth pads, change them every 3 to 4 hours, and store used ones in a wet bag until you can wash them. Never share pads with others. Also, avoid using scented pads or sprays as they can irritate. If you notice any unusual discharge, itching, or rash, see a health worker. Good hygiene during your period keeps you healthy and confident.

3. Managing Different Flow Levels

Menstrual flow varies from woman to woman and from day to day. On heavy days, you may need a pad with higher absorbency, such as overnight or maxi pads. On light days, you can use regular or panty liners. In Africa, many girls use disposable pads, but reusable cloth pads are also common. For heavy flow, choose a pad with wings and a longer length to prevent leaks. Change more frequently, every 3 to 4 hours. If you bleed through a pad quickly, consider using two pads overlapping or a pad plus a cloth pad for extra protection. For light flow, you can stretch to 6 hours. At night, use an overnight pad or a longer pad to avoid staining your bedding. If you use cloth pads, you can double them up on heavy days. To know your flow, track your period for a few months. Note how many pads you use per day. Heavy flow means soaking a pad in less than 2 hours. If you have heavy flow for more than 7 days, see a doctor. For moderate flow, change every 4 to 6 hours. Always carry spare pads in your bag. If you are in a rural area with limited access to pads, you can make your own reusable pads from cotton fabric. Ensure they are washed and dried properly. Remember, managing flow is about comfort and preventing leaks. Adjust your pad choice and changing schedule as needed. Don't be embarrassed to ask for help from a trusted adult or health worker.

4. Disposal Methods for Disposable Pads

Disposing of disposable pads properly is important for hygiene and the environment. Never flush a pad down the toilet; it will clog the pipes and cause blockages. Instead, wrap the used pad in the wrapper from the new pad, or in toilet paper, or a piece of newspaper. Then place it in a bin. If you are at home, have a dedicated bin with a lid in the bathroom. Line it with a plastic bag for easy disposal. In public places, look for a sanitary bin. If none, carry a small plastic bag to store the used pad until you find a bin. In many African cities, waste collection may be irregular, so double-wrap the pad to reduce odor and prevent pests. Do not throw pads in the open or in pit latrines, as they do not decompose quickly and can fill up the pit. If you use a pit latrine, consider a separate bin for pads. Some organizations promote incineration of pads, but burning plastic pads releases toxic fumes. Better to dispose in a landfill. If you have a incinerator designed for sanitary waste, follow instructions. For reusable pads, disposal is not applicable. Teach others in your community about proper disposal. Schools should provide sanitary bins in girls' toilets. If you are a business, you can offer disposal bags with pads. Always wash your hands after handling used pads. Proper disposal prevents the spread of disease and keeps the environment clean.

5. Cleaning and Care for Reusable Pads

Reusable pads are eco-friendly and cost-effective. To clean them, first rinse the pad in cold water immediately after use to remove blood. Do not use hot water as it sets stains. You can soak the pad in cold water with a little salt or vinegar for 30 minutes. Then wash with mild soap and cold water. Gently rub the fabric together to remove stains. Do not use bleach or harsh chemicals as they can damage the fabric and irritate your skin. Rinse thoroughly until water runs clear. Then hang to dry in direct sunlight, which disinfects. If you are in a humid area, you can iron the pad on a low setting to ensure it is completely dry. Never store pads while damp; they can grow mold. Wash reusable pads after each use. Have at least 5 to 10 pads so you can rotate. Store clean pads in a dry, clean place. For stains, you can use lemon juice and salt. If you are travelling, carry a wet bag to store used pads. In Africa, many women make their own pads from cotton flannel or old t-shirts. Ensure the fabric is soft and absorbent. Sew layers together. After washing, check for wear and tear. Replace pads when they become thin or lose absorbency. With proper care, reusable pads can last 1 to 2 years. Always wash your hands before and after handling pads. Proper cleaning prevents infections and keeps pads fresh.

6. Environmental Impact and Sustainable Disposal

Disposable sanitary pads have a significant environmental impact. A single pad takes 500 to 800 years to decompose in a landfill. The average woman uses 10,000 to 15,000 pads in her lifetime, creating a lot of waste. In Africa, improper disposal clogs drains and pollutes water sources. To reduce impact, consider sustainable alternatives like reusable cloth pads, menstrual cups, or biodegradable pads. Reusable pads can be washed and reused for years, reducing waste. Menstrual cups are made of medical-grade silicone and can last up to 10 years. If you use disposables, dispose of them properly in a bin, not in the environment. Some companies make compostable pads from banana fiber or bamboo, but they still need proper disposal. You can also support community programs that collect and incinerate pads safely. In schools, teach girls about sustainable options. For businesses, offer reusable pads as part of your product line. When disposing of disposable pads, avoid burning them as it releases toxic chemicals. Instead, landfill disposal is better. If you have a garden, you can compost biodegradable pads if they are certified compostable. But most pads are not. So the best is to reduce, reuse, and recycle where possible. By choosing reusable pads, you save money and protect the environment. Encourage others to do the same. Small actions can make a big difference.

7. Practical Reference

Practical Reference: Proper Usage and Disposal of Sanitary Pads

For any African agribusiness or social enterprise distributing sanitary pads, the difference between a product that sells once and a product that builds a loyal customer base lies in usage education. A pad that leaks, irritates, or is disposed of badly damages trust, generates complaints, and kills repeat purchase. This Practical Reference module gives trainers, community agents, and retail partners a business-first, field-ready guide to correct wear, hygiene, flow management, disposal, and reusable pad care with the environmental and commercial logic built in.

In markets where pads are sold through kiosks, agro-dealer networks, women's groups, or school programmes, the agent who can demonstrate correct use becomes the trusted supplier. That trust converts to volume. This lesson treats usage education as a sales and retention tool, not a welfare add-on.

Why Usage Knowledge Drives Revenue

Across sub-Saharan Africa, menstrual products are often available but under-used correctly. Research by UNICEF and partners shows that inadequate menstrual hygiene management (MHM) leads to missed school and work days, discomfort, and in some cases reproductive tract infections. For a distributor, each of these outcomes is a lost customer: a girl who leaks at school abandons the brand; a woman who develops irritation stops buying; a community that sees pads littered in latrines resists the product entirely. Teaching correct wear and disposal is therefore a customer lifetime value strategy.

1. How to Wear a Pad Correctly

Demonstrate these steps with a cloth model or a pair of underwear. Training agents should rehearse until the sequence is automatic:

  1. Wash hands with soap and water before handling the pad.
  2. Open the wrapper and remove the pad without touching the absorbent surface.
  3. Peel the backing paper to expose the adhesive strip.
  4. Centre the pad on the gusset of clean, dry underwear, front to back.
  5. Fold the wings around the edges of the underwear to secure the pad.
  6. Press gently so the adhesive grips the fabric.
  7. Dispose of the wrapper in a bin never in a latrine.

Business note: Selling pads with clear pictorial instructions on the pack reduces support calls and returns. If you import or repackage, invest in locally relevant illustrations.

2. Changing Frequency and Hygiene Practices

Disposable pads should be changed every 34 hours, or sooner on heavy flow days. Leaving a pad longer than 46 hours raises the risk of odour, irritation, and infection. Hands must be washed before and after every change. Genital area should be cleaned with water; scented soaps and vaginal deodorants should be avoided.

3. Managing Different Flow Levels

Stocking the right mix of absorbencies is a commercial decision. Light-flow panty liners have lower margins but high volume; heavy-flow night pads command premium pricing. Train retailers to ask customers about flow and recommend accordingly this increases basket size and satisfaction.

4. Disposal Methods for Disposable Pads

Poor disposal is the single biggest reputational risk for pad distribution in Africa. Pads flushed down pit latrines block them; pads dumped in open ground create visible waste and community resistance. Train users to:

  • Wrap the used pad in its wrapper, paper, or a newspaper sheet.
  • Place it in a covered bin with a liner, never directly into a latrine or toilet.
  • Where available, use incineration or municipal waste collection.
  • In schools and workplaces, advocate for discreet, lined disposal bins in every toilet cubicle.

5. Cleaning and Care for Reusable Pads

Reusable pads are a growing segment in East and Southern Africa, offering lower lifetime cost and reduced waste. Care instructions determine whether they last 6 months or 2 years a direct driver of customer satisfaction and repeat purchase of the brand's full range.

  1. Rinse in cold water immediately after use to prevent staining.
  2. Wash with mild soap; avoid bleach and fabric softener.
  3. Scrub gently; do not wring aggressively.
  4. Rinse until water runs clear.
  5. Sun-dry fully sunlight aids disinfection.
  6. Store clean, dry pads in a breathable bag.

6. Environmental Impact and Sustainable Disposal

A single disposable pad can take 500800 years to decompose, and contains plastics and superabsorbent polymers. In markets without reliable waste collection, this creates a visible environmental burden. Businesses can respond with:

  • Stocking reusable pads alongside disposables.
  • Offering biodegradable or compostable pad lines where supply allows.
  • Running take-back or safe-disposal partnerships with schools and clinics.
  • Using sustainability messaging as a premium brand differentiator.

SAFETY WARNING:

Never flush disposable pads down toilets or pit latrines. They do not break down and will block pipes and pits, causing overflow, health hazards, and costly repairs. Never share reusable pads between users without full washing and drying. If a user experiences persistent irritation, rash, or unusual odour, advise them to stop using the product and consult a health worker. Do not store pads in damp or dirty conditions contaminated products cause infection and destroy brand trust.

Quick Reference Checklist

  • Wash hands before and after every pad change.
  • Centre pad on clean, dry underwear; fold wings around edges.
  • Change disposable pads every 34 hours (3 hours on heavy days).
  • Match absorbency to flow: liner, regular, super, night.
  • Wrap used pads and place in a covered, lined bin never flush.
  • Rinse reusable pads in cold water immediately after use.
  • Wash reusables with mild soap, rinse clear, and sun-dry fully.
  • Stock both disposable and reusable options to serve all customer segments.
