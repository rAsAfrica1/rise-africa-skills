  • Agrochemicals: Never apply pesticides without full PPE (gloves, mask, long sleeves, boots). Respect the pre-harvest interval on the label â selling sprayed produce early is illegal and dangerous. Store chemicals locked, away from food and feed. Follow the FAO/WHO International Code of Conduct on Pesticide Management.
  • Aflatoxin: Maize and groundnuts with visible mould, discolouration or a bitter taste must not enter the food or feed chain. Aflatoxin is a carcinogen and aflatoxicosis outbreaks have killed hundreds in Kenya and Tanzania. Dry grain to below 13% moisture and test before sale. Reject contaminated lots â do not blend to dilute.
  • Milk and meat: Raw milk must be cooled within two hours of milking. Use only potable water for cleaning. Zoonotic disease (brucellosis, TB) spreads through unpasteurised dairy â pasteurise or boil.
  • Post-harvest chemicals: Use only registered storage pesticides and fumigants. Never use veterinary or crop chemicals on stored food outside label directions.
  • Traceability: Buyers and export markets increasingly require residue and mycotoxin test certificates. A single rejected consignment can end a contract permanently.

Quick Reference Checklist

  1. Know your unit economics before you plant. Write down input cost, labour, expected yield, expected price and post-harvest loss. If gross margin is under 25%, change the enterprise or the market.
  2. Aggregate before you negotiate. A single smallholder is a price-taker. A group of 20 farmers with 40 tonnes is a price-maker. Formalise the group and sign a joint supply agreement.
  3. Grade and sort every lot. Grading costs labour hours and returns 15â30% on farm-gate price. It is the cheapest margin improvement available.
  4. Cut post-harvest loss first. Drying, hermetic storage bags, crates instead of sacks for tomatoes, and a cooling tank for milk pay back within one season.
  5. Get the contract in writing. Verbal offtake promises collapse at harvest. Written agreements with volume, quality spec, price formula and payment terms unlock bank and SACCO credit.
  6. Test for aflatoxin and residues. Buy a rapid test kit or use a certified lab. One contaminated lot can destroy a buyer relationship and trigger a recall.
  7. Track cash, not just yield. Record every sale, every input purchase and every loan repayment. Businesses fail from cash-flow gaps, not from bad harvests alone.
  8. Insure and diversify. Index-based crop insurance and a second enterprise (e.g. poultry alongside maize) protect against a single-season wipeout.
