A Drone Business - What Would Someone Pay For?

~14 min read20 min gameIntermediate
Real customers

🧠 Brain Opener

Three people in your town want something you could do with a drone.

  • Who pays for drone work?
  • What do they actually want?
  • How much do they pay?
🎁 Reveal a good answer

Farmers, event organisers, house sellers, builders, and rescue teams pay for drone work. They want photos, maps, or videos - not a drone flight. Prices range from 20 dollars for a photo set to 200 dollars for a full mapping job. Never sell drone flying. Sell the result.

📖 Rudo's First Paid Job

Rudo was thirteen. She had been flying her drone for six months and could take good photos. Her father said, "Now let us see if you can earn from it."

Rudo thought hard. She made a list of everyone in her area. Farmers, a wedding coming up, a house for sale on the main road, and her aunt's shop.

She chose the house for sale. The owner, Mr Chikafu, had put up a sign but had no photos. Rudo walked to his gate and knocked.

"Good afternoon, sir. I saw your house is for sale. I can take photos of it from above - the whole plot, the road, the fields around it. Ten photos for 20 dollars. If you do not like them, you do not pay."

Mr Chikafu looked at her for a long moment. Then he said, "Come tomorrow at 7 in the morning."

Rudo arrived at 6:45. The light was soft. She took twenty photos and chose the ten best. She put them on a small USB stick and delivered them the next day.

Mr Chikafu looked at every photo. He chose one for the signboard. Then he counted out 20 dollars and said, "Next week my brother has a farm. I will tell him about you."

Rudo walked home with 20 dollars and a small business. Her father was right - the flying is easy. The selling is the skill.

💡 The Big Idea

Three services that pay today

(1) Farm mapping - farmers pay to see which parts of their field are dry or struggling. 20 to 100 dollars per job. (2) Event photography - weddings, church events, football matches. 30 to 150 dollars per hour. (3) Real-estate photos - sellers pay to show their land or house from above. 20 to 80 dollars per photo set.

📚 Three Deep Lessons

1

Sell the result, not the drone

A farmer does not want a drone flight. He wants to see which rows are dry. A wedding party does not want aerial footage. They want a video that shows the whole celebration from a beautiful angle. The minute you sell drone flying, you are competing with everyone. The minute you sell a photo set that sells your house, you are the only one offering that.

Before every pitch, write one sentence: I help [who] get [what] for [price]. If you cannot write that sentence, you are not ready to pitch.

2

The 30-second pitch

A pitch is not a speech. It is thirty seconds that answer four questions: who is the customer, what do they want, what do you deliver, what does it cost. Anything longer than 30 seconds and you lose them.

Practice out loud, in front of a mirror. Time yourself. If you go over 30 seconds, cut. The best pitches are short, specific, and end with a clear next step: would you like me to do it this Saturday morning?

3

The rules you must respect

Drone flying is regulated in every country. In Kenya, you register with the Kenya Civil Aviation Authority. In South Africa, with the South African Civil Aviation Authority. In Zimbabwe, with the Civil Aviation Authority of Zimbabwe. Rules vary but they all cover: registration, no-fly zones, altitude limits, and no flying over people.

Before you sell one job, ask a real adult to help you check the rules for your country. Rules are not there to stop you. They are there so that if something goes wrong, you are protected, your customer is protected, and you are not committing an offence.

🛠 Hands-On - Your One-Page Pitch

You need

  • A4 paper
  • Pen
  • Highlighter
  1. Divide the page into four squares.
  2. Top-left: WHO is your customer? Be specific - farmers with 5 or more hectares near Mutare.
  3. Top-right: WHAT do they want? Photos showing which parts of their field need water.
  4. Bottom-left: WHAT will you deliver? 20 aerial photos and 1 map, within 2 days.
  5. Bottom-right: HOW MUCH? 40 dollars. First 5 customers get a discount.
  6. Underneath: one sentence describing how they contact you.
Why it works: One page forces clarity. If you cannot fit your pitch on one page, your customer will not remember it.

🎮 Game - 30-Second Pitch Competition

3 to 20 players - 20 min

Each person pitches a drone service in 30 seconds. The class votes on: would you pay for this? Is the price fair? Did the pitch name exactly what you get? Highest-scoring pitch wins a paper trophy and gets to pitch again to a real adult.

🤖 AI-Era Skill

AI is very good at writing pitches. But a pitch must sound like YOU, not like a chatbot.

Try this prompt: "Write a 30-second pitch for a 12-year-old offering drone photography to a wedding party in Zimbabwe. Include price, what is delivered, and one reassurance about safety. Then rewrite it to sound like a 12-year-old, not an adult."

Read both versions aloud. The second should sound natural. If it still sounds like a robot, ask the AI to use simpler words and shorter sentences.

🌍 Real Stories from Africa

Zipline in Rwanda

Zipline started as a US company but chose Rwanda for its first operations because the need was clear. Today it delivers 75 percent of Rwanda's blood supply outside Kigali by drone. The best market is not the richest one - it is the one with the clearest problem.

Drone herding in Namibia

In Namibia, farmers use drones to move cattle herds at night without disturbing them. A drone with a thermal camera can see through darkness. The service costs less than hiring extra herders - and it never gets tired.

🏆 Project Challenge

Pitch to One Real Adult

Do not just practice - pitch for real.

  1. Pick one person - a farmer, a shop owner, a builder, someone selling land.
  2. Write your one-page pitch.
  3. Rehearse it aloud 5 times. Make it under 30 seconds.
  4. Ask the person a real question: would you pay for this?
  5. Whatever they answer, write down what they said.
  6. Adjust your pitch based on their answer. Pitch again to someone else.
