Marketing and Sales - Getting Your Seed to Buyers

🧠 Brain Opener

You can grow the best seed in Africa. If nobody knows it exists, you will not sell it. Marketing is the bridge between your field and your buyer wallet.

  • What is the difference between marketing and sales?
  • What are the three main channels for seed sales in Africa?
  • How do you price seed?
🎁 Reveal a strong answer

Marketing is everything you do to make people aware of you. Sales is the moment money changes hands. The three main channels are direct selling, agro-dealer networks, and cooperative or NGO contracts. You price by knowing cost, knowing the market, and knowing the value your seed delivers.

📖 Tendai Builds a Buyer Network

Tendai had 60 kilograms of certified organic kale seed in his storage. His germination was 92 percent. His purity was 98 percent. His packaging was clean. He had no idea how to sell it.

He took 20 packets to the local market and stood with them for three days. He sold four. Nobody knew who he was or why his seed cost 3 dollars per packet when the agro-dealer sold it for 1 dollar.

His uncle, who sold vegetables in Harare, gave him three pieces of advice.

First, go where seed buyers already go. Stop trying to create a market in the wrong place.

Second, show, do not tell. Give away a free trial packet to 20 farmers. Let them see the germination.

Third, come back the next season. Farming is seasonal. If you only show up once, they will not remember you.

Tendai did all three. He went to the three agro-dealers in the nearest town. He gave them a free packet each and a wholesale price of 1.20 dollars per packet (they could sell at 2 dollars and keep 80 cents).

He visited a farmer training centre and donated 10 packets to their demonstration plot. The trainer told 40 farmers about his seed.

He joined a WhatsApp group of 300 vegetable farmers in his province. He posted one photo of his kale seed with the caption: "92 percent germination. Certified organic. Free sample available."

By the end of the season, Tendai had sold 40 kilograms. He had 12 regular customers. He had a waiting list for the next season.

He had not created a market. He had joined one that already existed.

💡 The Big Idea

Go where buyers already are

You do not create a market for seed. You join the market that already exists. Farmers already buy seed from agro-dealers, cooperatives, and training centres. Your job is to place yourself in front of those buyers and prove your seed is better. Do not stand in an empty field shouting. Stand where the buyers already stand.

📚 Three Deep Lessons

1

The three main sales channels

Direct selling. You sell to the farmer yourself - at their farm, by WhatsApp, at a market stall. Margin: highest. Effort: highest. Best for small volumes and building relationships.

Agro-dealer network. You sell to a shop at wholesale. The shop sells to farmers at retail. Margin: split 50-50. Effort: low. Best for scaling volume quickly.

Cooperative or NGO contracts. A cooperative or NGO buys in bulk from you. Margin: moderate. Effort: documentation heavy. Best for large stable volumes. Requires registered producer status and often certification.

2

The demonstration method

African farmers do not buy seed based on advertising. They buy based on what their neighbour grew. The single most effective marketing method is the demonstration plot.

Plant a small plot of your seed where farmers can see it growing. Invite them to visit. Let them see the plant vigour, the pod set, the harvest. Give free samples to three or four respected farmers. Their result becomes your marketing.

A single well-run demonstration plot can generate 30 to 50 buyers in one season. No radio ad, no social media post, no flyer can match this in Africa.

3

Pricing seed correctly

Three numbers decide your price. Cost per unit. Total production cost divided by kilograms produced. Market price. What competitors charge. Value delivered. What the farmer saves or earns by using your seed.

Your minimum price is cost plus 30 percent. Your maximum price is what the market will bear. The middle is where you should sit - unless your seed is certified organic, in which case you can charge 20 to 30 percent above conventional.

Always price by the packet, not by the kilogram, when selling to smallholders. A farmer who will not pay 30 dollars for a kilogram will pay 3 dollars for a 10-gram packet. Same price per kilogram, different psychology.

🛠 Hands-On - Build a One-Page Sales Plan

You need

  • A notebook
  • A pen
  • Your product list
  1. Write down your three products with prices.
  2. Write down your three target customer types.
  3. Write down three places where those customers already go.
  4. Choose one channel to start with this season.
  5. Write down your wholesale price and retail price for each product.
  6. Plan one demonstration plot.
  7. Plan one WhatsApp group or one farmer meeting.
  8. Write the plan on one page. Pin it up.
Why it works: A one-page plan is realistic and gets executed. A twenty-page plan does not.

Try this prompt: "I sell certified organic kale seed in [country] at 3 dollars per 10-gram packet. Give me a one-week marketing plan: three channels, one demo, one sample strategy, and pricing on a wholesale and retail basis."

Talk to three real farmers about the plan. If they would not buy based on this plan, revise it.

🌍 Real Stories from Africa

Seed Co field days

Seed Co, the largest seed company in Southern Africa, runs over 400 field days a year. Farmers see the demonstration plots and buy on the spot. The company has been doing this for 40 years. The method has not changed because it works.

Kenya Kakamega farmer groups

A Kenyan organic seed producer joined three farmer WhatsApp groups with a total of 800 members. He posted weekly updates from his demonstration plot. He sold out his entire 40-kilogram harvest in three months through those groups alone.

🏆 Project Challenge

Run One Demonstration Plot

Set up a demonstration plot that will generate buyers.

  1. Pick a visible plot on a main path or near a market.
  2. Plant your best seed there.
  3. Put up a small sign: crop, variety, expected harvest date.
  4. Photograph the plot every week and post on WhatsApp.
  5. Invite 5 farmers to visit at flowering and again at harvest.
  6. Give 3 free samples to respected farmers.
  7. At harvest, run a small event. Show the seed. Take orders.
  8. Follow up with every farmer who visited within 3 days.
