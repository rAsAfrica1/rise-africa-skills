Market Research and Customers - Who Actually Buys Seed

🧠 Brain Opener

Most seed businesses fail because they never talked to a buyer. They grew seed first and looked for a market second. The successful ones do it backward.

  • Who are the four types of seed buyers?
  • What do you need to know about each one?
  • How do you find your first buyer?
🎁 Reveal a strong answer

The four buyer types are smallholder farmers, commercial farmers, NGOs and development programmes, and seed companies or exporters. For each you need to know: what variety they want, what volume, at what price, and when they buy. You find your first buyer by talking to at least 10 potential customers before you plant anything.

📖 Makanaka Interviews Buyers

Makanaka had done everything except talk to a buyer. She had prepared her plot. She had bought foundation seed. She had compost ready. She was two weeks from planting.

Her sister asked one question: "Who is going to buy this seed?"

Makanaka said: "Farmers."

"Which farmers? How many? At what price? When?"

Makanaka had no answers.

Her sister made her do homework. Two weeks of walking and talking. Ten interviews minimum.

Makanaka made a list. Three smallholder farmers near her plot. Two commercial vegetable growers in the nearest town. One NGO that ran a farmer training programme. Two agro-dealers. One seed company representative. One school with a garden programme.

She visited each one. She asked five questions.

"What crops do you buy seed for?"

"How much do you buy per year?"

"What do you pay per kilogram?"

"When do you buy?"

"What would make you switch to a new supplier?"

The answers were surprising. The smallholders bought mostly kale and tomato seed, in small packets, once a season. The commercial growers bought in bulk, twice a year. The NGO bought from registered producers only, in 5-kilogram lots, once a year in June. The agro-dealers bought on credit, paid in 30 days. The seed company representative said they source from certified producers only.

The school bought nothing - they collected seed from their own crops.

Makanaka changed her plan. Instead of planting everything for the open market, she planted the varieties the NGO and the two commercial growers wanted. She planted kale, tomato, and pepper - nothing else.

She also registered with the national seed authority before the harvest.

By the time she harvested, she had three buyers already committed. She sold everything within three weeks.

Makanaka now repeats the interview process every season. It is the first thing she does before planting.

💡 The Big Idea

Talk to ten buyers before you plant one seed

Market research is not a document. It is ten conversations before you spend one dollar on seed. The questions are simple. The answers tell you what to plant, how much, and at what price. Without this, you are farming for a hope, not a business.

📚 Three Deep Lessons

1

The four buyer types

Smallholder farmers. Buy small quantities. Price sensitive. Buy in small packets (10g to 100g). Buy at the start of each season. Cash sales. Source: local agro-dealers or direct.

Commercial farmers. Buy bulk quantities. Buy from registered producers. Price less sensitive, quality critical. Buy in 1kg to 25kg bags. Twice a year. Want delivery and invoice terms.

NGOs and development programmes. Buy from registered producers only. Bulk quantities. Two to three times a year. Payment via bank transfer. Want documentation and reports.

Seed companies and exporters. Buy from certified organic producers. Bulk quantities. Long contracts. Quality standards are strict. Payment terms 30 to 60 days. Highest prices.

2

The five questions to ask every buyer

What crops do you buy seed for? This tells you what to grow.

How much do you buy per year? This tells you the volume.

What do you pay per kilogram? This tells you the price range.

When do you buy? This tells you the calendar.

What would make you switch to a new supplier? This tells you the competitive advantage. Common answers: price, delivery speed, quality consistency, certification, small packet sizes, credit terms.

3

The 10-interview rule

Ten interviews give you a clear picture. Fewer than ten and you have a guess. More than twenty and you are delaying.

Interview in person. On the phone, people give shorter answers. In person, they will tell you stories - the real information.

Write every answer down. Even if the buyer says something you do not understand, write it down. Read the answers after all ten interviews. Patterns emerge.

🛠 Hands-On - Run Ten Buyer Interviews

You need

  • A notebook
  • A pen
  • Your list of 10 potential buyers
  1. Make a list of 10 potential buyers within 20 kilometres.
  2. Write down the five questions in your notebook.
  3. Visit each one in person.
  4. Ask the five questions. Write down every answer.
  5. Do not sell anything during these visits. Just learn.
  6. After the ten visits, review your notes.
  7. Identify the three buyers most likely to buy from you.
  8. Write a paragraph on the variety, volume, price, and timing for your first sale.
Why it works: Ten conversations before planting save six months of wasted work.

🎮 Game - Buyer Interview Role Play

4 to 12 players - 25 min

Pair up. One player is a farmer, the other is a buyer with a hidden preference (variety, volume, price). The farmer has 5 minutes to ask questions and discover the preference. Score by whether the farmer correctly identified what the buyer wants. Then swap.

🌍 Real Stories from Africa

WorldVeg market research Kenya

The World Vegetable Center runs buyer research programmes in Kenya before starting seed production with farmer groups. The research identifies exact varieties, volumes, and price points. Farmers who participate sell 100 percent of what they grow. Farmers who skip the research often sell only 40 percent.

Rwanda cooperative market study

A Rwandan cooperative of 60 farmers conducted buyer interviews before choosing their crops. They discovered that a local NGO needed 200 kilograms of onion seed but no producer was registered. They registered and captured the entire contract. Worth 30,000 dollars.

🏆 Project Challenge

Ten Interviews in Ten Days

Complete the 10-interview rule.

  1. List 10 buyers within 20 kilometres.
  2. Visit one buyer per day for 10 days.
  3. Ask the five questions each time.
  4. Write all answers in a notebook.
  5. Do not sell during interviews.
  6. At the end, write a one-page market summary.
  7. Identify your 3 best buyers and their exact needs.
  8. Plan next season based on what you learned.
