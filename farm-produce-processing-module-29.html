Sweet Corn Processing

8 lessons · 30 quiz questions · Cereals & Grains

1 Sweet corn varieties

Sweet corn (Zea mays var. saccharata) is harvested at the immature milk stage — 18–21 days after silking, when kernels are tender and sweet.

Standard (su): Traditional sweet corn. Sugar converts to starch within hours. Must process within 24 hours.

Sugar-enhanced (se): Higher sugar, more tender. Best for freezing and canning.

Supersweet (sh2): Very high sugar (30%+). Sugar stays for days. Best for fresh market and premium products.

For processing, sugar-enhanced is ideal — good sugar, good yield, tender kernels.

2 Husking and cleaning

Sweet corn must be processed within 24 hours of harvest or sugar turns to starch.

  1. Husk corn by hand or with a mechanical husker
  2. Remove silk completely (a soft brush helps)
  3. Trim ends — cut off the base and tip
  4. Wash in cold water
  5. Inspect for insect damage

Husking yield: 100 kg whole ears → 45–55 kg husked corn.

⚠ Sweet corn loses 50% of its sugar in 24 hours at room temp. Refrigerate at 0–4°C immediately after harvest.

3 Canning sweet corn

Canned sweet corn is a global commodity — sold in hundreds of millions of cans yearly.

  1. Cut kernels off cob (cream-style: scrape; whole-kernel: cut at 2/3 depth)
  2. Blanch 3 minutes
  3. Fill jars/cans with kernels and boiling water (1 tsp salt per 500 ml)
  4. Leave 2 cm headspace
  5. Pressure can:
    • Pint jars: 55 minutes at 11 PSI
    • Quart jars: 85 minutes at 11 PSI
  6. Cool and test seals

Shelf life: 2 years unopened.

Retail: $1.20–$2.50 per 400 g can. Big volume, thin margin.

⚠ Corn is low-acid. Pressure canner only — botulism risk.

4 Frozen corn kernels

Frozen sweet corn is the largest processed corn product globally — over $3 billion market.

  1. Cut kernels off cob
  2. Blanch 2 minutes
  3. Ice water bath 2 minutes
  4. Drain thoroughly
  5. IQF freeze on trays
  6. Pack in poly bags (500 g, 1 kg)

Yield: 100 kg whole ears → 40–45 kg frozen kernels.

Bulk: $2–$4 per kg. Retail: $4–$8 per 500 g bag.

5 Corn flour and mealie meal

Note: this uses field corn (dent corn), not sweet corn. Field corn is harvested dry and milled into flour.

  1. Harvest field corn when kernels are hard and dry (moisture 15–20%)
  2. Shell corn from cobs
  3. Dry to 12–14% moisture
  4. Clean (remove dirt, broken kernels)
  5. Mill in a hammer mill or roller mill
  6. Sieve to required fineness:
    • Coarse (mealie meal): 1–2 mm
    • Fine (corn flour): 250 micron

Yield: 100 kg dried field corn → 85–90 kg flour.

Retail: $1–$2 per kg (mealie meal); $1.50–$3 (fine flour).

6 Corn snacks

Value-added corn snacks are higher-margin than raw corn.

Corn chips (tortilla chips): Nixtamalise field corn (cook with lime), grind to masa, press into thin rounds, fry at 180°C. Retail $8–$15 per kg.

Puffs and curls: Extruded corn snacks. Requires extruder ($2,000–$10,000). Retail $6–$12 per kg.

Popcorn: Special popcorn variety. Pop at 180°C. Flavour and bag. Retail $8–$20 per kg.

💰 Popcorn is the easiest snack to start with — low equipment cost, high margin.

7 Corn oil basics

Corn oil is extracted from corn germ (the small embryo in each kernel).

  1. Separate germ from endosperm (dry or wet milling)
  2. Cold press or expeller press the germ
  3. Filter oil
  4. Bottle in dark glass

Yield: 100 kg dried corn → 3–5 kg germ → 1–2 kg oil.

Retail: $4–$8 per litre. Large-scale production needs solvent extraction ($500K+ plant) — not for small producers.

8 Storage and pests

Corn is highly susceptible to pests and aflatoxin (a toxic mould).

  • Moisture: Dry to 12–14% before storage (below 12% for long-term)
  • Temperature: Cool, dry storage (below 25°C)
  • Airtight containers: Hermetic bags or metal silos prevent weevils
  • Pest control: Food-grade diatomaceous earth or approved fumigants
  • Aflatoxin testing: Essential for export — must be below 20 ppb (EU limit is 4 ppb)

Properly stored dried corn keeps 12–24 months.

💰 Highest margin corn products: popcorn, corn chips, frozen sweet corn. Mealie meal is high volume, low margin. Position: pick one or two, do them well.
