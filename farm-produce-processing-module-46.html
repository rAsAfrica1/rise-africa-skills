Sales — Direct, Shops & Wholesale

8 lessons · 30 quiz questions · Business

1 Direct to consumer (markets, WhatsApp)

Direct sales have the highest margin — you keep 100% of the retail price. No shop takes a cut.

Channels:

  • Farmers' markets — high foot traffic, pay a stall fee
  • Pop-up stalls on busy streets
  • WhatsApp orders and deliveries
  • Facebook and Instagram shops
  • Own website with online ordering
  • Door-to-door in your neighbourhood

You also get immediate customer feedback — what they like, what they want next, what price they'll pay.

Downside: you sell one unit at a time. Slow growth, high effort. Best as a starting point while you build wholesale relationships.

2 Approaching independent shops

Independent corner shops, grocers and delis are the best wholesale entry point. Smaller, faster decisions than supermarkets.

How to approach:

  1. Visit in person — never email first
  2. Bring samples and a printed price list
  3. Ask to speak to the owner (not staff)
  4. Offer to stock on consignment for the first month (they pay only for what sells)
  5. Agree a re-order process — WhatsApp is easiest
  6. Follow up weekly for the first month

Offer them a wholesale price that gives a 30–40% margin. Also give them the retail price so they don't undercut each other.

💡 Ten independent shops is a better base than one supermarket contract. More resilient, faster payment, easier relationship.

3 Supermarket listings

Supermarkets are the biggest prize but the hardest to win.

Requirements:

  • Barcode (GS1)
  • Compliant labels (see Module 43)
  • HACCP certification
  • Insurance (product liability)
  • Consistent supply in agreed volumes
  • Payment terms: 30–90 days (they pay late)
  • Listing fees or slotting allowances (in some countries)

How to get in: Apply to the buying department. Bring samples, packaging, price list, and references from independent shops. Start with smaller supermarket chains before approaching the big ones.

Beware: supermarkets negotiate hard and can delist you overnight. Don't rely on them for more than 40% of sales.

4 Wholesalers and distributors

Wholesalers buy in bulk and distribute to many shops. They solve your distribution problem — one sale, hundreds of shops reached.

Wholesale price is 30–40% below retail. Margin is thin but volume is high.

Terms typically:

  • Minimum order quantities
  • 30-day payment
  • Delivery to their warehouse
  • Return of unsold or damaged stock (negotiate hard on this)

Not every wholesaler is right. Choose those who serve shops that match your product (e.g. organic wholesaler for organic products).

5 Export and cross-border sales

Export opens bigger markets but requires serious compliance.

Requirements:

  • Export licence from your country
  • Phytosanitary certificate (for plant products)
  • Food safety certification (HACCP, sometimes ISO 22000)
  • Compliant labels for destination country
  • Freight forwarder and customs broker
  • Payment terms via letter of credit or bank transfer

Target markets for African processed foods:

  • EU and UK — diaspora + health food shops
  • Middle East — dried fruits, spices, sauces
  • US — diaspora + specialty stores

Start with diaspora markets. They pay premium for authentic African products.

💰 Diaspora markets are 3–5× more profitable than local sales for the same product. Package and market accordingly.

6 Sales team basics

When you're ready to grow, a salesperson multiplies your reach.

Hiring: Look for someone with personality, persistence, and knowledge of your product. Pay a small base salary + commission (10–20% of sales).

Training:

  • Product knowledge — ingredients, benefits, price
  • Sales script — how to open, how to handle objections
  • Recording orders clearly
  • Reporting daily via WhatsApp or simple app

Motivation: Commission, competitions, recognition. Track sales by salesperson. Celebrate wins publicly.

7 Invoicing and payments

Getting paid is harder than selling. Set clear terms:

  • Cash on delivery — safest, use with new customers
  • Payment on order — for retail, before you ship
  • 30-day terms — for approved wholesale customers
  • 50% deposit — for large orders from new customers

Invoice essentials: Your business name and contact, customer details, invoice number, date, product list with quantities and prices, VAT (if applicable), total, payment terms, bank details, due date.

Chase late payments by day 7, day 21, day 30. Be polite but firm. Late payers cost you money — you're financing their business.

8 Repeat and loyalty customers

It costs 5× more to win a new customer than to keep an existing one. Focus on repeat sales.

Loyalty ideas:

  • WhatsApp broadcast when new batch ready
  • Discount on 5th order or after spending $50
  • Free sample of new product with orders
  • Personal thank-you message
  • Ask for feedback — and act on it

Track repeat customers: Simple notebook or spreadsheet — name, contact, order history, preferences, last order date. Send reminders when they haven't ordered in 30+ days.

Your best customers are the ones you already have. Never neglect them for new prospects.

💰 Mix the channels: direct for margin, shops for steady volume, wholesale for growth, export for premium. Never rely on just one.
