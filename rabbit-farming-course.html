Rabbit Farming for Profit

Hutch design, breeding cycles, nutrition, and market-ready production techniques for profitable rabbit farming in Africa.

1
Why Rabbit Farming is Profitable
Start

Rabbits reproduce fast, need little space, and convert feed to meat 6x more efficiently than cattle. They are the ultimate small-scale livestock.

  • A doe can produce 40-50 kits per year — far more than goats or pigs per female.
  • Rabbit meat is low in fat, high in protein, and accepted across all religious groups.
  • Startup cost is low: you can begin with 2 does and 1 buck for under ₦50,000.
Pro Tip: Start with 2 does + 1 buck. In 12 months, you can grow to 50+ rabbits with proper management.

Key Takeaway: Rabbit farming offers the highest reproduction rate per dollar invested in livestock.

Hutch Design & Housing
Locked

Rabbits need clean, dry, well-ventilated housing. Poor housing causes respiratory disease, sore hocks, and heat stress.

  • Each adult rabbit needs a cage of at least 60cm x 45cm x 45cm.
  • Build hutches off the ground (raised) to prevent predators and dampness.
  • Use wire mesh floors with a resting board to prevent sore hocks.
Pro Tip: Face hutches east to avoid direct afternoon sun. Rabbits die quickly from heat above 30°C.

Key Takeaway: Clean, raised, shaded hutches are the foundation of healthy rabbit production.

Breeds for African Climate
Locked

Not all breeds thrive in African heat. Choose heat-tolerant, fast-growing breeds for your climate and market.

  • New Zealand White: fast growth, excellent meat conversion, widely available.
  • Chinchilla: good heat tolerance, beautiful fur for dual-purpose (meat + pelt).
  • California White: similar to New Zealand, slightly better mothering instincts.
Pro Tip: Crossbreeding local breeds with exotic stock improves hardiness while maintaining growth rate.

Key Takeaway: New Zealand White and Chinchilla are the top choices for African rabbit farmers.

Breeding & Reproduction Management
Locked

Rabbits are induced ovulators — they ovulate after mating, not on a cycle. This means you can control reproduction precisely.

  • A doe is ready to breed at 5-6 months; a buck at 6-7 months.
  • Gestation is only 31 days. A doe can be re-bred 4-6 weeks after kindling.
  • Check for pregnancy at 14 days by palpating the abdomen for grape-sized embryos.
Pro Tip: Always take the doe to the buck's cage, not vice versa. Does are territorial and may fight.

Key Takeaway: Master breeding timing. One doe can produce 6 litters per year with good management.

Feeding & Nutrition
Locked

Rabbits are herbivores with a unique digestive system. They practice coprophagy (eating night feces) to digest cellulose fully.

  • Adult rabbits need 100-150g of pellets daily, plus unlimited fresh greens.
  • Good greens: pumpkin leaves, waterleaf, Tridax, sweet potato vines, and grass.
  • Avoid: cassava leaves (cyanide), mango leaves (tannins), and moldy feed.
Pro Tip: Introduce new greens gradually. Sudden diet changes cause fatal digestive upset (bloat).

Key Takeaway: Pellets + fresh greens + clean water = healthy, fast-growing rabbits.

Health & Common Diseases
Locked

Rabbits hide illness well. By the time you see symptoms, the disease is often advanced. Prevention and observation are key.

  • Coccidiosis: bloody diarrhea, bloated belly. Prevent with clean cages and coccidiostats in feed.
  • Snuffles (Pasteurella): nasal discharge, sneezing. Isolate infected rabbits immediately.
  • Sore hocks: caused by wire floors without rest boards. Treat with antiseptic and soft bedding.
Pro Tip: Check rabbits daily: eyes should be bright, nose dry, ears clean, and droppings firm and round.

Key Takeaway: Daily observation catches disease early. Cleanliness prevents 90% of rabbit health problems.

Kits & Weaning Care
Locked

The first 8 weeks determine the quality of your stock. Kits are born blind, naked, and completely dependent on the doe.

  • Kits nurse only 1-2 times daily for 3-5 minutes. Do not disturb the nest.
  • Check the nest at birth. Remove dead kits and ensure all are fed (round bellies = fed).
  • Wean at 6-8 weeks. Separate bucks from does at 12 weeks to prevent early breeding.
Pro Tip: If a doe rejects her litter, you can foster kits to another doe within 3 days of birth.

Key Takeaway: Hands-off management of the nest + observation = high kit survival rates.

Meat Production & Processing
Locked

Rabbits reach market weight (2-2.5 kg) at 12-16 weeks. Processing is simple and can be done on-farm.

  • Dispatch humanely: cervical dislocation or blunt force to the head (instant).
  • Bleed immediately, then skin while warm. Cold carcasses are harder to skin.
  • Dressing percentage is 50-55% — higher than chicken and comparable to pork.
Pro Tip: Process in the cool morning. Chill carcasses quickly and package in sealed bags for freshness.

Key Takeaway: On-farm processing keeps quality high and gives you control over pricing.
