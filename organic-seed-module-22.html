Business Model Canvas - The Whole Business on One Page

~15 min read30 min gameAdvanced
Shared phone

🧠 Brain Opener

A business model is a picture of how your business makes money. The Business Model Canvas puts the whole picture on one page. Nine blocks. One page. Clarity.

  • What are the nine blocks of the Business Model Canvas?
  • Why is one page better than a 50-page plan?
  • What is the most important block?
🎁 Reveal a strong answer

The nine blocks are: customer segments, value proposition, channels, customer relationships, revenue streams, key resources, key activities, key partnerships, and cost structure. One page is better because it forces clarity - you cannot hide behind long documents. The most important block is value proposition - why a customer chooses you.

📖 Rudo Builds a Canvas

Rudo had been running her seed business for a year. She was earning but she was not growing. She did not know why.

Her aunt, who taught business at a college in Harare, asked her a simple question: "Draw your business on one page."

Rudo started to write a long document. Her aunt stopped her.

"Not a document. A canvas. Nine boxes. One page. Take one hour."

Rudo drew nine boxes. She filled them in.

Customer segments: smallholder vegetable farmers near Harare, three commercial tomato growers, one NGO running a training programme.

Value proposition: certified organic seed, locally adapted variety, small packets available, delivery included for orders above 5 kilograms.

Channels: direct visits, WhatsApp orders, agro-dealers in three towns.

Customer relationships: personal visits every season, follow-up calls after delivery, free advice on planting.

Revenue streams: seed sales ($8,200 last season), training sessions ($600).

Key resources: 1 hectare of land, drip irrigation, certified producer status, my own labour.

Key activities: seed production, quality testing, packaging, selling.

Key partnerships: foundation seed supplier, PGS group, one transport company, national seed authority.

Cost structure: seed and inputs ($2,400), labour ($800), certification and testing ($300), packaging ($250), transport ($200), total $3,950.

She stared at the page. It was the whole business. She could see it for the first time.

But something was missing. She had no plan for scaling.

Her aunt pointed to the "customer segments" box. "You have four customers. If one leaves, you lose 25 percent of your income. Add more customers."

Then she pointed to "channels". "You sell directly. That works for 20 customers but not for 200. Add agro-dealers."

Rudo rewrote the canvas. Three new customer groups. Two new channels.

The next season her revenue went from $8,200 to $14,600. She had not worked harder. She had worked clearer.

💡 The Big Idea

The Business Model Canvas is your dashboard

A business plan is written once and forgotten. A canvas is used every week. It shows you where the business is strong, where it is weak, and where the next move is. Every serious business - from a small farm to a multinational - can be drawn in nine blocks.

📚 Three Deep Lessons

1

The nine blocks explained

1. Customer segments. Who are you selling to? Be specific. "Smallholder farmers within 20 km who grow vegetables" is specific. "Farmers" is not.

2. Value proposition. Why do they buy from you? Not "good seed" - every supplier has seed. "Certified organic seed in small packets delivered to your farm" is a value proposition.

3. Channels. How do customers reach you? Direct visit, WhatsApp, agro-dealer, market stall, online.

4. Customer relationships. How do you keep them? Follow-up calls, free advice, seasonal visits, a WhatsApp group.

5. Revenue streams. How do you earn? Seed sales, training, consultancy, subscriptions. Be specific with numbers.

6. Key resources. What do you own that makes the business work? Land, water, equipment, certification, knowledge, labour.

7. Key activities. What do you do every day or week? Production, testing, packaging, selling, record keeping.

8. Key partnerships. Who do you depend on? Suppliers, transporters, certifiers, buyers, government.

9. Cost structure. What do you spend money on? Inputs, labour, certification, packaging, transport, rent.

2

How to fill the canvas in one hour

Start with customer segments. Everything else follows from who you serve. Write it first.

Next, value proposition. Ask yourself: why would this customer choose me over the alternatives? If you cannot answer clearly, your value proposition needs work.

Then channels and customer relationships. How do they find you? How do you keep them?

Then revenue streams. Write actual numbers if you have them. Estimates if you do not.

Then the bottom row: key resources, key activities, key partnerships. These support the top row.

Last, cost structure. Every cost line should link to something above it.

3

Using the canvas to grow

A canvas is not a one-time document. It is a weekly tool. Every Sunday, look at it. Ask: which blocks grew this week? Which blocks need attention?

When revenue is low, look at customer segments. When customers leave, look at value proposition. When you are exhausted, look at key activities - you are probably doing things that should be outsourced or automated.

The biggest growth lever is almost always customer segments. Adding one new segment can double revenue with the same resources. Ask: who else needs what I produce?

🛠 Hands-On - Draw Your Own Canvas

You need

  • A large piece of paper or A4
  • A pen
  • One hour of quiet time
  1. Draw a 3x3 grid on the paper.
  2. Label the top row: Key Partners, Key Activities, Key Resources.
  3. Middle row: Value Proposition (centre), Customer Relationships (left), Channels (right).
  4. Bottom row: Cost Structure (left), Revenue Streams (centre), Customer Segments (right).
  5. Start with Customer Segments. Fill it in.
  6. Fill in Value Proposition. Be specific.
  7. Fill in Channels and Customer Relationships.
  8. Fill in Revenue Streams with numbers.
  9. Fill in the bottom left row: Key Resources, Activities, Partnerships.
  10. Fill in Cost Structure with numbers.
  11. Now look at the whole page. Where is the weakest block?
Why it works: The canvas shows you the whole business at once. Patterns emerge that a text document hides.

🎮 Game - Canvas Build Race

3 to 10 players - 30 min

Each player gets the same fictional seed business scenario. They have 20 minutes to fill a Business Model Canvas. Then each presents to the class. The class votes on the canvas that is clearest, most specific, and most realistic. Discuss: what made the winning canvas strong?
