1. Selling at Local Markets and Craft Fairs

Start by visiting your local market or craft fair as a customer. Note the busiest days and times. In many African markets, weekends and month-end are best because people have cash. Ask the market chairman about stall fees. In Nairobi's Maasai Market, a stall might cost KSh 1,000 per day; in Lagos, a small space can be â¦5,000. Book a corner spot if possibleâit gets more foot traffic.

Your display matters. Use a clean tablecloth, perhaps in a bright African print. Arrange your perfume oils and incense in tiers: small sample bottles in front, larger ones behind. Label each scent clearly with a name and price. For incense, have a burning stick so people can smell the smoke. Keep a small mirror and test strips so customers can try scents on their skin.

Price for the market. If your 10ml roll-on costs 500 naira to make, sell at 1,500 naira. Offer a 'market special': buy two, get a free sample. Accept mobile moneyâM-Pesa, MTN MoMo, Airtel Moneyâbecause many people don't carry cash. Have a notebook to record sales and customer contacts.

Engage passersby with a friendly 'Smell this oneâit's our best seller.' Don't sit behind your table; stand and invite. Keep a spray bottle of water to refresh your own scent. At the end of the day, note what sold best and what didn't. Repeat what works next time. Markets are also great for networking with other vendors who might stock your products.

2. Using WhatsApp and Facebook to Reach Customers

Create a WhatsApp Business account (free) and set your profile with a clear name like 'Amina's Scents' and a logo or photo of your products. Use the catalog feature to list each perfume oil and incense with prices. Write a short description: 'Long-lasting oud oil, 10ml, 2,000 naira.' Add a greeting message that auto-replies: 'Welcome! Browse our catalog and order via WhatsApp.'

Build a broadcast list of customers who give permission. Send at most two messages per weekâone new product or promotion, one helpful tip (e.g., 'How to make your scent last longer'). Personalize: 'Hi Chidi, we just got a fresh batch of sandalwood incense.' Use status updates to show behind-the-scenes: packing orders, new arrivals, happy customers.

On Facebook, join local buy-and-sell groups in your city or town. Post clear photos with prices and a call to action: 'DM to order.' Use Facebook Marketplace to list your products for free. Create a page for your business and invite friends to like it. Post customer testimonials and short videos of you making incense.

Run a simple ad: spend $5 to boost a post targeting your city and interests like 'perfume' or 'incense'. Track results by asking new customers 'How did you hear about us?' Respond to all comments and messages within an hour if possible. Use WhatsApp's 'Quick Replies' for common questions. Save all customer numbers in your phone with a tag like 'Perfume Customer' so you can follow up later.

3. Partnering with Hair Salons, Spas, and Churches

Hair salons and barbershops are goldmines. Customers sit for hours, relaxed and talking. Offer the salon owner a free 10ml roll-on and a small commissionâsay 20%âfor every bottle sold. Leave a display tray with 5-10 bottles and a sign: 'Ask for our exclusive scents.' Check stock weekly and collect payment. In Accra, a salon might sell 15 bottles a week for you.

Spas and massage parlors use oils and incense daily. Approach the manager with a wholesale price: sell your 10ml oils at 1,200 naira each when they buy 20 or more. Provide a free sample set so therapists can recommend scents to clients. Offer a 'spa exclusive' blendâlike a calming lavender and chamomileâthat only they sell. This builds loyalty.

Churches are community hubs. Many members buy incense for prayer and home use. Speak to the church administrator about selling after Sunday service. Set up a small table near the entrance with a sign: 'Frankincense and myrrhâpure and affordable.' Donate 10% of sales to the church's welfare fund; this builds goodwill. Also, offer a bulk discount for church events like weddings or conventions.

For all partnerships, put the agreement in writing: commission rate, payment schedule, and return policy. Visit regularly to build relationships. Ask partners for feedback on what scents sell best. A salon owner might tell you clients love vanilla; a spa might request more eucalyptus. Adjust your stock accordingly. Happy partners become your best salespeople.

4. Hosting Scent Parties and Demo Sessions

A scent party is like a Tupperware party but for perfume oils and incense. Invite 8-12 friends, family, and neighbors to your home or a community hall. Serve light refreshmentsâchin chin, samosas, or soft drinks. The goal is fun and discovery, not hard selling. Ask each guest to bring one friend who loves nice smells.

Start with a 10-minute demo. Show how to apply oil to pulse points: wrists, behind ears, base of throat. Pass around test strips for each scent. Let guests guess the notesâ'Is this vanilla or caramel?' For incense, light a stick and let the smoke fill the room. Explain the difference between synthetic and natural oils. Share a tip: 'Layer oud with rose for a unique signature scent.'

Offer a party special: buy any two items, get a third at 50% off. Have a 'scent bar' where guests can mix two oils to create a custom blendâcharge a small fee for the mixing session. Provide small bottles for them to take home. Encourage guests to post photos on WhatsApp and tag you. Give a free 5ml sample to anyone who books a future party.

Follow up within 48 hours with a thank-you message and a photo of the group. Ask for feedback: 'Which scent was your favorite?' Offer a loyalty card: buy 5, get 1 free. If the party goes well, ask the host to host another in a month. Scent parties can generate 20,000-50,000 naira in a single evening with low overhead. They also build a community around your brand.

5. Collecting Testimonials and Reviews

Testimonials build trust faster than any ad. After a customer buys, wait a few days, then send a WhatsApp message: 'Hi [Name], how are you enjoying the oud oil? If you have a minute, could you share a short review? It helps my small business grow.' Offer a small incentive: a 10% discount on their next order or a free sample.

Make it easy. Suggest a format: 'Just tell me what you liked and how you use it.' If they send a voice note, ask permission to quote them. For written reviews, copy the text and save it in a 'Testimonials' folder on your phone or Google Drive. Take a screenshot of WhatsApp chats (with permission) and blur out personal details if needed.

Use reviews everywhere. Put them on your WhatsApp catalog and Facebook page. Create a simple poster with 3-4 quotes and your contact number. Display it at market stalls and partner salons. In face-to-face selling, say, 'Mrs. Adeyemi said our lavender oil helped her sleep betterâwould you like to try it?'

Ask for video testimonials from loyal customers. A 30-second clip of someone saying 'I love this scent' is powerful on social media. If a customer complains, respond quickly and fix it. A resolved complaint can become a positive review: 'They replaced my broken bottle immediately.' Always thank the reviewer publicly. Over time, collect at least 20 reviews. They become your sales team, working 24/7.

6. Creating a Simple Loyalty Program

A loyalty program keeps customers coming back. Start simple: a paper card or a WhatsApp list. For every 1,000 naira spent, give one stamp or point. After 10 stamps, the customer gets a free 10ml perfume oil or a small incense pack. Print cards at a local print shop for 50 naira each. Write the customer's name on the card to make it personal.

If you prefer digital, use a notebook or a free app like Loyverse or a simple Google Sheet. Record each customer's name, phone number, and points. Send a WhatsApp message when they reach 8 points: 'You're 2 points away from a free gift!' This reminder drives repeat visits.

Offer tiered rewards. Bronze (0-5 purchases): 5% off. Silver (6-10): 10% off and free samples. Gold (11+): 15% off plus a birthday gift. Keep it easy to understand. Announce the program at markets and scent parties: 'Join our loyalty club and get free perfume!' Collect phone numbers with permission.

Run a 'double points' day once a month, like the last Saturday. Promote it on WhatsApp status and at partner salons. Track redemptions to see if the program is working. If customers aren't using their cards, simplify: maybe every 5th purchase is free. The goal is to make customers feel valued and special. A loyal customer spends 67% more over time, so this small effort pays off.

2. Using WhatsApp and Facebook to Reach Customers

WhatsApp is the single most powerful sales tool for African fragrance sellers. It is free, personal, and built for repeat orders. Use WhatsApp Business (free) to set a catalogue, auto-replies, and a business profile with your location and hours.

Post a daily status: a 15-second video of a customer smelling a new oil, a photo of a fresh batch of incense, a "sold out" update. Status views turn into DMs. Facebook works for reach: join local buy-and-sell groups, community pages, and church or women's groups. Post once a day, always with a price and a call to action ("DM to order, delivery in [town]").

3. Partnering with Hair Salons, Spas, and Churches

Salons and spas are perfect partners: clients sit for hours, smell your oils, and buy on impulse. Offer a 30â40% wholesale margin and place a small display of roll-ons and incense near the wash basins. Restock weekly and pay the owner a small commission on sales.

A scent party is a home-based selling event. Invite 8â15 friends, neighbours, or church members. Serve tea, pass around testers, and let guests rank their favourites. Sell bundles on the spot. Testimonials are your cheapest and most powerful advertising. After every sale, ask: "Can I share what you said?" Capture a short voice note or written quote, with permission. Post them on your WhatsApp status and Facebook page. Save screenshots in a "Social Proof" folder on your phone.

Keep it simple and paper-based if needed. A "buy 5, get 1 free" card works well. For WhatsApp customers, track purchases in a notebook or a free app like Google Sheets. Send a "thank you" message after every 5th purchase and a small gift (a sample vial, a discount code) to keep them coming back.
