1. How to conduct a client meeting in person or via WhatsApp call

Start by scheduling the meeting at a time that works for both of you. If in person, choose a neutral place like a cafÃ© or your office. If via WhatsApp call, confirm the time and ensure you have a stable internet connection. Before the call, prepare a notebook and pen, and have a simple questionnaire ready with key questions about the event. Begin the meeting by introducing yourself and your business. Be friendly and professional. Ask open-ended questions to understand their vision. For example, 'Tell me about the event you are planning.' Listen more than you talk. Take notes on important details like date, venue, guest count, and budget. If on WhatsApp, you can ask them to send pictures or voice notes for clarity. After the meeting, summarize what you discussed and send a follow-up message thanking them. This shows you are organized and serious. Always confirm the next steps, like sending a quote or visiting the venue. Remember, a good consultation builds trust and helps you avoid costly mistakes later.

2. Asking the right questions: guest count, budget, date, and cultural requirements

The success of any event depends on asking the right questions. Start with the date and time. Is it flexible? Check if it clashes with other events. Next, ask about the guest count. This affects everything from seating to catering. Get an exact number or a range. Then, discuss the budget. Be direct: 'What is your budget for decor and planning?' If they are unsure, give a range based on similar events. For example, a simple decor for 50 guests might cost between $500 and $1000. Cultural requirements are crucial in Africa. Ask about traditions, colors, and any specific rituals. For instance, in many African weddings, certain colors are symbolic. Also, ask about dietary restrictions and religious practices. Other important questions: venue (indoor or outdoor), theme, and must-have items. Write down all answers. If the client is unsure, offer suggestions based on your experience. Always confirm the answers to avoid misunderstandings. After the meeting, compile the information into a brief. This will guide your planning and quoting. Remember, thorough questioning saves time and money.

3. Creating a simple event brief template using pen and paper

An event brief is a summary of all the key details. You don't need a computer; pen and paper work perfectly. Start with a blank sheet. At the top, write 'Event Brief' and the date. Then, create sections. Section 1: Client details â name, phone, email. Section 2: Event details â type (wedding, birthday), date, time, venue. Section 3: Guest count â number of guests, VIPs. Section 4: Budget â total budget and breakdown if possible. Section 5: Decor requirements â colors, theme, specific items (e.g., 10 centerpieces, backdrop). Section 6: Cultural or special requests â traditions, rituals. Section 7: Timeline â key deadlines. Section 8: Notes â any other important information. Leave space at the bottom for both you and the client to sign and date. This makes it a simple agreement. Keep the brief concise, one or two pages. Use a ruler to draw lines for neatness. After filling it, read it back to the client to confirm accuracy. Then, make two copies: one for you, one for the client. This simple document prevents disputes and ensures you both are on the same page.

4. Managing client expectations for outdoor and indoor events

Outdoor events in Africa come with unique challenges: weather, insects, and power supply. Be upfront with clients about these. For example, if it's rainy season, suggest a tent or an indoor backup. Explain that wind can affect decor like balloons and drapes. For indoor events, discuss space limitations, ceiling height, and power outlets. Always have a plan B. For outdoor, that might mean renting a generator or having extra tarps. For indoor, check if you can hang decorations without damaging walls. Discuss setup and breakdown times. If the venue has strict rules, inform the client early. Also, talk about guest comfort: shade, seating, and access to toilets. Use real numbers: a 10x10 meter tent can cost around $300 in many African cities. Be honest about what is possible within their budget. If they want a lavish outdoor wedding but have a small budget, suggest alternatives like focusing on a few key decor items. Document all agreed expectations in the brief. This way, if something goes wrong, you have a record. Remember, under-promise and over-deliver.

5. Handling difficult clients and negotiating changes politely

Difficult clients can be demanding, indecisive, or rude. Stay calm and professional. Listen to their concerns without interrupting. Repeat back what they said to show you understand. For example, 'So you're saying the color scheme is not what you wanted?' Then, offer solutions. If they want changes after the brief is signed, explain that changes may affect cost and timeline. Use polite language: 'I can do that, but it will add $200 to the budget. Would you like to proceed?' If they push back, offer alternatives. For instance, if they want more flowers but have a fixed budget, suggest using cheaper flowers or reducing the number. Never argue. If a client becomes abusive, politely end the conversation and suggest continuing later. Always document changes in writing, even via WhatsApp. Get their confirmation. For negotiations, know your bottom line. Don't agree to work at a loss. If you can't meet their demands, it's okay to decline. Remember, your reputation is important. A difficult client can become a loyal one if you handle them well. Stay patient and focused on finding a win-win solution.

6. Confirming bookings with a written agreement or deposit receipt

Once the client agrees to your quote, confirm the booking in writing. A simple agreement or deposit receipt is enough. The agreement should include: your business name and contact, client name and contact, event date and venue, services to be provided, total cost, deposit amount, and balance due date. Also, include a cancellation policy. For example, 'Deposit is non-refundable if cancelled within 7 days of event.' Write it clearly, in duplicate. Both parties sign and date. Give one copy to the client. If the client pays a deposit, issue a receipt immediately. The receipt should show the amount paid, date, and remaining balance. You can buy a receipt book from any stationery shop for a few dollars. Always keep a copy for your records. In Africa, many deals are done verbally, but a written agreement protects you. It also makes you look professional. If the client is far, you can send the agreement via WhatsApp and ask them to reply 'I agree' and send a screenshot of the signed copy. For deposits, mobile money is common. Always confirm receipt and send a digital receipt if possible. This step secures your income and reduces no-shows.

3. Creating a Simple Event Brief Template (Pen and Paper)

You do not need software. A single A4 page, written by hand in front of the client, is more powerful than a printed form because the client sees you listening. Use this structure:

  1. Header: Client name, phone, email, date of consultation, your name.
  2. Event details: Type, date, start time, end time, venue name and address.
  3. Guest numbers: Confirmed, maximum, children, VIPs.
  4. Budget: Total, deposit amount, balance due date, payment method.
  5. Scope of work: Exactly what you will deliver â e.g., "Draping for 12 tables, head table backdrop, entrance arch, aisle runner."
  6. Exclusions: What you will NOT do â e.g., "Chair hire, catering, lighting, generator."
  7. Cultural requirements: Colours, seating, ceremony timing, restrictions.
  8. Change policy: "Changes after signing are charged at 15% of the changed item, and must be confirmed in writing 14 days before the event."
  9. Signatures: Client and service provider, with date.

Photograph the signed page and send it to the client on WhatsApp immediately. Keep the original in a physical file. Paper survives power cuts and lost phones.

If it does not rain, you have paid for peace of mind. I will not remove the tent from the quote to make the price cheaper, because if it rains I will be blamed."

â  SAFETY WARNING â Outdoor Events

Never erect a tent, stage, or truss on soft ground without anchoring it to stakes or weights rated for wind. In many parts of Africa, afternoon storms arrive within 20 minutes with strong gusts. Collapsed tents and falling trusses have killed and injured guests at weddings and church events. Also: never run generator cables through standing water, and never allow cooking fires or braziers inside or under a tent. If the venue has no safe power source, refuse the job or hire a licensed electrician â do not improvise with domestic extension cables.

5. Handling Difficult Clients and Negotiating Changes Politely

Difficult clients are usually scared clients. They are afraid the event will fail, the family will criticise them, or the money will run out. Your job is to reduce their fear with written clarity, not to win the argument.

The three-step method:

  1. Acknowledge: "I hear you. You want the entrance to look like the photo you sent."
  2. Explain the cost or risk: "That design needs 20 extra metres of fabric and four more hours of labour. That adds KES 18,000."
  3. Offer two options: "Option A: we do the full design and the budget rises. Option B: we keep the budget and use a simpler design that still looks beautiful. Which do you prefer?"

Never say "no." Always say "yes, and here is what it costs." Put every agreed change in a WhatsApp message and ask the client to reply "Confirmed." That reply is your evidence.

For the client who refuses to pay a deposit: hold the date for 48 hours only, in writing. After 48 hours, release the date. Do not spend money on a job that has no deposit.

A booking exists only when money has changed hands and a receipt has been issued. Your receipt must contain:

  • Your business name, phone, and physical address
  • Client name and phone number
  • Event date and venue
  • Total contract value
  • Deposit received and date received
  • Balance amount and due date
  • Scope summary (one line) and reference to the signed brief
  • Cancellation policy: "Deposit is non-refundable if cancelled within 14 days of the event."
  • Signature and stamp

Issue two copies â one for the client, one for your file. Send a photo of the signed receipt to the client on WhatsApp the same day.
