6. Health, Parasites and Disease Control

Sheep have specific health threats. Here is the prevention and treatment plan that keeps your flock alive.

Module 6 of 12 · Sheep Farming for Profit

The seven diseases that kill sheep

DiseaseCauseSignsPrevention
Peste des Petits Ruminants (PPR)VirusFever, discharge, mouth sores, diarrhoea, sudden deathAnnual vaccine. No cure.
Pasteurellosis (pneumonia)BacteriaCoughing, laboured breathing, feverVaccine, good ventilation, low stress
Enterotoxaemia (pulpy kidney)BacteriaSudden death, especially after feed changeVaccinate ewes 4 weeks pre-lambing, lambs at 6 weeks
Foot rotBacteriaLimping, smelly feet, swollen hoofTrim hooves, dry ground, footbath with zinc sulphate
Haemonchus (barber's pole worm)Internal parasitePale gums, bottle jaw, weight lossRotational grazing, FAMACHA scoring, deworm selectively
CoccidiosisProtozoaBloody diarrhoea in lambs, poor growthDry litter, coccidiostat in feed, hygiene
MastitisBacteriaHot swollen udder, stringy milk, lamb mortalityClean lambing pens, dry bedding, treat early

Internal parasites (worms)

Internal parasites are the biggest hidden cost in sheep farming. Signs:

  • Pale gums and eyelids (anaemia)
  • Bottle jaw (swelling under the jaw)
  • Weight loss, poor growth
  • Diarrhoea
  • Reduced wool quality

The FAMACHA system

FAMACHA uses eyelid colour to decide if a sheep needs deworming. Prevents over-deworming (which causes resistance).

ScoreEyelid colourAction
1Red, healthyNo treatment
2Red-pinkNo treatment
3PinkMonitor closely
4Pink-whiteDeworm now
5WhiteDeworm now, urgent - may need iron

Check 10-20 sheep per month. Only treat scores 4 and 5. This slows resistance.

Deworming schedule

Age/WhenAction
Lambs 6 weeksFirst deworm
Every 3 monthsRoutine deworm - but use FAMACHA to select
3 weeks before matingDeworm ewes
Before introducing new sheepDeworm during quarantine
Never deworm all sheep at once. Rotate actives and use FAMACHA. Worm resistance is a real and growing problem.

External parasites

ParasiteSignsControl
Sheep scab (mite)Intense itching, wool loss, crusty skinDipping, ivermectin injection, quarantine new stock
LiceRestlessness, hair loss, poor woolDusting or spray with approved acaricide
TicksVisible on skin, blood loss, disease transmissionDipping, keep pasture short, rotate paddocks
Blowfly strikeMaggots in dirty woolCrutching, dagging, fly strike treatment, prevention

Hoof care

  • Check hooves every 3-4 months.
  • Trim overgrown hooves with sharp hoof shears.
  • Cut back to the sole level - do not cut into the quick.
  • Footbath with 10% zinc sulphate after trimming if any sign of rot.
  • Provide dry, rocky areas for natural hoof wear.
  • Wet, muddy ground causes foot rot.

Body condition scoring

ScoreDescriptionAction
1Very thin - bones visibleFeed more, check for worms/disease
2ThinIncrease feed
3Ideal - ribs felt but not seenMaintain
4FatReduce feed if not pregnant
5ObeseReduce feed, check for pregnancy toxaemia

Check body condition monthly. Score 3 is target for ewes, 3-4 for rams in breeding season.

Quarantine protocol

  1. Separate pen 50-100 m from main flock.
  2. Separate boots and tools.
  3. Feed and water new sheep last.
  4. Watch for 21 days for any signs of disease.
  5. Deworm, vaccinate, and treat for external parasites before introduction.
  6. Introduce gradually - 1-2 sheep at a time to the main flock.
Never skip quarantine. One infected sheep can wipe out a flock with PPR, scab, or foot rot.

Daily health routine

  1. Morning: Walk the flock. Look for limping, coughing, dull eyes, off-feed animals.
  2. Midday: Check water and count animals - a missing sheep means a problem.
  3. Evening: Observe feeding behaviour. Sick sheep eat last or not at all.
  4. Weekly: FAMACHA check 10 sheep at random.
  5. Monthly: Body condition score all adults.
  6. Every 3 months: Hoof trimming, routine deworm (selected).
  7. Annually: PPR and pasteurellosis vaccines.
The prevention rule: Vaccines and biosecurity cost less than treatment. A $2 vaccine prevents a $100 loss. Do the routine.
Practical exercise 6: Set up a health calendar. Mark vaccination dates, deworm dates, hoof trimming, FAMACHA checks. Post it where you see it daily.
