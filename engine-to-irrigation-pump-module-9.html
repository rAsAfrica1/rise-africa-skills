9. Fuel System Setup for Continuous Operation

The fuel system must deliver clean fuel to the engine continuously for 6–10 hours per day, in dusty conditions, without leaks. Petrol and diesel systems are very different and require different handling. This module covers both in detail.

Module 9 of 12 · 6 lessons · ~60 minutes · 6 practical exercises · quiz at the end · Separate petrol and diesel sections

Lesson 9.1 — Petrol fuel system — components and setup

Petrol is volatile and dangerous. Every part of the system must be done right.

ComponentFunctionCost
Fuel tankStores fuel. Metal or approved plastic. 20–60 L typical.$40–$150
Fuel filler capSeals tank, vents vapour. Non-venting type if tank has separate vent.$5–$15
Fuel lineCarries fuel to engine. 6–8 mm inner diameter. Copper or steel.$15–$40
Inline fuel filterRemoves dust and water.$3–$10
Fuel pumpMechanical (on engine) or electric (in tank or inline).$20–$80
Fuel pressure regulatorKeeps fuel pressure stable.$15–$40
Return lineReturns excess fuel from engine back to tank (fuel-injected engines).$15–$40
Shutoff valveManual valve to cut fuel for service.$5–$15

Installation order (petrol):

  1. Mount the fuel tank away from the exhaust and engine heat. Minimum 500 mm from the exhaust manifold.
  2. Fit a shutoff valve at the tank outlet. This is a service valve — cheap insurance for fuel system work.
  3. Run the fuel line from tank to engine. Route away from heat sources. Secure every 300 mm with clips.
  4. Install the inline filter in the line, close to the engine (so you can easily change it). Filter arrow must point toward the engine.
  5. Connect to the fuel pump. Mechanical pump uses the camshaft drive on the engine. Electric pump mounts inline near the tank.
  6. For carburettor engines: pump outlet connects to carburettor inlet. No return line needed.
  7. For fuel-injected engines: pump outlet connects to fuel rail. Return line from regulator back to tank.
  8. Wire the electric fuel pump through a relay, powered from the ignition switch. Pump runs only when ignition is ON.
  9. Test for leaks: Switch on ignition (do not start engine), let the pump pressurise the system, and check every connection with a dry cloth. Any wet spot = leak.
Never install a petrol tank inside an enclosed space. Petrol vapour is heavier than air and collects at the bottom. One spark = explosion. Tank must be in an open, ventilated location with a vapour escape path.
Practical exercise 9.1: Source all petrol system components. Assemble and fit the system. Pressurise without starting the engine. Leak-test every connection.

Lesson 9.2 — Diesel fuel system — components and setup

Diesel systems are more complex — they must handle very high pressures and are highly sensitive to contamination.

ComponentFunctionCost
Fuel tankStores fuel. Metal or plastic. 40–100 L typical.$60–$250
Lift pumpLow-pressure pump from tank to high-pressure pump.$30–$100
Primary fuel filterCoarse filter with water separator. Critical for diesels.$25–$80
Secondary fuel filterFine filter, close to the pump.$10–$30
High-pressure pumpRaises pressure to 200–2,000 bar.Part of engine
InjectorsSpray fuel into combustion chamber.Part of engine
Return lineReturns excess fuel from injectors back to tank.$20–$50
Shutoff solenoidElectrically stops fuel to the injection pump (for shutdown).$20–$60
Manual shutoff cableBackup mechanical shutdown.$10–$25

Installation order (diesel):

  1. Mount the fuel tank high enough that gravity helps feed fuel. Diesel is thicker than petrol and cold weather can thicken it more.
  2. Fit a manual shutoff valve at the tank outlet — essential for filter changes.
  3. Fit the water separator (primary filter) close to the tank. This is the #1 protection against water contamination in Africa.
  4. Route the low-pressure line from tank through separator to lift pump.
  5. Mount the lift pump (electric or mechanical) close to the engine.
  6. Route the low-pressure line from lift pump through secondary filter to high-pressure pump.
  7. Connect the return line from injectors back to the tank. Must not be blocked.
  8. Wire the shutoff solenoid to the ignition switch. When ignition is OFF, the solenoid closes and stops the engine.
  9. Fit the manual shutoff cable as a backup. It overrides the solenoid in emergency.
  10. Prime the system. Loosen the bleed screw on the high-pressure pump. Operate the lift pump until fuel runs bubble-free. Tighten the bleed screw.
  11. Bleed injectors (if required). Loosen one injector line, crank the engine until fuel spits, tighten. Repeat for each injector.

Petrol features

  • Low pressure (0.2–3 bar)
  • Simple rubber or copper lines
  • Single filter usually enough
  • Vapour lock risk in hot weather
  • More fire risk — careful venting

Diesel features

  • High pressure (200–2,000 bar)
  • Steel lines for high-pressure side
  • Water separator essential
  • Air lock risk — must bleed
  • Contamination sensitivity — filter quality matters
Diesel in Africa often contains water and dirt. Always use a water separator plus a fine filter. Change fuel filters every 200–300 hours. Contaminated fuel destroys injection pumps and injectors worth $500–$2,000.
Practical exercise 9.2: Source all diesel system components including a water separator. Assemble, prime, and bleed the system. Confirm no air in the lines before starting.

Lesson 9.3 — Fuel tank selection and mounting

Tank typeBest forCostNotes
Salvaged car tankFirst-time builds$20–$80Check for rust and leaks
Boat tank (plastic)Clean installs$80–$200Approved, non-corrosive
Custom steel tankLarge diesel builds$150–$500Made by local welder
Drum (200 L)Larger operations$30–$80Needs proper fittings
Jerry cans on rackTemporary or backup$10–$25Not a permanent solution

Mounting rules:

  • Lower than the carburettor or injectors. Gravity feeds are unreliable; pumps are required above the fuel level.
  • Higher than the lift pump inlet. For diesel, a slight gravity assist helps.
  • Away from exhaust and hot surfaces. Minimum 500 mm from exhaust manifold.
  • On rubber mounts. Absorbs vibration and prevents cracks.
  • With a drain plug. To drain water and sludge at service time.
  • With a vent. Tank must breathe — otherwise pressure builds and fuel flows unevenly.
  • With a fuel gauge. Visible from operator position.
Practical exercise 9.3: Mount the fuel tank on the frame. Confirm accessibility for filling, drain plug, and vent. Check clearance from exhaust. Fill with 5 litres and check for leaks.

Lesson 9.4 — Filters and water separators

Filters are the cheapest insurance in the fuel system. Replace them on schedule, no exceptions.

Filter typeChange intervalCost
Petrol inline filterEvery 5,000 L or 200 hours$3–$10
Diesel primary (water separator)Every 3,000 L or 150 hours$15–$40
Diesel secondary (fine)Every 5,000 L or 200 hours$10–$30

When to change immediately (off schedule):

  • Visible dirt in the fuel bowl
  • Water in the diesel separator (drain daily, change at 25% accumulation)
  • Rough running or loss of power
  • Fuel filter light on (if fitted)
  • After filling from a suspect source

Water separator draining:

  1. Place a container under the drain plug of the water separator.
  2. Open the drain valve at the bottom of the separator bowl.
  3. Watch the fluid. Water is slightly denser and drips first (with a clear distinction from diesel).
  4. Close the valve when only clean diesel comes out.
  5. Dispose of drained fluid properly — do not pour on the ground.
  6. Do this daily in diesel engines — a habit that saves injectors.
The filter rule: Every 200 hours of operation, replace the primary and secondary fuel filters. 200 hours = about 25 days at 8 hours per day. This alone prevents 60% of injection system failures.
Practical exercise 9.4: Buy 3 spare filters for your system. Keep them in a clean, sealed plastic bag near the pump. Log the running hours and change when due.

Lesson 9.5 — Priming and bleeding the fuel system

Air in the fuel system stops the engine. This is a normal part of diesel ownership.

Petrol priming:

  1. If the tank is above the pump, gravity feeds the line.
  2. If mechanical pump: crank the engine for 10 seconds (with spark disabled), which operates the pump.
  3. If electric pump: switch on ignition. Pump runs 3–5 seconds and pressurises the system. Wait for the whine to change (pump loaded).
  4. Start the engine. If it starts and runs smoothly, priming is complete.

Diesel bleeding (essential):

  1. Ensure fuel tank has at least 20% fuel.
  2. Loosen the bleed screw on the fuel filter housing (top).
  3. Operate the manual primer pump (on the filter housing or lift pump) until bubble-free diesel comes out.
  4. Tighten the bleed screw.
  5. Loosen the bleed screw on the high-pressure pump.
  6. Operate the primer pump until bubble-free diesel comes out.
  7. Tighten the bleed screw.
  8. Crank the engine with the starter for 5 seconds. If it starts, great. If not, crack open one injector line (1/4 turn) while cranking until fuel spits out; then tighten. Repeat for one or two injectors.
  9. Once running, let it idle for 2 minutes to clear remaining air.

Petrol — bleeding

Usually not required. Petrol systems self-purge air quickly. If it does not start after priming, check for lack of fuel pressure or a blocked filter.

Diesel — bleeding

Always required after filter change, running out of fuel, or air entering the system. Spend the 5 minutes to bleed correctly — cranking a diesel with air in the system can damage the injection pump.

Never crank a diesel engine for more than 10 seconds continuously with air in the system. The high-pressure pump depends on the fuel for lubrication. Air = no lubrication = pump damage in minutes. Bleed thoroughly first.
Practical exercise 9.5: Practise bleeding your diesel system. Change a filter and go through the full bleed process. Time yourself — this is a 5-minute job once you know how.

Lesson 9.6 — Fire safety and fuel storage

Fuel is the biggest fire hazard on the site. Every operator must know the rules.

Storage methodSafety ratingNotes
Approved jerry can (metal)GoodShort-term storage only
Approved plastic containerGoodGround it before filling
Fuel drum (200 L)ModerateMust be in a ventilated, shaded spot
Built-in tank on machineBestDesigned for the purpose
Unapproved containersDangerousNever use

Fire safety rules:

  1. Fire extinguisher: ABC dry powder, minimum 5 kg, within 10 m of the pump. A second one near the fuel storage.
  2. No smoking within 20 m of the fuel or the pump.
  3. Refuel with the engine OFF. Let it cool for 5 minutes before opening the tank cap.
  4. No spills tolerated. Clean up immediately with rags and dispose of rags safely.
  5. Fuel storage away from the pump. Minimum 20 m from the running engine.
  6. Ground the container before filling (touch metal to metal to discharge static).
  7. Never store fuel in direct sun. Pressure builds, vapours expand.
  8. Post "NO SMOKING" signs. Legally required in some jurisdictions.
The fuel rule: Refuel with the engine off and cool. Never top up a hot tank. Petrol vapour + hot exhaust = instant fire. This rule alone prevents most fuel fires.
Practical exercise 9.6: Buy 2 x 5 kg dry powder extinguishers. Place one at the pump, one at the fuel store. Post a NO SMOKING sign. Train everyone on how to use the extinguisher — actually spray one in a test fire.
