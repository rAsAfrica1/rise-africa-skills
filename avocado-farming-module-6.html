6. Post-Harvest, Marketing and Long-Term Strategy

Two crates per tree or twenty - the difference is in post-harvest handling and market access.

Module 6 of 6 · Avocado Farming for Profit

Post-harvest handling

StepAction
Immediately after harvestMove to shaded collection point within 30 minutes
SortingRemove damaged, undersized, or blemished fruit
GradingGrade 1 (export), Grade 2 (local premium), Grade 3 (processing)
CoolingIf possible, cool to 6-8 C within 24 hours
PackingSingle-layer trays or shallow crates. Never dump.
TransportTo packhouse within 24-48 hours of harvest

Grading for export

Finding buyers

  1. Contact your national avocado export association or horticultural board.
  2. Visit local exporters and ask about their buying process.
  3. Meet with restaurants and hotels in your nearest city.
  4. Join a cooperative or farmer group - volume gets you export access.
  5. Consider Fair Trade or GlobalG.A.P. certification for premium prices.

Costs drop dramatically after year 4 - most of the initial spend is establishment. From year 6 onward, a hectare is a genuine long-term income asset.

Records for a long-term orchard

The long-term rule: Avocado is a 30-year asset. Treat the first 5 years as an investment, not a cost. After year 6, most of the work is harvesting and selling.
Practical exercise 6: Write a 5-year plan for your avocado plot. List: trees planted, expected yield per year, expected revenue, costs, and reinvestment decisions at each stage.
