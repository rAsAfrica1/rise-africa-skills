10. Slaughter, Meat Handling and Value-Adding

Dressed and processed sheep meat earns 1.5-3x the live price. Here is how to do it properly.

Module 10 of 12 · Sheep Farming for Profit

Why slaughter and process

Selling live sheep at market earns market rate. Dressed, graded, and processed meat earns 1.5-3x more. Butcheries and restaurants pay premium for consistent quality and food safety.

A 45 kg live lamb gives a 20-23 kg carcass. A 60 kg live mutton sheep gives a 27-30 kg carcass.

The slaughter process

  1. Withdraw feed 12 hours before. Keep water available.
  2. Rest the sheep in a quiet pen 24 hours before - stress affects meat quality.
  3. Weigh live weight. Record.
  4. Restrain humanely. Killing box or casting pen.
  5. Single sharp cut to the throat. Sever carotid and jugular. Full bleed 2-3 minutes.
  6. Skin immediately - sheep are skinned, not scalded. Take care not to cut the skin.
  7. Remove head, feet at hock, and internal organs.
  8. Save skins for tanning. Salt or air-dry if not tanning immediately.
  9. Wash carcass with clean cold water. Remove all blood.
  10. Chill to 0-4 C within 1 hour.

Skin and hide handling

Sheep skins are a valuable by-product. Handle them carefully:

  1. Remove skin carefully - no knife cuts.
  2. Salt immediately with coarse salt (500 g per skin).
  3. Roll flesh-side in, store in a cool dry place.
  4. Or air-dry stretched on a frame in the shade.
  5. Sell to tannery or leather worker - fresh skins pay $5-25 depending on quality.

Food safety - non-negotiable

  • Clean water only for washing carcass.
  • Clean knives and table - disinfect between sheep.
  • Separate evisceration area from wash area.
  • Chill within 1 hour - never leave warm carcasses in the sun.
  • Wrap or bag all meat for transport.
  • Record batch number and date for traceability.
  • Wash hands frequently with food-grade sanitiser.
Never sell meat from a sick sheep. Animals that died of disease must be burned or buried. Disease can pass to humans.
The processing rule: A $100 sheep sells for $100 live, or $150-250 processed and graded. The extra $50-150 is profit for 2-3 hours of work.
Practical exercise 10: Visit a butchery that sells mutton. Ask their price per kg for whole carcass vs individual cuts. Calculate the extra income if you processed your own sheep.
