1. The Broiler Business

Broilers either make money fast or lose money fast. This module gives you the numbers you need before you spend a cent.

Lesson 1.2 - Cost of a 200-bird batch

ItemUSD
200 day-old chicks (0.60-0.90 each)120-180
Feed (200 x 7 kg x 0.55/kg)770-850
Vaccines and medication30-50
Litter and bedding20-40
Electricity / gas for brooding20-50
Labour (own time valued at 1/hr)40-60
Total1,000-1,230

Revenue: 200 birds x 2.1 kg x 3.50/kg dressed = 1,470. Net margin: 240-470 per batch. At 6 batches/year: 1,400-2,800/year from a single 3m x 4m structure.

The break-even rule: Your cost per kg live weight must be under 1.80. Anything above and you are working for free. Weigh your feed, weigh your birds, know your number.

Lesson 1.3 - Where broilers fail

  • Buying cheap chicks from unverified hatcheries. Weak chicks die in week 1 and the whole batch is compromised.
  • Overstocking. More than 12 birds per m2 causes stunted growth, disease, and mortality.
  • Poor brooding in week 1. Chilled chicks never recover their growth curve.
  • Running out of feed for even 4 hours. Broilers eat continuously. A gap stalls weight gain permanently.
  • No buyer lined up before week 6. Birds over 2.5 kg start losing value and every extra day costs more feed.
Practical exercise 1.1: Call 3 chick suppliers and 3 feed suppliers. Write the total cost of a 200-bird batch.
Practical exercise 1.2: Visit 5 local buyers. Ask the dressed chicken price per kg. Multiply by 420 kg. That is your expected revenue.
