1. Taking Good Photos of Clothes with a Phone

Good photos sell clothes fast. You don't need a big camerayour phone is enough. Start by finding natural light. Stand near a window or go outside on a cloudy day (harsh sun creates bad shadows). Never use flash; it makes colours look fake. Use a plain background: a white wall, a clean bedsheet, or even a large cardboard box painted white. Hang the clothes on a hanger or lay them flat. For shirts and dresses, use a hanger against a wall. For trousers and skirts, fold them neatly or hang them by the waist. Take at least three photos per item: front, back, and a close-up of any detail like buttons, tags, or patterns. This helps buyers trust you.

Before you snap, wipe your phone lens with a soft clothdust makes photos blurry. Hold the phone steady with both hands, or rest it on a table. Tap the screen where you want the camera to focus (usually the middle of the garment). If your phone has a grid option, turn it on to keep lines straight. Shoot from slightly above or straight onnot from below, which makes clothes look strange. For measurements, place a tape measure next to the item and take a photo so buyers see the size clearly. If the item has flaws (small stain, missing button), photograph it and mention it in your description. Honesty builds repeat customers.

After taking photos, edit them lightly. Use free apps like Snapseed or your phone's built-in editor. Adjust brightness and contrast so colours look true. Don't over-editbuyers will be disappointed when the real item looks different. Crop out distractions like your feet or clutter. Save photos in a folder named after the item (e.g., 'Blue Jeans Size 32'). When posting online, use the best three to five photos. Clear, well-lit photos make your okrika look more valuable. You can charge 20-30% more than blurry, dark photos. Practice with a few items and compareyou'll see the difference immediately.

2. Setting Up a WhatsApp Business Catalog

WhatsApp Business is a free app designed for small sellers. Download it from Google Play or App Store (it's separate from your personal WhatsApp). During setup, enter your shop name (e.g., 'Amina's Okrika'), choose a category like 'Clothing', and add a profile photouse your best-selling item or your logo. Write a short description: 'Quality second-hand clothes at affordable prices. Delivery available.' Then set your business hours and location. This makes you look professional.

Now create your catalog. Tap 'Catalog' then 'Add New Item'. For each product, upload up to 5 photos, write a title (e.g., 'Men's Formal Shirt - Size M'), set a price in your local currency (e.g., 2500 Naira, 1500 KES), and add a short description. Include condition (e.g., 'Gently used, no stains') and measurements (chest, length). Save. Your catalog can hold many itemsorganise them into collections like 'Men', 'Women', 'Kids', 'Shoes'. Buyers can browse your catalog without chatting, which saves you time.

Share your catalog link on your personal WhatsApp status, in Facebook groups, and with friends. When a customer clicks the link, they see all your items and can order directly. You can also add a 'Greeting Message' that auto-replies when someone messages you for the first time: 'Welcome to Amina's Okrika! Browse our catalog and send a screenshot of what you like. We deliver within 2 days.' Set a 'Quick Reply' for common questions like 'How much is delivery?' or 'Do you have this in size L?' This speeds up your responses.

Use labels to organise chats: 'New Order', 'Paid', 'Delivered'. This helps you track. WhatsApp Business also gives statistics: how many views your catalog gets, how many orders. Check weekly to see what's popular. Remember to update your catalog when items are soldremove them or mark as 'Out of Stock'. A clean, updated catalog builds trust and makes repeat sales easier.

3. Using Facebook Marketplace and Instagram to Sell

Facebook Marketplace and Instagram are powerful free tools to reach more buyers. Start with Facebook Marketplace. Open your Facebook app, tap 'Marketplace', then 'Sell'. Choose 'Item for Sale', upload your photos, write a title and description, set a price, and select a category (e.g., 'Women's Clothing'). Add your location so local buyers can find you. You can list up to 20 items per day for free. Use keywords in your title: 'Okrika Dress Size 12', 'Thrift Jeans Slim Fit'. In the description, include measurements, condition, and your WhatsApp number for faster communication. Respond to messages quicklyFacebook shows your response rate. If someone asks 'Is this available?', reply within an hour. After a sale, mark the item as 'Sold' to avoid wasting time.

For Instagram, create a business account (free). Go to Settings > Account > Switch to Professional Account > Business. Add a category like 'Clothing Store' and a contact button (WhatsApp or email). Post clear photos of your items with a simple background. Use hashtags: #okrika #thriftfashion #nairobi #lagos #secondhandclothes. Post at least once a day. Use Stories to show new arrivals, behind-the-scenes, or customer reviews. Add a 'Swipe Up' link to your WhatsApp catalog if you have over 10,000 followersotherwise put the link in your bio. Engage with followers: reply to comments, like their photos, and follow similar thrift sellers. You can also do live videos showing clothespeople love seeing items in real time.

Cross-promote: share your Instagram posts to Facebook Marketplace and vice versa. Join local buy-and-sell groups on Facebook and post your items (follow group rules). Use Instagram Reels to show a quick try-on or styling tipthese get more views. Always include a call to action: 'DM to order' or 'WhatsApp link in bio'. Track which platform brings more sales. Many African sellers find Facebook Marketplace better for local buyers and Instagram better for building a brand. Use both consistently for best results.

4. Handling Online Orders and Delivery

Once a customer orders, confirm the details: item, price, delivery address, and phone number. Repeat the order back to them in a message: 'You ordered the blue dress, size 12, for 3000 Naira. Delivery to Yaba, Lagos, costs 500 Naira. Total 3500 Naira. Please send payment to [your account].' This avoids mistakes. Ask for payment before delivery, unless you trust the customer. For new customers, full payment upfront is safest. For repeat buyers, you might accept half now, half on deliverybut be careful.

Choose a delivery method. In many African cities, you can use local bus parks (e.g., 'God is Good' in Lagos, 'Kampala Coach' in Uganda) to send parcels cheaply. You pack the item in a sealed bag, write the recipient's name and phone number, and give it to the bus conductor. The buyer pays the conductor on arrival. This costs 500-1500 Naira depending on distance. Alternatively, use delivery apps like Gokada, Sendy, or Bolt Send. For national delivery, use courier companies like DHL, GIG Logistics, or Speedaf. Compare pricesask for a quote based on weight and destination. Always get a tracking number and share it with the customer.

Pack neatly: fold clothes, wrap in a plastic bag (to protect from rain), then put in a brown paper or poly mailer. Add a small thank-you noteit makes customers happy. Take a photo of the packed parcel before sending, and send it to the customer as proof. After delivery, follow up: 'Did you receive your package? How do you like it?' Ask for a review or a photo wearing the item. Offer a small discount on their next order if they post a photo and tag you. This encourages repeat business. If a customer complains, apologise and solve the problem quicklyreplace or refund if necessary. A good reputation spreads fast.

5. Receiving Payments Digitally

Digital payments are safer and faster than cash. In Africa, common options include mobile money (M-Pesa, Airtel Money, MTN MoMo), bank transfers, and payment apps like Paystack, Flutterwave, or Opay. Set up at least two methods so customers can choose. For mobile money, register with your local provider. For bank transfers, share your account number and name. For apps, create a free account and share your payment link. Always confirm payment before releasing goods. Ask the customer to send a screenshot of the payment confirmation. Check your own account to verify the money has arriveddon't rely only on screenshots, as they can be faked.

When pricing, include delivery costs in the total. For example, if an item is 2000 Naira and delivery is 500 Naira, ask for 2500 Naira. This avoids confusion. If you accept cash on delivery, only do it for small amounts and trusted customers. For international sales, use PayPal, Wise, or Western Unionbut be aware of fees. Always calculate fees and add them to the price. Keep a record of every transaction: date, item, amount, customer name, and payment method. Use a notebook or a free app like Wave or Kippa. This helps you track profit and avoid disputes.

Security is key. Never share your PIN or password. Use a separate phone number for business if possible. Enable two-factor authentication on payment apps. Beware of scams: if a buyer sends you a link to 'receive payment' or asks for your bank details to 'send money', it's likely a scam. Legitimate payments don't require you to click links or share OTPs. If in doubt, ask the customer to use a different method. Also, set a clear refund policy: 'No refunds after delivery unless item is not as described.' State this in your catalog and on your WhatsApp profile. With digital payments, you can sell to customers far away and grow your business beyond your neighbourhood.

6. Managing Customer Inquiries Online

Online customers ask many questions. Respond quicklywithin 1-2 hours during business hours. Slow replies lose sales. Use WhatsApp Business 'Quick Replies' for common questions: 'Yes, it's available', 'Delivery costs 500 Naira', 'Size M fits chest 38-40 inches'. Save these as shortcuts. When a new inquiry comes, greet politely: 'Hello! Thank you for your interest. Which item are you asking about?' Then answer clearly. If they ask for more photos, send them immediately. If they ask for a discount, you can offer a small one (e.g., 'I can do 10% off if you pay today') but don't undervalue your items.

Keep track of inquiries. Use labels in WhatsApp Business: 'New Inquiry', 'Negotiating', 'Paid', 'Delivered'. For customers who don't buy, follow up after 24 hours: 'Hi, just checking if you're still interested in the dress. I have another buyer asking.' This creates urgency. If they say no, thank them and ask if they'd like to be notified of new stock. Add them to a broadcast list (not a group) for new arrivals. Send broadcasts no more than twice a week to avoid annoying people.

Handle complaints professionally. If a customer says the item is not as described, apologise and offer a solution: refund, exchange, or partial refund. Ask for a photo of the issue. Solve it quickly to avoid bad reviews. After resolution, ask if they are satisfied. Happy customers become repeat buyers and refer others. Also, save all customer contacts in your phone with a tag like 'Okrika Customer - Lagos'. This helps you send targeted messages. Finally, set boundaries: don't reply to messages after 9 PM. Use an auto-reply: 'Thank you for your message. I'll respond first thing tomorrow.' This protects your personal time while keeping customers informed. Good communication builds a loyal customer base.

<h2>Practical Reference: Online Selling and Digital Tools for Okrika Businesses</h2>

<p>Second-hand clothing known across Africa as <em>okrika</em>, <em>mitumba</em>, or <em>salaula</em> is a multi-billion-dollar trade. In Kenya alone, the mitumba sector supports over 2 million livelihoods and generates hundreds of billions of shillings annually, according to the Kenya Association of Manufacturers. For the thrift entrepreneur, the digital shift is not optional: it is the difference between selling 10 items a week from a stall and selling 100 items a week across a city, a country, or a diaspora market.</p>

<p>This module covers the six core digital competencies every okrika seller needs: photographing clothes with a phone, setting up a WhatsApp Business catalog, selling on Facebook Marketplace and Instagram, handling online orders and delivery, receiving payments digitally, and managing customer inquiries. Each lesson is practical, low-cost, and built for the African market reality limited data, mobile-first customers, and cash-on-delivery expectations.</p>

<h3>Lesson 1: Taking Good Photos of Clothes with a Phone</h3>
<p>Your photo is your shop window. On Facebook Marketplace and Instagram, buyers scroll past poor images in under a second. You do not need a professional camera a mid-range Android phone (Tecno, Infinix, Samsung A-series) is enough if you follow three rules:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Light:</strong> Shoot outdoors in the morning (710 AM) or late afternoon (46 PM). Never use flash it distorts colour and creates harsh shadows.</li>
<li><strong>Background:</strong> Hang the garment on a plain wall, a white bed sheet, or a clean door. Avoid cluttered floors and plastic bags.</li>
<li><strong>Angles:</strong> Take at least 4 photos front, back, label/tag, and any flaw (stain, tear, missing button). Honesty about flaws reduces returns and disputes.</li>
</ul>
<p>Use free editing apps like Snapseed or the built-in editor to adjust brightness and crop. Do not over-filter buyers want to see the real colour.</p>

<h3>Lesson 2: Setting Up a WhatsApp Business Catalog</h3>
<p>WhatsApp is the dominant commerce channel in Africa, with over 200 million users across the continent. WhatsApp Business (free on Android and iOS) lets you create a catalog of up to 500 items with photos, prices, and descriptions. Steps:</p>
<ol>
<li>Download WhatsApp Business and register with your business number.</li>
<li>Complete your profile: business name, category (Clothing & Apparel), address, hours, and a clear profile photo (your logo or a flat-lay of your best stock).</li>
<li>Add a catalog item: photo, name, price, description, and a link if you have one.</li>
<li>Set up greeting and away messages so customers get an instant reply even when you are asleep.</li>
<li>Use labels (New Order, Paid, Shipped, Delivered) to track customers.</li>
</ol>

<h3>Lesson 3: Using Facebook Marketplace and Instagram to Sell</h3>
<p>Facebook Marketplace is free and reaches buyers in your city. Instagram is better for building a brand and reaching diaspora customers. Key practices:</p>
<ul>
<li>List on Marketplace with a clear title: "Ladies Zara Blazer Size M KSh 1,200 Nairobi CBD".</li>
<li>On Instagram, post 35 photos per item as a carousel, use 510 relevant hashtags (#NairobiThrift #OkrikaKenya #MitumbaFashion), and add a price in the caption.</li>
<li>Cross-post: share your Instagram post to your Facebook page and Marketplace listing.</li>
<li>Respond within 1 hour during peak times (evenings and weekends).</li>
</ul>

<h3>Lesson 4: Handling Online Orders and Delivery</h3>
<p>Delivery is where most okrika businesses lose money or reputation. Use a simple order form (Google Forms is free) or a WhatsApp order template. Confirm: item, size, price, delivery fee, address, and preferred payment method. Partner with boda-boda riders for same-day city delivery (KSh 150300 in Nairobi) and couriers like Sendy, Pick-up Mtaani, or G4S for upcountry. Always send a tracking message when the item ships.</p>

<h3>Lesson 5: Receiving Payments Digitally</h3>
<p>Digital payment is now standard. In Kenya, M-Pesa handles over 90% of mobile money transactions; in Ghana, MTN MoMo; in Nigeria, Opay and PalmPay; in South Africa, SnapScan and Yoco. Accept at least two methods: mobile money and bank transfer. For high-value items, request a 50% deposit before shipping. Always send a receipt via WhatsApp after payment.</p>

<h3>Lesson 6: Managing Customer Inquiries Online</h3>
<p>Speed and clarity win sales. Create a saved reply template for the five most common questions: price, size, availability, delivery fee, and payment method. Use WhatsApp Business quick replies to answer in one tap. Never argue publicly move disputes to DM or a phone call. Ask every satisfied buyer for a photo review; social proof drives repeat sales.</p>

<strong style="color:#c62828;"> SAFETY WARNING Online Fraud and Payment Scams</strong>
<p style="margin:8px 0 0 0; color:#333;">Never release goods before payment is confirmed in your account. Common scams include: fake M-Pesa SMS screenshots, "reverse payment" tricks, buyers who send extra money and ask for a refund, and fake couriers who collect goods and disappear. Always verify payment by checking your own M-Pesa statement or app not a screenshot. For high-value orders, meet in a public place (mall, police station, or busy market) or use a trusted courier with cash-on-delivery. Never share your PIN, OTP, or WhatsApp verification code with anyone, even if they claim to be from Safaricom or your bank.</p>
