1. Nutritional Requirements by Age

Turkeys need different amounts of protein, energy, vitamins, and minerals as they grow. In Africa, many farmers raise local or hybrid turkeys, but the same feeding rules apply. From day-old to 4 weeks (starter phase), feed a crumble with 28% protein. This supports rapid muscle and bone growth. You can buy commercial starter or mix your own: 50% maize, 30% soybean meal, 10% fishmeal, 5% sunflower cake, plus 5% vitamin-mineral premix. From 5 to 8 weeks (grower phase), reduce protein to 24%. Use 55% maize, 25% soybean meal, 8% fishmeal, 7% sunflower cake, 5% premix. From 9 to 14 weeks (finisher phase), protein drops to 20%. Mix 60% maize, 20% soybean meal, 5% fishmeal, 10% sunflower cake, 5% premix. After 15 weeks (adult or breeder), feed 16-18% protein. A simple mix: 65% maize, 15% soybean meal, 5% fishmeal, 10% sunflower cake, 5% premix. Always provide clean water. Energy comes mainly from maize, but you can replace some with cassava chips or sorghum if maize is expensive. However, cassava must be dried and ground; fresh cassava is poisonous. For vitamins, add dark green leaves like amaranth or moringa, chopped fine. Minerals: provide oyster shell or limestone grit free-choice for calcium. In hot African climates, turkeys eat less, so increase protein and energy density by 5-10% during heat stress. Weigh your turkeys weekly using a hanging scale. A 4-week-old should weigh about 0.8-1 kg; at 8 weeks, 3-4 kg; at 12 weeks, 6-7 kg; at 16 weeks, 9-12 kg. Adjust feed if weights lag. Never use mouldy feed â it kills turkeys. Store feed in dry, raised containers. This age-based plan gives you healthy, fast-growing birds.

2. Feed Ingredients and Formulation

To make your own turkey feed in Africa, you need energy sources, protein sources, minerals, and vitamins. Common energy feeds: maize (best), sorghum, millet, cassava chips (dried), and sweet potato chips. Maize has about 9% protein and high energy. Protein feeds: soybean meal (44-48% protein), fishmeal (55-65%), groundnut cake (40-45%), sunflower cake (30-35%), and cottonseed cake (but use limited â gossypol is toxic). Animal protein like fishmeal is costly but vital for young turkeys. You can also use termites, maggots, or earthworms as protein, but dry and grind them. Minerals: limestone, oyster shell, bone meal, and salt. Vitamins: dark green leaves (moringa, amaranth, sweet potato leaves), plus commercial premix. A simple formulation method is the Pearson Square. For example, to make a 24% protein grower mash using maize (9% protein) and soybean meal (44% protein): Draw a square. Put 24 in centre. Left corners: 9 and 44. Subtract diagonally: 44-24=20 parts maize; 24-9=15 parts soybean meal. So 20 parts maize + 15 parts soybean meal = 35 parts total. To make 100 kg, use (20/35)*100 = 57 kg maize, (15/35)*100 = 43 kg soybean meal. Then add 5% premix (5 kg) and 2% limestone (2 kg), reducing maize to 52 kg. Mix thoroughly on a clean concrete floor or tarpaulin. Never mix on bare ground. Formulate fresh each week. For finisher, use 20% protein: with maize (9%) and soybean (44%): 44-20=24 parts maize; 20-9=11 parts soybean. Total 35. For 100 kg: (24/35)*100=68.5 kg maize, (11/35)*100=31.5 kg soybean. Add 5 kg premix and 2 kg limestone. Adjust maize to 61.5 kg. Always test ingredients for mould. Store in airtight bags. This method saves money and uses local crops.

3. Feeding Programs for Different Stages

A feeding program tells you what, how much, and when to feed your turkeys from day-old to market. In Africa, follow this schedule. Week 1: Feed starter crumble (28% protein) free-choice. Use shallow trays or chick feeders. Provide 24-hour light for first 3 days, then 14 hours light. Each poult eats about 15-20 g per day. Week 2-4: Continue starter, but increase feeder space. By 4 weeks, each turkey eats 60-80 g per day. Weigh weekly. Week 5-8: Switch to grower (24% protein). Feed twice daily â morning and evening. Each bird eats 120-180 g per day. Provide grit. Week 9-14: Finisher (20% protein). Feed 200-300 g per day. At 12 weeks, males weigh 6-7 kg, females 4-5 kg. Week 15-18: If selling as whole birds, continue finisher. For breeders, switch to breeder feed (16-18% protein) with extra calcium. Feed 250-350 g per day. Always provide clean water â turkeys drink 2-3 times their feed weight. In hot weather, feed early morning and late evening when cool. Use hanging feeders to reduce waste. A simple rule: a turkey eats about 0.5 kg feed per kg body weight per week? No â actually, feed conversion is 2.5-3 kg feed per kg gain. So a 10 kg turkey eats 25-30 kg feed total. Keep records: date, feed type, amount, number of birds. Adjust if birds look thin or fat. Never feed mouldy or wet feed. For free-range systems, supplement with kitchen scraps, greens, and insects, but still give commercial feed for balanced nutrition. This program ensures steady growth and good profits.

4. Feeders and Waterers

Choosing the right feeders and waterers saves feed, keeps turkeys healthy, and reduces labour. For young poults (0-4 weeks), use shallow trays or egg flats for feed and shallow water troughs with marbles or stones to prevent drowning. You can make a simple feeder from a clean plastic bottle: cut a hole in the side, fill with feed, and hang it. For 50 poults, provide 5-6 trays. From 5 weeks onward, use hanging tube feeders or trough feeders. A good hanging feeder has a pan at the bottom and a tube on top. Hang it so the bottom of the pan is at the level of the turkey's back. This reduces spillage. For 100 turkeys, provide 4-5 tube feeders (each 10-15 kg capacity). For water, use plastic drinkers or automatic nipple drinkers. Nipple drinkers are clean and save water, but cost more. A simple drinker: a 20-litre bucket with small holes in the lid, inverted on a tray. For 100 birds, provide 3-4 drinkers. Always have water available â turkeys die quickly without it. Clean feeders and waterers daily with soap and water. In hot climates, place waterers in shade. Use feeders that prevent birds from standing in feed â turkeys scratch and waste. For free-range, use movable feeders and waterers. Never use rusty metal containers â rust is toxic. Check for sharp edges. Raise feeders on blocks as birds grow. A good rule: 1 metre of feeding space per 10 adult turkeys; 1 metre of water space per 15 turkeys. Adjust as they grow. Keep records of feed consumed per day to spot problems. Well-designed feeders and waterers pay for themselves.

5. Feed Storage and Quality

Storing feed properly prevents mould, pests, and nutrient loss â critical in Africa's heat and humidity. First, buy or mix only what you can use in 2-3 weeks. Mouldy feed contains aflatoxins that damage the liver and kill turkeys. Store feed in clean, dry, airtight containers: plastic drums with lids, metal bins, or heavy-duty sacks on pallets. Never store on the floor â moisture rises. Keep the store cool, dark, and well-ventilated. Ideal temperature below 25Â°C and humidity below 70%. If you live in a humid area, add a desiccant like quicklime in a cloth bag. Check feed weekly for clumping, off-smell, or discoloration. Good feed smells fresh, like maize or soy. If you see green or black mould, throw it away â do not feed it. To prevent pests, keep the store clean, seal cracks, and use traps or cats. Avoid chemical pesticides near feed. For homemade mixes, cool ingredients completely before bagging. Never mix hot feed â condensation causes mould. Use feed within 4 weeks in hot weather, 8 weeks in cool. Label bags with date and feed type. Rotate stock â first in, first out. If you buy commercial feed, check the expiry date and bag integrity. Store away from chemicals like petrol or kerosene â turkeys absorb odours. For small quantities, keep in sealed plastic buckets with lids. A simple test: squeeze a handful â if it clumps and stays, it's too wet. Dry it in the sun on a clean tarp, but not for too long (vitamins degrade). Always provide fresh feed. Stale feed reduces growth and egg production. Good storage saves money and lives.

6. Supplements and Additives

Supplements and additives boost growth, health, and egg production when your base feed is lacking. In Africa, common supplements include vitamins, minerals, amino acids, and natural products. Start with a commercial vitamin-mineral premix â add 0.25-0.5% to your feed. For example, in 100 kg feed, add 250-500 g premix. This provides vitamins A, D3, E, K, B-complex, and trace minerals like zinc, iron, copper, manganese. If premix is unavailable, use alternatives: crushed oyster shell or limestone for calcium (2-3% of feed for layers). For vitamins, dry and crush dark green leaves (moringa, amaranth, sweet potato) â add 5-10% of feed. For protein, add 2-5% fishmeal or 1-2% lysine and methionine (if you can buy them). Probiotics: mix 1 tablespoon of plain yoghurt per litre of drinking water for 3 days after stress. Prebiotics: add 1% dried brewer's yeast. For gut health, add 0.5% charcoal powder â it absorbs toxins. For respiratory health, add crushed garlic (1 clove per 10 birds) or ginger to feed. For heat stress, add vitamin C (100 mg per litre water) and electrolytes. For eggshell quality, add 3% oyster shell free-choice. Antibiotics: only use under veterinary advice â misuse creates resistance. Never use human antibiotics. Growth promoters like paylean are banned in many countries. Natural growth promoters: 1% turmeric powder, 0.5% black pepper. Always mix additives thoroughly â small amounts can be toxic if uneven. Use a clean bucket and stir for 5 minutes. Store additives in sealed containers away from light. Check expiry dates. Introduce new supplements gradually over 3 days. Keep records of what you add and when. If birds show signs of toxicity (drooping, diarrhea), stop immediately and give fresh water. Supplements are not a substitute for good feed and clean water.

Practical Reference: Turkey Nutrition and Feeding

This lesson is a working reference for African turkey producers who need to make fast, profitable decisions on feed. Feed represents 65â75% of total turkey production cost in most African markets, so every gram of feed must convert into saleable body weight. Whether you are raising 100 poults in a backyard pen in Kumasi or 5,000 birds in a semi-commercial house near Nairobi, the principles below apply. Keep this page printed and posted in your feed store.

Turkish and European feed tables (NRC, Aviagen, BUT) provide the scientific baseline, but African producers must adapt to locally available ingredients: maize, soybean meal, sunflower cake, fishmeal, cassava, groundnut cake, and cottonseed meal. The goal is simple: meet the bird's nutrient needs at the lowest cost per kilogram of live weight gained.

Feeding Programmes for Different Stages

Starter (0â4 weeks): Feed crumbles or small pellets. Poults have small beaks and need easy-to-pick feed. Use a high-protein starter (28% CP) with 11.7 MJ/kg ME. In Africa, where soybean meal is costly, replace up to 15% with high-quality fishmeal and groundnut cake, but supplement lysine and methionine. Provide feed in shallow trays or chick feeders for the first 10 days.

Grower (5â12 weeks): Switch to mash or 3mm pellets. Protein drops to 22â24% CP. This is the phase of rapid frame development. Do not restrict feed unless you are raising breeder stock. In free-range or semi-intensive systems common in West Africa, supplement with kitchen waste, green forage (amaranth, sweet potato vines), and termites, but keep commercial feed as the base.

Finisher (13â20 weeks): Protein drops to 18â20% CP, energy rises to 12.6 MJ/kg. The goal is efficient weight gain and fat deposition. In hot African climates, add 0.5% sodium bicarbonate to the water to reduce heat stress. Withdraw feed 8â12 hours before slaughter to reduce gut content and improve dressing percentage.

Breeder (20+ weeks): Feed restricted to prevent obesity. Use a breeder ration with 16â18% CP and 2.5â3% calcium. Provide oyster shell free-choice. In Africa, breeder turkeys often suffer from vitamin E and selenium deficiency; supplement if fertility drops.

Feeders and Waterers

Poor feeder design wastes 5â15% of feed â a direct profit loss. Use linear feeders or round hanging feeders. Allow 5â7 cm of feeder space per bird for growing turkeys and 10â12 cm for adults. Adjust feeder height so the lip is level with the bird's back to reduce spillage. For waterers, use bell drinkers or nipple lines. Turkeys need 1.5â2 cm of water space per bird. Clean waterers daily â biofilm harbours Salmonella and E. coli.

â  SAFETY WARNING â AFLATOXIN AND MOULDY FEED

Never feed mouldy maize, groundnut cake, or any grain with visible green, black, or yellow mould. Aflatoxin B1 causes liver damage, immune suppression, and mortality in turkeys at levels as low as 20 ppb. In African storage conditions (high humidity, poor ventilation), aflatoxin develops within 48 hours of moisture exposure. Always:

  • Test feed ingredients with a simple aflatoxin test kit before mixing.
  • Store feed in dry, raised, ventilated rooms â never on bare concrete or earth floors.
  • Use feed within 4 weeks of mixing; discard any feed that smells musty or is clumped.
  • Add a commercial toxin binder (e.g., hydrated sodium calcium aluminosilicate) at 1â2 kg/tonne if risk is high.

Feed Storage and Quality

In most of sub-Saharan Africa, heat and humidity destroy feed quality faster than anywhere else. Follow these rules:

  • Moisture: Keep feed below 12% moisture. Store in woven polypropylene bags on wooden pallets 10 cm off the floor and 30 cm from walls.
  • Temperature: Store in the coolest part of the farm. Above 30Â°C, vitamin potency drops by 10â20% per month.
  • Pests: Use airtight containers or add food-grade diatomaceous earth. Rats and weevils consume 2â5% of stored feed.
  • Rotation: First in, first out. Never mix old feed with new.
  • Quality check: Smell, touch, and visually inspect every batch. Good feed smells fresh, flows freely, and has uniform particle size.

Supplements and Additives

Supplements are not a substitute for good feed, but they protect margins when ingredient quality is variable.

  • Amino acids: Lysine and methionine are the first limiting in African maizeâgroundnut rations. Add 0.1â0.2% L-lysine and 0.05â0.1% DL-methionine.
  • Vitamins: A, D3, E, and B-complex are critical. Use a commercial poultry vitamin premix at 0.25â0.5%.
  • Minerals: Calcium and available phosphorus. Use limestone and dicalcium phosphate. Do not rely on bone meal unless sterilised.
  • Probiotics and prebiotics: Improve gut health and reduce antibiotic use. Common in commercial African farms.
  • Coccidiostats: Essential in deep litter systems. Rotate products to prevent resistance.
  • Toxin binders: Use when maize or groundnut cake quality is uncertain.

Quick Reference Checklist

  1. Weigh feed weekly. Track feed conversion ratio (FCR). Target: 2.5â3.0 kg feed per kg gain.
  2. Match feed to age. Never feed starter to finishers â it wastes protein and money.
  3. Provide clean water at all times. Turkeys drink 1.5â2Ã more than chickens. Check waterers twice daily.
  4. Adjust feeder height weekly. Lip at bird's back level. Reduce spillage to below 3%.
  5. Test for aflatoxin. Reject any mouldy ingredient. Use a binder if risk is high.
  6. Store feed properly. Dry, raised, ventilated, pest-free. Use within 4 weeks.
  7. Supplement amino acids. Lysine and methionine are almost always deficient in local rations.
  8. Record everything. Feed in, weight out, mortality, and feed cost per bird. Data drives profit.
