1. Managing Correspondence and Email Overload

As a personal assistant in Africa, you will handle email for busy executives who receive 100+ messages daily. Start by setting up filters in Gmail or Outlook. For example, create a label 'Urgent' for emails from the boss's spouse or key clients, and auto-forward invoices to a 'Finance' folder. Use tools like Boomerang or FollowUpThen to schedule remindersâfree versions work. A practical rule: check email three times a day (9am, 1pm, 4pm) instead of constantly. In your first week, ask your client which senders are VIPs. Draft template replies for common requests: meeting confirmations, 'I'll get back to you,' and payment acknowledgements. Save these in a Google Doc or Notion page. For WhatsApp, which is huge in Africa, use the 'Archive' and 'Pin' features. If your client gets 50 WhatsApp messages a day, you can respond to simple ones like 'What's the address?' with a saved location pin. Always confirm receipt within 2 hours during work hours. For physical mail, scan important documents using a phone app like CamScanner and email them to the client. Keep a log of all correspondence in a simple spreadsheet: Date, Sender, Subject, Action Taken, Status. This prevents missed follow-ups. If you're in Nigeria or Kenya, expect many emails about invoices and mobile money payments (M-Pesa, Flutterwave). Learn to spot phishingânever click links from unknown senders. Finally, set an out-of-office reply when your client travels. A good assistant reduces email volume by 40% within a month by unsubscribing from newsletters and delegating routine replies.

2. Scheduling Appointments and Making Reservations

Scheduling is the backbone of a PA's job. Use Google Calendar or Calendly to avoid double-booking. For a client in Lagos, you might manage 15 appointments a week. Always confirm time zonesâAfrica has multiple (WAT, EAT, SAST). When booking a doctor's appointment at a private hospital like Aga Khan in Nairobi, call ahead and ask for the doctor's secretary's direct line. For restaurant reservations, use apps like OpenTable or call directly. In Accra, for a business lunch at Santoku, book 3 days ahead. For travel reservations, use local platforms: Travelstart for flights, Booking.com for hotels. Always get a confirmation number and save it in a shared Google Sheet with columns: Date, Time, Location, Contact, Confirmation #. Send a reminder to your client 24 hours before via WhatsApp and email. For recurring meetings, set up a Zoom link and test it 30 minutes prior. If a client is running late, call the other party to rescheduleâdon't just text. For personal appointments like salon visits, use Fresha. Keep a buffer of 30 minutes between meetings. If you're in South Africa, use SnapScan for parking payments. Always ask: 'What is the cancellation policy?' For flights, note that Ethiopian Airlines and Kenya Airways have different baggage rules. Use TripIt to organize itineraries. Finally, keep a 'waitlist' for popular restaurantsâcall at 5pm for last-minute cancellations. A good scheduler saves a client 5 hours a week.

3. Bill Paying and Basic Bookkeeping for Clients

Handling bills requires accuracy. Start by listing all recurring bills: rent, electricity (e.g., prepaid meters in Nigeria), water, internet (MTN, Safaricom), DSTV, security, and school fees. Use a simple spreadsheet with columns: Bill, Amount, Due Date, Paid Date, Method, Receipt #. For mobile money, use M-Pesa, Airtel Money, or MoMo. Pay bills 3 days early to avoid disconnection. For electricity, buy tokens via apps like Quickteller or Kuda. Keep all receiptsâscan them into a folder like 'Client X - 2025 Bills'. For bookkeeping, use Wave or Zoho Books (free versions). Record every expense: date, description, amount, category. If your client gives you a petty cash of 50,000 Naira, track every naira. Reconcile weekly: compare your records with bank statements. For invoices, use tools like Invoice2go. If a client owes a vendor, follow up politely: 'Just checking on payment for Invoice #123.' For tax, in Kenya, KRA has iTax; in Nigeria, FIRS. You don't need to file, but keep records for 5 years. For personal bills like school fees, pay via bank transfer and get a stamped receipt. Never mix client money with your own. Use a separate wallet or account. If you're paying cash, get a signed receipt with the vendor's name and phone number. At month-end, produce a one-page summary: total spent, categories, and any unpaid bills. This builds trust. A good bookkeeper saves a client 10% on late fees.

4. Travel Planning and Itinerary Management

Travel planning for an African executive means handling visas, flights, and ground transport. Start 4 weeks ahead for international trips. For a trip from Johannesburg to London, check visa requirements (UK visa takes 3 weeks). Use Google Flights to compare prices, but book via the airline directly or a reputable agent like Flight Centre. For intra-Africa flights, use Kenya Airways, Ethiopian, or RwandAir. Always book refundable fares if possible. Create a detailed itinerary in Google Docs with: flight numbers, times, hotel addresses, and emergency contacts. Share it with the client and their family. For airport transfers, pre-book Uber or Bolt, or use local services like Little Cab in Kenya. In Lagos, use LagRide. For hotels, choose ones with free cancellation and good security. In Nairobi, the Sarova Stanley is popular. Pack a travel kit for your client: adapters, SIM cards (Airtel or MTN), and local currency (e.g., 200 USD worth). For visas, use VFS Global or TLScontact. Keep digital and physical copies of passports, yellow fever cards, and travel insurance. For long trips, schedule rest days. Use TripCase or TripIt to send alerts. If a flight is delayed, rebook immediately via the airline app. For ground transport in rural areas, hire a trusted driverâask other PAs for referrals. Always have a backup plan: if a flight is cancelled, know the next available. After the trip, file all receipts for expense reports. A good travel PA saves 20% on travel costs by booking early and using loyalty points.

5. Event Planning and Coordination

Event planning for a PA can range from a 10-person dinner to a 200-person conference. Start with a budget. For a corporate event in Nairobi, expect to spend 2,000 KES per person for a sit-down lunch. Use a checklist: venue, catering, AV, invitations, decorations, and transport. For venues, check hotels like Radisson Blu or local gardens. Always visit the venue and take photos. For catering, get quotes from 3 vendors. In Lagos, use Eventful or Eat.Drink.Lagos for referrals. For invitations, use WhatsApp groups and Evite. Send reminders 1 week and 1 day before. For AV, hire a technicianâtest microphones and projectors 2 hours before. For decorations, use local florists and fabric markets (e.g., Balogun Market in Lagos). For a 50-person event, you need at least 2 helpers. Assign roles: registration, ushering, and food service. Keep a master timeline: setup 3 hours before, guest arrival, speeches, meal, and cleanup. For payment, use mobile money or bank transferânever cash without a receipt. After the event, send thank-you notes within 48 hours. Collect feedback via Google Forms. For virtual events, use Zoom or Google Meet and do a dry run. Always have a contingency: extra chairs, umbrellas, and a first-aid kit. A good event PA can handle 3 events a month and still keep the client calm.

6. Gift Shopping and Personal Shopping Services

Gift shopping requires knowing the recipient's tastes. For a client's spouse's birthday, ask about favorite colors, scents, and sizes. In Africa, thoughtful gifts include: a bespoke Ankara outfit (from a tailor in Accra or Lagos), a spa voucher (e.g., at Serena Hotel), or a gourmet hamper from local delis. Budget: 20,000-50,000 Naira for a nice gift. Use online stores like Jumia, Konga, or Takealot. For personal shopping, get the client's measurements and preferred brands. For a business wardrobe, shop at stores like Zara or local boutiques. Always keep receipts and ask about return policies. For grocery shopping, use apps like Glovo or Mr D. For a weekly grocery run, expect to spend 30,000 KES in Nairobi. For gifts, wrap them nicelyâuse local artisans for custom wrapping. If you're shipping, use DHL or local couriers like G4S. For last-minute gifts, consider digital options: Spotify gift cards, Netflix, or mobile data bundles (e.g., 10GB from MTN). For corporate gifts, order branded items like notebooks and pens from local printers. Keep a gift log: recipient, occasion, gift, cost, date. This prevents duplicates. For personal shopping, always confirm the budget and style before buying. Send photos for approval before purchase. If you're in South Africa, use Mr Price or Woolworths. For luxury items, use boutiques in Sandton. A good personal shopper saves a client 5 hours a week and avoids gift disasters.

Practical Reference: Personal Assistance Tasks and Administrative Support

In African markets, a personal assistant (PA) or errand professional is often the operational backbone of a busy executive, entrepreneur, or diaspora client. The role blends discretion, speed, and financial accuracy. This module gives you the practical systems to manage correspondence, schedule appointments, pay bills, plan travel, coordinate events, and handle personal shoppingâwith Africa-specific tools and safeguards.

Managing Correspondence and Email Overload

African clients often operate across email, WhatsApp, SMS, and phone calls. Your job is to create a single source of truth. Use a shared inbox or label system (Gmail labels: Action, Waiting, Archive, Client Name). Set a triage rule: respond within 2 hours to urgent items, same day to routine items, and flag anything requiring the client's personal decision. Draft template replies for common requestsâmeeting confirmations, payment reminders, and travel queriesâso you are not rewriting from scratch. For WhatsApp, use starred messages and labels; for email, use filters and canned responses. Never delete a client instruction without archiving it. In low-bandwidth areas, use offline mode in Gmail and download attachments before travelling.

Scheduling Appointments and Making Reservations

Time zones matter: Lagos is WAT (UTC+1), Nairobi is EAT (UTC+3), Johannesburg is SAST (UTC+2), and Cairo is EET (UTC+2). Always confirm the client's time zone before booking. Use Calendly, Google Calendar appointment slots, or a shared calendar with colour codes: green for confirmed, yellow for pending, red for conflicts. For reservationsârestaurants, hotels, flightsâbook directly where possible and request written confirmation via email or SMS. In Africa, popular booking platforms include Jumia Travel, Booking.com, and local hotel WhatsApp numbers. Always capture: date, time, location, contact person, confirmation number, and cancellation policy. Send the client a calendar invite plus a WhatsApp summary 24 hours before.

Bill Paying and Basic Bookkeeping for Clients

This is where trust is won or lost. Use a simple ledger (Google Sheets or Excel) with columns: Date, Payee, Amount, Currency, Method, Reference, Receipt Attached, Category. In Africa, payments may be made via M-Pesa, MoMo, Paystack, Flutterwave, bank transfer, or cash. For each payment, obtain a receipt or transaction message and save it in a shared folder named by month. Never use personal funds without written approval. Reconcile weekly. If the client has multiple currencies (e.g., USD, NGN, KES), record the exchange rate used and the source. For basic bookkeeping, track income and expenses, categorise by type (travel, office, personal, utilities), and produce a monthly summary. Use tools like Wave (free accounting) or Zoho Books if the client needs more structure.

Travel Planning and Itinerary Management

An African PA must plan for visa requirements, yellow fever certificates, and airline baggage rules that vary by carrier. Build a master itinerary in Google Docs or Notion with sections: flights, accommodation, ground transport, meetings, emergency contacts, and documents. Include airline PNR, hotel confirmation, driver name and number, and local embassy contact. For multi-country trips, check visa-on-arrival eligibility and transit rules. Book airport transfers in advanceâUber and Bolt operate in major cities, but in smaller towns arrange a trusted local driver. Always share the itinerary with the client and a secondary emergency contact. Print two copies: one for the client, one for you.

Event Planning and Coordination

Whether it is a board meeting, a product launch, or a family celebration, use a checklist-based approach. Start with: objective, date, venue, budget, guest list, vendors, and run-of-show. In Africa, venue booking often requires a deposit via mobile money or bank transfer; get a written contract. Confirm catering, AV, and security separately. Create a vendor contact sheet and a minute-by-minute run sheet. Assign a point person for each task. On the day, arrive early, test Wi-Fi and power backup, and keep a printed guest list. After the event, send thank-you notes and collect receipts for reconciliation.

Gift Shopping and Personal Shopping Services

Personal shopping requires knowing the client's preferences, sizes, and budget. Keep a client profile note: favourite brands, sizes, allergies, and gift occasions. For gifts, consider local artisan markets (e.g., Maasai Market in Nairobi, Makola Market in Accra, Rosebank Africa Craft in Johannesburg) for unique items. For online shopping, use Jumia, Konga, Takealot, or Amazon with a local address. Always send a photo of the item before purchase if the client is not present. Keep receipts for returns. For corporate gifts, order in bulk early and confirm delivery dates.
