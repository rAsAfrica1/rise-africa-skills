Sesame Processing

8 lessons · 30 quiz questions · Nuts & Seeds

1 Sesame varieties

Sesame (Sesamum indicum) is one of the oldest oilseed crops, grown in Africa and Asia for thousands of years.

White sesame: Most common. Light colour, mild flavour. Best for tahini, oil and bakery.

Black sesame: Stronger flavour, higher antioxidants. Best for Asian cuisine and premium markets.

Brown sesame: Unhulled. Nutty flavour. Best for oil extraction.

For export and premium markets, choose white hulled sesame. For local oil production, brown works fine.

2 Cleaning and dehulling

Sesame seeds are tiny and easily contaminated with sand and stones.

  1. Screen to remove stones and larger debris
  2. Wash in water to remove dust and light chaff
  3. Salt-water flotation separates heavy stones
  4. Dry seeds to 6–8% moisture
  5. Dehull: soak in water 4–6 hours, then rub gently to remove skins
  6. Dry again

Hulled white sesame commands premium prices (2–3× unhulled).

3 Sesame oil extraction

Sesame oil is prized for its stability (long shelf life) and nutty flavour.

Cold press: Premium oil. 40–45% yield. Light colour.

Expeller press: Standard oil. 45–50% yield. Slightly darker.

Traditional (ghani): Stone press. Slow, produces highest-quality oil (especially in India).

  1. Clean and dehull seeds
  2. Roast lightly for flavour (optional)
  3. Press in oil expeller
  4. Filter through cloth
  5. Bottle in dark glass or PET

Yield: 100 kg seeds → 40–50 kg oil + 50 kg cake.

Retail: $8–$20 per litre (sesame oil is premium-priced).

4 Tahini production

Tahini is sesame paste — used in hummus, baba ganoush and Middle Eastern cuisine.

  1. Soak hulled white sesame 4 hours
  2. Drain and dry
  3. Roast at 150°C for 10–15 minutes (light golden)
  4. Cool completely
  5. Grind in a stone mill or colloid mill until smooth and runny
  6. Add salt (1%)
  7. Fill jars at 65°C, seal

Yield: 100 kg hulled seeds → 95 kg tahini.

Retail: $10–$20 per 500 g jar. Premium organic: $15–$30.

5 Roasted sesame seeds

Roasted sesame is used in bakery, sushi, Asian cuisine and snacking.

  1. Clean seeds
  2. Roast at 150°C for 5–10 minutes, stirring constantly (they burn fast)
  3. Cool completely
  4. Pack in foil pouches or jars

Yield: 100 kg raw → 95 kg roasted.

Retail: $5–$10 per kg. Premium hulled: $8–$15 per kg.

6 Halva and sweets

Halva is a sesame-based sweet popular in the Middle East, Eastern Europe and South Asia.

Basic halva recipe:

  • 1 kg tahini
  • 600 g sugar
  • 200 ml water
  • Boil sugar and water to 140°C (soft crack stage)
  • Mix into tahini slowly
  • Add vanilla, pistachios, or chocolate
  • Pour into moulds, cool, cut

Retail: $15–$30 per kg. High margin, simple process.

7 Sesame flour

Sesame flour is used in gluten-free baking and as a nutrient supplement.

  1. Roast and cool seeds
  2. Grind in a hammer mill
  3. Sieve through 250 micron
  4. Pack in dark airtight bags (oils go rancid)

Yield: 100 kg seeds → 90–95 kg flour.

Retail: $6–$12 per kg.

8 Export and grading

Sesame is a top-10 agricultural export commodity for many African countries (Sudan, Ethiopia, Nigeria, Tanzania).

Export grades:

  • Grade A (99% purity, 99% hulled white)
  • Grade B (98% purity)
  • Grade C (mix of colours, unhulled)

Export markets: Japan, China, EU, Middle East. Prices (2026): Grade A hulled white $1,500–$2,500 per tonne FOB. Organic premium +30–50%.

Compliance: Phytosanitary certificate, pesticide residue test, aflatoxin test, moisture under 6%.

💰 Tahini and halva are the highest-margin sesame products. Bulk seed export is high volume, low margin, but Africa's biggest sesame business.
