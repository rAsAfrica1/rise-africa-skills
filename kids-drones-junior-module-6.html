Kids Drones — Junior

Module 6 of 8: Mapping Your Area — Draw What You Cannot See

Shared phone

🧠 Brain Opener — 60 seconds

Before anything else

Stand on a chair. Look down at your room. What do you see that you cannot see from floor level? Write one sentence: what is different from above?

📚 Lessons

1. A drone sees what a person cannot

From 30 metres up, a football field looks like a green rectangle. A farm looks like a patchwork. A road looks like a line. Photos from that height can be stitched together into a map — and a map is useful because it shows everything at once, in proportion. Farmers use drone maps to spot dry patches. Builders use them to measure land. Rescue teams use them to find lost people.

2. The 3-part drill

Round 1: draw your school from memory, top-down. No labels yet. Round 2: walk the school and count the buildings. Add them to your map. Round 3: think about where a drone photo would show something you missed — a blocked path, a low roof, a hidden garden.

3. Where the idea breaks

A map is not the truth — it is a picture with choices. What did the mapmaker include? What did they leave out? Who drew the lines, and why there? Ask these questions every time you see a map. It is a habit that will serve you in maths, geography, and history.

🎯 Cross-Skill Links

♟️
Chess

A knight sees squares a pawn cannot. A bishop sees diagonals a rook cannot. A drone sees what a person on the ground cannot. Every vantage point reveals and hides something.

🎹
Music & Piano

A musician reading a score sees the whole piece — beginning, middle, end — in one glance. A drone map is a score of the land. Both are views from outside time.

🀄
Chinese-Style Drill

地图 (dìtú) = map. 地 (ground) + 图 (picture) = a picture of the ground.

🤖 Real-Time AI-Era Skill

Take a photo of a place you know well from an unusual angle — from a chair, from a window, from a mirror. Ask Claude: "What is the same and what is different between this view and the usual view?" Then think about what a drone photo would add.

🎮 Game

Do it now

Map from memory. Each person draws their home from above on paper. Then walk home and check. Score: number of rooms you got right, out of the real number. Winner has the most accurate memory map.
