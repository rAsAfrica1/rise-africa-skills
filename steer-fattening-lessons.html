Flagship course

Steer Fattening - The Complete Course

Ten modules. Sixty written lessons. Ten knowledge checks. A full costed batch plan as the final project. Buy lean cattle, feed them safely, and sell them finished - with real gain, intake and dressing figures, given as ranges where the science gives ranges.

How this course works

Every module has six written lessons, a five-question knowledge check, and one written capstone piece. Work through them in order: modules 1 to 3 are buying and setting up, 4 to 7 are feeding and keeping cattle alive and gaining, and 8 to 10 are the money - costing, selling and growing. Skipping the feeding half is how people lose whole batches.

These are written lessons, not video lessons. Approximately 38,341 words of teaching text, plus checklists, tables and quizzes. Lifetime access.

Each module also carries three recommended videos - free films made by other people (FAO, university extension services, and working African cattle farmers) that we have checked play and are on topic. They are extra watching, not part of the course we wrote.

The numbers this course is built on

  • A finishing period for store cattle commonly runs 90 to 120 days, sometimes up to 150 depending on the animal you start with and the ration you feed.
  • Average daily gain on a well balanced finishing ration commonly runs 1.0 to 1.5 kg per day. Poor rations, worms or ticks pull it well below that.
  • Feed conversion in finishing cattle commonly runs roughly 6 to 10 kg of dry matter per kg of gain.
  • Cattle must be stepped up onto a high concentrate diet gradually over about 14 to 21 days. Rushing it causes acidosis, and acidosis kills cattle and profits.
  • Dressing percentage commonly runs 50 to 58 percent. That is why a live weight price and a carcass weight price can never be compared directly.
  • Ticks and tick-borne disease are a first-rank threat across the region. Dipping on a strict schedule is not optional.
  • Withdrawal periods on veterinary medicines must be observed before slaughter. Ignore them and the meat is unsafe and can be condemned.

Sources: published beef production research and African feedlot and smallholder studies. Figures are ranges. Your cattle, breed, feed and management will move them. Grading schemes, movement permits and slaughter rules differ by country - confirm yours with your vet or local livestock officer.

Iron-clad price check. Before you buy cattle, feed, or build a pen, call three suppliers and three buyers and compare. This course teaches the method and the real biology. You confirm the local prices, the local grading scheme and the market demand.
