Interlocking Paving Block

8 lessons · 30 quiz questions · Easy difficulty

1 Types of interlocking pavers

Paving blocks (also called pavers) are concrete blocks laid on a prepared base to make driveways, roads and yards. They lock together so they can't move sideways.

Common shapes:

  • I-shape (dumbell): Traditional, most common in Africa. Simple to make, cheap moulds.
  • S-shape (Zig-Zag): Curved sides give more interlock. Popular in Europe.
  • Rectangular: Straight edges. Best for large areas with pattern design.
  • Cosmic / Hexagon: Decorative markets — high-end paving.

For your first paver: I-shape, 200 × 100 × 60 mm. Simple, universal moulds available, high demand everywhere.

2 Paver mould design

Paver moulds differ from block moulds in three ways:

  • Shallow depth: Pavers are 60–80 mm thick (blocks are 100–225 mm).
  • Flat, smooth top: Pavers need a smooth top surface — special mould plate on top.
  • Chamfered edges: 2–3 mm rounded edges make pavers lay flat without chipping.

Moulds can be:

  • Single-cavity: One paver per cycle. Slow but cheap. £80–£200.
  • Multi-cavity: 6–12 pavers per cycle. Fast, expensive. £500–£3,000 per set.

Most African producers use 6-cavity moulds on a vibrating machine — 6 pavers every 15 seconds = 1,440 per hour potential.

3 High-strength mix recipe

Pavers face vehicle loads, so they need a stronger mix than blocks. Standard:

  • Cement: 1 part
  • Sharp sand: 1.5 parts
  • Crushed stone (4–6 mm): 2.5 parts
  • Water: earth-damp

That's richer than dense block mix — cement is about 15–17% by weight. A 50 kg bag makes 18–22 pavers of 200 × 100 × 60 mm.

For high-traffic areas (public roads, petrol stations):

  • Add 5–10% more cement
  • Use stone max 6 mm (never larger)
  • Vibrate longer (8–10 seconds)
  • Cure for 14 days minimum

Target strength: 35–50 N/mm² at 28 days (higher than blocks). Pavers must survive trucks.

4 Compaction pressure required

Pavers need higher pressure than blocks. Two options:

Vibrating machine (common in Africa): Multi-cavity mould on a vibrating table. Compacts by vibration + static pressure. Ideal for 200–2,000 pavers/day. Cost: $3,000–$15,000.

Hydraulic press: High-pressure press (like a large hydraulic jack). Better compaction, stronger pavers, less cement needed. Cost: $15,000–$80,000 for small units.

For your first year, a vibrating machine works well. Move to hydraulic only when volume justifies it.

Never skip vibration. Under-compacted pavers crumble within weeks under traffic.

5 Demoulding pavers

Pavers demould immediately after casting (unlike blocks). The process:

  1. Fill the multi-cavity mould with mix
  2. Vibrate 6–8 seconds
  3. Level the top with a straight edge
  4. Lift the mould straight up — the pavers stay on the pallet below
  5. Move the pallet of green pavers to the curing area
  6. Repeat with a fresh pallet

Green pavers are softer than green blocks — handle carefully. Stack only 4–6 high on the pallet and don't move for 24 hours.

After 24 hours, pavers can be stacked 8–10 high and covered with plastic for curing.

6 Laying patterns

Pavers look better when laid in specific patterns. Popular ones:

  • Herringbone: 45° interlocking — strongest for driveways. No lateral movement.
  • Running bond: Straight rows offset by half — classic look, easy to lay.
  • Basket weave: Pairs of pavers at right angles — decorative, mid-strength.
  • Stack bond: Straight grid — weakest, only for decorative or pedestrian areas.

Tell your customers about herringbone — it's the strongest and most common. Many buyers don't know the difference. Educate them and you sell more pavers per m² (herringbone uses 5–10% more pavers).

7 Colouring pavers

Coloured pavers sell at 20–40% premium. Three ways to add colour:

1. Pigment in the mix: Add 2–5% iron oxide pigment to the whole mix. Uniform colour throughout. £0.80–£2 per paver extra. Best for solid colours.

2. Face layer pigment: Only the top 5 mm is coloured. Uses 80% less pigment but requires a two-stage fill. £0.30–£0.80 per paver.

3. Surface staining: Colour the top with a cement-based stain after demoulding. Cheapest but fades over 3–5 years.

Popular colours: charcoal, terracotta, sandstone, charcoal. Mix and match for design work.

Pigment sources: iron oxide (reds, browns, blacks), chromium oxide (greens), titanium dioxide (whites). Buy from construction chemical suppliers.

8 Pricing pavers per m²

Pavers are typically sold by the m² rather than per unit. Standard I-shape 200 × 100 mm covers 50 pavers per m².

Sample pricing (Africa, 2026):

  • Standard grey pavers: $6–$12 per m²
  • Coloured pavers: $10–$20 per m²
  • Premium (S-shape, cosmic, textured): $15–$30 per m²
  • Delivered + laid: add $3–$6 per m²

Production cost per m²: $4–$8 (materials + labour + overheads). Margin: 30–60%.

Where the volume is:

  • Residential driveways — 20–80 m² each
  • Commercial yards — 200–2,000 m²
  • Local authority contracts — 1,000+ m²
  • Garden paths — small but steady

Best buyers: contractors, landscapers, and local councils. Direct sales to homeowners via social media also work well.

💰 Pavers are a high-margin addition to any block yard. Same machine + a new mould. Big market in cities.
