Pig Health and Disease Control

Learning Objectives

  • Identify common signs of illness in pigs, such as loss of appetite, lethargy, and abnormal breathing.
  • Implement biosecurity measures to prevent disease introduction and spread on your farm.
  • Develop a vaccination schedule for your pigs based on local disease risks.
  • Treat minor pig ailments using affordable and locally available remedies.
  • Recognize reportable diseases and know how to contact veterinary authorities.
  • Maintain clean housing and proper waste management to reduce disease incidence.

Key Concepts

Common Pig Diseases in Africa

African swine fever (ASF) is a deadly viral disease with no vaccine, requiring strict biosecurity. Other common diseases include swine erysipelas, mange, and worm infestations. Early detection is key: watch for fever, skin lesions, diarrhea, and coughing. Isolate sick pigs immediately and consult a vet.

Biosecurity Basics

Limit visitors and ensure they wear clean boots and clothing. Quarantine new pigs for at least 30 days before mixing with your herd. Disinfect pens regularly and control rodents and birds. Do not feed kitchen scraps that may contain contaminated meat.

Vaccination and Deworming

Vaccinate against diseases like erysipelas and leptospirosis where available. Deworm pigs every 3-4 months using drugs like ivermectin or albendazole. Follow the correct dosage and withdrawal periods. Keep records of all treatments for better management.

Recognizing and Treating Ailments

For scours (diarrhea), provide clean water and electrolytes, and use antibiotics if bacterial. Treat wounds with antiseptics and keep flies away. For mange, use topical or injectable acaricides. Always consult a veterinarian before using any medication.

Housing and Sanitation

Ensure pens have good drainage and are easy to clean. Provide dry bedding and adequate space to reduce stress. Remove manure daily and compost it away from pig areas. Clean water and feed troughs regularly to prevent contamination.

Working with Veterinarians

Establish a relationship with a local vet for routine check-ups and emergencies. Report sudden deaths or unusual symptoms to authorities immediately. Participate in community disease surveillance programs. Keep a record of vet visits and treatments.
