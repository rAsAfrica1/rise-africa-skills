Pig Health, Records, and Marketing

Learning Objectives

  • Identify common pig diseases in Africa and implement biosecurity measures
  • Keep accurate production records to monitor growth and profitability
  • Develop a vaccination schedule for pigs in tropical environments
  • Analyze market trends and set competitive prices for pig products
  • Implement parasite control strategies for pigs in African farms
  • Create a simple business plan for a small-scale piggery

Key Concepts

Biosecurity on African Pig Farms

Biosecurity prevents disease entry and spread. In African smallholder settings, limit visitors, use footbaths, quarantine new pigs for 30 days, and isolate sick animals. Proper disposal of dead pigs and manure reduces contamination. These practices are low-cost and highly effective.

Record Keeping for Profitability

Records track feed, weight, health, and sales. Use a simple notebook or mobile app to record daily feed intake, weekly weights, and expenses. Calculate feed conversion ratio and cost per kg of gain. This data helps identify inefficiencies and make informed decisions.

Vaccination Schedule for Pigs

Vaccinate against common diseases like African swine fever (no vaccine available, focus on prevention), classical swine fever, and erysipelas where available. In Africa, consult local vets for regional schedules. Typically, piglets receive vaccines at 4-6 weeks and boosters later. Maintain cold chain for vaccine efficacy.

Parasite Control in Pigs

Internal parasites (roundworms) and external parasites (mange mites, ticks) reduce growth. Deworm pigs every 3-4 months with ivermectin or albendazole. Treat mange with ivermectin injections or topical sprays. Keep pens dry and clean to break parasite life cycles.

Market Analysis and Pricing

Understand local demand: pork is popular in urban areas. Monitor prices at markets, festivals, and hotels. Sell at peak demand (Christmas, Easter) for higher prices. Form cooperatives to negotiate better prices and access larger markets. Value addition (sausages, bacon) increases profits.

Business Planning for Pig Farms

A simple plan includes goals, budget, and cash flow. Estimate costs: feed (70% of expenses), housing, health, and labor. Project income from pig sales. Start small and reinvest profits. Seek training and microfinance to expand. Keep records to track progress.
