Pig Health and Disease Management

Learning Objectives

  • Identify common signs of illness in pigs such as loss of appetite, lethargy, and abnormal breathing.
  • Implement biosecurity measures to prevent disease introduction and spread on the farm.
  • Develop a vaccination schedule for pigs based on local disease prevalence.
  • Treat common pig diseases using affordable and available medications.
  • Practice proper sanitation and disinfection of pig pens and equipment.
  • Recognize notifiable diseases and report them to veterinary authorities promptly.

Key Concepts

Biosecurity on the Pig Farm

Biosecurity involves measures to prevent the introduction and spread of diseases. In Africa, where veterinary services may be limited, simple practices like isolating new pigs for at least 30 days, limiting visitors, and providing footbaths at entry points are crucial. Keep different age groups separate to avoid cross-contamination. Proper disposal of dead pigs and waste prevents disease spread. Regular cleaning and disinfection of pens and equipment are essential.

Common Pig Diseases in Africa

African swine fever (ASF) is a devastating viral disease with no vaccine; prevention through strict biosecurity is key. Other common diseases include swine erysipelas, pasteurellosis, and parasitic infections like mange and worms. Gastrointestinal infections such as colibacillosis and salmonellosis cause diarrhea, especially in piglets. Respiratory diseases like pneumonia are often triggered by poor ventilation and overcrowding. Early detection and treatment can reduce mortality and economic losses.

Vaccination and Deworming Schedules

Vaccination protects pigs against diseases like erysipelas, hog cholera (where available), and foot-and-mouth disease. In many African countries, vaccines for ASF are not available, so focus on other diseases. Deworm pigs every 3-4 months using affordable dewormers like albendazole or ivermectin. Piglets should be dewormed at 2, 4, and 8 weeks of age. Keep records of all treatments for effective management.

Recognizing Sick Pigs

Healthy pigs are active, eat well, and have smooth coats. Sick pigs may isolate themselves, have reduced appetite, and show abnormal breathing or coughing. Check for diarrhea, vomiting, skin lesions, or swollen joints. Temperature above 40°C indicates fever. Observe pigs daily, especially during feeding, to spot early signs. Isolate sick pigs immediately to prevent spread.

Treatment and Medication

Use antibiotics only when necessary and under veterinary guidance to avoid resistance. Common antibiotics include oxytetracycline and penicillin, available in many African agro-vet shops. For diarrhea, use electrolytes and antibiotics if bacterial. Treat wounds with antiseptics like iodine. Always follow withdrawal periods before slaughter. Record all treatments and consult a vet if unsure.

Housing and Sanitation for Health

Proper housing reduces stress and disease. Ensure good ventilation, adequate space, and dry flooring. Concrete floors are easier to clean than dirt floors. Clean pens daily, remove manure, and disinfect weekly using affordable disinfectants like bleach or lime. Provide clean water and feed to prevent contamination. Control rodents and insects that can carry diseases.
