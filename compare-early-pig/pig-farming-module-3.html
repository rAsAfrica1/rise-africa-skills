Pig Housing, Feeding, and Health

Learning Objectives

  • Design and construct a low-cost pig pen using locally available materials like bamboo, mud bricks, and thatch.
  • Formulate balanced pig rations using locally available feedstuffs such as maize, cassava, and soybean meal.
  • Implement a routine health program including vaccination, deworming, and biosecurity measures for pigs.
  • Identify common pig diseases and parasites in Africa and apply appropriate treatment and prevention strategies.
  • Manage pig waste to produce compost and biogas, reducing environmental impact and generating extra income.
  • Keep accurate records of feed, health, and production to monitor pig performance and profitability.

Key Concepts

Low-Cost Pig Pen Construction

A good pig pen protects pigs from predators, theft, and extreme weather. In rural Africa, pens can be built using bamboo, wooden poles, mud bricks, and thatched roofs. The floor should be slightly sloped for drainage, and a concrete floor is ideal but expensive; rammed earth or wooden slats are alternatives. Ensure adequate space: 1-2 square meters per pig, with a wallow for cooling in hot climates. Fencing should be sturdy to prevent escape and keep out wild animals.

Locally Sourced Pig Feed Formulation

Pigs are omnivores and can thrive on a variety of local feedstuffs. Common energy sources include maize, cassava peels, sweet potato vines, and brewers' waste. Protein can come from soybean meal, fish meal, sunflower cake, and legume leaves like leucaena. Mix feed to provide 14-18% crude protein for growing pigs. Always include mineral and vitamin premixes or provide access to mineral licks. Avoid moldy feeds, which can cause aflatoxicosis.

Routine Health Management

Prevention is better than cure. Vaccinate pigs against African swine fever (where available), classical swine fever, and foot-and-mouth disease as per local veterinary advice. Deworm every 3-4 months using drugs like ivermectin or albendazole. Practice biosecurity: quarantine new pigs for 2 weeks, disinfect pens regularly, and control visitors. Provide clean water and avoid feeding swill that may contain meat, which can spread diseases.

Common Diseases and Parasites

African swine fever is a deadly viral disease with no vaccine; control relies on strict biosecurity and culling. Other common diseases include swine erysipelas, mange (caused by mites), and internal parasites like roundworms. Symptoms to watch for: loss of appetite, fever, skin lesions, diarrhea, and coughing. Treat bacterial infections with antibiotics under veterinary guidance. Use acaricides to control ticks and mange mites.

Waste Management and Biogas Production

Pig manure is rich in nutrients and can be used to make compost for crops or generate biogas for cooking and lighting. A simple biogas digester can be built using a plastic tank or brick dome. The slurry from biogas production is an excellent organic fertilizer. Collect manure daily to keep pens clean and reduce flies. Compost manure with crop residues for 4-6 weeks before applying to fields to kill pathogens.

Record Keeping for Profitability

Keeping simple records helps you track feed costs, health treatments, and sales. Record daily feed intake, weight gain, and any deaths. Use a notebook or a simple spreadsheet on a phone. Calculate feed conversion ratio (FCR) to assess efficiency. Monitor market prices to decide the best time to sell. Good records also help access loans and prove profitability to investors.
