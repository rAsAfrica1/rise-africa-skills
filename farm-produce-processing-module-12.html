Butternut & Squash Processing

8 lessons · 30 quiz questions · Cucurbits

1 Variety selection

Butternut (Cucurbita moschata) and other winter squash are the premium end of the pumpkin family. They have denser flesh, higher sugar, and better storage than field pumpkins.

Waltham Butternut: The classic. 20–25 cm long, 1–1.5 kg, deep orange flesh. Best for puree.

Butterbush: Compact plant, good for small farms. Similar fruit.

Acorn squash: Small, ribbed, sweet yellow flesh. Best for roasting and stuffing.

Spaghetti squash: Flesh separates into strands when cooked. Novelty market only.

Hubbard: Large, grey-blue skin, sweet orange flesh. High flesh yield. Great for large-scale puree.

Choose Waltham for most processing. It's a proven, widely grown variety.

2 Washing and cutting

Butternut has thin skin and can be cooked with it on. But for processing, peel for a cleaner product.

  1. Wash the outside thoroughly with a vegetable brush
  2. Cut off both ends
  3. Cut in half lengthwise
  4. Scoop out seeds and pulp (save seeds for roasting)
  5. Cut into slices or cubes
  6. Peel (a Y-peeler works well; or use a knife)
  7. Cube to size

Butternut is easier to peel after partial roasting — the skin slips off. Consider this for puree production.

💡 Butternut skin is edible and nutritious. If you're roasting, leave it on.

3 Puree production

Butternut puree is a premium product — better flavour than field pumpkin puree. Sold to baby food companies, restaurants and retail.

  1. Cut and clean butternut
  2. Roast at 190°C for 40 minutes or steam for 20 minutes
  3. Scoop flesh from skin (if roasted)
  4. Blend smooth
  5. Adjust consistency with water (thicker for pie, thinner for soup)
  6. Heat to 90°C
  7. Hot-fill into jars or retort pouches
  8. Process for low-acid food safety (pressure canner or retort)

Yield: 100 kg butternut → 55–65 kg puree.

Retail: $4–$8 per 500 g jar. Wholesale to manufacturers: $2–$3 per kg.

4 Dried butternut cubes

Dried butternut is used in soup mixes, ready meals and as a snack. It's a bulk commodity that sells steadily.

  1. Peel and cube 1 cm pieces
  2. Blanch for 3 minutes (preserves colour and vitamins)
  3. Cool in cold water
  4. Dry at 60°C for 8–10 hours
  5. Target moisture: 6–8%
  6. Cool, then package in moisture-barrier bags

Yield: 100 kg fresh → 10–14 kg dried.

Bulk price: $8–$15 per kg. Manufacturers buy in tonnes.

5 Butternut soup mix

This is a value-added dry mix — bigger margins than selling dried cubes alone.

Recipe per 500 g bag: 300 g dried butternut + 100 g milk powder + 50 g corn flour + 30 g spice blend (nutmeg, ginger, cinnamon, salt, pepper) + 20 g dried onion.

To make soup: Add 1 L boiling water, simmer 10 minutes.

Retail: $8–$15 per 500 g bag. Strong winter sales.

6 Flour and baking uses

Butternut flour is gluten-free and slightly sweet — good for cakes, pancakes and baby food.

  1. Peel and cube butternut
  2. Steam 10 minutes
  3. Dry at 55°C for 10–12 hours
  4. Mill to fine powder
  5. Sieve through 250 micron
  6. Package in dark airtight bags

Yield: 100 kg fresh → 8–12 kg flour.

Retail: $15–$25 per kg. Used in gluten-free baking, baby food, pet food.

7 Frozen products

Frozen butternut is a major product for supermarkets and food service.

Frozen puree: Pack puree in bags, freeze at −18°C. 12-month shelf life.

Frozen cubes: Blanced cubes, individually quick frozen (IQF), packed 1 kg bags. Sells to restaurants and ready-meal makers.

Retail: $3–$5 per kg frozen cube. Wholesale: $1.50–$2.50 per kg.

8 Packaging and cold chain

Packaging by product:

  • Puree — glass jars, cans, retort pouches
  • Dried cubes — foil bags, 100 g–5 kg
  • Soup mix — foil pouches with zip
  • Flour — 500 g–5 kg dark bags
  • Frozen — poly bags, 500 g–1 kg

Cold chain:

  • Fresh whole butternut stores 3–6 months at 10–12°C, 60% humidity
  • Frozen products: −18°C, 12 months
  • Dried products: cool dry storage, 12–18 months
  • Puree (canned): 24 months unopened
💰 Butternut is worth 2–3× the price of field pumpkin. Grow or source butternut over standard pumpkin wherever possible.
