1. Common Products: Fruits, Vegetables, and Grains

In this lesson, you will learn the Spanish names for common fruits, vegetables, and grains that you might sell at your market stall or shop. Knowing these words will help you communicate with Spanish-speaking customers and suppliers. Let's start with fruits. "Manzana" means apple, "pltano" means banana, "naranja" means orange, "mango" means mango, "pia" means pineapple, "sanda" means watermelon, "uva" means grape, and "limn" means lemon. For vegetables: "tomate" is tomato, "cebolla" is onion, "ajo" is garlic, "papa" is potato, "zanahoria" is carrot, "repollo" is cabbage, "espinaca" is spinach, and "pimiento" is pepper. Grains include "arroz" (rice), "maz" (corn), "trigo" (wheat), "mijo" (millet), and "sorgo" (sorghum). These are staples in many African markets. Practice saying each word aloud. Write them on cards and stick them on the actual products in your stall. For example, put "manzana" on your apples. This helps you remember. Also, learn the plural forms: "manzanas", "pltanos", etc. When a customer asks, "Cunto cuestan las manzanas?" (How much are the apples?), you can respond. Try to use these words daily. If you sell in a bilingual area, labeling your goods in both Spanish and your local language can attract more customers. For instance, in Equatorial Guinea, Spanish is official, so this is directly useful. Even in other African countries, Spanish-speaking tourists or expatriates may visit your stall. Knowing these words gives you an advantage. Remember, repetition is key. Spend 10 minutes each morning reviewing the names. Soon, you'll be able to list all your products in Spanish without hesitation.

2. Money and Currency Exchange Terms

Handling money is central to any business. In Spanish, "dinero" means money. "Moneda" can mean coin or currency. "Billete" is banknote. "Cambio" means change or exchange rate. When you exchange money, you go to a "casa de cambio" (exchange house) or "banco" (bank). The "tipo de cambio" is the exchange rate. For example, if you want to change US dollars to Spanish pesetas (though Spain uses euros now), you would ask, "Cul es el tipo de cambio?" (What is the exchange rate?). In many African countries, like Equatorial Guinea, the currency is the Central African CFA franc, called "franco CFA" in Spanish. In Morocco, it's the dirham, "drham". In Western Sahara, the Moroccan dirham is used. You might also deal with euros in Spanish-speaking countries. Common phrases: "Cunto cuesta?" (How much does it cost?), "Es muy caro" (It's very expensive), "Es barato" (It's cheap), "Puedo pagar con tarjeta?" (Can I pay by card?), "Solo efectivo" (Cash only). When giving change, say "Su cambio" (Your change). Learn numbers well: uno, dos, tres, cuatro, cinco, seis, siete, ocho, nueve, diez, veinte, cincuenta, cien, mil. For prices like 1,500 CFA, say "mil quinientos francos". Practice counting money in Spanish. If you sell a product for 500 francs and the customer gives 1000, you say "Su cambio son quinientos francos". Also, know how to negotiate: "Me puede hacer un descuento?" (Can you give me a discount?). Being fluent in money terms builds trust with customers. Keep a small notebook with these phrases and review them before market days.

3. Weights and Measures for Selling Goods

When selling goods like rice, beans, or flour, you need to talk about weights and measures. In Spanish, "peso" means weight, but also is the currency in some countries. "Medida" means measure. "Kilo" is kilogram, "gramo" is gram, "libra" is pound, "litro" is liter, "metro" is meter. For solids, you might sell by "kilo" or "libra". For liquids, by "litro". Common phrases: "Un kilo de arroz, por favor" (One kilo of rice, please). "Cunto pesa?" (How much does it weigh?). "Necesito medio kilo" (I need half a kilo). "Un cuarto de kilo" (a quarter kilo). In many African markets, people use "tazas" (cups) or "latas" (cans) as measures, but Spanish uses standard metric. Learn to convert: 1 kilo = 1000 gramos. 1 libra = 453 gramos approximately. 1 litro = 1000 mililitros. When selling, you might say "Son 500 gramos" (It's 500 grams). If you use a scale, say "La balanza" (the scale). "Pselo, por favor" (Weigh it, please). For lengths, like fabric, you sell by "metro". "Un metro de tela" (one meter of fabric). "Medio metro" (half a meter). Also, learn fractions: "medio" (half), "un cuarto" (quarter), "tres cuartos" (three quarters). Practice with actual items: weigh a bag of rice, say its weight in Spanish. If you sell in bulk, you might say "Al por mayor" (wholesale) or "Al por menor" (retail). Knowing these terms helps you avoid mistakes and communicate clearly. Make a chart with conversions and keep it at your stall.

4. Colors and Sizes for Describing Merchandise

Describing your products by color and size helps customers find what they want. In Spanish, colors include: "rojo" (red), "azul" (blue), "verde" (green), "amarillo" (yellow), "negro" (black), "blanco" (white), "naranja" (orange), "morado" (purple), "rosa" (pink), "marrn" (brown), "gris" (gray). Sizes: "grande" (big), "pequeo" (small), "mediano" (medium), "largo" (long), "corto" (short), "ancho" (wide), "estrecho" (narrow). When describing merchandise, the adjective usually comes after the noun. For example, "camisa roja" (red shirt), "zapatos negros" (black shoes). But you can also say "gran cantidad" (large quantity). For size, "talla" is clothing size, "tamao" is general size. "Qu talla usa?" (What size do you wear?). "Tiene esto en talla grande?" (Do you have this in large?). For fruits and vegetables, you might say "pltanos maduros" (ripe bananas), "tomates verdes" (green tomatoes). "Fresas rojas" (red strawberries). Also, learn to compare: "ms grande que" (bigger than), "ms pequeo que" (smaller than). "Este es ms barato" (This one is cheaper). When a customer asks for a specific color, you can point and say "El rojo" (The red one). Practice by labeling items in your stall with color and size tags in Spanish. For example, "Cesta grande" (large basket), "Bolsa pequea" (small bag). This not only helps you learn but also attracts Spanish-speaking customers. If you sell clothing, know "camiseta" (t-shirt), "pantaln" (pants), "vestido" (dress). Describe them: "Vestido azul talla mediana" (Blue dress, medium size). Being precise reduces returns and builds reputation.

5. Basic Adjectives for Quality and Condition

Adjectives describe the quality and condition of your goods. In Spanish, common ones: "bueno" (good), "malo" (bad), "nuevo" (new), "viejo" (old), "fresco" (fresh), "podrido" (rotten), "seco" (dry), "hmedo" (wet), "duro" (hard), "suave" (soft), "fuerte" (strong), "dbil" (weak), "caro" (expensive), "barato" (cheap), "limpio" (clean), "sucio" (dirty), "roto" (broken), "entero" (whole), "autntico" (authentic), "falso" (fake). When selling, you want to highlight positive qualities. For example, "Frutas frescas" (fresh fruits), "Productos de alta calidad" (high quality products). If something is damaged, you might say "Est roto" (It's broken) or "Est en mal estado" (It's in bad condition). Customers may ask: "Est fresco?" (Is it fresh?), "Es nuevo?" (Is it new?), "Es de buena calidad?" (Is it good quality?). You can respond: "S, est muy fresco" (Yes, it's very fresh). "Es de buena calidad" (It's good quality). Learn to use "muy" (very) and "bastante" (quite) to emphasize: "muy bueno" (very good), "bastante barato" (quite cheap). Also, comparatives: "mejor" (better), "peor" (worse). "Este es mejor que aquel" (This is better than that one). Superlatives: "el mejor" (the best), "el peor" (the worst). Practice by describing your products daily. If you sell used items, be honest: "Est usado pero en buen estado" (It's used but in good condition). This builds trust. Make a list of adjectives and opposites, and review them before interacting with customers.

6. Practice: Label Your Products in Spanish

Now it's time to put everything together. In this practice lesson, you will create labels for your products in Spanish. This hands-on activity will reinforce the vocabulary from the previous lessons. Start by choosing 10 products you sell regularly. For each, write the Spanish name, a color, a size, and a quality adjective. For example, if you sell rice, you might write: "Arroz blanco, bolsa grande, buena calidad". If you sell tomatoes: "Tomates rojos, medianos, frescos". Use index cards or small pieces of paper. Write clearly in large letters. Attach these labels to your products or display them on your stall. This helps you remember and shows customers you are professional. Next, practice saying the labels aloud. Imagine a customer asking about each product. For instance, "Cunto cuestan los tomates rojos?" (How much are the red tomatoes?). You respond with the price in Spanish. Also, practice giving change. If a customer buys two kilos of rice at 500 francs per kilo, total is 1000 francs. If they pay with 2000, say "Su cambio son mil francos". Do this exercise daily for a week. You can also create a small dictionary notebook with all the words you've learned. Organize by category: fruits, vegetables, grains, money, measures, colors, sizes, adjectives. Review it each morning. To test yourself, cover the Spanish words and see if you can recall them from the English. Or ask a friend to quiz you. Finally, consider making a bilingual sign for your stall: "Frutas Frescas / Fresh Fruits". This attracts both Spanish and English speakers. In Equatorial Guinea, where Spanish is official, this is especially useful. Even in other African countries, tourists appreciate the effort. Keep practicing, and soon you'll be confident in Spanish business conversations.

7. Practical Reference

Practical Reference: Building Your Business Vocabulary in Spanish

This practical reference module is designed for African agribusiness entrepreneurs, traders, and market vendors who sell fruits, vegetables, grains, and other agricultural products in Spanish-speaking markets or to Spanish-speaking buyers. Whether you are negotiating at a market stall in Malabo, Equatorial Guinea, exporting cocoa to Latin America, or serving customers in the growing Hispanic communities across Africa, the vocabulary in this lesson will help you describe products, quote prices, discuss measurements, and close sales with confidence.

Equatorial Guinea is the only African country where Spanish is an official language, with over 1.1 million speakers. Additionally, Spanish is spoken in the Canary Islands, a major trading hub for West African agricultural exports, and is increasingly used in international agribusiness negotiations with Latin American buyers from Mexico, Colombia, Peru, and Chile. Mastering product vocabulary in Spanish gives you a competitive edge when labeling goods, writing invoices, and communicating with buyers.

3. Weights and Measures for Selling Goods

Accurate measurement vocabulary prevents disputes and builds trust with buyers. In most of Africa, the metric system is used, but you may encounter traditional measures in local markets.

  • el kilo (kg) kilogram: "Un kilo de arroz, por favor."
  • el gramo (g) gram: "Cien gramos de especias."
  • la tonelada (t) metric ton: "Exportamos diez toneladas de cacao."
  • el litro (L) liter: "Dos litros de aceite de palma."
  • el saco sack: "Un saco de maz de 50 kilos."
  • la caja box/crate: "Una caja de tomates."
  • el racimo bunch: "Un racimo de pltanos."
  • la docena dozen: "Una docena de huevos."
  • al por mayor wholesale: "Vendemos al por mayor."
  • al por menor retail: "Tambin vendemos al por menor."

4. Colors and Sizes for Describing Merchandise

Describing the appearance of your products helps buyers identify quality and variety. Colors and sizes are essential descriptors in market transactions.

Colors (los colores): rojo (red), verde (green), amarillo (yellow), naranja (orange), marrn (brown), blanco (white), negro (black), morado (purple).

Sizes (las tallas / los tamaos): grande (large), mediano (medium), pequeo (small), extra grande (extra-large), delgado (thin), grueso (thick), largo (long), corto (short).

Example: "Tengo mangos amarillos grandes y mangos verdes pequeos." (I have large yellow mangoes and small green mangoes.)

5. Basic Adjectives for Quality and Condition

Quality adjectives allow you to justify your price and reassure buyers. Use these terms when describing the condition of your goods:

  • fresco/a fresh: "Productos frescos del da."
  • maduro/a ripe: "Pltanos maduros, listos para comer."
  • verde unripe/green: "Tomates verdes para cocinar."
  • seco/a dry: "Granos secos, bien almacenados."
  • orgnico/a organic: "Cultivado de forma orgnica."
  • de primera calidad first quality: "Cacao de primera calidad."
  • daado/a damaged: "No acepto productos daados."
  • limpio/a clean: "Granos limpios, sin piedras."
  • en buen estado in good condition: "La mercanca lleg en buen estado."
  • certificado/a certified: "Producto certificado para exportacin."

ADVERTENCIA DE SEGURIDAD / SAFETY WARNING

When labeling products for export or sale, never use misleading quality terms such as "orgnico" or "de primera calidad" unless your product is certified by a recognized authority. In Equatorial Guinea, contact the Ministerio de Agricultura, Ganadera y Pesca for certification requirements. False labeling can result in fines, confiscation of goods, and loss of export licenses. Always verify that your Spanish labels accurately reflect the actual condition, weight, and origin of your products. When in doubt, consult a certified translator or trade advisor before printing labels.
