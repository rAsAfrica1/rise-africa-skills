10. Housing, Fencing and Facilities

Housing keeps goats dry, safe and productive. This module shows how to build it right at any budget.

Module 10 of 12 · Goat Farming for Profit

Why housing matters

Goats hate rain and mud. Wet goats get pneumonia, foot rot, and parasites. Good housing cuts mortality by 60-80% and increases growth rate.

A 20-doe flock needs 40-50 m2 of shelter plus 100+ m2 of run space.

House design principles

  1. Raised floor. Slatted wood or concrete raised 30-50 cm above ground. Prevents damp. Critical in wet areas.
  2. Open sides with mesh. Two or three sides open for airflow. Mesh 2 cm x 2 cm keeps out wild birds and rodents.
  3. Roof overhang 60 cm minimum. Rain and sun protection.
  4. North-south orientation. Avoids full sun on the long wall.
  5. Roll-down curtains. Tarp or feed sacks for cold nights and rain.
  6. Feeding rack. Hay off the ground. Prevents waste and parasite infection.
  7. Water trough. Clean water always available. Mounted 30-40 cm high.
  8. Mineral block holder. Free-choice minerals always available.
  9. Kidding pen. Separate, quiet, clean area for births.
  10. Sick pen. Isolated 20+ m from main house. Easy to disinfect.

Slatted floor system

The best floor for goats. Timber slats with 1.5-2 cm gaps. Allows droppings to fall through, keeping goats clean and dry.

  • Slat width: 5-8 cm.
  • Gap: 1.5-2 cm (small enough kids cannot slip through).
  • Slat height: raised 50-80 cm above ground.
  • Slope: slight - helps droppings fall.
  • Underneath: concrete or packed earth, easy to scrape clean.

Rotational grazing

Divide grazing land into 4-6 paddocks. Move goats every 3-7 days.

  • Worm burden drops 60-80%
  • Pasture recovers better
  • Less overgrazing
  • More animals per hectare
  • Better feed quality

Feed and water systems

  • Hay rack: Mounted at chest height. Prevents hay waste and parasite pickup.
  • Feed trough: 30-40 cm per goat. Divider to stop hogging.
  • Water trough: 30-40 cm high, easy to drain and clean.
  • Mineral holder: Weatherproof, always stocked.
  • Nipple drinkers: Ideal but not common for goats - trough is standard.

The dry rule: Goats must have dry feet and dry bodies. Wet goat = sick goat. Raised floor and roof are non-negotiable.
