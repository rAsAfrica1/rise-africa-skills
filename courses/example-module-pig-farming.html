Step-by-Step Instructions

1
Assess Your Current Situation
Evaluate your available land, climate, and resources to determine farm feasibility
What to do:

1. Walk around your property and identify available space (minimum 10 m² per pig)
2. Note water source proximity and ease of access
3. Assess existing shelter from sun and wind
4. Check drainage patterns on your land
5. Document soil type (sandy, clay, loamy)
6. Record your region's climate: temperature range, rainy season
7. Sketch a rough property map showing these features

Questions to answer: Do I have access to clean water year-round? Is my land well-drained? Can I protect pigs from extreme heat? Do I have shelter materials available?
2
Plan Your Pigsty Layout
Design the layout based on sustainable principles and animal welfare
What to do:

1. Draw a basic layout on paper showing: sleeping area, feeding area, water trough, waste management section
2. Ensure sleeping area is shaded and dry
3. Place feeding area away from water source to prevent contamination
4. Design drainage channels to direct waste away from living area
5. Plan for natural ventilation (openings or elevated roof)
6. Calculate space: At least 2 m² per piglet, 4 m² per adult pig
7. Research local building regulations in your area

Layout checklist: Separate eating, sleeping, and waste areas? Good drainage? Shade protection? Natural ventilation? Storm-proof roof?
3
Gather Building Materials
Identify and source local, affordable building materials
Material options (from cheapest to most durable):

• Posts: Timber (acacia, teak) or concrete pillars
• Walls: Mud bricks, timber planks, bamboo, wattle-and-daub
• Roof: Thatch, corrugated iron sheets, wooden tiles
• Flooring: Compacted earth, sand, concrete (for durability)
• Tools: Shovel, hammer, saw, measuring tape

Action items:
1. Visit local building material suppliers
2. Get quotes for each material type
3. Calculate quantities needed based on your layout
4. Create a cost estimate spreadsheet
5. Identify suppliers with reliable delivery
6. Check material durability in your climate
4
Research Local Feed Options
Identify affordable, nutritious feed sources in your area
Types of pig feed:

• Grains: Maize, sorghum, cassava meal (energy source)
• Protein: Beans, legumes, fish meal, termites (building blocks)
• Vegetables: Kitchen scraps, sweet potato vines, pumpkin
• Commercial feed: Mixed pellets with balanced nutrients

What to research:
1. Visit local markets and note feed prices per kg
2. Identify 5-10 affordable local feed sources
3. Ask farmers what they currently feed their pigs
4. Calculate daily feed requirements per pig (2-3% of body weight)
5. Create a monthly feed cost estimate
6. Identify seasonal variations in feed availability
7. Find a reliable feed supplier (individual or shop)
5
Connect with Support Resources
Build your support network for ongoing learning and help
Essential contacts to establish:

1. Veterinarian: Find local vet, note phone number and location
2. Agricultural Extension Officer: Visit local extension office
3. Farmers' Group: Join local farming association or cooperative
4. Feed Supplier: Establish relationship with reliable supplier
5. Market Trader: Connect with buyer for future pig sales
6. Online Communities: Join farmer WhatsApp/Facebook groups

Information to gather from each contact:
• Name and phone number
• Location/address
• Services they offer
• Pricing/costs
• Best time to contact
• Group meetings or training schedules
6
Create Your Farm Plan Document
Compile all information into a comprehensive farm plan
Your farm plan should include:

1. Farm Overview: Location, size, climate zone
2. Pigsty Design: Hand-drawn or digital layout diagram
3. Financial Plan: Construction costs, monthly feed costs, revenue estimate
4. Feed Strategy: List of 5+ local feed options with prices
5. Health Plan: Veterinarian contact, vaccination schedule
6. Support Network: List of key contacts (vet, extension, supplier)
7. Timeline: When will you construct, stock, and operate
8. Risk Assessment: What could go wrong? How will you handle it?

Storage: Save copies in two places (one physical, one digital backup)

Success Criteria - You'll know you've succeeded when:

  • You've created a detailed map of your property showing water, shelter, and space
  • You have a hand-drawn or digital pigsty layout that includes separate areas for sleeping, eating, and waste
  • You've obtained material cost quotes and created a construction budget
  • You have a list of 5-10 local feed options with prices per kg and monthly cost estimate
  • You've identified and contacted your local veterinarian
  • You've joined a farmers' group or community for ongoing support
  • You have a written farm plan document saved in at least two locations
  • You can explain to someone why sustainable farming matters for your long-term success

Real-World Application Project

Project: Design and Document Your Farm Plan

Objective: Create a comprehensive plan for starting your pig farm based on your local context, resources, and research.

What you need to submit:

  • Farm Layout Diagram: Hand-drawn or digital sketch showing pigsty design with measurements. Include sleeping area, feeding area, water supply, and waste management section.
  • Feed Options List: A list of 5-10 locally-available pig feed options with: name, source, cost per kg, and estimated monthly cost for 3-5 pigs.
  • Construction Cost Estimate: List of materials needed, quantities, unit prices, and total construction cost for your pigsty.
  • Support Network Document: Contact information and notes about 3-5 local resources: veterinarian, agricultural extension officer, farmer group, feed supplier.
  • Reflection Essay: 300-500 words describing: your farm plan, potential challenges you anticipate, how you'll address them, and what you learned from this module.
