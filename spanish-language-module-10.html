1. Reporting Theft or Loss of Goods

If your goods are stolen in a Spanish-speaking country, go to the nearest police station (comisara) immediately. In most countries, you must file a denuncia (official report) within 24 hours for insurance claims. Say: 'Quiero denunciar un robo' (I want to report a theft). Give details: what was stolen, where, and when. For example: 'Me robaron mi cartera en el mercado a las 3 de la tarde' (They stole my wallet in the market at 3 PM). If you lost something, say 'He perdido mi pasaporte' (I have lost my passport). You will need your passport or ID. Ask for a copy of the denuncia; it's called a 'copia de la denuncia'. Keep it safe. In many African countries, police may ask for a 'refresco' (bribe), but in Spanish-speaking countries, it's illegal. If asked for money, politely say 'No entiendo' or ask for a receipt. For stolen phones, report to your operator. In Spain, dial 091 for police; in Mexico, 911. Learn numbers: 1 uno, 2 dos, 3 tres, 4 cuatro, 5 cinco, 6 seis, 7 siete, 8 ocho, 9 nueve, 10 diez. Practice: 'Necesito ayuda, me han robado' (I need help, I've been robbed). Always carry a photocopy of your passport. If you lose goods in transit, contact the transport company. For example: 'Perd mi equipaje en el autobs' (I lost my luggage on the bus). They may ask for your ticket number. Use 'Puede ayudarme?' (Can you help me?). In an emergency, call 112 in Spain, 911 in most Latin American countries. Memorize these phrases: 'Soy extranjero' (I am a foreigner), 'No hablo bien espaol' (I don't speak Spanish well). Write down the officer's name and badge number. If you don't understand, say 'Puede repetir ms despacio?' (Can you repeat more slowly?). Get a copy of the report for your embassy. This is crucial for replacing documents. In some countries, you can file online, but in-person is safer. Always stay calm and polite.

2. Asking for Help in a Medical Emergency

In a medical emergency, call for an ambulance immediately. In Spain, dial 112; in Mexico, 911; in Argentina, 107. Say: 'Necesito una ambulancia' (I need an ambulance). Give your location: 'Estoy en la calle...' (I am on ... street). Describe the problem: 'Mi amigo tiene dolor en el pecho' (My friend has chest pain). If someone is unconscious, say 'Est inconsciente' (He/She is unconscious). For bleeding, say 'Est sangrando' (He/She is bleeding). Learn body parts: cabeza (head), pecho (chest), estmago (stomach), brazo (arm), pierna (leg). If you are sick, say 'Me siento mal' (I feel bad). For fever: 'Tengo fiebre' (I have a fever). For pain: 'Me duele aqu' (It hurts here). Point to the area. At the hospital, say 'Necesito un mdico' (I need a doctor). If you have insurance, say 'Tengo seguro mdico' (I have medical insurance). Show your card. If not, ask 'Cunto cuesta la consulta?' (How much does the consultation cost?). In many African countries, you might go to a clinic first. But in Spanish-speaking countries, hospitals (hospitales) handle emergencies. Pharmacies (farmacias) can help for minor issues. Say 'Necesito medicina para...' (I need medicine for...). For diarrhea: 'Tengo diarrea'. For malaria, say 'Tengo paludismo' (in Spain) or 'Tengo malaria' (in Latin America). Always carry a list of allergies in Spanish: 'Soy alrgico a la penicilina' (I am allergic to penicillin). If you need an interpreter, ask 'Hay alguien que hable ingls?' (Is there someone who speaks English?). But try to use Spanish. Practice: 'Llame a una ambulancia, por favor' (Call an ambulance, please). In an emergency, every second counts. Speak clearly and slowly. If you don't know the word, describe it: 'Un dolor como fuego' (A pain like fire). Keep emergency numbers saved in your phone. Also, learn 'Dnde est el hospital ms cercano?' (Where is the nearest hospital?). For minor injuries, you can go to a pharmacy and ask for 'una curita' (a band-aid) or 'agua oxigenada' (hydrogen peroxide). In case of snakebite, say 'Me mordi una serpiente' (A snake bit me). Immobilize the area and get help fast.

3. Dealing with Police or Officials

If stopped by police in a Spanish-speaking country, stay calm and polite. Say 'Buenos das, oficial' (Good morning, officer). Show your ID or passport when asked: 'Aqu est mi pasaporte' (Here is my passport). If you don't understand, say 'No entiendo, puede hablar ms despacio?' (I don't understand, can you speak more slowly?). Police may ask what you are doing: 'Qu est haciendo aqu?' (What are you doing here?). Answer simply: 'Soy turista' (I am a tourist) or 'Estoy trabajando' (I am working). If they ask for money, it's a bribe. In many countries, this is illegal. You can say 'No tengo dinero' (I don't have money) or 'Prefiero pagar la multa en la comisara' (I prefer to pay the fine at the police station). If you are arrested, say 'Quiero llamar a mi embajada' (I want to call my embassy). You have the right to a phone call. Learn: 'No he hecho nada malo' (I haven't done anything wrong). If you are in a car, they may ask for your license: 'Su licencia de conducir, por favor' (Your driver's license, please). Give it. If they claim you broke a rule, ask 'Cul es la multa?' (What is the fine?). Fines are often paid at a bank or police station. Never hand over your passport as a bribe. Keep a photocopy. If you are mistreated, note the officer's name and badge number: 'Cul es su nombre y nmero de placa?' (What is your name and badge number?). Report to your embassy. In some countries, police may target foreigners. Avoid confrontations. If you are innocent, say 'Llame a mi abogado' (Call my lawyer). If you don't have one, ask for a public defender: 'Necesito un abogado de oficio'. In Spain, you can ask for 'asistencia jurdica gratuita' if you have no money. Always be respectful. Use 'usted' (formal you) with officials. Say 'Gracias, oficial' when leaving. If you are asked to pay a fine on the spot, ask for a receipt: 'Me puede dar un recibo?' (Can you give me a receipt?). If they refuse, it's likely a bribe. Stay calm and don't argue. Your safety is priority. Memorize: 'No hablo espaol muy bien' (I don't speak Spanish very well). This may make them more patient.

4. Resolving Disputes with Customers or Suppliers

In business, disputes happen. In Spanish, say 'Tenemos un problema' (We have a problem). If a customer complains, listen and say 'Lo siento, cmo puedo ayudarle?' (I'm sorry, how can I help you?). If they claim a product is defective, ask 'Puede mostrarme el producto?' (Can you show me the product?). For a refund, say 'Podemos darle un reembolso' (We can give you a refund) or 'Podemos cambiarlo' (We can exchange it). If you can't agree, suggest 'Hablemos con el gerente' (Let's talk to the manager). For supplier disputes, say 'El pedido lleg tarde' (The order arrived late). If goods are damaged, say 'Los productos estn daados'. Ask for a discount: 'Puede darnos un descuento?' (Can you give us a discount?). If they refuse, say 'Necesitamos resolver esto' (We need to resolve this). In many African countries, business is done face-to-face. In Spanish-speaking countries, it's similar. Use polite phrases: 'Por favor' (Please), 'Gracias' (Thank you). If you need to escalate, say 'Voy a hablar con mi abogado' (I will talk to my lawyer). But try to negotiate first. Learn numbers for prices: 100 cien, 200 doscientos, 500 quinientos, 1000 mil. For a contract, say 'Quiero revisar el contrato' (I want to review the contract). If a customer doesn't pay, say 'Necesitamos el pago' (We need the payment). You can offer a payment plan: 'Puede pagar en dos partes?' (Can you pay in two parts?). If they still refuse, say 'Tendremos que tomar acciones legales' (We will have to take legal action). But always keep a record: 'Tengo los recibos' (I have the receipts). For suppliers, if quality is bad, say 'No podemos aceptar esto' (We cannot accept this). Ask for a replacement: 'Enve un reemplazo, por favor' (Send a replacement, please). If they don't respond, call them: 'Le llamo por el pedido' (I'm calling about the order). Use email for written proof. In Spanish, email is 'correo electrnico'. Write 'Estimado seor/seora' (Dear Sir/Madam). End with 'Atentamente' (Sincerely). If you need mediation, contact a local business association. In Spain, 'Cmara de Comercio'. In Mexico, 'CANACO'. They can help. Always stay professional. Don't shout. Say 'Entiendo su posicin' (I understand your position). This helps resolve disputes faster.

5. Contacting Your Embassy or Consulate

If you lose your passport or are in trouble, contact your embassy. In Spanish, say 'Necesito contactar mi embajada' (I need to contact my embassy). Find the address and phone number before you travel. In an emergency, call them. Say 'Soy ciudadano de [pas]' (I am a citizen of [country]). For example: 'Soy ciudadano de Nigeria'. If you need a new passport, say 'He perdido mi pasaporte' (I have lost my passport). They will ask for a police report: 'Necesito la denuncia de la polica'. Bring a photocopy of your old passport, photos, and ID. The fee for a new passport varies: in Spain, it's about 30 euros; in Mexico, around 1000 pesos. Ask 'Cunto cuesta el nuevo pasaporte?' (How much does the new passport cost?). If you are arrested, say 'Quiero que notifiquen a mi embajada' (I want you to notify my embassy). The embassy can provide a list of lawyers: 'Necesito una lista de abogados' (I need a list of lawyers). They can also help with medical emergencies: 'Necesito ayuda mdica' (I need medical help). If you are a victim of crime, they can assist: 'He sido vctima de un crimen' (I have been a victim of a crime). For lost money, they may help contact family: 'Pueden contactar a mi familia?' (Can you contact my family?). But embassies do not give money. They can help you transfer funds. Say 'Necesito recibir dinero de mi familia' (I need to receive money from my family). In some countries, you can go to the consulate for notary services: 'Necesito un notario' (I need a notary). For voter registration, ask 'Puedo votar desde aqu?' (Can I vote from here?). Always keep your embassy's emergency number. In Spain, many embassies are in Madrid. In Mexico, in Mexico City. In Argentina, in Buenos Aires. If you are in a remote area, call first. Say 'Estoy en [ciudad]' (I am in [city]). They may direct you to a consulate. Learn: 'Dnde est la embajada de [pas]?' (Where is the embassy of [country]?). If you don't speak Spanish, ask 'Habla ingls?' (Do you speak English?). But try Spanish. Practice: 'Necesito ayuda consular' (I need consular help). Keep a copy of your passport and visa in a safe place. Also, register with your embassy when you arrive: 'Quiero registrarme' (I want to register). This helps in emergencies.

6. Practice: Role-play a Dispute Resolution

Now practice a dispute resolution. Imagine you are a customer who bought a phone that doesn't work. You go to the shop. Say: 'Compr este telfono ayer y no funciona' (I bought this phone yesterday and it doesn't work). The seller says: 'Lo siento, no podemos devolver el dinero' (I'm sorry, we can't refund your money). You reply: 'Pero est defectuoso. Quiero un reemplazo o mi dinero' (But it's defective. I want a replacement or my money). Seller: 'Podemos repararlo' (We can repair it). You: 'No, quiero uno nuevo' (No, I want a new one). If they refuse, say: 'Voy a llamar a la polica' (I will call the police) or 'Voy a poner una queja' (I will file a complaint). In many countries, consumer protection laws are strong. In Spain, you can ask for 'hoja de reclamaciones' (complaint form). Say: 'Quiero la hoja de reclamaciones'. They must provide it. Fill it out and take a copy. For suppliers, role-play: You ordered 100 shirts but received 80. Say: 'Ped 100 camisas pero recib 80' (I ordered 100 shirts but received 80). Supplier: 'Lo siento, hubo un error' (Sorry, there was a mistake). You: 'Necesito las 20 camisas restantes o un reembolso' (I need the remaining 20 shirts or a refund). Supplier: 'Le enviaremos las camisas maana' (We'll send the shirts tomorrow). You: 'Gracias, pero si no llegan, cancelar el pedido' (Thanks, but if they don't arrive, I'll cancel the order). Always stay calm. Use 'por favor' and 'gracias'. If you need to escalate, say: 'Voy a contactar a un abogado' (I will contact a lawyer). For practice, write a script. Use these phrases: 'Podemos llegar a un acuerdo?' (Can we reach an agreement?), 'Qu propone?' (What do you propose?). If you are the seller, say: 'Entiendo su frustracin' (I understand your frustration). Offer a solution: 'Le dar un descuento del 10%' (I'll give you a 10% discount). If the customer agrees, say: 'Gracias por su comprensin' (Thank you for your understanding). Practice with a friend. Record yourself. Listen for pronunciation. In Spanish, 'r' is rolled. Practice 'reembolso' (refund) and 'queja' (complaint). Also learn 'garanta' (warranty). Ask: 'Tiene garanta?' (Does it have a warranty?). If yes, say: 'Quiero usar la garanta' (I want to use the warranty). If no, say: 'Entonces quiero mi dinero' (Then I want my money). Remember, in many African countries, negotiation is common. In Spanish-speaking countries, it's similar. Be firm but polite. This role-play prepares you for real situations. Practice until you feel confident.

<h2>Lesson 1: Reporting Theft or Loss of Goods Denuncia por Robo o Prdida</h2>

<p>Theft and loss are the most common emergencies in African trade corridors. In Equatorial Guinea, cargo theft at the port of Malabo or along the Bata corridor can wipe out a season's margin. In Ceuta, warehouse break-ins are a recurring risk for cross-border traders. The Spanish legal term you need is <strong>denuncia</strong> (formal police report), not <strong>reclamacin</strong> (a civil claim). Without a denuncia, your insurance claim and your tax deduction are invalid.</p>

<p>Key phrases for the police station or Guardia Civil office:</p>
<ul>
<li>"Quiero poner una denuncia por robo de mercanca." I want to file a report for theft of goods.</li>
<li>"Los hechos ocurrieron el [fecha] a las [hora] en [lugar]." The events occurred on [date] at [time] in [place].</li>
<li>"Tengo facturas, fotografas y testigos." I have invoices, photographs, and witnesses.</li>
<li>"Necesito una copia de la denuncia para mi seguro." I need a copy of the report for my insurance.</li>
</ul>

<p>Always request the <strong>nmero de diligencias</strong> (case reference number). This is the single most important piece of information for follow-up. In Equatorial Guinea, reports are filed with the <strong>Polica Nacional</strong> or <strong>Guardia Civil</strong>; in Ceuta and Melilla, the <strong>Polica Nacional</strong> handles criminal complaints.</p>

<h2>Lesson 2: Asking for Help in a Medical Emergency Emergencia Mdica</h2>

<p>Medical emergencies in African trade contexts are complicated by geography, language, and insurance coverage. In Equatorial Guinea, the best-equipped hospitals are in Malabo and Bata; in Ceuta and Melilla, you have access to Spanish public healthcare. The key is to communicate clearly and quickly.</p>

<p>Essential phrases:</p>
<ul>
<li>"Necesito una ambulancia." I need an ambulance.</li>
<li>"Mi compaero tiene fiebre alta / dolor en el pecho / dificultad para respirar." My colleague has a high fever / chest pain / difficulty breathing.</li>
<li>"Somos alrgicos a [penicilina, etc.]." We are allergic to [penicillin, etc.].</li>
<li>"Cul es el hospital ms cercano?" Which is the nearest hospital?</li>
<li>"Tengo seguro mdico internacional." I have international medical insurance.</li>
</ul>

<p>In Equatorial Guinea, emergency numbers are <strong>112</strong> (general) and <strong>115</strong> (ambulance). In Ceuta and Melilla, dial <strong>112</strong>. Always carry a printed card with your blood type, allergies, and insurance policy number in Spanish.</p>

<h2>Lesson 3: Dealing with Police or Officials Trato con la Polica o Funcionarios</h2>

<p>Police and customs officials in Spanish-speaking Africa have broad discretionary powers. Your goal is to be respectful, documented, and persistent. Never argue; always ask for the officer's name and badge number.</p>

<p>Key phrases:</p>
<ul>
<li>"Buenos das, soy [nombre], representante de [empresa]." Good morning, I am [name], representative of [company].</li>
<li>"Puede indicarme su nombre y nmero de placa?" Can you tell me your name and badge number?</li>
<li>"Tengo toda la documentacin en regla." I have all the documentation in order.</li>
<li>"Quiero hablar con un superior." I want to speak with a supervisor.</li>
<li>"Puedo llamar a mi abogado / embajada?" Can I call my lawyer / embassy?</li>
</ul>

<p style="margin: 0 0 8px 0; font-weight: bold; color: #b71c1c; font-size: 16px;"> SAFETY WARNING NEVER PAY BRIBES</p>
<p style="margin: 0; color: #2e3a4d; font-size: 14px;">Paying a bribe (<em>soborno</em> or <em>coima</em>) to a police officer or customs official is a criminal offense under Spanish law, Equatorial Guinean law, and the African Union Convention on Preventing and Combating Corruption. It exposes you to arrest, deportation, and permanent blacklisting from public contracts. If asked for money, say: <strong>"No puedo hacer eso. Es ilegal. Quiero cumplir con la ley."</strong> (I cannot do that. It is illegal. I want to comply with the law.) Then contact your embassy immediately. Document the officer's name, badge number, time, and location.</p>

<h2>Lesson 4: Resolving Disputes with Customers or Suppliers Resolucin de Disputas</h2>

<p>Disputes in African agribusiness often arise from quality claims, late delivery, or payment delays. The Spanish business culture values formality and written records. A verbal agreement is not enough.</p>

<p>Key phrases for a formal meeting:</p>
<ul>
<li>"Quiero resolver este problema de manera amistosa." I want to resolve this problem amicably.</li>
<li>"Segn el contrato, la entrega deba realizarse el [fecha]." According to the contract, delivery was due on [date].</li>
<li>"Propongo una reunin con nuestros abogados." I propose a meeting with our lawyers.</li>
<li>"Si no llegamos a un acuerdo, tendr que acudir a la Cmara de Comercio." If we do not reach an agreement, I will have to go to the Chamber of Commerce.</li>
</ul>

<h2>Lesson 5: Contacting Your Embassy or Consulate Contacto con la Embajada</h2>

<p>Your embassy is your last line of defense, not your first. Contact them when: you are arrested, your passport is lost, you are a victim of a serious crime, or a dispute has escalated beyond local resolution.</p>

<p>Key phrases:</p>
<ul>
<li>"Soy ciudadano/a de [pas] y necesito asistencia consular." I am a citizen of [country] and I need consular assistance.</li>
<li>"He sido detenido/a y no me han informado de mis derechos." I have been detained and have not been informed of my rights.</li>
<li>"He perdido mi pasaporte." I have lost my passport.</li>
<li>"Necesito una lista de abogados que hablen ingls/francs." I need a list of English/French-speaking lawyers.</li>
</ul>

<p>Save these contacts before you travel: Spanish Embassy in Malabo (+240 333 09 21 00), Spanish Consulate in Bata, Moroccan Consulate in Dakhla, and your home country's embassy in Madrid or Rabat.</p>

<h2>Lesson 6: Practice Role-play a Dispute Resolution</h2>

<p>Set up a 10-minute role-play with a colleague. One person plays the African supplier who delivered 20% less cocoa than contracted; the other plays the Spanish-speaking buyer. Use the phrases from Lesson 4. The buyer must: (1) state the problem factually, (2) reference the contract, (3) propose a solution, (4) set a deadline, and (5) state the consequence of non-resolution. The supplier must: (1) acknowledge the issue, (2) explain the cause, (3) offer a counter-proposal, and (4) agree on next steps. Record the role-play and review it for clarity, tone, and vocabulary accuracy.</p>

<h2>Quick Reference Checklist</h2>
<ol>
<li><strong>Document everything:</strong> Photos, invoices, names, times, locations, and witness contact details. No document, no case.</li>
<li><strong>Get the case number:</strong> Always request the <em>nmero de diligencias</em> or <em>nmero de expediente</em> from police or officials.</li>
<li><strong>Never pay bribes:</strong> It is illegal and destroys your business reputation. Report solicitation to your embassy.</li>
<li><strong>Carry a Spanish emergency card:</strong> Blood type, allergies, insurance policy number, embassy contact, and key phrases.</li>
<li><strong>Use written communication:</strong> Follow up every verbal conversation with an email or WhatsApp message summarizing what was agreed.</li>
<li><strong>Know your escalation ladder:</strong> Document Inform Negotiate Report Embassy. Do not skip steps.</li>
<li><strong>Save emergency numbers:</strong> 112 (EU/general), 115 (Equatorial Guinea ambulance), local police, embassy, and your lawyer.</li>
<li><strong>Review contracts before disputes:</strong> Ensure jurisdiction, arbitration clause, and delivery terms are in Spanish and enforceable in the relevant African market.</li>
</ol>
