9. Record Keeping and Pedigrees

Every cat in a breeding cattery must have a record. This is how you build a business that survives scrutiny.

Module 9 of 12 · Cat Breeding for Profit

Why records matter in cat breeding

Cats are high-value animals with legal and ethical obligations. Records are essential for:

  • Tracking lineage and avoiding inbreeding
  • Knowing which cats produce best
  • Meeting cat registry and regulatory requirements
  • Proving health testing and vaccine status
  • Supporting kitten sales with documentation
  • Managing costs per cat and litter
  • Meeting buyer and vet enquiries

Pedigree records

A pedigree is a documented record of a cat''s ancestors, usually 3-5 generations back. It tells buyers what to expect in the kitten.

Pedigree levelWhat it shows
3-generationParents, grandparents, great-grandparents
4-generationAdds great-great-grandparents (14 total cats)
5-generationAdds the next generation (30 total cats)

For a pedigree to have value, it must come from a recognised cat registry (CFA, TICA, GCCF, FIFe, SACC, etc.) with registered ancestors.

Blood typing

Cats have three blood types: A, B, AB. Type A is most common; type B occurs in certain breeds (British Shorthair, Devon Rex, others). Type AB is very rare.

If a type B queen is bred to a type A stud, kittens may inherit type A blood. Their mother''s colostrum (which contains anti-A antibodies) destroys their red blood cells - this is neonatal isoerythrolysis and can kill the whole litter.

QueenStudRisk
AANone
ABNone (kittens type A or AB)
BBNone
BAHigh - kittens may be type A and die from mother''s antibodies

Always test the blood type of every breeding cat before mating.

Kitten sales contracts

Every kitten should be sold with a written contract. Essential elements:

  1. Identification: Kitten name, breed, sex, DOB, microchip, registration number, colour, markings.
  2. Sire and dam: Names, registration numbers, health test results.
  3. Purchase price and deposit.
  4. Vaccination and deworming record.
  5. Health guarantee. Typically 48-72 hours for vet check, and 12-24 months for genetic conditions.
  6. Return policy. Some breeders require return of the kitten if the buyer cannot keep it.
  7. Spay/neuter agreement. For pet-quality kittens sold without breeding rights.
  8. Indoor-only clause (if applicable).
  9. Delivery / collection date.
  10. Signatures of seller, buyer, and witness.

Software and tools

  • Breeders Assistant - dedicated cat breeding management
  • PawPrint or Cat Breeder Software - registry-integrated tools
  • Excel / Google Sheets - flexible, cheap, works for any scale
  • Xero / QuickBooks - accounting for larger operations
  • Notebook - a physical daily log is still the simplest and most reliable

Data protection and confidentiality

  • Keep buyer records secure. Do not share buyer details with third parties without consent.
  • Health test results can be shared publicly (they help buyers and the breed).
  • Contracts should be stored for at least 5 years.
  • Vaccination records should be kept for the life of the cat.
  • If selling online, comply with local data protection laws.
The records rule: A cattery without records is a hobby. Detailed records tell you which cats earn their keep, which crosses work, which buyers pay on time, and where to cut costs. They also protect you legally when a buyer or regulator asks questions.
