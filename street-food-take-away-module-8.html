1. Churros and Chocolate Sauce

Churros are a popular street snack made from simple dough, fried until golden, and rolled in sugar. In Africa, you can make them with local ingredients. Start with 250g of all-purpose flour, 250ml of water, 1 tablespoon of sugar, and a pinch of salt. Boil water with sugar and salt, then remove from heat and stir in flour until a dough forms. Let it cool slightly, then transfer to a piping bag with a star nozzle. Heat oil in a deep pan to 180Â°C. Pipe 10cm strips into the oil, fry until golden brown, about 2 minutes per side. Drain on paper towels. For the chocolate sauce, melt 100g of dark chocolate with 100ml of milk and 1 tablespoon of sugar over low heat. Stir until smooth. You can also add a pinch of chili for a kick. Serve churros hot with the sauce in small cups. For street sales, pre-make the dough and keep it in a piping bag. Fry to order so they stay crispy. Package in paper cones or small boxes with a lid. Price per portion: 3-5 churros with sauce. In many African cities, you can sell each portion for 50-100 cents. Use local chocolate if available, or cocoa powder mixed with milk. This treat is cheap to make and has high profit margins. Keep oil clean by straining after each use. Train staff to pipe evenly. Display with a sign showing fresh frying. This lesson gives you a practical recipe and business tips to start selling churros today.

2. Crepes and Waffles on the Go

Crepes and waffles are versatile street foods that can be served sweet or savory. For crepes, mix 200g flour, 2 eggs, 300ml milk, 1 tablespoon sugar, and a pinch of salt. Whisk until smooth. Let batter rest for 30 minutes. Heat a non-stick pan with a little oil. Pour 60ml batter, swirl to coat, cook 1 minute, flip, cook 30 seconds. For waffles, use 250g flour, 2 eggs, 300ml milk, 2 tablespoons sugar, 1 tablespoon baking powder, and 50g melted butter. Mix and cook in a waffle iron until golden. In Africa, you can use a charcoal or gas stove with a waffle iron. Serve with local jams, honey, nutella, or fresh fruit like banana and mango. For street sales, pre-make crepes and waffles and keep them warm in a cooler box or insulated container. You can also cook to order for freshness. Package in paper wraps or small boxes. Add toppings like powdered sugar, chocolate sauce, or coconut. Price: 1-2 crepes or 1 waffle per portion. Sell for 1-2 dollars. To increase sales, offer combos with coffee or tea. Use a display with samples. Train staff to fold crepes neatly. This lesson helps you set up a crepe and waffle stand with minimal equipment.

3. Ice Cream and Gelato: Serving and Packaging

Ice cream and gelato are high-demand treats in hot African climates. You can make your own without a machine. For a basic ice cream, whip 500ml heavy cream with 200ml condensed milk and 1 teaspoon vanilla. Fold in crushed biscuits or fruit. Freeze for 6 hours. For gelato, use 500ml milk, 200ml cream, 150g sugar, and 4 egg yolks. Heat milk and cream, whisk yolks and sugar, combine and cook until thick. Cool and freeze, stirring every 30 minutes for 3 hours. For street sales, invest in a chest freezer or a cooler with dry ice. Serve in scoops using a scoop or spoon. Packaging options: cups, cones, or small tubs. In Africa, you can use recycled plastic cups or buy biodegradable ones. Keep servings consistent: one scoop is about 60ml. Price per scoop: 50 cents to 1 dollar. Offer toppings like chocolate sauce, nuts, or fruit. For hygiene, keep scoops in clean water between uses. Display in a freezer with a clear lid so customers see the product. Use a chalkboard menu. Sell at events, schools, and busy streets. This lesson covers production, packaging, and sales for ice cream and gelato.

4. Fried Doughs: Zeppole, Doughnuts, and Beignets

Fried doughs are cheap, filling, and popular across Africa. Zeppole are Italian doughnuts, doughnuts are ring-shaped, and beignets are square French doughnuts. All use similar dough. Basic recipe: 500g flour, 7g yeast, 50g sugar, 1 teaspoon salt, 250ml warm milk, 2 eggs, 50g butter. Mix and knead for 10 minutes. Let rise for 1 hour. For zeppole, scoop spoonfuls into hot oil (180Â°C) and fry until golden. For doughnuts, roll and cut rings, let rise again, then fry. For beignets, roll and cut squares, fry, and dust with powdered sugar. In Africa, you can add local flavors: cinnamon, nutmeg, or coconut. Serve plain or with chocolate sauce, honey, or jam. For street sales, pre-make dough and fry to order. Keep oil at the right temperature with a thermometer. Drain on paper towels. Package in paper bags or boxes. Price: 3-5 pieces per portion, sell for 50 cents to 1 dollar. To increase profit, offer fillings like custard or jam. Use a display with samples. Train staff to fry evenly. This lesson gives you three recipes and business tips for fried doughs.

5. Bubble Tea and Sweet Beverages

Bubble tea, also known as boba tea, is a sweet drink with chewy tapioca pearls. It originated in Taiwan but is now popular worldwide, including Africa. To make it, you need tapioca pearls, tea, milk, and sugar. Cook 100g tapioca pearls in boiling water for 20 minutes until soft. Drain and mix with 2 tablespoons of honey or sugar syrup. Brew 4 tea bags in 500ml hot water for 5 minutes. Add 200ml milk and sugar to taste. Cool. To serve, put pearls in a cup, add ice, and pour tea over. Use a wide straw. For street sales, pre-cook pearls and keep them in syrup. Assemble drinks to order. You can also offer fruit-flavored bubble tea using fruit syrups or fresh fruit. Other sweet beverages: smoothies, iced coffee, and lemonade. For smoothies, blend fruit with milk or yogurt and ice. For iced coffee, brew strong coffee, cool, and mix with milk and sugar. For lemonade, mix lemon juice, water, and sugar. Package in sealed cups with lids. Price: 1-2 dollars per cup. Offer loyalty cards. Display with colorful straws and flavors. This lesson covers preparation, packaging, and sales for bubble tea and sweet drinks.

6. Portion Control and Display for Impulse Sales

Portion control is key to profitability and customer satisfaction. For sweet treats, consistent portions mean you know your costs and customers get what they expect. Use measuring tools: scoops for ice cream, spoons for dough, and cups for drinks. For churros, a portion is 3-5 pieces. For crepes, one crepe. For waffles, one waffle. For ice cream, one scoop (60ml). For fried doughs, 3-5 pieces. For bubble tea, one cup (300ml). Weigh or count to ensure consistency. Display is crucial for impulse sales. Use clear containers, bright colors, and height. Place treats at eye level. Use signs with prices and names. For street stalls, use a table with a clean cloth, baskets, and tongs. Keep samples visible. For ice cream, a freezer with a clear top. For drinks, a cooler with ice and cups. Use lighting at night. Arrange items in rows, with best-sellers at the front. Change display often to keep it fresh. Offer combos: e.g., churros + drink for a discount. Train staff to upsell. This lesson helps you maximize sales through portion control and attractive displays.

Portion Control and Display for Impulse Sales

Impulse buying is triggered by sight, smell, and speed. A customer walking past your stall decides in under seven seconds. Your display must answer three questions instantly: What is it? How much? How fast?

Portion control rules:

  • Use a fixed scoop, ladle, or piping length. Never eyeball.
  • Weigh dough balls once per batch, not once per piece â then count.
  • Pre-portion sauces into small cups (30â50 ml). Sauce is the biggest hidden cost in churros and crepes.
  • Display the portion, not the ingredient. A customer buys "6 churros with chocolate" â not "200g of dough."
  • Keep a tally sheet. If you sold 40 portions and used 3 kg of flour instead of 2.4 kg, your portioning drifted.

Display rules:

  • Height sells. Raise your best product to eye level using a simple wooden crate or tiered tray.
  • Motion sells. A rotating doughnut display or a sizzling crepe griddle pulls traffic.
  • Colour contrast sells. Stack golden doughnuts against a dark cloth; place red sauce beside white cream.
  • Price clearly, in local currency, large enough to read from two metres.
  • Offer one "hero" combo â e.g., churros + bubble tea at a small discount â to raise average basket value.
