Client Workflows - From First Meeting to Final Delivery

🧠 Brain Opener

A real client does not pay for a chat. They pay for a finished deliverable, delivered on time, at the agreed price, in the agreed format. The workflow from first contact to final delivery is the whole business.

  • What are the five stages of a client project?
  • What can go wrong at each stage?
  • How do you keep a client coming back?
🎁 Reveal a good answer

Five stages: brief, proposal, build, review, deliver. Things go wrong when any stage is skipped. Clients come back when the delivery matches the proposal exactly, and when the process was smooth.

📖 Simba's First Paying Client

Simba, sixteen, in Nairobi, had been making posters for family friends for two years. Small jobs, small prices. Then his cousin introduced him to a real client - a new cafe opening on Ngong Road.

The owner, Mrs Mwangi, wanted a logo, a menu, and a social media launch plan. Three deliverables. Big job. Real money.

Simba wanted to say yes immediately. His cousin stopped him. "Ask for a meeting first," she said. "Sit with her. Take notes. Then write a proposal."

The meeting lasted forty minutes. Simba asked questions: what is the cafe called, what does it serve, who are the customers, when does it open, what is the budget, who else is involved, what does success look like. He wrote every answer in a notebook.

That night he wrote a one-page proposal. Three deliverables. Price for each. Timeline for each. What he needed from her. What he would do. What happened if he was late. What happened if she changed her mind.

Mrs Mwangi read it. She made one change - the timeline for the social media plan was too tight. Simba agreed. They both signed.

Over the next three weeks, Simba delivered all three. The logo on day four. The menu on day ten. The launch plan on day eighteen, three days before opening.

Mrs Mwangi paid in full and referred him to two other new businesses on the same street. Simba did not just get a first client. He got a workflow he has used ever since.

💡 The Big Idea

The five stages of client work

Brief (understand the client). Proposal (agree the exact terms). Build (do the work). Review (check with the client). Deliver (hand it over and get paid). Every stage matters. Skipping the brief means you build the wrong thing. Skipping the proposal means you argue about the price. Skipping the review means you deliver something the client did not want.

📚 Three Deep Lessons

1

The brief - asking the right questions

The brief is the meeting before the work. Ask: what exactly do you need, who is it for, when do you need it, what is your budget, who else is involved, what does success look like. Take notes. Repeat the answers back to check you understood.

Beginners skip the brief. They hear one thing and start working. Then they discover the client meant something else. The brief is not optional. It is the map for the whole project.

2

The proposal - agreeing the terms

A proposal is one page with six things: deliverables, price for each, timeline for each, what you need from the client, what happens if you are late, what happens if they change their mind. Both sides sign. This protects both of you.

Do not start work without a signed proposal. Ever. The proposal is what turns a conversation into a contract. Without it, the client can change anything at any time and you have no protection.

3

Build, review, deliver

During the build, send updates. Not daily, but at the halfway point and one week before delivery. Clients hate silence. A short update - "halfway done, on schedule" - keeps the relationship healthy.

The review is when the client sees a draft and asks for changes. Agree in advance how many rounds of changes are included. Two rounds is normal. Above that, charge for changes.

Delivery is the final step. On time. In the format agreed. With a short handover note: how to use the deliverable, what to do if there is a problem, how to reach you. Then send the invoice. Then ask for a referral.

🛠 Hands-On - Write a Proposal for a Real Client

You need

  • A notebook
  • A pen
  • A real person who needs something done
  1. Find one real person who needs one thing done (a poster, a website page, a menu, an event plan).
  2. Meet them for a fifteen-minute brief. Take notes.
  3. Write a one-page proposal with six parts: deliverables, price, timeline, what you need, late rules, change rules.
  4. Send it. Ask for one meeting to discuss.
  5. Get their agreement before you start any work.
  6. Deliver. Ask for one referral.
Why it works: The proposal is the difference between a favour and a business. Favours do not scale. Businesses do.

🎮 Game - Proposal Pitch

3 to 12 players - 25 min

One player acts as the client. The rest write proposals for the same job. Each proposal must have all six sections. The client reviews every proposal and picks the one that is clearest and most honest. Then the winner negotiates a price with the client in front of the class. The negotiation is scored on: (1) did they hold their price, (2) did they offer anything to the client, (3) did they stay polite.

🤖 AI-Era Skill

AI can help you draft proposals, but every number must come from your own judgement.

Try this prompt: "I am writing a proposal for a small cafe that needs a logo, a menu, and a social media plan. What should I include in each section, and what questions should I ask the client before I quote a price?"

Check the questions against the ones you would ask yourself. Then ask the actual client before quoting anything. The AI can prepare you. Only the client can tell you the truth.

🏆 Project Challenge

Complete One Real Client Project

  2. Do a fifteen-minute brief. Take notes.
  3. Write a one-page proposal. Send it. Get agreement.
