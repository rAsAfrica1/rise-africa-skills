4. Feeding, Watering and Weed Control

Cabbage is a heavy feeder. The right fertiliser schedule gives tight, heavy heads.

Module 4 of 6 · Cabbage Farming for Profit

Soil preparation before planting

InputRateWhen
Agricultural lime1-2 t/ha (if pH below 6.0)4-6 weeks before planting
Well-rotted manure or compost10-20 t/ha2-4 weeks before planting
DAP or compound150-200 kg/haAt planting, in the planting hole

The feeding schedule

StageProductRate
Week 3 after transplantCAN or urea150 kg/ha
Week 5CAN100-150 kg/ha
Head formation (week 7-9)CAN + SOP100 + 50 kg/ha

Split nitrogen into 2-3 applications. One big dose is lost to rain and leaching.

Signs of nutrient deficiency

NutrientSymptomFix
NitrogenPale yellow lower leaves, stunted growthCAN top dress

Watering

StageDry seasonRainy season
Week 1 after transplantEvery day, lightSkip if raining
Weeks 2-4Every 2-3 daysEvery 4-5 days
Head formation (weeks 5-10)Every 2 days, deepEvery 3-4 days
Final 2 weeks before harvestReduce 30%Reduce 30%
Never let cabbage dry out during head formation. Irregular watering causes split heads and reduces market value.

Weed control

  • First weeding: 2-3 weeks after transplant.
  • Second weeding: 5-6 weeks.
  • Mulch: 5-8 cm dry grass or straw reduces weeds 70% and conserves water.
  • Never hoe deeply - cabbage roots are shallow. Light scratching only.

Mulching

Mulch every cabbage bed with 5-8 cm of dry grass or straw. Benefits:

  • Reduces evaporation 30-50%
  • Suppresses weeds
  • Keeps soil cool - cabbage hates heat
  • Prevents soil splash onto leaves
The split-head rule: Cabbage splits when a dry spell is followed by heavy watering. Consistent moisture prevents it.
Practical exercise 4: Mulch half your cabbage bed. Leave the other half bare. Compare soil moisture, weed pressure, and head size at harvest.
