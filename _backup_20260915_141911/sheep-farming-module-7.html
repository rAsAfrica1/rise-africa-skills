7. Wool Production and Shearing

A wool fleece adds -30 per sheep per year. For wool breeds, it's a second income on top of meat.

Module 7 of 12 · Sheep Farming for Profit

Why wool matters

For wool breeds, shearing adds a second income stream on top of meat. A Merino fleece (3-6 kg) sells for $5-25 depending on quality. Dual-purpose breeds (Dohne Merino, Corriedale) produce both good meat and good wool. Even meat breeds produce some wool value.

Breed typeFleece weight/yearFleece value
Merino (fine wool)4-6 kg$15-40
Dohne Merino (medium)3-5 kg$10-25
Corriedale (medium)3-5 kg$8-20
Dual purpose cross2-4 kg$5-15
Hair sheep (Dorper, etc.)0-1 kg$0-5

Hair sheep (Dorper, Red Maasai, Blackhead Persian) do not produce valuable wool - they shed hair. Their value is entirely in meat.

Shearing schedule

ClimateShearing frequencyBest time
Cool highlandsOnce a yearEarly spring (before hot season)
Warm climateTwice a yearSpring and late summer
Tropical/hotTwice a year or shave allBefore each hot season

Never shear in cold weather - sheep can die from exposure. Give them 4-6 weeks to grow some wool back before the coldest month.

Pre-shearing preparation

  1. Withdraw feed 12 hours before - reduces stress and belly content.
  2. Keep water available - hydration reduces stress.
  3. Clean the shearing area - flat, dry, no debris.
  4. Gather equipment - shears (electric or hand), sharpener, iodine, marking crayon.
  5. Have a level surface - a shearing board or clean tarpaulin.
  6. Work in dry weather - wet wool is hard to shear and store.

Shearing methods

MethodSpeedCostBest for
Electric shears (professional)30-60 sheep/day$5-10 per sheepAny size flock
Hand shears (blade)5-10 sheep/dayFree if you own themSmall flocks, self-sufficiency
Specialist team100-300 sheep/day$3-6 per sheepLarge flocks, quality fleeces

Shearing technique

  1. Restrain the sheep by sitting it up on its rump - the "shearing position".
  2. Start at the belly - up the centre, then outwards.
  3. Move to the legs and crutch area - remove dirty wool first.
  4. Shear up the side of the body, keeping the skin stretched with your free hand.
  5. Long, smooth strokes - do not go back over the same area.
  6. Shear the neck and head last.
  7. Keep the fleece as one piece if possible - it separates into "fleece" (from the back and sides) and "skirtings" (from belly and crutch).
  8. Roll the fleece with the clean side out for grading.
  9. Treat any cuts with iodine.
Never shear a cut near the skin without treating. Even small cuts get infected. Iodine immediately.

Classing fleeces

Storage and sale

  • Store wool in dry, cool, ventilated sacks.
  • Keep off the ground on pallets.
  • Away from mice and moths - use camphor or dried lavender between bales.
  • Sell directly to a wool mill, textile factory, or wool-buyer agent for the best price.
  • Hand-spinners and craft markets pay a premium for clean, small quantities.
  • Wool mills pay by clean weight, not greasy weight - a clean fleece is worth more.

The wool rule: Shear on time. A fleece left on the sheep too long loses quality and the sheep suffers in heat. Shearing is not optional - it is basic husbandry.
