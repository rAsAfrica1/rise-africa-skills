4. Pellet Making

A ration formula is useless if the fish cannot eat it. This module teaches you how to make sinking and floating pellets on a small farm.

Module 4 of 6 · Fish Feed Production · quiz at the end

Lesson 4.1 - Sinking vs floating pellets

TypeFishAdvantageHow made
SinkingCatfish, tilapia bottom-feedingFish can find at bottom, less wasteWet mix pressed through a simple pelletiser or hand-pressed
Slow-sinkingTilapia, most fishFish can feed mid-waterWet mix pressed and dried without extrusion
FloatingTilapia, some catfishFarmer can see how much is eaten - no wasteExtruder with heat and pressure. More expensive equipment.

For most small farms, slow-sinking pellets are the practical choice. An extruder for floating pellets costs $800-3,000.

Lesson 4.2 - Step-by-step pellet making (small scale)

  1. Grind every ingredient to fine flour. Rice bran and cassava especially need to be very fine.
  2. Weigh every ingredient into a mixing bowl or tray.
  3. Mix dry ingredients thoroughly - 5 minutes of turning.
  4. Add oil and mix again.
  5. Add water slowly, about 30-40% of the dry weight, until the mix holds together when squeezed.
  6. Knead like bread dough for 3-5 minutes until uniform.
  7. Press through a meat mincer (or a simple hand pelletiser) with a 2-6 mm die, depending on fish size.
  8. Break pellets into short lengths (5-8 mm).
  9. Dry in the sun on clean trays or drying racks for 2-3 days until completely hard.
  10. Store in sealed sacks in a dry room.

Lesson 4.5 - Drying and storage

  • Dry in the sun on trays or racks. 2-3 days. Turn twice daily.
  • Target moisture: under 10%. A pellet that crumbles in your hand is dry enough.
  • Store in sealed sacks in a dry, dark room on pallets.
  • Use within 4 weeks for best quality. Fats go rancid in stored fish feed.
  • Never store damp pellets. Mould kills fish.
Never feed mouldy pellets. Aflatoxin from mouldy feed kills fish fast and has no cure. If the bag smells musty or you see any green or black patches, throw it away.
Practical exercise 4.1: Make 5 kg of sinking pellets using a meat mincer. Dry in the sun. Test how long a pellet holds together in a glass of water.
Practical exercise 4.2: Weigh your dried pellets. Compare wet weight to dry weight. That tells you the dry matter %.
