🧠 Brain Opener

AI learns from data. If the data is unfair, the AI is unfair. This is not a small problem. It has cost people jobs, loans, and freedom.

  • What is bias?
  • Can a machine be racist or sexist?
  • How do you find bias in a dataset?
🎁 Reveal a good answer

Bias is a systematic unfairness - not a random mistake, but a pattern that repeatedly disadvantages one group. A machine cannot be racist by itself, but it can learn racist patterns from data produced by racist systems. You find bias by asking: who is in this data, who is missing, and what does the AI do to each group?

📖 Chipo Audits the Exam Algorithm

Chipo was sixteen, in Harare, and had just discovered that her school used an AI to grade essays. The AI was trained on thousands of past essays.

She asked her teacher a simple question: "Whose essays was it trained on?"

The teacher did not know. She asked the headmaster. He did not know either. He asked the company that supplied the AI. The company sent a polite email explaining that the training data was proprietary.

Chipo was not satisfied. She wrote to the company. She asked specific questions: how many essays? From which schools? Which years? What languages? What regions?

Six weeks later, the company replied. The AI had been trained on 200,000 essays from schools in four countries. None of them African. None of them in Shona or Ndebele. Almost all of them urban.

Chipo wrote a short report. She showed it to her teacher. Her teacher showed it to the headmaster. The headmaster cancelled the contract.

The AI was not evil. It was not even aware of what it was doing. But it had been trained on data that did not include students like Chipo. It would have systematically graded her essays worse than an equivalent essay from a European student.

Chipo did not become famous. But for the students in her school, she had done something important. She had asked who was missing.

💡 The Big Idea

Bias hides in who is missing

Bias in AI usually comes from data that is incomplete, unbalanced, or reflects past unfairness. The AI cannot see the missing people. It only sees what is in the data. So the fix is not in the AI - it is in the data. Ask who is missing, and you have found the bias.

📚 Three Deep Lessons

1

Three types of bias

Historical bias: the data reflects a world that was unfair. If past hiring data shows mostly men were hired for engineering, the AI learns that men are engineers. Sampling bias: the data only covers part of the world. If you train a health AI only on city patients, it fails in rural areas. Label bias: the humans who labelled the data brought their own prejudices.

2

Real cases that changed laws

In 2018, Amazon built an AI to screen job applications. It was trained on ten years of resumes - mostly from men. It learned to downgrade resumes that mentioned women's colleges or women's chess clubs. Amazon cancelled the project.

In 2016, a US court used an AI called COMPAS to predict reoffending. An investigation found the AI was twice as likely to wrongly label a black defendant as high risk compared to a white defendant. The case changed how several US states use AI in courts.

In 2019, a major US hospital used an AI to decide which patients needed extra care. It was trained on past medical spending as a proxy for illness. Because less had been spent on Black patients historically, the AI concluded Black patients were healthier. It under-referred them by over 50 percent.

3

How to find bias in your own data

Ask five questions. Who is in the data? List the groups. Who is missing? List the groups not there. What does the AI do to each group? Test each one. Where did the labels come from? Were the labellers fair? Would you accept this if it were you? The last question is the most important one.

🛠 Hands-On - Audit a Small Dataset

You need

  • A notebook
  • Access to a small dataset - any list of examples with labels
  1. Find a small dataset - a list of names, a set of scores, a collection of photos.
  2. Write down the categories: age, gender, region, language, income.
  3. Count how many of each category are in the data.
  4. Write down which categories are missing.
  5. Write down what the AI would get wrong for each missing group.
  6. Write one sentence: This AI will fail for [group] because [reason].
Why it works: Every real AI team runs an audit like this before training. You can do it on paper.

🤖 AI-Era Skill

AI can learn from unfair data. Ask it to point out the bias in a scenario.

Try this prompt: "A company trained an AI to choose which job applicants to interview. The training data came from 10 years of hires, and in that period 90 percent of hires were men. What bias will the AI learn, and how would you fix it?"

The AI should say: the AI will learn that men are more likely to be good hires, and it will downgrade women. Fixes: collect more balanced data, remove gender from inputs, test outcomes for each group.

🌍 Real Stories from Africa

Amazon hiring AI, 2018

Amazon built an AI to rank job applicants. It was trained on resumes submitted over 10 years, and the tech industry had hired mostly men. The AI learned to penalise resumes that mentioned women. Amazon shut the project down. It is now a standard case study in AI ethics courses worldwide.

US healthcare algorithm, 2019

A widely-used US healthcare AI decided which patients needed extra care. It used past medical spending as a measure. Because less had historically been spent on Black patients, the AI assumed they were healthier. It under-referred over 50 percent of eligible Black patients. The algorithm was replaced after the discovery.

🏆 Project Challenge

Audit Three Real AIs

Find three real AI systems and audit each for bias.

  1. Pick three AIs you actually use or hear about.
  2. For each, find out what data it was trained on. If you cannot find out, note that - it is itself a problem.
  3. Write down who is missing from the data.
  4. Write down what the AI would get wrong for those missing groups.
  5. Write 3 sentences per AI recommending a fix.
  6. Share your audit with a teacher or parent.
