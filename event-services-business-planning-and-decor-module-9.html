1. Greeting clients respectfully in local languages and English

When a client walks into your event planning office or calls you for the first time, the first few seconds set the tone. In Africa, respect is shown through language. Start by greeting in the local language of your area, then switch to English if needed. For example, in Kenya, say 'Habari yako?' (How are you?) or 'Jambo' (Hello). In Nigeria, 'Bawo ni?' (Yoruba) or 'Sannu' (Hausa). In South Africa, 'Sawubona' (Zulu) or 'Molo' (Xhosa). In Ghana, 'Maakye' (Good morning) in Twi. These greetings show you value their culture.

After the local greeting, introduce yourself clearly: 'My name is [Your Name] from [Your Business]. I am happy to help you plan your event.' Use a warm tone and make eye contact if in person. If on phone, speak clearly and slowly. Ask how you may help: 'How can I assist you today?' Listen without interrupting.

In many African cultures, it is polite to ask about family or health before business. A simple 'How is your family?' or 'How are you today?' is appreciated. But keep it brief. Then move to business.

If the client speaks mainly English, still offer a local greeting. If they respond in a local language, try to continue in that language if you can. If not, apologize politely: 'I am still learning, but I will do my best.' Clients appreciate the effort.

Remember to use titles like 'Sir', 'Madam', 'Baba', 'Mama', 'Aunty', or 'Uncle' as appropriate. In many African settings, using these titles shows respect. For example, 'Good morning, Madam. How can I help you?'

Also, be mindful of body language: a slight bow, a handshake (if appropriate), or a smile goes a long way. In some cultures, a handshake with both hands is respectful.

Finally, always end the greeting by thanking them for choosing your business: 'Thank you for considering us. We are here to serve you.' This sets a positive tone for the entire conversation.

Practice these greetings with friends or family until you are comfortable. The more you use them, the more natural they become. A respectful greeting can turn a simple inquiry into a long-term client.

2. Responding to inquiries within 24 hours via phone or WhatsApp

In the event business, speed matters. A client who sends an inquiry is likely contacting several planners. If you respond within 24 hours, you have a much higher chance of winning the job. Aim to respond within 1 hour during working hours, but never later than 24 hours.

Use WhatsApp as your main tool. It is widely used across Africa, cheap, and allows you to send photos, voice notes, and documents. Set up a WhatsApp Business account (free) with your business name, logo, and a catalog of your services. When a message comes in, reply with a template but personalize it. For example: 'Hello [Client Name], thank you for your message. I am [Your Name] from [Business]. I would love to help with your event. Could you please share the date, location, and number of guests?'

If you receive many inquiries, create quick replies in WhatsApp Business: one for pricing, one for availability, one for booking process. But always add a personal touch.

If you cannot answer fully within 24 hours, send a holding message: 'Thank you for your inquiry. I am checking availability and will get back to you by [specific time].' This shows you are professional.

For phone calls, answer within 3 rings. If you miss a call, call back within 24 hours. Keep a notebook or a simple Excel sheet to track inquiries: date, client name, contact, event date, status, and follow-up date. This ensures you never forget.

When responding, always ask for the event date, location, type of event, number of guests, and budget range. This helps you give a quick quote. If you need time, say: 'I will send you a detailed quote by tomorrow at 5 PM.' Then do it.

Remember, communication is not just about speed but also clarity. Use simple English or the client's preferred language. Avoid jargon. If you promise something, keep it.

After sending a quote, follow up within 2 days: 'Hello, did you receive the quote? Do you have any questions?' This shows you care.

Finally, always thank the client for their inquiry, even if they don't book. You never know when they will come back or refer someone.

3. Listening to client feedback and making adjustments

Client feedback is a gift. It tells you what you did well and what you need to improve. In the event business, feedback can come during planning, on the event day, or after. Listen carefully and take notes.

When a client gives feedback, whether positive or negative, thank them first: 'Thank you for telling me this. I appreciate your honesty.' Then ask clarifying questions: 'Can you tell me more about what you didn't like?' or 'What would you prefer instead?' This shows you are listening, not defending.

During planning, if a client says they want a different color scheme or a different layout, don't argue. Say: 'Let me see how we can adjust that.' Then propose alternatives. For example, if they want red and gold but your supplier only has red and silver, suggest: 'We can use red and gold with some silver accents, or I can source gold fabric at an extra cost of 5000 naira. Which do you prefer?' Give options, not problems.

On the event day, if a client complains about timing (e.g., decor not ready on time), apologize sincerely and fix it quickly. Say: 'I am sorry for the delay. We are working on it now and will have it ready in 30 minutes.' Then update them every 15 minutes until done.

After the event, send a feedback form via WhatsApp or Google Forms. Ask specific questions: 'How would you rate the decor? How was the communication? What could we improve?' Offer a small discount on their next event if they complete the form.

Use the feedback. If multiple clients say your response time is slow, set an automatic reply. If they say your prices are high, consider adding cheaper packages. If they love your creativity, highlight that in your marketing.

Keep a feedback log. Review it monthly. Make one improvement each month. This is how you grow.

Remember, a client who complains is giving you a chance to keep their business. A client who says nothing may just leave. So welcome feedback, and always adjust.

4. Handling complaints about decor or timing professionally

Complaints are inevitable in the event business. How you handle them determines whether you keep the client and your reputation. When a client complains about decor or timing, follow these steps:

1. Listen without interrupting. Let them vent. Take notes.
2. Apologize sincerely, even if you think it's not your fault. Say: 'I am sorry you are not happy with the decor. I understand why you are upset.'
3. Ask questions to understand the exact issue: 'Which part of the decor is not as you expected?' or 'What time did you expect the setup to be ready?'
4. Offer a solution immediately. If the decor is wrong, fix it: 'We can change the centerpieces to white roses within 2 hours. Will that work?' If timing was off, explain what happened but focus on the fix: 'The truck broke down, but we have now hired another van and will be done by 3 PM.'
5. If the mistake is yours, compensate. Offer a discount, a free add-on, or a partial refund. For example: 'I will take 20% off the total for the inconvenience.'
6. Follow up after the event to ensure they are satisfied.

Never argue with a client in public. If they are angry at the venue, take them aside. Speak calmly and softly.

If the complaint is about timing, always build a buffer into your schedule. For a wedding, aim to finish decor 2 hours before guests arrive. If you promise 2 PM, plan to finish by 12 PM.

If a client complains on social media, respond publicly with an apology and a request to continue in private: 'We are sorry to hear this. Please send us a direct message so we can resolve it.' Then resolve it quickly.

Keep a record of all complaints and how you resolved them. This helps you avoid repeating mistakes.

Remember, a well-handled complaint can turn an unhappy client into a loyal one. They will tell others how you fixed the problem. So treat every complaint as a chance to prove your professionalism.

5. Following up after the event to thank the client

The event is over, but your job is not done. Following up after the event is crucial for building long-term relationships. Within 24 to 48 hours after the event, send a thank-you message via WhatsApp or a phone call.

Start with: 'Hello [Client Name], thank you for choosing us for your [event type]. We hope you and your guests enjoyed it. It was an honor to be part of your special day.'

Then ask for feedback: 'We would love to hear your thoughts. How was your experience with our decor and service?' If they are happy, ask for a testimonial or a review. Say: 'If you are happy, would you mind writing a short review on our Google Business page or Facebook? It would help us a lot.'

If they had issues, listen and offer to make it right. For example: 'I am sorry the lighting was not as bright as you wanted. We can offer a 15% discount on your next event.'

Also, send a small gift or a photo. For example, a framed picture of the decor or a short video highlight. This creates a lasting memory.

In your thank-you message, remind them of your referral program: 'If you refer a friend, you get 10% off your next booking.' This encourages word-of-mouth.

Keep a client database. Record their event date, preferences, and contact. On the anniversary of their event, send a message: 'Happy anniversary! We hope you are still enjoying the memories. Let us know if you need anything for your next celebration.'

For corporate clients, send a follow-up email with a summary of the event and a thank-you. Offer to plan their next event at a 5% discount if they book within 3 months.

Following up shows you care beyond the money. It turns a one-time client into a repeat client. Many event planners in Africa get 60% of their bookings from repeat clients and referrals. So never skip this step.

Make it a habit: after every event, set a reminder to follow up within 2 days. Your business will grow through relationships.

6. Building long-term relationships for future bookings

In the event business, your reputation and relationships are your biggest assets. A single client can bring you 5 more through referrals. So focus on building long-term relationships.

First, stay in touch. Don't only contact clients when you need business. Send occasional messages: 'Hello, how are you? I saw this beautiful decor idea and thought of you.' Or send a birthday message. Use a simple CRM tool like Google Contacts or a notebook to remember important dates.

Second, offer loyalty rewards. For example, after a client's second event with you, give them a 10% discount. After the third, give them a free add-on like a photo booth or extra centerpieces. This makes them feel valued.

Third, be reliable. Always deliver what you promise. If you say you will call at 10 AM, call at 10 AM. If you say the decor will be ready by 2 PM, be ready by 1:30 PM. Reliability builds trust.

Fourth, ask for referrals. After a successful event, say: 'If you know anyone planning an event, please pass on my number. I will take good care of them.' You can also offer a referral fee: 'For every friend you refer who books, you get 5,000 naira.'

Fifth, engage on social media. Post photos of your events (with permission). Tag clients. Respond to comments. This keeps you top of mind.

Sixth, host a small annual appreciation event for your top clients. For example, a free cocktail evening where you showcase new decor trends. This builds community.

Seventh, handle mistakes gracefully. If something goes wrong, fix it and follow up. Clients remember how you made them feel, not just the decor.

Finally, be patient. Relationships take time. A client may not book again for 2 years, but if you stay in touch, they will remember you.

In Africa, business is personal. People buy from people they trust. So invest in relationships. Your future bookings depend on it.

7. Practical Reference

Practical Reference: Customer Service and Communication in African Event Services

In the event services business planning, dcor, rentals, catering coordination, and on-site management your product is only half the story. The other half is how you communicate. In African markets, where trust is built through relationships and reputation travels fast through WhatsApp groups, church networks, and family referrals, customer service is not a soft skill. It is a revenue driver. A client who feels respected will book you again, refer you to three friends, and defend your business in public. A client who feels ignored will post about it, and in a market where most bookings come from word of mouth, that costs you real money.

This lesson gives you a practical, business-first framework for handling clients from first greeting to repeat booking. It covers language, response time, feedback loops, complaints, follow-up, and relationship building with templates and tools you can use today.

1. Greeting Clients Respectfully in Local Languages and English

In most African markets, the first few seconds of an interaction set the tone for the entire business relationship. A greeting is not just politeness it is a signal that you see the client as a person, not a transaction. In many cultures across the continent, skipping the greeting and going straight to business is considered rude and can cost you the deal before you even quote a price.

Start with a local greeting, then transition into English (or the client's preferred language). Here are practical examples from major African markets:

Business tip: Train every staff member who answers the phone or WhatsApp to use at least one local greeting correctly. Mispronounced greetings can backfire so practice. If you are not sure of the client's language, start with English and ask, "Which language would you prefer?" That question itself shows respect.

2. Responding to Inquiries Within 24 Hours via Phone or WhatsApp

In African event services, WhatsApp is the dominant business communication channel. A 2023 study by GSMA found that over 85% of small businesses in Sub-Saharan Africa use WhatsApp for customer communication. Clients expect a reply within hours, not days. If you take 48 hours to respond, they have already booked someone else.

Inquiry Type Target Response Time Channel Action
New booking inquiry Within 2 hours (max 24) WhatsApp / Phone Acknowledge, ask event date, guest count, budget range
Quote request Within 24 hours WhatsApp / Email Send itemised quote with photos of previous work
Complaint or issue Within 1 hour Phone call preferred Listen, apologise, offer solution, confirm in writing
Follow-up question Within 4 hours WhatsApp Answer clearly, confirm next step
Post-event feedback Within 48 hours after event WhatsApp / Call Thank client, ask for review and referral

Source: Adapted from GSMA, Mobile Economy Sub-Saharan Africa (2023); and International Finance Corporation (IFC), SME Customer Service Toolkit for Emerging Markets (2022).

Practical setup: Use WhatsApp Business with auto-reply for after-hours messages: "Thank you for contacting [Business]. We have received your message and will respond by [time] tomorrow. For urgent matters, call [number]." This alone prevents clients from feeling ignored.

SAFETY WARNING Client Data and Payment Fraud

Never share client phone numbers, addresses, or event details with third parties without written consent. In several African markets, fraudsters pose as clients or suppliers to redirect deposits. Always verify payments through official bank or mobile money statements not screenshots. Be especially cautious of "urgent" requests to change bank details via WhatsApp. Confirm any payment change by phone call to a known number. Protect client data in line with your country's data protection law (e.g., Kenya's Data Protection Act 2019, Nigeria's NDPR 2019, South Africa's POPIA 2013).

3. Listening to Client Feedback and Making Adjustments

Feedback is free market research. In the event business, clients often give feedback in the moment during setup, during the event, or in a casual comment afterward. Your job is to listen actively, repeat back what you heard, and adjust where possible.

Active listening steps:

  1. Stop and face the client. Put down your phone. Make eye contact.
  2. Listen without interrupting. Let them finish, even if you disagree.
  3. Repeat back. "So what I'm hearing is that the table centrepieces are too tall and block conversation. Is that correct?"
  4. Acknowledge and act. "You're right. We can lower them now. Give me 10 minutes."
  5. Follow up. After the event, ask: "Did the adjustment work for you?"

Business tip: Keep a simple feedback log (a notebook or WhatsApp note) of every comment positive and negative. Review it monthly. Patterns will show you what to fix in your standard dcor packages or timing schedules.

4. Handling Complaints About Dcor or Timing Professionally

Complaints are inevitable. How you handle them determines whether you keep the client or lose them forever. Use the LAST method:

  • L Listen. Let the client speak fully without defence.
  • A Apologise. Say sorry for the experience, even if the fault is not entirely yours. "I'm sorry this happened. Let me fix it."
  • S Solve. Offer a concrete solution with a timeline. "We can replace the broken centrepieces within 30 minutes."
  • T Thank. Thank them for telling you. "Thank you for letting me know this helps us do better."

Example complaint wrong dcor colour: A client booked gold and white, but the team set up silver and white. The client is upset 30 minutes before guests arrive.

Professional response: "I am very sorry. This is our mistake. We have gold covers in the van. Give us 20 minutes to change the chair sashes and table runners. I will personally check every table. And I will deduct 10% from your final invoice for the inconvenience."

Example complaint late setup: The dcor team arrives 45 minutes late. The client is stressed.

Professional response: "I apologise for the delay. We had a transport issue and I should have called you earlier. We are now setting up and will finish by [time]. To make it right, we will handle the cleanup at no extra cost and give you a discount on your next booking."

Business tip: Never argue with a client in front of guests. Move the conversation aside, lower your voice, and solve the problem first. Discuss fault and compensation after the event.

5. Following Up After the Event to Thank the Client

Most event businesses disappear after the event. This is a missed opportunity. A follow-up message within 48 hours does three things: it shows professionalism, it opens the door for feedback, and it creates a reason to ask for a referral or review.

WhatsApp follow-up template:

"Good morning [Client Name]. Thank you for trusting [Business] with your [event type] on [date]. It was an honour to be part of your celebration. We would love to hear your feedback what did you like, and what could we improve? If you were happy, a Google review or a referral to a friend would mean a lot to us. We look forward to serving you again. [Your Name]"

Business tip: Send a small thank-you gesture where appropriate a digital photo album of the dcor setup, or a discount code for the next booking. This costs little but builds loyalty.

6. Building Long-Term Relationships for Future Bookings

In African event services, repeat clients and referrals are the cheapest form of marketing. A satisfied client who books you for a wedding will often call you for a birthday, a corporate function, or a baby shower. Their friends will call too.

Relationship-building actions:

  • Remember key dates. Send a WhatsApp message on the client's event anniversary or birthday.
  • Offer loyalty pricing. "As a returning client, you get 10% off your next booking."
  • Ask for referrals directly. "If you know anyone planning an event, please share my number."
  • Stay visible. Post your recent work on WhatsApp Status, Instagram, and Facebook. Clients who see your work regularly will remember you.
  • Be reliable. Reliability is the strongest relationship builder in markets where many vendors are unpredictable.

Quick Reference Checklist

  • Greet every client in a local language, then English never skip the greeting.
  • Reply to all inquiries within 24 hours; urgent complaints within 1 hour.
  • Use WhatsApp Business with auto-reply for after-hours messages.
  • Listen to feedback without interrupting; repeat it back and adjust.
  • Handle complaints with the LAST method: Listen, Apologise, Solve, Thank.
  • Send a thank-you follow-up within 48 hours after every event.
  • Ask for a review or referral in every follow-up message.
  • Log feedback monthly and fix recurring issues in your standard packages.
  • Protect client data and verify all payments through official statements.
