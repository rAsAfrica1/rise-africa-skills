Lessons

1. Introduction and Why It Matters

Finishing is the difference between a metal piece that looks like scrap and one that sells for top price. In African markets, a well-finished metal chair or table can fetch 40-60% more than an unfinished one. Finishing protects against rust, which is the number one killer of metal furniture in humid coastal cities like Mombasa, Lagos, or Durban. It also adds colour, texture, and style that match local tastesfrom bright powder-coated colours popular in Nairobi to antique patinas favoured in Marrakech. Without finishing, even the strongest welded frame will corrode within months, especially during rainy seasons. You have three main options: paint (cheap, easy, but less durable), powder coating (tough, professional, needs electricity and a oven), and patina (chemical treatment that gives an aged, artistic look). Each has its place. For a beginner, paint is the best starting point because you can do it in your workshop with minimal equipment. Powder coating requires sending pieces to a specialist, but its worth it for high-end clients. Patina is for artistic pieces and restoration work. This module will teach you all three, step by step, using tools and materials available in most African cities. By the end, youll be able to finish a metal stool or table that looks professional and lasts for years. Remember: finishing is 30% of the total work time but 70% of the customers first impression. Dont rush it.

2. Tools and Materials

To do a professional finish, you need the right tools. For painting: an angle grinder with a wire cup brush (for removing rust and old paint), sandpaper (80, 120, 220 grit), clean rags, a spray gun or aerosol cans, and a compressor if using a gun. For powder coating, youll need a powder coating gun (if doing it yourself) or a local powder coater, an oven that reaches 200C, and a sandblasting setup or chemical stripper. For patina: chemical solutions like liver of sulfur, copper sulfate, or ferric chloride, plus a torch for heat patina, and wax or lacquer for sealing. Essential safety gear: nitrile gloves, respirator mask (not just a dust mask), safety goggles, and a fire extinguisher. In Africa, you can source most of these from hardware stores in big cities or from online suppliers like Jumia or local industrial markets. For paint, use automotive-grade acrylic enamel or epoxy paintthey cost more but last 3-5 times longer than cheap enamel. A 1-litre can of quality paint covers about 8-10 square metres. For powder coating, polyester powders are best for outdoor furniture. For patina, you can make your own copper sulfate solution by dissolving 50g of copper sulfate in 1 litre of water. Always buy from reputable suppliers; fake chemicals wont give consistent results. Budget: a basic paint finishing kit (grinder, sandpaper, spray gun, compressor) costs around $300-500. Powder coating setup is $800-1500. Patina chemicals are cheap ($20-50). Start with paint, then add powder coating when you have steady orders.

3. Step by Step Method

Step 1: Preparation. Remove all rust, mill scale, and old paint using an angle grinder with a wire cup brush. Then sand with 80-grit, then 120-grit, then 220-grit for a smooth surface. Wipe down with a clean rag and a degreaser (acetone or lacquer thinner). Any oil or dust will cause the finish to peel. Step 2: Priming. For paint, apply a metal primer (zinc phosphate or epoxy) within 30 minutes of cleaning. Spray a thin coat, let it flash off for 10-15 minutes, then apply a second coat. For powder coating, the metal is first sandblasted or chemically cleaned, then the powder is sprayed electrostatically and baked at 200C for 10-15 minutes. For patina, you skip primer and apply chemicals directly to clean metal. Step 3: Application. For paint, spray two to three thin coats, waiting 15-20 minutes between coats. Keep the gun 20-25 cm from the surface and move in steady passes. For powder coating, the piece is hung and grounded, then powder is sprayed until a uniform layer forms, then baked. For patina, brush or spray the chemical solution, let it react for 5-20 minutes, then rinse with water and dry. Step 4: Sealing. For paint, no sealing needed if using quality paint. For powder coating, the baking process is the seal. For patina, apply a clear lacquer or wax to stop the reaction and protect the finish. Step 5: Curing. Let paint cure for 24-48 hours before use. Powder coating is ready immediately after cooling. Patina should cure for 24 hours before handling. Always test on a small scrap piece first.

4. Common Mistakes and Fixes

Mistake 1: Not cleaning the metal properly. Dust, oil, or rust will make paint or powder flake off. Fix: always degrease with acetone and sand to bare metal. Mistake 2: Applying too thick a coat. Thick paint runs, sags, and takes forever to dry. Fix: apply multiple thin coats instead of one thick one. Mistake 3: Painting in humid or dusty conditions. Humidity causes blushing (white haze) and dust sticks to wet paint. Fix: paint in a well-ventilated but dust-free area, ideally indoors with a fan. If humidity is above 70%, wait for a dry day. Mistake 4: Using the wrong thinner. Cheap thinner can react with paint and cause orange peel. Fix: use the thinner recommended by the paint manufacturer. Mistake 5: Not grounding the piece for powder coating. Without proper grounding, powder wont stick. Fix: ensure the hook and rack are clean and conductive. Mistake 6: Overheating powder coating. If the oven is too hot or the piece bakes too long, the powder turns yellow or brittle. Fix: use an oven thermometer and stick to 200C for 10-15 minutes. Mistake 7: Patina turning out too dark or uneven. Fix: dilute the chemical, apply in thin layers, and build up slowly. Mistake 8: Skipping the sealing step on patina. Without sealer, the patina will continue to react and may rub off on clothes. Fix: always apply a clear coat or wax. Mistake 9: Not letting paint cure fully. Using the furniture too soon causes scratches. Fix: wait 48 hours. Mistake 10: Using a dirty spray gun. This causes spitting and uneven finish. Fix: clean your gun immediately after every use with the appropriate solvent.

5. Safety and Best Practice

Finishing involves chemicals and processes that can harm you if youre careless. Always wear nitrile gloves when handling paint, thinners, and patina chemicals. Latex gloves are not enoughthey dissolve. Wear a respirator mask with organic vapour cartridges when spraying paint or powder. A dust mask is useless against fumes. Work in a well-ventilated area: open doors and windows, use a fan to blow fumes away from you, and never work in a closed room. Keep a fire extinguisher nearby because paint thinners and solvents are highly flammable. Store chemicals in original labelled containers, away from heat and children. For powder coating, the oven must be in a separate, fireproof area. Never use a home oven for powder coatingresidues are toxic. For patina, some chemicals like liver of sulfur smell like rotten eggs; work outdoors or with strong ventilation. Best practices: mix only what you need, never return used chemicals to the original container, and dispose of waste properlynever pour down the drain. Keep a first aid kit with eye wash and burn cream. If you get chemicals on your skin, wash immediately with soap and water. If in eyes, rinse for 15 minutes and seek medical help. Also, protect your work area with drop cloths or cardboard. And always test your finish on a scrap piece before applying to the final product. Finally, maintain your tools: clean spray guns, change respirator cartridges regularly, and inspect hoses for leaks. Safety is not optionalits part of professionalism.

6. Practical Exercise

For this exercise, you will finish a small metal stool or side table using paint. You will need: one bare metal stool (welded and cleaned), 80/120/220 grit sandpaper, angle grinder with wire brush, acetone, clean rags, metal primer, acrylic enamel paint (colour of your choice), spray gun or aerosol cans, respirator, gloves, and a dust-free area. Step 1: Grind all welds and rust with the wire brush, then sand with 80, 120, and 220 grit. Wipe with acetone. Step 2: Spray a thin coat of primer. Wait 15 minutes, then spray a second coat. Let dry for 1 hour. Step 3: Spray the first colour coat. Keep the gun 20 cm away, use steady passes. Wait 20 minutes. Spray second coat. Wait 20 minutes. Spray third coat if needed for full coverage. Step 4: Let cure for 48 hours. Step 5: Inspect for runs, dust, or missed spots. If found, sand lightly with 400 grit and touch up. Step 6: Take a photo and compare with a professionally finished piece. Note what you could improve. For extra practice, try a patina on a small piece of scrap metal: clean it, apply copper sulfate solution, let it react, rinse, dry, and seal with wax. Record the time and results. This exercise should take 3-4 hours over two days. Once you can consistently produce a smooth, even finish, you are ready to charge clients for finishing services. Remember: the quality of your finish determines your reputation.

<h3 style="margin-top:0; color:#c0392b;"> RED SAFETY WARNING Isocyanate and Solvent Exposure</h3>
<p style="margin-bottom:8px;"><strong>Two-pack polyurethane and epoxy hardeners contain isocyanates.</strong> Isocyanate exposure can cause occupational asthma, which is permanent and can be fatal. Solvent-based paints and thinners can cause chronic nerve damage and are highly flammable.</p>
<p style="margin-bottom:8px;"><strong>Mandatory controls in every African workshop:</strong></p>
<ul style="margin:0 0 8px 18px;">
<li>Wear a <strong>supplied-air respirator or an approved organic vapour / particulate cartridge mask</strong> a dust mask is not enough.</li>
<li>Spray only in a <strong>well-ventilated booth or open-sided structure</strong>; never in a closed room.</li>
<li>Keep <strong>no ignition sources</strong> within 10 m; store thinners in a locked, grounded metal cabinet.</li>
<li>Wear nitrile gloves and eye protection; wash skin immediately after contact.</li>
<li>Keep a <strong>dry powder or CO fire extinguisher</strong> within 5 m of the spray area.</li>
<li>Train every worker on the safety data sheet (SDS) before first use this is a legal requirement in Kenya (DOSHS), Nigeria (NOSH), and Ghana (GSA).</li>
</ul>
<p style="margin-bottom:0;"><em>If a worker develops wheezing, chest tightness, or shortness of breath after spraying, stop work and seek medical attention immediately. Report the exposure.</em></p>

<h3>Quick Reference Checklist Bench-Side Card</h3>
<ol style="font-family:Arial, sans-serif; font-size:15px; line-height:1.7;">
<li><strong>Inspect and prep steel:</strong> Remove mill scale, rust, weld spatter, and oil. Grind to bare metal on all visible surfaces. Wipe with solvent and let flash off.</li>
<li><strong>Apply pretreatment:</strong> Zinc or iron phosphate conversion coat on all surfaces, including edges and inside tubes where possible. Rinse and dry fully.</li>
<li><strong>Prime within 4 hours:</strong> Apply epoxy or zinc-rich primer at the specified DFT. Check with a gauge. Do not leave bare steel overnight in humid conditions.</li>
<li><strong>Mix and thin correctly:</strong> Follow the technical data sheet (TDS) exactly. Use the correct thinner, at the correct ratio, at the correct temperature.</li>
<li><strong>Control the environment:</strong> Spray when temperature is 1530C and relative humidity is below 85%. Avoid direct sun and strong wind.</li>
<li><strong>Apply build and topcoats:</strong> Thin wet coats, 2025 cm spray distance, 50% overlap. Allow flash-off between coats.</li>
<li><strong>Inspect before cure:</strong> Check for runs, sags, pinholes, and dry spray. Fix defects before the film hardens.</li>
<li><strong>Cure and protect:</strong> Respect full cure time before handling, packing, or delivery. Stack with foam or paper between finished parts.</li>
</ol>
