Many pesticides sold in open-air markets across Africa are counterfeit, expired, or repackaged without labels. A 2021 study by the Centre for Agriculture and Bioscience International (CABI) found that up to 40% of pesticides sampled in Kenya, Nigeria, and Tanzania were substandard. Using them is not just a crop risk â it is a poisoning risk for you, your workers, and your customers.

  • Never buy pesticides from unlicensed vendors or in unlabelled containers. Buy only from registered agro-dealers with a valid licence.
  • Always wear gloves, boots, and a face mask when mixing or spraying. No exceptions â not even for "mild" chemicals.
  • Respect the pre-harvest interval (PHI) on the label. Spraying tomatoes the day before harvest and selling them is a criminal act in most jurisdictions and a health hazard.
  • Store chemicals in a locked cabinet, away from feed, food, and children. Never decant into soda bottles.
  • If you suspect poisoning, call your local poison control centre or the nearest health facility immediately. Do not induce vomiting unless instructed.

Quick Reference Checklist â Daily and Weekly Actions for the Farm Manager

  1. Morning mortality check: Count dead birds or wilted trays before 8 a.m. Record the number. A spike above 1% in a single day means you investigate immediately â do not wait for tomorrow.
  2. Feed and water intake: Weigh or estimate feed consumed. If intake drops by more than 10% from the previous day, check for disease, heat stress, or feed spoilage.
  3. Biosecurity gate: Ensure the footbath is charged with fresh disinfectant. No visitor enters the production area without boots and a clean coat. This one habit prevents the worst outbreaks.
  4. Cold chain check: For horticulture, verify that your cooler or evaporative cooling chamber is below 10Â°C for leafy greens and below 12Â°C for tomatoes. If you have no cooler, harvest in the early morning and sell within 6 hours.
  5. Record the money: Every sale, every input purchase, every labour payment. If you do not know your cost per kg, you cannot know if you are profitable. Use a notebook or a simple phone app â but use something.
  6. Weekly weight sample: Weigh 10 birds or 10 fruit crates. Compare to the benchmark table above. If you are below "average," change something this week â feed, water, or stocking density.
  7. Chemical and vaccine inventory: Check expiry dates. Rotate stock. Discard anything expired according to local regulations â never use it "just this once."
  8. Market price scan: Call two buyers or check the local market price before you sell. Knowing the price in the next town can add 10â20% to your margin.
