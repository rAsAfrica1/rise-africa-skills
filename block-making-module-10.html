Paving Slab / Flag

8 lessons · 30 quiz questions · Easy difficulty

1 Slab vs paver

Slabs (also called flags) are the larger, flatter cousins of pavers. Key differences:

FeaturePaverSlab
Size200 × 100 mm typical300 × 300 to 900 × 600 mm
Thickness60-80 mm30-50 mm
Weight1.5-2 kg8-30 kg
UseTraffic areasPatios, footpaths, decorative
MakingMachine, small mouldsWet casting in large moulds

Slabs are typically wet-cast (pourable concrete) rather than press-vibrated like pavers. They use larger moulds and set more slowly.

2 Slab mould sizes

Standard slab sizes (with typical thickness):

  • 300 × 300 × 30 mm — smallest, most common, easy to handle
  • 400 × 400 × 40 mm — garden paving, patios
  • 450 × 450 × 40 mm — pedestrian areas, larger patios
  • 600 × 600 × 50 mm — public areas, commercial
  • 900 × 600 × 50 mm — high-end landscaping, decorative

Moulds are made from:

  • Steel: Longest life (10,000+ slabs). Best for volume. £150–£600 per mould.
  • Fibreglass: Light, cheaper, shorter life (1,000–3,000 slabs). £80–£300 per mould.
  • Rubber moulds: For textured surfaces (cobble, slate patterns). £100–£400 each.

Start with 2–3 fibreglass moulds of 300 × 300 × 30 mm. Cost: £250–£900 to begin.

3 Mixing decorative slabs

Slab mix is wetter than paver mix — it needs to flow into large moulds and self-level.

Standard slab mix:

  • Cement: 1 part
  • Sharp sand: 2 parts
  • Crushed stone (4–6 mm): 2 parts
  • Water: enough for a thick pourable consistency

Cement use: 1 bag (50 kg) makes 4–6 slabs of 400 × 400 × 40 mm.

For textured slabs (cobble, slate, wood-grain): use a rubber mould with the pattern. Pour the mix, vibrate briefly on a vibrating table, and demould after 24 hours. The pattern transfers from the rubber mould.

For polished slabs: use a two-layer pour — a base layer of normal concrete, then a smooth top layer of fine cement-sand mix. Polish with wet cloth after curing.

4 Pigments and oxides

Coloured slabs sell for 2–3× plain concrete slabs. Pigments used:

  • Iron oxide (red, brown, black): $2–$5 per kg. Most common. 3–5% of cement weight.
  • Chromium oxide (green): $8–$15 per kg. Less common. 2–4% of cement weight.
  • Titanium dioxide (white): $5–$10 per kg. 5–8% of cement weight.
  • Carbon black: $1–$3 per kg. 2–3% of cement weight.

How to add pigment:

  1. Weigh the pigment (never guess)
  2. Mix pigment dry with a small amount of cement first (prevents clumping)
  3. Add this premix to the main batch
  4. Mix for 2 minutes longer than usual

Pigment adds £1–£5 per m² of slab. Retail price increase: £8–£20 per m². Big margin.

5 Tamping flat surfaces

Flat top surfaces are what sell slabs. Two techniques:

Vibrating table: Place the mould on a vibrating table and pour. Vibration removes air bubbles and levels the mix. Cost: $500–$3,000 for a table sized 1 m².

Hand tamping: Pour mix into mould, then tamp with a wooden float or steel trowel. Back-and-forth motion settles the mix and gives a smooth surface. Slower but works for small volume.

For textured or patterned slabs, only vibrate lightly — over-vibration loses the pattern.

After tamping, cover the mould with plastic to cure. Do not let it dry out.

6 Demoulding large slabs

Slabs are heavy — 400 × 400 × 40 mm slab weighs about 16 kg. Demoulding needs care:

  1. Leave slabs in the mould for 24 hours (longer than pavers)
  2. Flip the mould onto a soft surface (foam or sand) — not on hard concrete
  3. Gently tap the mould sides with a rubber mallet
  4. Lift the mould straight up
  5. The slab stays on the soft surface
  6. Move to curing area (two people for large slabs)

Large slabs crack if dropped. Work slowly. Two people minimum for 600 × 600 mm slabs.

7 Curing and finishing

Slabs cure the same way — 7 days damp, 28 days full strength. But finishing matters:

  • Brushed finish: After demoulding, brush the surface with a stiff broom to expose the aggregate. Non-slip, decorative.
  • Smooth finish: Keep the surface sealed with plastic sheet so it dries slowly. Smoother, more polished look.
  • Exposed aggregate: Wash the top 1–2 mm with a hose after 6 hours — the cement washes away, revealing the aggregate. Premium decorative finish.
  • Sealing: After full cure, apply a clear concrete sealer. Protects against staining and enhances colour. £2–£5 per m².

Cured slabs are ready to sell at 7 days, full strength at 28 days.

8 Bulk sales to landscapers

Slabs sell to different customers than pavers. Focus your sales effort:

Landscapers and garden designers: Buy in bulk for high-end projects. Want consistent colour and texture.

Homeowners doing DIY: Buy in small quantities for patios. Instagram and Pinterest are the biggest channels.

Builders and contractors: Buy for footpaths, driveways, and public walkways.

Councils and municipalities: Buy in large volumes for public realm projects.

Pricing (Africa, 2026):

  • Plain grey slabs: $8–$15 per m²
  • Coloured slabs: $15–$25 per m²
  • Textured/patterned: $18–$30 per m²
  • Exposed aggregate premium: $25–$45 per m²

Cost to make: $4–$8 per m². Margin: 40–60%.

💰 Slabs are the highest-margin paving product. Lower volume than pavers, but 2–3× the price per m². Perfect for premium markets.
