6. Weed, Pest and Disease Control

Rice is a poor competitor for the first 4-6 weeks. What you do in that window decides the crop.

Module 6 of 12 · Rice Farming for Profit

Weed control - the biggest yield factor

Rice is a poor competitor in the first 4-6 weeks after planting. Weeds can reduce yield by 40-80% if not controlled.

The critical weed-free period

StageWeed competition
Days 0-15Critical - must be weed-free
Days 15-30Very important
Days 30-45Rice canopy closes, less competition
Days 45+Rice shades out most weeds

Blast - the biggest rice disease

Rice blast (Magnaporthe oryzae) causes 10-30% yield loss worldwide. Signs:

  • Leaf blast: diamond-shaped spots with grey centre and brown border
  • Neck blast: black-brown lesion on panicle neck, causing panicle to fall over
  • Node blast: black lesions on stems

Control:

  • Resistant varieties (best option)
  • Balanced nitrogen - excess N makes plants susceptible
  • Silicon application - strengthens stems
  • Fungicide: tricyclazole or isoprothiolane at booting and flowering
  • Seed treatment before planting

Integrated Pest Management (IPM) for rice

  1. Prevent: Resistant varieties, clean seed, balanced nutrition, correct spacing.
  2. Monitor: Weekly scouting, pheromone traps, sticky traps.
  3. Cultural: Water management, crop rotation, field sanitation.
  4. Biological: Encourage natural enemies (spiders, wasps, dragonflies).
  5. Chemical: Only at threshold, correct product, correct rate, respect PHI.

Quelea birds - the African rice problem

Quelea are small weaver birds that gather in flocks of millions. They can strip a rice field in a day.

  • Harvest as soon as grain is mature - do not wait
  • Community scaring: noise, flags, whistles, reflective tape
  • Some countries use controlled spraying (by government)
  • Plant early-maturing varieties to finish before birds arrive
  • Plant a small "sacrificial" plot away from the main field

Snails - the transplant problem

Golden apple snails (introduced species) eat young transplanted seedlings. Control:

  • Handpick snails and egg masses (pink clusters on stems)
  • Drain the field briefly - snails drown or dry out
  • Introduce ducks after transplanting - they eat snails
  • Use molluscicide (niclosamide) if heavy infestation
  • Transplant older, tougher seedlings
The weed rule: Weed rice at 2, 4, and 6 weeks after transplant. Miss any of these windows and you lose 20-40% of yield. Weeding early beats weeding often.
Practical exercise 6: Scout your rice weekly for 6 weeks. Record weeds, pests, and diseases. Note which areas have the most pressure. Spray only above threshold.
