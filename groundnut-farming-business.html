The Business Seed — Groundnut Farming

This is not a farming course. It is a small business blueprint. Read this before you buy a single seed.

What is here

1. The Business Idea 2. Three Capital Tiers 3. Value-Addition Ladder 4. Buyers You Will Approach 5. One-Page Plan

1. The Business Idea

You are training to run one of these real businesses around groundnut:

  • Raw kernel production — grow, dry, sort, bag and sell to local market and processors
  • Peanut butter manufacturing — kernels → roasted → ground → jarred → retail (3–5x margin)
  • Groundnut oil extraction — kernels → press → bottled oil + cake for feed
  • Seed multiplication — grow certified varieties under contract, sell seed at 2–3x food price

2. Three Capital Tiers

Tier 1 — Smallholder Producer

–

0.5–1 hectare. Certified seed, gypsum, basic disease control. Sell raw kernels at local market and to one processor. Save seed for next season from best plants.

Tier 2 — Commercial Producer + Peanut Butter

,500 – ,000

2–4 hectares plus a small grinder (–2,000). Aflatoxin testing at harvest. Sell graded kernels to processors and packaged peanut butter under your own label.

Tier 3 — Groundnut Enterprise

,000+

5+ hectares or contract farming. Own mill, roaster, press, packaging line. Sell kernels, butter, oil and seed into three markets at once. 3–8 staff.

Rule: start at the tier your water and market can support. A 1-hectare plot run well beats 5 hectares run badly.

3. Value-Addition Ladder

Master one rung, then climb. Each rung pays for the equipment of the next.

4. Buyers You Will Approach

  • Local market vendors — daily small lots, cash
  • Urban retail and supermarkets — weekly, 30-day terms, requires packaging
  • Peanut butter and snack processors — 5–50 t lots, aflatoxin tested
  • Oil millers — lower grade, volume absorption
  • Export aggregators — 20 t containers, strict aflatoxin, uniform grade
  • Seed companies — certified seed multiplication contracts
Rule: name three real buyers before you plant. Then sell to at least three different buyer types so no single failure kills the season.

5. Your One-Page Plan

  1. Business type: which of the four above?
  2. Capital tier: how much will you invest?
  3. Land and water: hectares and water source.
  4. Variety: which type and specific variety?
  5. Target yield: kg/ha (aim 1.5 t/ha in year 1).
  6. Buyers: three named contacts.
  7. Revenue target: price × yield.
  8. Total cost: every line.
  9. Net margin: revenue − cost.
  10. Value step: single product to add in 12 months (butter, oil, seed).
  11. Scaling trigger: condition before expanding.
