Choosing a breeding female

  • Age: 18-24 months at first breeding (never before second heat).
  • Health: hip scored (for large breeds), eye tested, DNA clear for breed-specific diseases.
  • Conformation: correct for the breed standard - a good bitch produces good puppies.
  • Temperament: calm, confident, not aggressive. Aggression is heritable.
  • Pedigree: registered, known lineage, no hereditary defects in the line.
  • Reproductive history: has she whelped successfully? Any complications?
  • Body condition: fit, not fat, well-muscled, healthy coat.
  • Vaccination status: up to date, titres tested if possible.
  • Kennel cough vaccinated if she meets other dogs.

Choosing a stud male

  • Proven producer: has he sired healthy litters before?
  • Health clearances: hip, elbow, eye, heart, DNA tests depending on breed.
  • Temperament: confident, calm, willing, not aggressive.
  • Conformation: excellent - he influences 50% of every litter.
  • Pedigree: unrelated to your female for genetic diversity.
  • Age: 18 months to 7 years is peak fertility.

If you don't own a stud male, you can use artificial insemination from chilled or frozen semen, or pay a stud fee to a proven male. Stud fees typically run $200-2,000 depending on the male's reputation.

Never breed from a dog without health clearances. Hip dysplasia, eye disease, and bleeding disorders pass to offspring. Testing costs $50-300 per test but saves you thousands in vet bills and reputation damage.

Breeds to avoid for beginners

  • Highly aggressive breeds - legality and liability issues in many countries
  • Brachycephalic breeds (flat-faced: Bulldog, Pug) - very high c-section rates
  • Giant breeds (Mastiff, Great Dane) - expensive to feed, short lifespan, difficult whelping
  • Rare or niche breeds - hard to find buyers
  • Breeds with banned status in your area - some countries ban specific breeds entirely
The breed rule: Pick one breed and stick with it for 5 years. Become known for that breed. Reputation sells puppies - not variety.
Practical exercise 2: Contact 3 breeders of your chosen breed. Ask about health testing, pedigree, and puppy prices. Attend a breed club event if there is one nearby.
