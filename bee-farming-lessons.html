Course contents

🐝 Bee Farming and Honey Production

Beekeeping as a business for African smallholders - African bees and how they differ, top-bar and frame hives, the beekeeping year, forage, pests and disease, harvesting, and honey that meets the Codex standard.

What this course covers

12 modules · 72 written lessons · about 56,915 words · 360 quiz questions · 12 capstone projects.

Every lesson has learning objectives, worked numbers, a "do this today" task and a knowledge check. Videos are free material made by other people; the written lessons are the course.

Module 16 lessons

The Business of Beekeeping

Beekeeping is the rare enterprise that earns from land you do not own and does not compete with your crops for space. This module covers what bees actually pay on a smallholding, what a hive produces besides honey, who buys it and on what terms, how to build a startup cost list from your own local prices, the records that turn guesswork into management, and how to size your first apiary. Every price in the reference material for this course is obsolete or non-African, so this module teaches a costing method and sends you out to price your own market this month.

  1. Why Bees Pay on Small Land
  2. What You Can Actually Sell
  3. Your Customers and What They Buy
  4. Counting the Real Startup Cost
  5. The Records That Keep You Honest
  6. Deciding Your Scale

Module 26 lessons

The Colony and How It Works

A colony is a single animal made of many bodies, and every management decision you will ever make is derived from how it works. This module covers the three castes and how to tell them apart on a comb, the development timings that set the whole beekeeping calendar, why a colony's strength today was decided three weeks ago, how bees build and organise the nest and why comb spacing for African bees differs from the European figure, the chemical signals that hold a colony together, and how to read a hive from the outside before you disturb it. Commercially this is the module that stops you opening hives without a reason, which is the commonest cause of colony loss in Africa.

  1. The Three Castes
  2. The Brood Cycle Day by Day
  3. How a Colony Grows and Shrinks
  4. Comb, Nest Architecture and Bee Space
  5. Pheromones and Colony Communication
  6. Reading a Colony Before You Open It

Module 36 lessons

African Bees and What They Demand

African bees are not European bees with a suntan. Defensiveness, absconding and heavy swarming are not defects to be managed away; they are the adaptive traits that let these bees survive long dearths, heavy predation and no treatment. This module covers the subspecies you will actually meet, how to work safely when the published safety numbers do not exist, why colonies walk out and how to stop them, why swarming is also your free colony supply, how to select the bees you want to keep, and how to use a suit and a smoker properly. Commercially this is the module that decides whether you still have colonies at the end of your first year.

  1. Apis mellifera scutellata and Its Relatives
  2. Defensiveness and Working Safely
  3. Absconding: Why Colonies Leave
  4. Swarming and How It Differs from Absconding
  5. Choosing Which Colonies to Keep
  6. Protective Equipment and Smoker Craft

Module 46 lessons

Hives and Equipment

The hive is the largest single item of capital in a beekeeping business, and the wrong choice costs money for years. This module teaches the three classes of hive, the exact dimensions that are sourced and the ones that are not, why African comb spacing is 32-34 mm and what happens when you cut bars to a European pattern, and how to cost hives and equipment from three local quotations instead of from a book. All prices in the reference are unverified or two decades old, so the module teaches the costing method and sends you to the market.

  1. The Kenyan Top Bar Hive
  2. The Tanzanian Top Bar Hive
  3. Langstroth and Frame Hives
  4. Traditional Log and Bark Hives
  5. Comb Spacing and Why the Number Matters
  6. Building or Buying: Costing Your Equipment

Module 56 lessons

Siting and Setting Up an Apiary

Where you put your hives decides your crop, your safety and whether you still own your bees next year. This module covers the forage radius that sets everything else, how many hives one site can carry, water and shade in a hot climate, the hung hive on greased wire that is Africa's answer to ants and honey badger, the safety distances that protect people and tethered livestock, and the losses that empty an apiary. It ends with a step by step build of a first ten-hive site.

  3. Spacing, Stands and Hive Security
  4. Distance from People, Paths and Livestock

Module 66 lessons

Getting Bees Into Your Hives

In most of Africa you do not buy bees, you attract them, and that is the single biggest cost advantage African beekeeping has over European. This module covers baiting hives so swarms move in, catching and hiving a swarm safely, splitting a strong colony to make a second one, the rules for buying a colony without buying disease, timing acquisition against the rains and the flow, and what to do and not do in the first eight weeks while a new colony decides whether to stay.

  2. Catching and Transferring a Swarm
  3. Splitting a Strong Colony
  4. Buying Colonies
  5. Timing With the Season

Module 76 lessons

The Beekeeping Year

Almost every beekeeping book you can buy in Africa was written for a temperate country where the year is bounded by winter. In most of sub-Saharan Africa the year is bounded by dearth, and the calendar runs rains, flowering, flow, dearth. This module teaches you to build your own local calendar from your own flowering dates, to time splits, feeding, space and harvest against it, and to inspect in a way that does not drive your bees out of the hive.

  1. The African Beekeeping Calendar
  2. Managing the Build-Up
  3. Managing the Flow
  4. Managing the Dearth
  5. Feeding: When, What and Whether
  6. Inspection Routines and Records

Module 86 lessons

Bee Forage

Your honey crop is not made in the hive. It is made in the flowers within a few kilometres of it, and no amount of good management can pull nectar out of a landscape that has none. This module teaches you to survey and value the forage in your own circle, to work out how many colonies it can carry, to read the real African floral calendars that research has recorded, to build your own, to plant and protect the trees that carry your bees through the dearth, and to judge honestly whether moving hives to forage is worth the risk.

  1. What Bees Need From a Landscape
  2. Forage Radius and Carrying Capacity
  3. African Forage Plants and Their Seasons
  4. Reading Your Own Floral Calendar
  5. Planting and Protecting Forage
  6. Migratory Beekeeping

Module 96 lessons

Pests, Diseases and What Controls Them

Pests and disease decide whether an apiary survives its third year. This module covers the six bee diseases the world reports on, the mite that changed beekeeping everywhere, the beetle that is native to Africa and devastating everywhere else, the moth that eats more African equipment than any other pest, the two foulbroods and nosema, and how to write a health plan for your own apiary. It also draws a hard line: this course names no treatment product, no dose and no withdrawal period, because residues in honey are a food-safety and export matter decided by your own national authority, not by a course.

  1. The Notifiable Diseases
  2. Varroa destructor
  3. Small Hive Beetle
  4. Wax Moth
  5. Foulbrood and Nosema
  6. Building an Apiary Health Plan

Module 106 lessons

Harvesting

Harvesting is the moment your whole year is either converted into money or thrown away. This module covers the single sourced ripeness test, how to select honey comb without touching brood, the hygiene rules that decide whether your crop is food or waste, why moisture is the parameter African beekeepers actually fail on, how to handle the crop between the hive and the store, and how much honey to leave the colony so it survives to give you another crop. The gap between crude honey in a bucket and clean strained honey to standard is where nearly all the lost value in African beekeeping sits, and it opens or closes at harvest.

  1. Knowing When to Harvest
  2. Selecting Combs and Avoiding Brood
  3. Harvest Hygiene
  4. Moisture and Why It Decides Everything
  5. Handling the Crop After Cutting
  6. Leaving Enough Behind

Module 116 lessons

Processing, Quality and Selling Honey

The gap between crude honey in a bucket and clean honey that meets an international standard is where nearly all the lost value in African beekeeping sits, and closing it costs discipline rather than capital. This module covers getting honey out of comb, straining and settling it clear, the Codex composition limits a buyer will test you against including the tropical HMF allowance, why crystallisation is normal, how to pack and label, and how to grade and price your crop. Every price is local and every legal rule is national, so this module teaches you what to measure and who to ask.

  1. Extraction Methods
  2. Straining, Settling and Clarity
  3. The Codex Honey Standard
  4. Crystallisation and Storage
  5. Packaging and Labelling
  6. Grading and Pricing Your Honey

Module 126 lessons

Wax, Other Hive Products and Growing the Business

A hive makes more than honey, and the second crop is thrown away across Africa every harvest season. This module puts a number on beeswax and shows why the wax return is the strongest commercial argument there is for top-bar hives, covers propolis, pollen and royal jelly honestly including what should be left alone, makes the case for pollination to a neighbouring farmer, then teaches you to cost your own operation per hive, reinvest in the right order and build a twelve-month plan around your own flow rather than a printed calendar.

  1. Beeswax: Rendering and Value
  2. Propolis, Pollen and Royal Jelly
  3. Pollination Services
  4. Costing Your Operation Per Hive
  5. Reinvesting and Scaling
  6. A Twelve-Month Plan
