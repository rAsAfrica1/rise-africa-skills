Planting and Crop Establishment - Getting It Right First Time

~14 min read15 min gameBeginner
No device needed

🧠 Brain Opener

The first two weeks decide the whole season. Weak establishment cannot be fixed later. Strong establishment carries you through drought, pests, and mistakes. Get this right and you are already ahead.

  • Why does planting depth matter so much?
  • What is the correct seed rate for vegetable seed crops?
  • How do you know when to plant?
🎁 Reveal a strong answer

Planting depth matters because seeds have limited energy to reach the surface. Too deep and they run out of energy before they see light. Too shallow and they dry out. The correct seed rate is the number of seeds needed to give the right plant density - usually 3 to 5 times the final plant count, to allow for losses. You plant when soil moisture, temperature, and the rainy season all align for your specific crop.

📖 Makanaka Plants Twice

Makanaka was twenty-four and had her first 100 square metre seed plot. She had prepared the soil. She had compost ready. She had her seed packet in her hand.

She planted the tomato seed one centimetre deep, as the packet said. She watered carefully. She waited.

Three weeks later, only 40 percent of the seeds had germinated. The rest were missing.

She asked her aunt, a farmer of thirty years, what had gone wrong.

"Show me how you planted," her aunt said.

Makanaka showed her. She pushed a finger into the soil, dropped a seed, covered it, pressed down firmly, watered.

"You pressed too hard," her aunt said. "The seed is trapped under a crust. It cannot push through."

Makanaka had never thought about the soil crust. She planted again.

This time she dropped the seed in, covered it gently, and watered with a fine spray. She did not press.

Ten days later, 92 percent of the seeds had germinated.

She learned three things that day. First, gentle beats strong when planting. Second, thin soil covering is enough. Third, the first two weeks are where the crop is won or lost.

She sold 3 kilograms of tomato seed from her 100 square metres that first season. Two years later she was producing 20 kilograms per season.

💡 The Big Idea

Establishment is where the crop is decided

Everything before planting is preparation. Everything after planting is response. The planting itself is the moment when the crop is decided. Depth, spacing, timing, and gentle handling during this moment determine 80 percent of the outcome.

📚 Three Deep Lessons

1

The three rules of planting depth

Rule one - follow the seed packet. Tomato 0.5 to 1 centimetre. Pepper 0.5 centimetre. Kale 1 centimetre. Amaranth 0.5 centimetre. Maize 3 to 5 centimetres. Do not guess.

Rule two - match soil moisture. Plant into moist soil, not dry. Water the day before if needed. Then cover lightly and water gently after.

Rule three - do not press down hard. Firm the soil just enough to remove air pockets. Pressing hard creates a crust that young shoots cannot break through.

2

Seed rate - more than you think

You plant more seed than the final plant count because not all seeds germinate, some seedlings die, and some are removed by thinning or roguing.

For direct-sown vegetables: 3 to 5 times the final plant count.

For transplanted vegetables: 2 to 3 times the final plant count.

For cereals and legumes: 1.2 to 1.5 times the final plant count.

Calculate: if you want 1,000 tomato plants at the end, plant 2,500 to 3,000 seeds. This gives you margin for losses.

3

Sowing dates - the timing matters

Vegetable seed crops need to flower and set seed during the dry season. If they flower in the middle of the rains, the flowers rot before seed sets.

For Southern Africa, plant tomatoes in March-April so they flower July-August. For East Africa, plant in February-March so they flower May-June.

For grain seed crops, plant at the start of the rains so the plant matures into the dry season. Maize for seed: plant in November. Groundnut for seed: plant in November-December.

Ask three experienced seed producers in your area what dates they use. Do not invent your own calendar.

🛠 Hands-On - Plant a 10 Square Metre Test Plot

You need

  • Your seed
  • Well-prepared soil
  • A measuring tape
  • A pen
  1. Mark a 2 by 5 metre plot.
  2. Mark rows 30 centimetres apart.
  3. Plant seeds at the correct depth for your crop.
  4. Water gently with a fine spray.
  5. Do not press the soil down hard.
  6. Record the date in your notebook.
  7. Check every morning. Count how many days until first germination.
  8. Record the germination rate after 14 days.
Why it works: A 10 square metre test plot teaches you your crop in one week, at the cost of about 10 seeds. Never plant your whole field with a new crop without a test plot first.

🌍 Real Stories from Africa

Kenya tomato seed germination

A Kenyan tomato seed producer improved germination from 55 to 94 percent by changing three things: watering the day before planting, dropping rather than pressing the seed, and watering with a fine spray after. Same seed, same land, doubled result.

Tanzania amaranth spacing

Tanzanian amaranth seed producers plant at 45 centimetres between rows and 15 centimetres within rows. This gives 120,000 plants per hectare and produces 70 to 90 kilograms of seed. Closer spacing reduces seed yield. Wider spacing wastes land.

🏆 Project Challenge

Plant Two Test Plots

Plant one plot with careful technique and one with rough technique. Compare.

  1. Prepare two 2 by 5 metre plots the same way.
  2. Plant the first plot using the correct technique - correct depth, gentle covering, fine spray.
  3. Plant the second plot using a rough technique - deeper, pressed down hard, no fine spray.
  4. Record both dates.
  5. Water both the same.
  6. Count germination in both after 14 days.
  7. Write down the difference. This teaches you why technique matters.
