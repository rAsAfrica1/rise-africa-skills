1. Introduction and Why It Matters

When you smelt gold ore or scrap, you get a dore bara mix of gold, silver, copper, and other metals. That dore bar might be only 70% to 90% gold. Refining takes it to 99.5% or higher, which is what banks and international buyers want. In Africa, most small-scale miners sell dore bars at a big discount because they lack refining skills. If you refine your own gold, you can sell directly to jewelers or refineries at a much higher price. For example, a 100-gram dore bar at 80% purity contains 80 grams of gold. If gold is $60 per gram, the raw dore is worth $4,800. But if you refine it to 99.5%, you get 99.5 grams of pure gold worth $5,970a gain of over $1,100. That difference is your profit. Refining also removes toxic metals like lead and mercury, making your gold safer to handle and sell. In countries like Ghana, Tanzania, and South Africa, local refineries pay better for pure gold. But many artisanal miners don't know how to refine. This module teaches you simple, low-cost methods using everyday chemicals and tools. You will learn to melt, mix with acids, and recover pure gold. The process takes practice, but with careful work, you can turn your dore into a product that meets international standards. Remember: refining is not just about chemistryit's about quality control and safety. By the end of this module, you will be able to set up a small refining operation and produce gold that buyers trust.

2. Tools and Materials

To refine gold, you need a few basic tools and chemicals. First, a fume hood or a well-ventilated outdoor areaacid fumes are dangerous. You'll need heat-resistant gloves, safety goggles, and a rubber apron. For melting, get a propane torch or a small furnace that reaches 1,100C. A graphite crucible is best for melting gold; it withstands high heat and doesn't contaminate the metal. You'll also need a set of tongs, a mold for pouring bars, and a digital scale accurate to 0.01 grams. For the acid refining, you need nitric acid (65% concentration) and hydrochloric acid (32%). These are sold at chemical supply shops or industrial markets. You'll also need urea, sodium metabisulfite (or ferrous sulfate), and distilled water. Never use tap waterminerals can ruin the reaction. For filtering, get borosilicate glass beakers (1,000 ml and 2,000 ml), glass stirring rods, and filter papers. A vacuum filter is helpful but not required. You'll also need a pH testing kit or strips. For safety, keep a bucket of baking soda nearby to neutralize acid spills. In Africa, many of these items are available in large cities like Nairobi, Lagos, or Johannesburg. If you can't find nitric acid, you can make a substitute using potassium nitrate and sulfuric acid, but that's more dangerous. Start with small quantities50 grams of dore at a time. A basic setup costs about $500 to $1,000, but you can start with less if you already have a torch and crucible. Always store acids in sealed plastic containers, away from sunlight and children.

3. Step by Step Method

Start by melting your dore bar. Place it in a graphite crucible and heat with a propane torch until it's liquidabout 1,060C. Pour it into a thin ingot or granules to increase surface area. Let it cool. Now, weigh the dore. For every 100 grams of dore, you'll need about 300 ml of nitric acid. Put the dore in a glass beaker and add the nitric acid slowly. This dissolves silver, copper, and other base metals, but not gold. Let it sit for 2-3 hours; you'll see brown fumes (nitrogen dioxide)that's why you need ventilation. When the reaction stops, carefully pour off the liquid. The remaining mud is your gold. But it still contains some impurities. Now, make aqua regia: mix 3 parts hydrochloric acid with 1 part nitric acid. For 100 grams of gold, use 300 ml of aqua regia. Add it to the gold mud. This dissolves the gold into a yellow solution. Filter the solution to remove any solids. Next, neutralize the acid by slowly adding urea until it stops fizzing. Then, add sodium metabisulfiteabout 1 gram per gram of gold. Stir gently; the gold will precipitate as a brown powder. Let it settle for 2 hours. Pour off the liquid and wash the powder with hot distilled water three times. Finally, melt the powder in a crucible with a pinch of borax. Pour into a mold. You now have 99.5% pure gold. Test with a gold testing kit or send a sample to a lab. The whole process takes about 6-8 hours for 100 grams.

4. Common Mistakes and Fixes

One common mistake is using too much nitric acid. If you add too much, you waste chemicals and create more fumes. Fix: measure carefully300 ml per 100 grams of dore. Another mistake is not filtering the aqua regia solution properly. Any solid particles will contaminate your final gold. Fix: use a fine filter paper (Whatman No. 1) and filter twice. A third mistake is adding sodium metabisulfite too fast. This can cause the gold to precipitate as fine particles that are hard to settle. Fix: add it slowly while stirring, and wait for the fizzing to stop before adding more. Many refiners forget to neutralize the acid with urea. If you skip this, the metabisulfite won't work well, and you'll lose gold. Fix: add urea until the solution stops bubbling. Another error is not washing the gold powder enough. Trapped acid will make your final bar brittle. Fix: wash with hot distilled water at least three times, until the water is clear. Also, some people use tap water, which introduces minerals. Fix: always use distilled water. In Africa, power cuts are common. If your furnace stops, the gold may not melt properly. Fix: use a propane torch as backup. Finally, many refiners don't test their final gold. Fix: buy a gold testing kit (acid test or electronic tester) and check purity. If it's below 99%, re-refine. Remember, small mistakes cost money. Keep a notebook and record every step, so you can repeat what works.

5. Safety and Best Practice

Refining gold uses dangerous chemicals. Nitric acid and hydrochloric acid can burn your skin and eyes. Always wear safety goggles, a face shield, and acid-resistant gloves. Work outdoors or in a fume hoodnever in a closed room. The brown fumes from nitric acid are toxic; if you smell them, move away and improve ventilation. Keep a bucket of baking soda and water nearby to neutralize spills. If acid touches your skin, rinse with plenty of water for 15 minutes, then apply baking soda. If you get it in your eyes, rinse and go to a clinic immediately. Never add water to acidalways add acid to water. When mixing aqua regia, do it slowly and in a glass container. Store acids in labeled plastic bottles, away from metals and organic materials. Keep a fire extinguisher rated for chemical fires. Do not eat, drink, or smoke while refining. Wash your hands thoroughly after work. For best practice, refine in small batchesno more than 100 grams at a time. This reduces risk and makes it easier to control. Use a dedicated area with a concrete floor and good drainage. Dispose of waste acid properly: neutralize with baking soda until pH is 7, then take to a hazardous waste facility. Never pour acid down the drain. Train anyone who works with you. Keep a first-aid kit and a phone nearby. In Africa, many accidents happen because miners rush or use makeshift tools. Take your time. Your health is more valuable than gold.

6. Practical Exercise

For this exercise, you will refine a small dore bar. Start with 50 grams of dore that you know contains at least 70% gold. If you don't have dore, use gold jewelry or coins that are at least 18 karat. First, melt the dore and pour it into thin flakes. Weigh the flakes. Put them in a 1,000 ml beaker. Add 150 ml of nitric acid. Let it react for 2 hours in a ventilated area. When the reaction stops, carefully pour off the acid into a waste container. Add 150 ml of fresh nitric acid and let it react again for 1 hour. Pour off. The remaining mud is gold. Now, prepare aqua regia: mix 150 ml hydrochloric acid with 50 ml nitric acid. Add this to the gold mud. Stir gently until the gold dissolves. Filter the solution through filter paper into a clean beaker. Add urea slowly until it stops fizzing. Then add 50 grams of sodium metabisulfite. Stir and let it settle for 2 hours. The gold will drop as a brown powder. Pour off the liquid. Wash the powder with 200 ml hot distilled water, let it settle, and pour off. Repeat three times. Finally, melt the powder in a crucible with a pinch of borax. Pour into a mold. Weigh your pure gold. Calculate your yield: (weight of pure gold / weight of dore) x 100. If you started with 50 grams of 80% dore, you should get about 40 grams of pure gold. If your yield is lower, check for mistakes. Test the purity with a gold testing kit. Record everything in a notebook. Repeat the exercise with another 50 grams to improve your skill. Always follow safety rules.

<h2>Practical Reference: Refining Gold from Dore to Pure Metal</h2>

<p>This practical reference consolidates the working knowledge a small-to-medium African gold refining operation needs to move from dor bars (typically 7090% gold) to fine gold of 99.5%+ purity. It is written for the operator who already understands the basics and now needs a single, reliable document to consult at the bench. Whether you are refining for your own export licence, toll-refining for artisanal miners, or preparing metal for a jeweller or central bank buyer, the parameters below are the ones that determine your yield, your costs, and your reputation.</p>

<p>Across Africa, the refining step is where most value is either captured or lost. Ghana, South Africa, Tanzania, Mali, Burkina Faso, Zimbabwe, and the DRC all produce dor, but a large share of it leaves the continent unrefined because in-country refining capacity is thin. The operator who can consistently produce 99.5%+ gold and document it can charge a refining premium, sell directly to international buyers, and avoid the discounts that come with selling dor. That is the business case for this module.</p>

<div style="border:2px solid #b00020;background:#fdecea;padding:12px;margin:18px 0;border-radius:6px;">
<strong style="color:#b00020;"> SAFETY WARNING Aqua regia and nitric acid</strong>
<p style="margin:8px 0 0 0;">Aqua regia releases toxic nitrogen oxides and chlorine gas. Never mix acids without a functioning fume hood or scrubber, chemical-resistant gloves, goggles, and an apron. Nitric acid causes severe burns and can be fatal if inhaled in confined spaces. Always add acid to solution, never water to acid. Keep a Class D extinguisher and a spill kit within reach, and never work alone. In many African jurisdictions, acid refining without an environmental permit from the national EPA (e.g., Ghana EPA, NEMC Tanzania, NEMA Kenya) is illegal confirm your licence before you buy reagents.</p>

<h3>Quick Reference Checklist</h3>
<ol style="line-height:1.7;">
<li><strong>Assay before you refine.</strong> Take a representative sample and fire-assay the dor. You cannot calculate yield or charge a toll fee without it.</li>
<li><strong>Weigh and record everything.</strong> Dor in, reagents used, gold out. A calibrated scale and a bound logbook protect your margin and your audit trail.</li>
<li><strong>Choose the right route.</strong> High silver acid/inquartation. High gold, low silver aqua regia. Match the route to the feed, not to habit.</li>
<li><strong>Control the fume.</strong> Fume hood or scrubber on at all times. Test airflow before every batch.</li>
<li><strong>Wash thoroughly.</strong> Residual acid or salts in the precipitated gold cause spitting and low purity at melt. Wash to neutral pH.</li>
<li><strong>Melt clean.</strong> Use a fresh crucible and clean flux. Contaminated crucibles are a leading cause of failed assays.</li>
<li><strong>Assay the product.</strong> Refine, then re-assay. Sell on the assay certificate, not on assumption.</li>
<li><strong>Manage waste legally.</strong> Spent acid and residues are hazardous. Neutralise, store, and dispose through a licensed handler.</li>
</ol>

<h3>Business Notes for African Operators</h3>
<p>Refining is a service business as much as a metallurgical one. ASM miners in Ghana, Tanzania, and Zimbabwe will pay a toll fee of 38% of the gold value to have their dor refined to a sellable product, provided you return the metal quickly and transparently. Build your margin on throughput and trust, not on hidden losses. Document every batch, invite the client to witness the weighing, and issue a signed assay certificate. That reputation is what lets you graduate from toll refining to buying dor outright and selling fine gold directly to international buyers.</p>

<p>Also factor in the cost of compliance. Environmental permits, acid storage licences, and assay certification are not optional if you want bankable clients. The operators who survive the next decade in African gold will be the ones whose paperwork is as clean as their metal.</p>
