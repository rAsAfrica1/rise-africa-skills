Sunflower Processing

8 lessons · 30 quiz questions · Nuts & Seeds

1 Sunflower varieties

Sunflower (Helianthus annuus) is a major oilseed crop worldwide. Africa (especially South Africa, Tanzania, Zambia) is a growing producer.

Oil-type sunflower: Black seeds with thin hulls. 40–50% oil content. Best for oil extraction.

Confectionery-type sunflower: Striped seeds with larger kernels. Best for snacking and baking.

Birdseed varieties: Small seeds with high hull content. Used for birdfeed.

For oil, choose high-oleic or standard oil-type. For snacking, choose confectionery types.

2 Harvesting and drying

Sunflower heads are ready to harvest when the back of the head turns yellow-brown and seeds are firm.

  1. Cut heads with 20–30 cm of stalk
  2. Dry heads in sun 3–5 days
  3. Rub seeds off heads by hand or with a beater
  4. Winnow to remove chaff
  5. Dry seeds further to 8–10% moisture for storage
  6. Store in dry, dark conditions

Yield: 1 hectare → 1–2 tonnes seed.

⚠ High moisture (over 12%) leads to mould and aflatoxin. Dry properly before storage.

3 Seed cleaning and grading

Clean sunflower seed sells at premium for snacking. Dirty seed is only good for oil.

  1. Screen to remove stones, chaff, weed seeds
  2. Dehull if for confectionery use (oil-type is pressed with hulls on)
  3. Grade by size — large, medium, small
  4. Remove broken or discoloured seeds

Cleaning yield: 100 kg raw seed → 90–95 kg cleaned seed.

4 Sunflower oil pressing

Sunflower oil is a top-5 cooking oil globally. High in Vitamin E and linoleic acid.

Cold press: Press at low temp. 35–40% oil yield. Premium quality.

Expeller press: Press at higher temp. 40–45% oil yield. Standard quality.

  1. Clean seeds
  2. Dehull (optional but improves oil quality)
  3. Flake seeds (roller mill)
  4. Heat to 80°C (optional)
  5. Press in oil expeller
  6. Filter oil
  7. Bottle in dark glass or PET

Yield: 100 kg seed → 35–45 kg oil + 50 kg cake.

Retail: $3–$7 per litre. Cold-pressed premium: $8–$15 per litre.

5 Roasted sunflower seeds

Roasted sunflower seeds are a popular snack worldwide.

  1. Soak in salted water 30 minutes (for salted version)
  2. Drain and pat dry
  3. Roast at 160°C for 15–20 minutes, stirring every 5 minutes
  4. Cool completely
  5. Pack in foil pouches or jars

Yield: 100 kg raw seed → 85–90 kg roasted.

Retail: $6–$12 per kg. Premium flavoured: $10–$18 per kg.

6 Sunflower seed butter

Sunflower seed butter (sunbutter) is a growing peanut-free alternative.

  1. Roast seeds (160°C, 20 min)
  2. Cool and grind in a heavy-duty processor or colloid mill
  3. Add salt (1%) and oil (2–3%) if needed
  4. For smooth: process 5–10 minutes
  5. Fill jars at 65°C, seal

Yield: 100 kg seeds → 95 kg butter.

Retail: $10–$20 per 500 g jar. Higher than peanut butter due to allergen-free positioning.

7 Sunflower cake for feed

Sunflower cake (leftover after oil extraction) is a valuable animal feed.

Protein content: 28–35%.

Uses:

  • Dairy cattle feed — high energy, good protein
  • Poultry feed (limited — high fibre)
  • Pig feed (limited — high fibre)
  • Fish feed (with other ingredients)

Bulk price: $0.30–$0.60 per kg to feed mills. High volume, low margin — but no waste.

8 Packaging

Packaging by product:

  • Sunflower oil — dark glass or PET (500 ml, 1 L, 5 L)
  • Roasted seeds — foil pouches (100 g, 250 g, 500 g)
  • Sunbutter — glass jars (250 g, 500 g)
  • Sunflower cake — poly sacks (25 kg, 50 kg)

Shelf life:

  • Oil: 12–18 months sealed
  • Roasted seeds: 6–9 months in foil
  • Sunbutter: 12 months sealed
  • Sunflower cake: 3 months (goes rancid)
💰 Sunflower oil is the highest-volume product. Sunbutter is the highest-margin product. Roasted seeds are a simple, high-margin snack. Pick based on your market.
