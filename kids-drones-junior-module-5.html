The Sky Has Rules

The sky is shared, so flying is not just about skill — it is about law and other people's safety.

🎒 What you need

  • the shared phone to look up your own country's rules
  • paper to write your own pilot's pledge
  • a grown-up to talk it through with

✋ Do this

  1. Learn the big one first: never fly near an airport or where aeroplanes take off and land. This is banned almost everywhere, and it is dangerous, not just naughty.
  2. Learn the second: in many African countries — Kenya, Nigeria, Ghana, Rwanda, South Africa and others — drones must be registered and you need permission before flying. Rules differ by country, so you must check YOUR country's civil aviation authority.
  3. Search on the phone for your own country's civil aviation authority drone rules. Write down two real rules you find.
  4. Now the people rules: do not fly over crowds, do not fly over someone's home or compound without asking, keep the drone where you can see it.
  5. Ask yourself the question that matters: 'How would I feel if a stranger's camera hovered over my yard?' Write your answer down.
  6. Write and sign your own Pilot's Pledge: five rules in your own words. Stick it somewhere you will see it.

💡 Why it works

Drones are genuinely useful in Africa — carrying blood and medicine to clinics, counting cattle, checking crops and finding people after floods — and all of that depends on pilots being trusted. One careless flight near an airport or over a private compound damages that trust for everybody. Rules are what keep a good tool from being banned.

🔥 Challenge

Find out one real, useful job drones do in your own country and one thing that is banned. Tell someone both facts.
