9. Breeding Program and Genetics

The right buck doubles the value of every kid. Here is how to run a breeding program.

Module 9 of 12 · Goat Farming for Profit

Why breeding matters

Inherited traits decide 40-60% of a goat's productivity. A good buck passes fast growth, disease resistance, and better mothering to every kid. A poor buck drags down the whole flock for years.

Buy from a registered breeder, not from a market. Market bucks carry disease and unknown genetics.

One buck per 25-30 does is standard. Younger or older bucks serve fewer.

Heat lasts 24-48 hours. Breed 12-18 hours after heat begins, or twice - morning and evening of the same day.

Inbreeding and line breeding

Never breed father to daughter, brother to sister, or mother to son. Inbreeding causes:

  • Smaller, weaker kids
  • Higher mortality
  • Reduced milk yield
  • Poor fertility
  • Increased genetic defects

Rule: Change the buck every 2 years. Buy a new buck from a different line.

Best smallholder approach: keep does at 50% Boer (hardy + good growth) and use a full Boer buck.

Selection of replacement does

  1. Keep the best 20-30% of daughters. Choose from does that kid twins, mother well, and stay healthy.
  2. Track production per doe - kids weaned, kg weaned, milk yield.
  3. Cull daughters of problem does. Mastitis, poor mothering, and low yield are heritable.
  4. Weight at 6 months matters. A heavier doeling becomes a better producer.
  5. Do not keep all daughters. Overstocking ruins feed economics.

The buck rule: Buy the best buck you can afford. He influences 50% of every kid for the 2 years he serves. A cheap buck costs you money for years.
Practical exercise 9: Set up a breeding record book. Record every breeding date. Mark kidding dates on your calendar. After 2 seasons, review which does produce best.
