Water Supply and Recycling

8 lessons · 30 quiz questions · Business

1 Water quality for concrete

Not all water is suitable for concrete. The wrong water weakens blocks or causes early setting.

Acceptable water:

  • Clean mains water — ideal
  • Clean borehole water — usually fine
  • Fresh river water — usually fine
  • Rainwater — excellent quality

Unacceptable water:

  • Sea water — chloride causes corrosion and efflorescence
  • Water with sugar or organic matter — stops cement setting
  • Water with oil or grease — prevents bonding
  • Acid or alkali water — attacks cement

Test rule: If you can drink it, it's usually safe for concrete. If you can't, test it first.

⚠ Never use water from a sugar factory, dairy, or tannery. Organic contamination halts setting entirely.

2 Borehole vs mains

Two main water sources for a block yard.

Mains water:

  • Reliable supply in urban areas
  • Costs $1.50–$4 per 1,000 L
  • Risk of interruptions during drought
  • Water restrictions may apply in summer

Borehole:

  • Best value long-term — after initial investment
  • Drilling cost: $3,000–$15,000
  • Ongoing cost: electricity to run pump, minimal
  • Independent of municipal supply

Decision: For a small yard on mains, stay on mains. For medium or large yards (2,000+ blocks/day), drilling a borehole pays back in 2–4 years.

3 Curing water volumes

Curing consumes more water than mixing for most yards.

Water use per 1,000 blocks:

  • Mixing: 150–200 L (mixed into concrete)
  • Curing (7 days, 2 sprays/day): 300–600 L
  • Cleaning: 100–200 L
  • Total: 550–1,000 L per 1,000 blocks

For 5,000 blocks/day: 3,000–5,000 litres/day. That's 750,000–1.25M litres/year.

Plan storage to hold 3 days of water in case of interruption — 15,000 L minimum.

4 Recycling wash water

Water used to wash mixers and equipment contains cement — but that's not waste, it's a resource.

How to recycle:

  1. Direct all washout water to a settling tank or pit
  2. Solids settle to the bottom
  3. Clear water on top can be reused for mixing (up to 30% of mix water) or curing
  4. Sludge at the bottom is disposed of or used as fill

Why recycle:

  • Saves 20–30% of water bills
  • Prevents cement-laden water entering drains (environmental regulations)
  • Sludge can be dried and used as fill material

A 3-chamber settling pit costs $500–$2,000 to build. Pays back in under a year.

5 Rainwater harvesting

Rainwater is free, clean, and ideal for concrete.

System:

  • Gutters on factory and shed roofs
  • First-flush diverter (discards first 5 minutes of rain — carries dirt)
  • Storage tanks — 10,000–50,000 L
  • Filtration (simple screen + sand filter)
  • Overflow to soakaway

Yield calculation: A 500 m² roof in a region with 800 mm annual rain captures 400,000 L/year. That's 30–50% of a small yard's water needs.

Cost: $2,000–$8,000 for gutters, tanks, and filtration. Payback 2–4 years depending on mains water cost.

6 Storage tanks

Water storage gives you independence from supply interruptions.

Tank types:

  • Plastic (poly) tanks: 5,000–20,000 L. $500–$2,500. Easy install.
  • Concrete tanks: 20,000–100,000 L. $3,000–$15,000. Built on site.
  • Steel tanks: Rare in Africa due to corrosion.
  • Underground tanks: Space-saving, cool water.

Covered tanks prevent evaporation, algae, and mosquito breeding.

Sizing: 3 days of water use minimum. For 5,000 blocks/day, 3 days = 15,000 L.

Install a float valve to auto-fill from mains or borehole.

7 Water cost per block

Water is a small but real cost. Know it.

Example (mains water at $2 per 1,000 L):

  • Mixing: 0.2 L per block × $0.002 = $0.0004
  • Curing: 0.4 L per block × $0.002 = $0.0008
  • Cleaning: 0.15 L per block × $0.002 = $0.0003
  • Total: about $0.0015 per block

For 5,000 blocks/day: $7.50/day = $1,875/year.

Recycling wash water can cut this by 20–30%. Rainwater harvesting can cut it 50%+.

On a borehole, the cost per block is negligible — mostly electricity for the pump.

8 Cutting the water bill

Water is often overlooked, but savings add up.

Seven ways to reduce water cost:

  1. Fix leaks immediately — a dripping tap wastes 20 L/day = 7,000 L/year
  2. Recycle washout water — settle and reuse (up to 30% of mix)
  3. Harvest rainwater — free water from your roof
  4. Cover storage tanks — stops evaporation (10–15% loss)
  5. Use drip irrigation for curing — not spray hoses
  6. Train staff — one hour's training saves thousands of litres
  7. Drill a borehole — if water bill is over $200/month

A 20% water saving on a 5,000-block/day yard is £375/year — small, but free money.

💰 Water is often the cheapest cost per block, but it's also the easiest to waste. Fix leaks, recycle, harvest. Small habits compound.
