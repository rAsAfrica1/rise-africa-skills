Selling to Farmers and Self-Builders

8 lessons · 30 quiz questions · Business

1 Farmer block uses

Farmers buy blocks for practical reasons, not prestige. Understand their needs and you'll win their business.

What farmers build with blocks:

  • Pig pens, chicken houses, cattle kraals
  • Feed storage rooms
  • Grain silos and storage
  • Greenhouses and shade houses
  • Workshops and tool sheds
  • Small dams and water tanks
  • Farmhouse and staff housing
  • Security walls

They buy in smaller quantities than builders — 500–5,000 blocks per project. But there are far more of them, and they buy continuously.

💡 Farmers don't negotiate as hard as builders. They value honesty and reliability more than the last 5%.

2 Self-builder buying habits

Self-builders are individuals building their own home or extension. They buy with their own money, often over months or years.

What matters to them:

  • Price: Every dollar counts. They'll compare you to 3 other suppliers.
  • Flexibility: They may buy in small quantities (200 blocks at a time).
  • Advice: They don't know how many blocks they need. Help them calculate.
  • Payment plans: Some need to pay over weeks or months. Consider layby.
  • Delivery: They don't have trucks. Delivery is essential.

Self-builders spread the word if they like you. In the community, reputation is everything.

3 Small-quantity pricing

Small orders cost more per block to fulfil (loading, delivery, admin). Charge accordingly — but don't scare customers away.

Tiered pricing example (dense 440 × 215 × 100 mm block):

  • 1–500 blocks: $0.90 each
  • 501–2,000 blocks: $0.80 each
  • 2,001–10,000 blocks: $0.70 each
  • 10,001+ blocks: $0.60 each

Or: fixed price per block, but minimum delivery order of 100 blocks. Below that, they collect.

Explain the tiers: "The price drops with volume because we save on handling." Customers accept this — it's fair and transparent.

4 Delivery options

Delivery is often the deciding factor for farmers and self-builders.

Delivery options:

  • Own truck: Best for regular routes. Cost: $15,000–$40,000 for a used tipper truck.
  • Hired lorry: Per delivery. Cost: $50–$150 depending on distance.
  • Bakkie pickup: Small loads (200–500 blocks). Cheaper for short trips.
  • Customer collects: Cheapest, but limits your market.
  • Shared delivery: Combine 3–4 nearby orders into one trip. Efficient.

Delivery pricing: Charge per km or per load. Typical: $30 flat fee within 10 km, then $1/km beyond.

Never deliver for free to a new customer unless it's a small first order — delivery costs money.

5 Cash vs credit

Farmers and self-builders have less access to credit than builders. But offering credit is risky.

Payment options to offer:

  • Cash on delivery: Standard. Give 2% discount for cash.
  • Mobile money on delivery: EcoCash, M-Pesa, etc. Same terms as cash.
  • Layby: Customer pays over 4–12 weeks, gets blocks when fully paid. Protects you (no default risk — you keep the goods).
  • Small credit: For known customers only. Up to $200 max, 30-day terms.

Why not offer credit: Farmers' income is seasonal — they may not pay until harvest. Self-builders may lose their job. Bad debt destroys small yards.

Layby is the safe middle ground.

6 Loyalty discounts

Repeat farmers and self-builders deserve recognition.

Loyalty program ideas:

  • After 5 orders, get 5% off the 6th
  • Free delivery on orders over $500 for regular customers
  • Priority delivery scheduling during busy periods
  • End-of-year thank-you — small gift or bonus blocks

Track customers in a simple notebook or spreadsheet:

  • Name, phone, address
  • Order history (dates, quantities, block types)
  • Total lifetime spend
  • Last order date

After 90 days without an order, contact them: "Hi, it's been a while. Anything you're building soon?"

7 Social media selling

In Africa, WhatsApp, Facebook, and Instagram are where farmers and self-builders find suppliers.

WhatsApp Business:

  • Set up a Business profile with logo, address, hours
  • Use the Catalog feature to show block types with photos and prices
  • Auto-reply for out-of-hours enquiries
  • Broadcast list (256 contacts max) for new stock announcements

Facebook:

  • Page for your business — post weekly
  • Photos of finished projects (walls, houses, pens)
  • Facebook Marketplace listings for each block type
  • Join local community groups and share builds

Instagram:

  • Visual feed — completed walls, delivery trucks, production process
  • Stories — daily updates from the yard
  • Reels — quick "how blocks are made" videos get high engagement

Post 3–5 times a week. Reply to every message within 2 hours during business hours.

8 Trade shows and markets

Physical presence still wins in rural markets.

Local trade shows:

  • Agricultural shows — farmers gather, perfect audience
  • Building expos — developers, builders, suppliers
  • Community markets — sell to walk-in customers
  • Church and community events — sponsor, distribute flyers

Booth setup:

  • Sample blocks displayed (dense, hollow, paver, decorative)
  • Printed price list
  • Banner with your brand name and phone number
  • Photos of completed projects
  • Free samples or small giveaway blocks for kids (memory hook)

Cost: Booth fees range from $20 (local market) to $500 (big expo). Worth it for the exposure.

💰 Farmers and self-builders are the bread and butter of a small yard. Lower volumes, but steady, low-risk, and loyal if treated well.
