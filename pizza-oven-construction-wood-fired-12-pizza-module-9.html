1. Dough Preparation and Hydration for High Heat

For a wood-fired oven at 400â450Â°C, your dough must handle intense heat without burning. Use strong flour: bread flour or '00' pizza flour with 12â13% protein. In Africa, if you can't find '00', use all-purpose flour mixed with 10% cassava flour for crispness. For 4 x 12-inch pizzas, mix 500g flour, 325g water (65% hydration), 10g salt, 2g instant yeast, and 1 tablespoon olive oil. Higher hydration (70%) gives a lighter crust but is harder to stretch; start at 65% and adjust. Water should be lukewarm (30Â°C) to activate yeast. Mix by hand: dissolve yeast in water, add flour, rest 20 minutes (autolyse), then add salt and oil. Knead 8â10 minutes until smooth. Let rise covered at room temperature for 2 hours, then divide into 4 balls (about 210g each). Ball tightly, place in oiled trays, cover, and cold-ferment in a fridge for 24â48 hours. Cold fermentation develops flavour and makes dough easier to stretch. Remove from fridge 2 hours before baking. If you have no fridge, ferment at room temperature for 4â6 hours, but flavour will be milder. For a 12-inch pizza, each ball should weigh 200â220g. Too heavy, and the centre won't cook in 90 seconds; too light, and it tears. Test hydration: poke dough; it should spring back slowly. If it springs back fast, it's under-proofed; if it doesn't spring back, it's over-proofed. In hot African climates, reduce yeast to 1g and use cold water to slow fermentation. Always cover dough to prevent skin formation. This dough recipe is your foundation for all six lessons.

2. Stretching and Shaping 12-Inch Pizza Bases

Never use a rolling pinâit crushes the gas bubbles that make a puffy, charred crust. Work on a lightly floured surface (use semolina or rice flour, not wheat flour, to prevent sticking). Take one 210g dough ball, dust both sides, and press from the centre outward with your fingertips, leaving a 1.5cm border untouched for the cornicione (rim). Then lift the dough onto the backs of your knuckles and gently stretch it by rotating your hands, letting gravity help. Aim for a 30cm (12-inch) circle. If the dough resists, let it rest 5 minutes, then continue. For beginners, press the dough on a counter into a 25cm circle, then drape it over your fists and stretch to 30cm. The base should be thin in the middle (3â4mm) and thicker at the rim (1â1.5cm). If you tear a hole, pinch it closed and press gently. Do not overwork the edges. Once shaped, place the base on a wooden peel dusted with semolina. Shake the peel to ensure the dough slides. If it sticks, lift an edge and blow under it, or dust more semolina. For a 12-inch oven, your pizza must fit exactlyâtoo large and it won't turn; too small and it burns. Practice with a tape measure. In humid African conditions, dough can become sticky; keep your hands dry and use minimal flour. If you're making many pizzas, shape only one at a time and keep the rest covered. A well-shaped base is the difference between a soggy centre and a perfectly baked pizza in 60â90 seconds.

3. Sauce and Topping Strategies for Quick Baking

In a 400â450Â°C oven, your pizza bakes in 60â90 seconds. That means toppings must be pre-cooked or very thin. For sauce, use raw crushed tomatoesânot cooked. Spread 60â80ml (about 4 tablespoons) per 12-inch pizza using the back of a ladle, leaving a 1.5cm border. Too much sauce makes a soggy centre. For cheese, use low-moisture mozzarella, shredded or torn. Fresh mozzarella has too much water and will pool. If using fresh, slice and drain on paper towels for 30 minutes. Use 100â120g per pizza. For toppings, slice vegetables paper-thin (2â3mm) with a mandoline or sharp knife. Good options: thinly sliced onions, bell peppers, mushrooms, spinach, and cured meats like salami or ham. Arrange toppings evenly, leaving gaps for heat to circulate. Add fresh herbs like basil after baking, not before, as they burn. Drizzle olive oil over the top before baking for a golden crust. Pre-heat your oven for at least 45 minutes until the floor is white-hot. A hot oven and dry toppings are key to a 90-second bake.

4. Using a Pizza Peel for Loading and Turning

A pizza peel is your most important tool. You need two: a wooden peel for loading (wood doesn't conduct heat, so dough won't stick) and a metal peel for turning and removing (thin metal slides easily under the pizza). For a 12-inch pizza, use a 30â35cm peel. Before shaping, dust the wooden peel generously with semolina or rice flourânot wheat flour, which burns. Shake off excess. Shape your dough on a separate surface, then transfer it to the peel. Work quickly: sauce, cheese, and toppings within 2 minutes, or the dough will stick. Before loading, shake the peel gently side to side. The pizza should slide freely. If it sticks, lift an edge and blow under it, or use a spatula to loosen. Open the oven door, position the peel at a slight angle, and slide the pizza onto the hot floor with a quick forward-and-back motion. Do not drop itâslide it. Close the door immediately. After 30 seconds, use the metal peel to lift the edge and check the bottom. If it's browning too fast, turn the pizza 90 degrees. In a wood-fired oven, the hottest spot is near the fire. For even baking, rotate the pizza every 20â30 seconds. Use the metal peel to slide under the pizza and spin it. If the pizza puffs up, prick large bubbles with a fork or a dough docker. After 60â90 seconds, the crust should be charred in spots and the cheese melted. Remove with the metal peel, sliding it fully under the pizza. Never leave the pizza on the peel after bakingâit will steam and soften. Clean your peel between pizzas; burnt flour leaves a bitter taste. Practice loading and turning with a cold oven first.

5. Baking a 12-Inch Pizza in 60-90 Seconds

Your wood-fired oven must be at 400â450Â°C (750â850Â°F) on the floor. Use an infrared thermometer to check. If you don't have one, throw a pinch of flour on the floorâit should turn black in 2 seconds. The fire should be at the back or side, with flames licking the dome. Let the oven heat for 45â60 minutes. For a 12-inch pizza, the baking time is 60â90 seconds. Any longer, and the base burns before the cheese melts. Any shorter, and the centre is raw. Here's the sequence: slide the pizza onto the floor near the fire. After 20 seconds, use the metal peel to lift the edge and check for charring. If it's dark, rotate 180 degrees. After another 20 seconds, rotate again. The pizza should have leopard-spotted char on the rim and a crisp bottom. If the top is not melting, lift the pizza towards the dome for 10 seconds (called 'doming'). If the bottom is burning but the top is raw, your floor is too hot; move the pizza to a cooler spot or reduce the fire. In Africa, wood types matter: use hardwood like acacia or eucalyptus, not softwood, which sparks and burns fast. Keep a small fire going on the side, adding one or two small logs every 10 minutes. Never use wet wood. For multiple pizzas, bake one at a time and keep the fire consistent. After baking, rest the pizza on a wire rack for 30 seconds before slicingâthis prevents sogginess. Cut into 6 or 8 slices with a pizza wheel or sharp knife. Serve immediately. With practice, you'll hit 75 seconds consistently. Remember: the oven is ready when the dome is white and the floor is hot but not smoking.

6. Troubleshooting Common Baking Issues

Even with practice, problems happen. Here's how to fix the six most common issues. 1) Soggy centre: too much sauce or wet toppings. Use less sauce (60ml) and drain mozzarella. Pre-cook vegetables. 2) Burnt bottom, raw top: floor is too hot. Move pizza to a cooler spot or dome it for 10 seconds. Reduce fire. 3) Raw bottom, burnt top: floor not hot enough. Reheat oven longer; the floor must be 400Â°C. 4) Dough sticks to peel: not enough semolina or you took too long. Dust peel generously, work fast, and shake before loading. 5) Pizza tears when stretching: dough is too cold or over-proofed. Let it warm up 2 hours; if over-proofed, re-ball and rest 30 minutes. 6) Cheese not melting: oven not hot enough or cheese too cold. Use room-temperature low-moisture mozzarella. 7) Crust too pale: oven not hot enough or dough too thick. Stretch thinner and increase heat. 8) Bitter taste: burnt flour on peel. Clean peel between pizzas. 9) Pizza won't slide off peel: too many toppings or wet dough. Use less toppings and dust with semolina. 10) Uneven baking: rotate pizza every 20 seconds. For African conditions, high humidity makes dough stickyâuse less water in dough (60% hydration) and more semolina. If your oven has no thermometer, use the flour test. Always keep a spray bottle of water to control flare-ups. If flames rise too high, sprinkle a little water on the fire. Never use a wet cloth on a hot oven. Practice with a simple margherita until you can bake consistently in 75 seconds. Then add toppings. Troubleshooting is about observation: watch the crust, smell the aroma, and adjust. With time, you'll develop intuition.
