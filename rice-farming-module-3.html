3. Land Preparation and Water Control

Rice is 50% a water crop. Land prep and water control decide your yield more than anything else.

Module 3 of 12 · Rice Farming for Profit

Why land preparation matters

Rice needs a level, puddled field with controlled water. Poor land preparation causes:

  • Uneven water distribution (dry spots and flooded spots)
  • Higher weed pressure
  • Poor fertiliser efficiency (nutrients wash away)
  • Reduced yield by 20-40%

The four preparation steps

1. Clearing

  • Clear the previous crop residue, weeds, and stumps.
  • Remove stones, roots, and trash.
  • Do this at the end of the dry season, before rains begin.

2. Primary tillage

  • Plough to 20-25 cm deep. Break up any hardpan - rice roots need depth.
  • Hand hoe for small plots (0.1-0.3 ha); animal or tractor plough for larger.
  • Best done 3-6 weeks before planting.

3. Secondary tillage (puddling)

  • Flood the field with 5-10 cm of water.
  • Plough and harrow again in the water. This creates a soft mud (puddle).
  • Puddling seals the soil, reduces water loss, and kills weeds.
  • Level the field carefully - this is critical.

4. Bund formation

  • Bunds are raised soil walls around the field, 20-30 cm high, 30-40 cm wide at the base.
  • They hold the water in the field.
  • Repair bunds every season.
  • Leave gaps for water inlet and outlet.

Land levelling - the single biggest factor

A 5 cm difference in soil level across a field can cause 20-30% yield loss. High spots dry out, low spots drown the rice.

Water control systems

Inlet

  • Where water enters the field. Located at the highest corner.
  • Simple wooden or metal gate, or just a gap in the bund.
  • Control flow to avoid eroding the field.

Outlet

  • Where water exits the field. Located at the lowest corner.
  • Must allow drainage at end of season.
  • Bund overflow (a lower section of bund) allows excess water to escape without washing away the whole bund.

Drainage channels

  • Surround the field with a shallow drain 30-40 cm deep.
  • Carry excess water away during heavy rain.
  • Prevent the field from flooding unexpectedly.

Never let rice dry out during flowering. Water stress at flowering causes panicle sterility - empty grains. This is the single biggest cause of yield loss in rain-fed rice.

System of Rice Intensification (SRI)

SRI is a low-input method that increases yield. Key principles:

  1. Transplant young seedlings (8-12 days old, not 21-30 days).
  2. Plant 1 seedling per hill, not 3-4.
  3. Wide spacing (25-30 cm × 25-30 cm) instead of dense planting.
  4. Intermittent irrigation - keep soil moist, not continuously flooded.
  5. Weed early and often.
  6. Add organic matter (compost, manure) to the soil.

SRI can increase yield 20-50% with 30-50% less water and seed. But it requires more labour for weeding.

Intercropping and rotation

  • Rice-bean rotation - beans after rice fix nitrogen
  • Rice-fish - fish in flooded rice fields (integrated farming)
  • Rice-duck - ducks eat weeds and insects, add manure
  • Rice-azolla - floating fern fixes nitrogen in flooded fields
  • Rice-vegetable - quick vegetables between rice crops

Investment in water infrastructure

For irrigated rice, the water infrastructure is 60-80% of the initial cost. Consider:

  • Solar pump ($800-3,000) vs diesel pump ($500-2,500 + fuel)
  • Piped canal or concrete-lined channel (reduces water loss 20-30%)
  • Storage pond or reservoir to capture rainfall
  • Shared infrastructure (with neighbours or cooperative) to reduce cost
The water rule: Level the field, control the water, and rice will reward you. A 5 cm soil level difference costs 20-30% of yield. Level first, plant second.
Practical exercise 3: Measure the water level at 5 different points in your rice plot. If the difference is more than 5 cm, level it before planting.
