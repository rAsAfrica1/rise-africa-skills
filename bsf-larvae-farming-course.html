â  SAFETY WARNING â CHEMICALS, VACCINES AND FOOD SAFETY

Poultry production involves handling veterinary chemicals, vaccines, and disinfectants that can harm you and leave residues in meat and eggs if misused. Follow these rules without exception:

  • Withdrawal periods are not optional. Never sell meat or eggs during the withdrawal period stated on any drug or vaccine label. Residues can make consumers sick and will get you banned from formal buyers.
  • Wear gloves and a mask when handling disinfectants, formalin, or any concentrated chemical. Work in ventilated space.
  • Never use human antibiotics on poultry. This drives antimicrobial resistance and is illegal in most markets.
  • Store vaccines cold and correctly. A broken cold chain means a useless vaccine and a dead flock.
  • Wash hands and change clothes after handling birds or chemicals, especially before preparing or eating food.
  • Dispose of dead birds and chemical containers properly. Never sell or consume dead birds. Bury or incinerate away from water sources.
  • If you or a worker feel unwell after chemical exposure, seek medical attention immediately and bring the product label.

Quick Reference Checklist

  1. Record feed intake daily. Without feed records you cannot calculate FCR, and FCR is where your profit lives.
  2. Weigh a sample of birds weekly. Ten birds per house is enough to spot a growth problem before it costs you a whole cycle.
  3. Track mortality every single day. A sudden spike means disease, heat stress, or a ventilation failure â act within 24 hours.
  4. Buy chicks only from certified hatcheries. Cheap chicks from unknown sources carry disease that will wipe out your margin.
  5. Calculate cost per kg or cost per egg every cycle. If you do not know this number, you are guessing at your own profitability.
  6. Maintain a strict biosecurity routine: footbaths, limited visitors, clean water, and separation between age groups.
  7. Secure your market before you stock the house. A confirmed buyer at a known price is worth more than a slightly cheaper chick.
  8. Keep a simple cash book. Revenue, feed cost, labour, and medicine â four columns. Review weekly, not yearly.
