Drone Laws in Africa - Flying Legally, Country by Country

🧠 Brain Opener

Drone laws are not the same in every country. What is legal in Kenya may be illegal in Zimbabwe. What is fine for a hobbyist may be a crime for a commercial operator. Learn the rules before you fly.

  • Who regulates drones in your country?
  • What is the difference between hobby and commercial drone flying?
  • What are the penalties for flying without a licence?
🎁 Reveal a good answer

Most African countries regulate drones through their civil aviation authority. Hobby flying usually needs no licence but has strict limits on altitude, distance, and no-fly zones. Commercial flying always requires registration and often a licence. Penalties range from fines to prison, depending on the country.

📖 Tafadzwa Studies the Rules

Tafadzwa was seventeen and had built a small drone business. He was earning 150 dollars a month shooting property videos in Harare.

Then a client asked him to shoot footage near the Harare International Airport. "Just a few shots from outside the fence," the client said. "They will pay you 300 dollars."

Tafadzwa said he would think about it. He called his cousin who worked at the Civil Aviation Authority of Zimbabwe.

His cousin did not laugh. She said one sentence: "Do you want to spend five years in prison?"

Tafadzwa went online that night. He found the actual law. No flying within 5 kilometres of an aerodrome. No flying above 120 metres. No flying over people. Every commercial flight required a written authorisation from CAAZ. The fine for a first offence was up to 5,000 dollars or a year in prison.

He called the client back and said no.

The client was annoyed. "Everyone does it," he said. "Nobody gets caught."

"I got caught yesterday," Tafadzwa said. "By myself. I read the law."

He spent the next month studying drone regulations for Zimbabwe, Kenya, South Africa, and Nigeria. He wrote a one-page summary of each. He pinned them on his wall.

From that day on, every commercial job he took was registered. Every flight was authorised. He charged slightly more than unregistered pilots because he did the paperwork. His clients preferred it. Legal pilots did not cancel a shoot because the police arrived.

💡 The Big Idea

Drone laws exist to protect everyone

Every drone law in every country was written after a real incident. The rules are not meant to stop you. They are meant to keep drones from flying into jet engines, from spying on people, from crashing into crowds. Following them protects your clients, your business, and you.

📚 Three Deep Lessons

1

The common rules across Africa

Almost every African country has these rules. Maximum altitude: 120 metres (400 feet). No flying within 5 kilometres of an airport. No flying over crowds, stadiums, or public events. No flying without visual line of sight. No flying at night without special authorisation.

The specifics vary. Kenya requires drone registration for all drones above 250 grams. South Africa requires an RPL (Remote Pilot Licence) for all commercial flying. Nigeria requires a licence from the NCAA. Zimbabwe requires authorisation from CAAZ.

2

Hobby vs commercial

Hobby flying usually means flying for fun, no payment, no client. Commercial flying means flying for money, for a client, or for a business purpose - even if you are not paid directly.

The rules are much stricter for commercial flying. If you shoot a video for a client, you are commercial. If you shoot it for yourself and never sell it, you are hobby. The line matters because the penalties differ significantly.

3

Getting licensed - the practical path

For most African countries, the process is: register the drone, register as an operator, sit a theory exam, complete flight training, receive a licence, apply for authorisation for each flight. The whole process takes three to six months and costs 200 to 1,000 dollars depending on the country.

It is worth it. Licensed pilots can charge three to five times more than unlicensed ones because clients know the work is legally safe. Most commercial drone businesses in Africa are licensed. The ones that are not are usually caught eventually.

🛠 Hands-On - Write Your Country Drone Rules Summary

You need

  • A notebook
  • A computer or phone with internet access
  • The website of your country civil aviation authority
  1. Search for your country civil aviation authority drone rules.
  2. Find the official PDF or page listing the rules.
  3. Write down: maximum altitude, no-fly zones, registration requirements, commercial requirements.
  4. List the penalties for breaking each rule.
  5. Write a one-page summary.
  6. Pin it up. Refer to it before every flight.
Why it works: Knowing the rules is the difference between a business and a court case.

🌍 Real Stories from Africa

Kenya drone licensing, 2020

Kenya introduced a comprehensive drone regulation in 2020. All drones above 250 grams must be registered. Commercial operators must be licensed. Since 2020, over 3,000 drones have been registered. Unregistered drones are confiscated on sight.

South Africa RPL

South Africa requires a Remote Pilot Licence for all commercial drone flying. The licence takes three to six months to obtain and costs around 1,000 dollars. Licensed pilots charge 300 to 800 dollars per day. Unlicensed pilots face fines of up to 5,000 dollars.
