4. Pruning, Watering and Fertilising

A neglected citrus tree produces 50 fruits. A well-managed one produces 400. Here is the routine that closes the gap.

Module 4 of 6 · Citrus Farming for Profit

Why prune citrus

  • Controls tree height - fruit within reach
  • Opens the canopy for airflow - less disease
  • Removes dead, diseased, and crossing branches
  • Promotes new fruiting wood (citrus fruits on 1-2 year old wood)
  • Increases fruit size and quality

The ideal tree shape

Citrus should be trained to a vase or open-centre shape:

  1. Year 1: Select 3-4 main scaffold branches, evenly spaced around the trunk, at 40-60 cm height.
  2. Year 2: Remove any branches growing inward or crossing. Keep the centre open.
  3. Year 3: Continue to open the centre. Remove water shoots.
  4. Year 4+: Maintenance only. Remove water shoots, dead wood, and any branch blocking light.

Pruning calendar

WhenWhat
After harvestMain pruning - remove dead wood, thin the canopy
Mid-seasonLight pruning - water shoots, diseased wood
AnytimeRemove suckers from below graft union immediately

Never prune citrus heavily in the same year you want a big crop - pruning reduces the next crop.

Water shoots and suckers

  • Water shoots: vigorous vertical shoots from older branches. Remove them - they take energy and produce no fruit.
  • Suckers below graft union: from the rootstock. Remove immediately. They can take over the tree.
  • Check monthly. Cut or snap at the base.

Watering mature trees

SeasonWater per tree per week
Dry season150-250 L
Cool season80-120 L
Rainy season0-50 L

Water stress during flowering and fruit set causes fruit drop. Never let a citrus tree dry out at these stages.

Plus micronutrients:

  • Zinc sulphate: 30-50 g foliar per tree per year - prevents little leaf
  • Magnesium (Epsom salt): 100-200 g foliar or soil
  • Boron: 10-20 g foliar - careful, narrow margin
  • Iron: foliar chelate if yellowing young leaves

Mulching

Maintain 5-10 cm mulch around each tree, keeping 10 cm clear of the trunk. Benefits:

  • Reduces evaporation 30-50%
  • Suppresses weeds
  • Adds organic matter
  • Prevents soil splash onto fruit
  • Reduces fruit fly breeding sites
The watering rule: Never let a citrus tree dry out during flowering or fruit set. Water stress causes fruit drop and cuts your harvest in half.
