5. Breeding and Foaling

Horse breeding is a 12-month cycle from mating to foal. Every step has to be right.

Module 5 of 12 · Horse Breeding for Profit

Breeding basics

Age at first breeding (stallion)3-5 years

Breeding season - why timing matters

Horses are seasonal breeders. Mares come into heat with increasing daylight. In temperate zones, the natural breeding season is April-September (northern hemisphere) or October-March (southern hemisphere).

In tropical Africa, mares often cycle year-round. But for best conception rates and to time foaling for good weather, aim for:

Your climateBest breeding timeFoaling
Summer rainfallOctober-DecemberSeptember-November (before rains)
Winter rainfallSeptember-NovemberAugust-October (spring grass)
EquatorialAny time (with management)Schedule for best forage

Signs of heat in mares

  • Squatting and urinating frequently
  • Winking (opening and closing of the vulva)
  • Squaling, especially at other horses
  • Tail raised
  • Standing to be mounted
  • Swollen vulva with discharge
  • Restless, moody, difficult to handle
  • Seeking the stallion

Heat lasts 5-7 days. Mares should be bred every other day while in heat, or on day 2-5 of heat.

Breeding methods

MethodDescriptionBest for

Hand breeding is best for small operations: safer, more control, records are accurate.

Pre-breeding checks

  • Mare: body condition score 3-4, sound, good teeth, no reproductive issues
  • Stallion: fertility tested, sound, calm temperament
  • Vaccinations up to date: EHV-1/4 (rhinopneumonitis), influenza, tetanus, EVA
  • Deworming at least 2 weeks before breeding
  • Uterine swab: screen for infections (especially in mares with previous problems)
  • Teeth checked: horses must chew well to maintain condition for pregnancy

Early diagnosis (day 14-16) allows re-breeding if the mare is not pregnant - keeping the foaling interval to 12 months.

Pregnancy care

StageFeed and care
Early (months 0-5)Maintenance diet, mild exercise, routine care
Mid (months 5-8)Continue routine, monitor condition, no heavy work
Late (months 8-11)Increase feed, vaccinate 4-6 weeks pre-foaling, prepare foaling area
  • Vaccinate for tetanus and EHV-1/4 (rhinopneumonitis) 4-6 weeks before foaling.
  • Deworm 4 weeks before foaling.
  • Reduce work in the last 3 months.
  • Move to a foaling paddock 4-6 weeks before due date.

Foaling - the three stages

Stage 1 (1-4 hours)

  • Restlessness, pacing, sweating
  • Frequent urination
  • Looking at flanks
  • Milk dripping from teats
  • Isolating from herd

Stage 2 (20-40 minutes)

  • Water breaks
  • Mare lies down
  • Front feet and head appear
  • Foal delivered within 30 minutes of water breaking

Stage 3 (1-3 hours)

  • Placenta passes
  • Foal attempts to stand (within 1 hour)
  • Foal nurses (within 2 hours)

Foaling complications - when to call the vet

ProblemAction
No foal 30 min after water breaksCall vet immediately

Mare exhausted, straining without progressCall vet
Placenta retained over 3 hoursCall vet
Foal not nursing by 3 hoursAssist or call vet
Mare rejects foalRestrain mare, facilitate nursing, call vet
Never pull hard on a foal. Horse legs are fragile. If the foal is not coming out with the mare''s own contractions, call the vet. A broken leg means the foal is lost.

Care of newborn foal

  1. Clear nostrils if there is a membrane covering them.
  2. Let the mare lick the foal dry - bond forms within hours.
  3. Ensure foal nurses within 2 hours - colostrum is critical.
  4. Dip navel in iodine - prevents infection.
  5. Check the foal passes meconium (first dung) within 12 hours.
  6. Weigh and record birth weight (typically 40-60 kg).
  7. Vaccinate at 3-6 months for tetanus, influenza, EHV.
  8. Deworm at 2, 4, 6, 8, 12 weeks - foals are very susceptible.

Care of the mare post-foaling

  • Provide fresh water, good hay, and a warm mash.
  • Keep her quiet for 48 hours.
  • Watch for milk fever (rare in horses) or retained placenta.
  • Check udder for mastitis daily for the first week.
  • Begin light exercise (walking) after 1 week.
  • First heat usually 5-12 days post-foaling (foal heat). Do not breed on this heat if she had a difficult foaling.

Weaning

AgeMethod
4-6 monthsTraditional weaning - separate gradually
6 monthsStandard - foal well established on solids
Pre-weaning preparationIntroduce creep feed at 1-2 months, so foal is eating well before weaning

Weaning tips:

  • Wean in a quiet paddock - two young horses together is less stressful.
  • Feed a high-quality ration for 2-3 weeks post-weaning.
  • Vaccinate and deworm at weaning.
  • Watch for pneumonia or diarrhoea - stress weakens immunity.
The 12-month rule: A mare should produce one foal per year. To achieve this, breed back within 30-45 days of foaling. This requires good nutrition and monitoring for foal heat.
Practical exercise 5: Record your mare''s heat cycles for 3 months. Mark each heat date on a calendar. Predict the next heat. Plan breeding accordingly.
