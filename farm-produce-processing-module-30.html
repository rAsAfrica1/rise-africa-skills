Rice Processing

8 lessons · 30 quiz questions · Cereals & Grains

1 Paddy varieties

Rice (Oryza sativa) comes in two main subspecies and many varieties.

Indica: Long-grain, grown in tropics. Varieties: Basmati, Jasmine, IR64, local African varieties.

Japonica: Short-grain, cooler climates. Sticky when cooked. Used for sushi and risotto.

Aromatic: Basmati, Jasmine, Thai fragrant. Premium priced.

African rice (Oryza glaberrima): Indigenous to West Africa. Hardy but low yield. Being crossed with Asian rice to improve.

For processing, choose varieties with good milling yield (over 60% head rice).

2 Cleaning and dehusking

Paddy (rice with husk) needs to be cleaned and dehusked before further processing.

  1. Clean paddy — remove stones, straw, immature grains
  2. Use a destoner and aspirator
  3. Dehusk using a rubber-roll huller or steel huller
  4. Rubber roll huller is gentler — fewer broken grains
  5. Separate husk from brown rice (aspiration)

Byproduct: Rice husk (~20% of paddy weight) — used for fuel, bedding, compost.

💡 Save the husk. It's excellent fuel for drying, parboiling or brick kilns.

3 Polishing and grading

Brown rice has bran layers that are removed to make white rice.

  1. Pass brown rice through a polisher (abrasive or friction type)
  2. Remove bran layers — 8–12% of brown rice weight
  3. Polish to desired level (white, lightly milled, or brown)
  4. Grade by length:
    • Head rice (whole grains) — premium
    • Broken rice (60–75% of grain) — mid-price
    • Brewers rice (under 50%) — lowest price, used for beer and rice flour

Total milling yield: 100 kg paddy → 60–65 kg white rice (55 kg head rice + 10 kg broken).

4 Broken rice uses

Broken rice is 15–20% of milled output. Don't throw it away — sell it!

  • Rice flour: Mill broken rice into fine flour. Used in gluten-free baking, baby food, noodles.
  • Rice noodles: Traditional Asian product. Requires extruder.
  • Rice milk: Blend with water, strain, sweeten. Growing dairy-alternative market.
  • Brewing: Sold to breweries for beer and sake.
  • Animal feed: Low-grade but steady market.

Rice flour retail: $1.50–$4 per kg. Rice milk: $3–$6 per litre.

5 Parboiled rice

Parboiled rice is paddy soaked, steamed, and dried before milling. This drives nutrients from bran into the grain, reduces breakage, and gives firmer cooked rice.

  1. Soak paddy in hot water (60°C) for 4–6 hours
  2. Steam 15–20 minutes under pressure
  3. Dry to 12–14% moisture
  4. Mill as normal

Parboiled rice is popular in India, West Africa, and the Middle East. Commands 10–20% higher price than raw rice.

6 Packaging

Packaging by product:

  • White rice — poly bags (1 kg, 5 kg, 25 kg)
  • Premium (Basmati, Jasmine) — cloth or paper bags (1–5 kg)
  • Rice flour — foil bags or paper (500 g, 1 kg, 5 kg)
  • Parboiled — 1 kg, 5 kg, 25 kg, 50 kg poly sacks

Shelf life: White rice stores 2+ years in dry conditions. Brown rice 6 months (bran goes rancid). Parboiled 2+ years.

⚠ Brown rice has a short shelf life because of the oil-rich bran. Sell within 6 months or refrigerate.

7 Rice bran and oil

Rice bran (8–10% of paddy weight) is a valuable byproduct.

  • Rice bran oil: Extracted from fresh bran. High in oryzanol (antioxidant). Premium cooking oil. Retail $5–$10 per litre.
  • Animal feed: Bran is a nutritious livestock feed.
  • Fermented bran: Used in Korean and Japanese pickling (nukazuke).

Bran must be stabilised within 24 hours of milling (heat treatment) or it goes rancid fast.

8 Storage and moisture control

Rice storage is all about moisture and pest control.

  • Moisture: 12–14% for storage (14% maximum)
  • Temperature: Cool, dry (below 25°C)
  • Pests: Rice weevils and moths are the biggest threat
  • Protection: Hermetic storage bags, metal silos, or CO₂ fumigation
  • Testing: Aflatoxin and pesticide residues for export

Well-stored white rice keeps 2–3 years. Poorly stored rice goes mouldy or infested within months.

💰 Highest margin rice products: premium aromatic rice (Basmati, Jasmine), rice flour, rice milk, parboiled rice. Plain white rice is high volume, low margin.
