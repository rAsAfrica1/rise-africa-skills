Cashew Processing

8 lessons · 30 quiz questions · Nuts & Seeds

1 Cashew varieties

Cashew (Anacardium occidentale) produces both a nut (the "cashew") and a fruit (the "cashew apple"). Most processing focuses on the nut.

Cashew nut grades (by size):

  • W180 (Jumbo): 180 nuts per lb. Premium.
  • W210: 210 per lb. Standard premium.
  • W240: 240 per lb. Mid-grade.
  • W320: 320 per lb. Most common export grade.
  • W450: 450 per lb. Smaller.
  • Splits (S): Broken pieces.

Grading by size is the biggest price driver. Larger = higher price.

2 Raw cashew handling

Raw cashew nuts (RCN) come inside a hard shell surrounded by a caustic liquid (CNSL — cashew nut shell liquid).

  1. Dry RCN in sun 3–5 days to 8–10% moisture
  2. Clean — remove stones, foreign matter
  3. Grade by size before processing
  4. Store in dry conditions (away from moisture and pests)

RCN storage: 6–12 months in proper conditions. Poor storage leads to mould and aflatoxin.

⚠ CNSL causes severe skin burns. Always wear protective gloves and eye protection when handling raw cashew shells.

3 Steaming and shelling

The shell must be cracked without damaging the kernel.

  1. Steam raw nuts at 100°C for 10–15 minutes (softens shell, reduces CNSL splash)
  2. Cool
  3. Shell with a mechanical sheller (or by hand with a shell-cracker tool)
  4. Separate kernels from shells
  5. Save CNSL — it's valuable for lubricants and resins

Shelling yield: 100 kg RCN → 22–25 kg kernel + 70 kg shell.

💡 CNSL (cashew nut shell liquid) sells for $300–$600 per tonne to industrial buyers. Don't throw away the shells!

4 Testa (skin) removal

Kernels have a thin inner skin (testa) that must be removed for a clean white cashew.

  1. Dry kernels in sun 4–8 hours (loosens testa)
  2. Rub kernels between hands or with a mechanical peeler
  3. For stubborn testa: steam 5 minutes, then peel
  4. Sort by completeness: whole, split, broken

Whole kernels sell at premium. Broken pieces sell for confectionery and cooking.

5 Grading whole vs broken

Grading is where most of the price difference comes from.

Whole grades (top prices):

  • W180 (Jumbo) — biggest, most expensive
  • W210 — slightly smaller
  • W240, W320, W450 — progressively smaller

Broken grades (lower prices):

  • Splits (S) — halves
  • Pieces (LWP, SWP, BB) — quarter pieces and small bits

W320 is the most common export grade. Jumbo W180 commands 3–5× the price of W450.

6 Roasting and salting

Roasted salted cashews are the biggest retail product.

  1. Dry roasted: bake at 160°C for 12–15 minutes, stirring
  2. Oil roasted: fry at 150°C for 3–4 minutes
  3. Salt: toss with oil and salt (1.5%)
  4. Cool completely
  5. Pack in foil pouches or jars

Yield: 100 kg kernels → 90–95 kg roasted.

Retail: $15–$30 per kg.

7 Cashew butter and cashew milk

Cashew butter: Same process as peanut butter. Roast, grind with salt and oil. Retail $12–$25 per 500 g jar.

Cashew milk: Soak raw cashews 4 hours, blend with water (1:4), strain, add sweetener and vanilla. Retail $3–$6 per litre.

Both are premium products due to the higher cost of cashews vs other nuts.

8 Export standards

Cashew is one of Africa's biggest agricultural exports (Côte d'Ivoire, Tanzania, Nigeria, Ghana, Benin).

Export grades: W180, W210, W240, W320, W450, Splits, Pieces.

Export markets: India, Vietnam (processing), EU, US (consumption). Prices (2026): W320 = $7–$10 per kg FOB. W180 = $12–$18 per kg FOB.

Compliance:

  • Phytosanitary certificate
  • Aflatoxin test (under 10 ppb for EU)
  • Pesticide residue test
  • Moisture under 5%
  • HACCP certification for premium buyers
💰 Africa exports mostly raw cashew nuts (RCN) to India and Vietnam — earning $1,500–$2,500 per tonne. Processing locally into kernels earns 5× more. This is the single biggest opportunity in African cashew.
