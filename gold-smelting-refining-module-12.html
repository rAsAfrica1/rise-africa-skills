Lessons

1. Introduction and Why It Matters

You have spent time and money refining your gold to high purity. Now you need to sell it for the best possible price. In Africa, many small-scale miners and refiners lose money because they sell to the first buyer who comes along, often a local trader who offers a low price and no transparency. This module will teach you how to sell your refined gold safely, legally, and for a fair price. You will learn about different buyers, how to test purity, how to negotiate, and how to avoid common scams.

Gold is a global commodity. Its price is set daily in London and New York, and you can check it on your phone using apps like Kitco or Gold Price Live. For example, if the global gold price is $60 per gram, a 10-gram bar of 99.9% pure gold is worth about $600. But local buyers may offer you only $45 per gram, claiming they need a profit margin. That is a 25% loss for you. If you sell 100 grams, you lose $1,500 enough to buy a new furnace or pay school fees.

Why does this matter? Because selling well means you can reinvest in better equipment, safer chemicals, and more gold. It also means you can support your family and community. In countries like Ghana, Tanzania, and South Africa, there are legal gold buying centers and licensed exporters who pay close to international prices. But you must know how to find them and how to prove your gold's purity. You also need to understand taxes and export rules to avoid trouble with the law.

This module is practical. You will learn to use a touchstone and acid test kit, a digital scale, and a crucible for melting samples. You will learn to calculate the value of your gold based on the day's price and purity. You will also learn how to negotiate with buyers, get a written receipt, and choose between selling locally or exporting. By the end, you will be able to turn your refined gold into cash that reflects its true worth. Remember: knowledge is your best tool for getting a fair deal.

2. Tools and Materials

To sell refined gold properly, you need a few basic tools and materials. First, a precise digital scale that measures in grams to at least two decimal places (0.01g). A good scale costs between $50 and $150. You will use it to weigh your gold accurately before any sale. Second, a touchstone and acid test kit. This includes a black stone (touchstone) and small bottles of nitric acid and hydrochloric acid, or a ready-made gold testing kit. These cost about $30 to $80. They help you verify purity and show buyers you are serious.

Third, a melting kit: a small crucible, tongs, and a torch or furnace. You need this to melt a small sample of your gold into a button or bar for testing. If you already refine gold, you have these. Fourth, a calculator or smartphone with a gold price app. You must check the live gold price daily. Apps like Kitco, Gold Price Live, or Reuters give free updates. Fifth, a notebook and pen to record weights, purity, prices, and buyer details. This protects you if there is a dispute.

You also need safety gear: gloves, goggles, and a mask when handling acids or melting gold. For selling, you need a clean container or sealed bag for your gold, and a written receipt book. If you plan to export, you will need official documents: a mining license, export permit, and tax clearance. In many African countries, you must sell through a licensed dealer or the central bank. For example, in Ghana, the Precious Minerals Marketing Company (PMMC) is the official buyer. In Tanzania, you can sell to licensed exporters through the Bank of Tanzania. Always ask for a receipt that shows weight, purity, price per gram, and total amount.

Finally, consider a small fire assay kit if you want to test purity more accurately. Fire assay is the standard method, but it requires a furnace and cupels. A basic kit costs around $200. If you cannot afford it, rely on acid testing and a reputable buyer's assay. Remember, the goal is to prove your gold's value so you can negotiate from a position of strength. With these tools, you are ready to sell.

3. Step by Step Method

Step 1: Weigh your gold. Use your digital scale to record the exact weight in grams. For example, you have 50.25 grams of refined gold. Write this down. Step 2: Test purity. Use your acid test kit or touchstone. Rub the gold on the touchstone, then apply a drop of nitric acid. If the mark disappears, it is not gold. If it remains, test with aqua regia (a mix of nitric and hydrochloric acid) to compare against a known standard. For high purity (99%+), the mark should not react. Alternatively, melt a small sample and send it to a lab for fire assay. A lab test costs $20-$50 but gives a certificate.

Step 3: Check the live gold price. Use your phone to find the current price per gram for 99.9% pure gold. For example, if the price is $60 per gram, your 50.25 grams at 99.9% purity is worth 50.25 x $60 = $3,015. If your gold is 95% pure, adjust: 50.25 x 0.95 x $60 = $2,864.25. Step 4: Find buyers. Options include local gold dealers, licensed exporters, jewelry makers, and online platforms like GoldDealers.com or local Facebook groups. Visit at least three buyers to compare offers. Ask each for their price per gram and any fees. Step 5: Negotiate. Show your test results and the live price. A fair buyer will offer 90-95% of the live price. If they offer less, ask why. Some buyers deduct for refining costs, but if your gold is already refined, that is not fair. Be polite but firm.

Step 6: Complete the sale. Once you agree on a price, weigh the gold in front of the buyer on a calibrated scale. Get a written receipt with the buyer's name, license number, weight, purity, price per gram, total amount, and date. Count your money carefully. If selling internationally, use a bank transfer or secure payment method. Step 7: Record the transaction. Note the buyer, amount, and any taxes paid. This helps with future sales and tax compliance. Step 8: Reinvest. Use your earnings to buy more gold, improve your refining setup, or save for export permits. Always keep some cash for emergencies. By following these steps, you ensure you get a fair price and avoid being cheated.

4. Common Mistakes and Fixes

Mistake 1: Selling without testing purity. Many sellers accept the buyer's word on purity and get paid for lower grade gold. Fix: Always test your gold with an acid kit or fire assay before selling. If you cannot test, insist on a joint test with the buyer at a reputable lab. Mistake 2: Not checking the live gold price. If you do not know the current price, you cannot negotiate. Fix: Check a reliable app like Kitco every morning. Write down the price per gram for 24K, 22K, and 18K gold.

Mistake 3: Selling to unlicensed buyers. This can lead to arrest, confiscation, or not getting paid. Fix: Only sell to licensed dealers or official buying centers. In Ghana, use PMMC; in Tanzania, use licensed exporters; in South Africa, use the Rand Refinery or registered dealers. Ask to see their license. Mistake 4: Accepting cash without a receipt. If the buyer later claims you sold fake gold, you have no proof. Fix: Always get a written receipt with all details. Use a receipt book with numbered pages. Take a photo of the buyer's ID and license.

Mistake 5: Ignoring taxes and export rules. Selling gold without paying taxes can result in fines or jail. Fix: Learn your country's tax rate for gold sales. In many African countries, there is a 3-5% export tax or royalty. If you export, get an export permit and pay the required fees. Mistake 6: Selling all your gold at once. If you sell everything, you have no stock to refine and sell later. Fix: Sell in batches. Keep at least 20% of your refined gold for future sales when prices are higher.

Mistake 7: Using a faulty scale. A scale that is off by 0.1 gram can cost you $6 per sale. Fix: Calibrate your scale with a 100-gram calibration weight every week. Buy a calibration weight for $10. Mistake 8: Trusting a buyer who pressures you. Scammers create urgency. Fix: Walk away if you feel rushed. Take your gold home and come back another day. A fair buyer will not pressure you. By avoiding these mistakes, you protect your money and your reputation.

5. Safety and Best Practice

Safety when selling gold is not just about physical harm; it is also about financial and legal safety. First, personal safety. When carrying gold to a buyer, do not travel alone. Use a trusted friend or family member. Do not announce that you are carrying gold. Use a plain bag or pocket. If you are selling a large amount, arrange for a bank transfer or meet at a police station or bank. In some cities, gold buyers have secure offices. Avoid meeting in dark alleys or private homes.

Second, chemical safety. If you test purity with acids, wear gloves, goggles, and a mask. Work in a well-ventilated area. Never add water to acid; always add acid to water. Keep a bucket of sand or baking soda nearby to neutralize spills. Store acids in locked cabinets away from children. Third, legal safety. Only sell to licensed buyers. Keep a copy of your mining license, export permit, and tax receipts. If a buyer offers to pay cash without a receipt, refuse. That is a sign of illegal activity. In many African countries, you must declare gold sales for tax purposes. Failure to do so can lead to fines or imprisonment.

Best practices for selling: Always get a written receipt. Record the buyer's name, license number, weight, purity, price per gram, total amount, and date. Take a photo of the receipt and the buyer's ID. Keep a logbook of all sales. This helps you track your income and prove legitimacy. Another best practice: Build relationships with two or three reliable buyers. Do not rely on one buyer. Competition gives you better prices. Also, consider joining a gold miners' association or cooperative. These groups often negotiate better prices and provide legal support.

Finally, plan for the future. Use your earnings to invest in better refining equipment, safety gear, and training. Save at least 10% of each sale in a bank account or mobile money. This cushion helps you during price dips or emergencies. Remember, selling gold is a business. Treat it with the same care you give to smelting and refining. By following safety and best practices, you protect yourself, your family, and your community.

6. Practical Exercise

Now it is time to practice what you have learned. This exercise will guide you through a simulated gold sale. You will need: a digital scale, a calculator, a phone with a gold price app, a notebook, and a partner to act as a buyer. If you have real gold, use a small amount (1-5 grams) for practice. Otherwise, use a metal object of similar weight. Step 1: Weigh your gold. Record the weight in grams. For example, 5.00 grams. Step 2: Check the live gold price. Suppose it is $60 per gram for 99.9% pure gold. Calculate the full value: 5.00 x $60 = $300. Step 3: Determine your gold's purity. If you refined it, assume 99% purity. Adjust value: 5.00 x 0.99 x $60 = $297. Write this down.

Step 4: Role-play as a seller. Ask your partner to act as a buyer. The buyer should offer you $250 for the 5 grams. That is $50 per gram, which is 83% of the live price. Your job is to negotiate. Show the buyer your weight, purity test (use a touchstone if available), and the live price. Explain that a fair price is 90-95% of the live price, so $267 to $282. Ask the buyer to justify the lower offer. Practice being firm but polite. Step 5: Close the deal. Agree on a price, say $275. Calculate the total: 5.00 x $55 = $275. Write a receipt with all details. Have your partner sign it. Step 6: Record the transaction in your notebook. Note the date, buyer's name, weight, purity, price per gram, total, and any fees.

Step 7: Reflect. What went well? What was difficult? Did you feel pressured? How could you improve next time? Repeat the exercise with different gold prices and purity levels. For example, try 10 grams at 95% purity with a live price of $55 per gram. Calculate the fair value and negotiate. Step 8: Research real buyers. Use your phone to find three licensed gold buyers in your area or country. Write down their names, addresses, phone numbers, and license numbers. Call one and ask about their buying process, price, and fees. This prepares you for real sales. By completing this exercise, you build confidence and skill. Remember, negotiation is a skill that improves with practice. The more you practice, the better price you will get for your refined gold.

<h2>Quick Reference Checklist</h2>
<ol>
<li><strong>Batch ID stamped</strong> on every bar or button before it leaves the smelting bench.</li>
<li><strong>Calibrated scale</strong> certificate of calibration dated within the last 12 months.</li>
<li><strong>Independent assay</strong> XRF or fire assay from a lab not related to the buyer.</li>
<li><strong>Licence and permit</strong> current, original, and matching the seller's legal name.</li>
<li><strong>Chain-of-custody form</strong> signed at every handover point, with time and location.</li>
<li><strong>Written price agreement</strong> spot reference, discount, and settlement date in writing.</li>
<li><strong>Bank or mobile money settlement</strong> avoid cash above regulatory thresholds.</li>
<li><strong>Archive everything</strong> assay, invoice, receipt, photos of sealed bars for 5 years.</li>
</ol>
