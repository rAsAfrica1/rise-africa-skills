â  SAFETY WARNING â AGROCHEMICALS AND FOOD HANDLING

  • Never store pesticides, veterinary drugs or treated seed in the same room, container or vehicle as food, feed or drinking water. Cross-contamination has caused fatal poisonings in multiple African markets.
  • Always wear gloves, boots, long sleeves, eye protection and a respirator when mixing or spraying. Re-entry intervals on the label are not optional.
  • Observe the full pre-harvest interval (PHI) before harvesting any crop treated with pesticide. Selling produce inside the PHI can breach national residue limits and destroy a buyer contract.
  • Only use products registered by your national regulator (e.g. KEPHIS in Kenya, NAFDAC in Nigeria, EPA in Ghana, PCPB where applicable). Counterfeit and banned products circulate widely â buy only from licensed agro-dealers and keep receipts.
  • For aflatoxin risk in maize and groundnuts: dry to below 13% moisture, store off the floor on pallets, and never blend visibly mouldy grain into a clean lot. Aflatoxin is a carcinogen and is not removed by cooking or milling.
  • Wash hands and equipment between handling raw manure, raw animal products and finished food. Keep a first-aid kit and the national poison control number posted at the workplace.

Quick Reference Checklist

  1. Know your unit economics. Calculate revenue, variable cost and gross margin per hectare, per bird, or per tonne before you scale. If you cannot state your gross margin, you are not ready to borrow.
  2. Secure market before production. Have at least one written or verbal off-take commitment for 60% of expected output before planting or stocking.
  3. Plan for losses. Budget for 10â20% post-harvest loss unless you have verified storage, cold chain or hermetic bags in place.
  4. Own more than one stage. Combine production with aggregation, or aggregation with light processing, to capture more of the consumer price.
  5. Get the paperwork right. Business registration, tax PIN, food-handling certificate and product registration are prerequisites for selling to supermarkets, processors and institutional buyers.
  6. Manage cash, not just profit. Map your cash flow month by month. Most African agri-SMEs fail from cash-flow timing, not from lack of profit.
  7. Comply on chemicals and food safety. Keep spray records, observe pre-harvest intervals and store agrochemicals separately from food at all times.
  8. Track three numbers weekly: cash on hand, stock on hand, and receivables. Everything else can wait for month-end.
