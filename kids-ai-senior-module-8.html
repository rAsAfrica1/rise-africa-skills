🧠 Brain Opener

AI is a powerful tool. Like every powerful tool, it can help enormously or cause real harm. The difference is what people choose to do with it. This final module is about your choices.

  • What are the biggest risks of AI today?
  • What can you personally do about them?
  • What kind of AI user do you want to become?
🎁 Reveal a good answer

The biggest risks are: misuse (deepfakes, fraud, disinformation), bias (unfair decisions at scale), job disruption (some jobs changing fast), and concentration of power (a few companies controlling the technology). What you can do: use AI honestly, verify before sharing, ask who benefits, learn deeply enough to shape how it is used, and refuse to build things that hurt people.

📖 Kudzai Makes a Choice

Kudzai was seventeen and had a choice to make.

He had built a small business using AI - creating websites for local shops. He was good at it. He charged 100 dollars per site. He was making 400 dollars a month.

Then a man called him. The man wanted a website for a crypto investment club. He would pay 500 dollars. He said it had to look professional. He asked Kudzai not to ask too many questions.

Kudzai said he would think about it.

He searched for the man name. Nothing. He searched for the club. Nothing. He read about crypto scams in Zimbabwe. There were dozens. Many of them had websites that looked exactly as professional as the man wanted.

Kudzai called his aunt, who was a lawyer. She listened.

"You are asking whether it is illegal," she said. "That is not the right question. The right question is: will people be hurt? If you build this site and someone loses their savings, will you be able to say you did not know?"

"I would know."

"Then you have your answer."

Kudzai turned the job down. The man called twice more, offering more money. Kudzai said no both times.

Two months later, a newspaper reported a scam in Harare. The company had taken 300,000 dollars from over 200 people. They had a website. Kudzai checked - it was not his, but it was exactly the kind of site he could have built.

He slept well that night. He was not a hero. He had just refused to build something that would hurt people. That was the minimum.

💡 The Big Idea

Your choices decide what AI becomes

AI does not have ethics. You do. Every AI system ever built was built by someone who made choices: what data to use, what to optimise for, who to exclude, what not to build. Those choices add up. In twenty years, the AI landscape will be shaped by what the people of today chose. You are one of those people.

📚 Three Deep Lessons

1

The four big risks

Misuse: deepfakes, fraud, disinformation, harassment. AI makes these cheaper and faster to produce. Bias: unfair decisions at scale - loans, hiring, policing, health. A biased human hurts a few people. A biased AI hurts thousands.

Job disruption: some jobs will change fast. Support roles, junior copywriting, basic data entry. Others will grow - oversight, integration, judgement. Concentration of power: a handful of companies own the biggest models. This shapes what the technology becomes and who benefits.

2

What you can do

Be honest about AI use. Say when AI helped. Never claim credit for work the AI did. Verify before sharing. Do not forward images, videos, or claims without checking. Ask who benefits. Every AI product is built for someone. Find out for whom.

Learn deeply enough to shape it. A user accepts the tool. A builder changes it. A designer decides what it is for. Aim to be more than a user. Refuse to build harm. Some jobs are not worth the money. Know your line before someone asks you to cross it.

3

The path forward

Africa is not behind. It is early. The people building AI for the next 20 years are being trained right now, in classrooms like the one you are sitting in. The tools are cheap. The data is available. The problems are real.

The AI that will serve Africa best is not built in a lab in California. It is built by people who understand African languages, African markets, African health systems, African farming, African governance. Those people are you.

🛠 Hands-On - Write Your AI Code of Conduct

You need

  • A notebook
  • A pen
  • An hour of quiet thought
  1. Write down five things you will always do with AI.
  2. Write down five things you will never do with AI.
  3. Write down two grey areas you are unsure about.
  4. For each grey area, write down what you would need to know to decide.
  5. Sign and date the document.
  6. Show it to a parent or teacher. Ask them to add one line.
Why it works: A code of conduct is worth more written down than thought about. Once it is on paper, it is easier to hold yourself to it.

🌍 Real Stories from Africa

Deepfake fraud in South Africa, 2023

A South African company was defrauded of 250,000 dollars by a video call using deepfakes of its CEO and a supplier. The AI-generated faces were nearly indistinguishable from real ones. The fraud would have been impossible ten years ago. This is why verification is now a life skill.

Open-source African AI, 2024

A group of African researchers released an open-source language model trained on 20 African languages. They made the data and the model public so any African developer could build on it. The choice to be open was an ethical one - it keeps power distributed rather than concentrated.

🏆 Project Challenge

Your AI Future - A Letter to Yourself

Write a letter to the person you will be in five years.

  1. Write down what you want to build with AI in the next five years.
  2. Write down what you refuse to build - your personal line.
  3. Write down who you want to help with AI - a specific group of people.
  4. Write down what you will do if you are offered money to cross your line.
  5. Seal the letter. Put the date on it. Give it to someone you trust.
  6. Ask them to give it back to you in five years.
  7. Then start building today what you wrote.
