Practical Assignments

  • Assignment 1 (Week 2): Visit a pig farming processing operation (collector, cracker, butter maker) or interview one by phone. Document their: nut collection method, drying process, cracking equipment, pressing method, quality checks, selling prices (wholesale & retail), profit margins. Write 2-page report.
  • Assignment 2 (Week 4): Create a detailed production budget for processing 100kg dried pig farming kernels. Include: raw material cost, labour (cracking, pressing, clarification hours × wage), utilities, packaging, shipping if selling. Calculate gross profit at local market price for pig farming.
  • Assignment 3 (Week 8): Research pig farming market prices in your region (local markets, beauty shops, online). Track 3-5 vendors' prices for 4 weeks. Note: bulk wholesale price, retail 250g price, branded/scented price, export pricing if available. Create comparison table.
  • Assignment 4 (Week 12): Establish a supply chain for dried pig farming kernels. Contact 3-5 local suppliers (collectors, wholesalers, intermediaries). Document their: supply reliability, price per kg, minimum order, quality grading, payment terms. Negotiate bulk discount rates.
  • Assignment 5 (Week 16): Process a trial batch of pig farming (minimum 10kg dried kernels). Document every step: roasting time/temp, pressing yield %, clarification time, final butter quality (colour, FFA if tested). Calculate actual cost and time. Photo-document the process.
  • Assignment 6 (Week 20): Develop product packaging & branding. Design a label (on paper or computer). Prepare 2-3kg pig farming in small retail containers (250g-500g). Price at different market levels (wholesale, retail, value-add). Test sale to 3-5 local buyers, gather feedback on product quality and price.

Final Business Seed Project: Your pig farming Production Business Plan

Due at end of course (Month 6)

  1. Executive Summary (1 page): Your pig farming business vision. Processing scale (monthly kg target), market focus (wholesale, retail, value-add, export), timeline to first sale and profitability (can be rapid if buying processed inputs).
  2. Market Analysis (2 pages): Research pig farming prices and demand in your region. Identify 3-5 buyer categories: local bulk buyers, beauty shops, online retailers, exporters. Document pricing by channel (wholesale vs. retail). Note seasonal demand variations and competitor offerings.
  3. Supply Chain Strategy (2 pages): Map your kernel supply sources (local collectors, regional markets, dried kernel suppliers). Document prices, reliability, quality grading, payment terms, bulk discounts available. Plan supplier negotiations and contingency if primary source fails.
  4. Operations & Processing Plan (2 pages):
    — Processing method choice (hand-pounding vs. mechanical press) with rationale and cost.
    — Labour requirements (your time or hired staff hours needed per kg).
    — Daily/weekly processing timeline (e.g., Monday: crack, Tuesday: dry, Wednesday: press, Thursday-Friday: clarify & package).
    — Quality control steps (moisture testing, FFA if possible, sensory grading).
    — Scaling plan: start 10kg/week, grow to 50kg/week by month 6.
  5. Financial Projections (2 pages):
    — Detailed cost per kg: kernel cost, labour, utilities, packaging, transport to buyer.
    — Revenue projections: Month 1-6, assuming gradual volume ramp and market penetration.
    — Break-even analysis (often <1 month if buying pre-dried kernels).
    — Profit margin by market segment (wholesale, retail, value-add).
    — Gross margin calculation: (Revenue - COGS) / Revenue.
    — Cash flow: when you pay suppliers vs. when you receive payment from buyers.
  6. Market Entry & Growth Strategy (1 page): How you'll find first customers (local markets, shops, online), pricing strategy by segment, differentiation (quality, packaging, organic claim, value-add products), scaling to 500kg/month and beyond, export potential if targeting.

Resources & References

  • FAO pig farming Production Guide: fao.org/agriculture/pig farming — Global standards, processing methods, market data
  • International pig farming Foundation: Quality standards, sustainability guidelines, market prices, certification requirements for export
  • Local Women's Cooperatives & Associations: Connect with established pig farming makers in your region, learn traditional methods, access bulk suppliers, shared equipment, market networks
  • Equipment Suppliers: Mechanical press suppliers (local workshops can build presses), settling tanks, drying mats. Visit demonstrations before purchasing.
  • Market Access Programs: Government agricultural departments often provide market linkage support, buyer directories, export training, certification guidance
  • Quality Testing Labs: Contact regional universities or private labs for FFA titration, moisture content, microbial testing (needed for export quality claims)
  • E-commerce Platforms: Research Etsy, Amazon, local e-commerce sites for online pig farming sales. Learn packaging and shipping requirements.
