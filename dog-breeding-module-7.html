7. Puppy Development and Socialisation

The first 16 weeks decide a puppy's temperament for life. This is where premium puppies are made.

Module 7 of 12 · Dog Breeding for Profit

Why the first 16 weeks matter

A puppy's brain develops faster than at any other time of life. The experiences in the first 16 weeks shape how the adult dog will behave - confident or fearful, friendly or aggressive, trainable or difficult. This is called the socialisation window, and it closes between 12 and 16 weeks.

Puppies that miss this window often become shy, aggressive, or difficult to train. Those puppies do not sell well - or worse, get returned. Socialisation is a business necessity, not a nice-to-have.

The socialisation checklist

Expose puppies to all of these positively, before 16 weeks:

CategoryExamples
PeopleMen, women, children, elderly, people in hats, people with beards, people of different ethnicities
AnimalsOther dogs, cats, livestock, birds (from a distance)
SoundsTraffic, thunder, vacuum, doorbell, sirens, fireworks (at low volume, gradually increasing)
SurfacesGrass, gravel, concrete, wood floors, tiles, carpet, mud
EnvironmentsCars, buses, markets, parks, vet clinic, stairs, lifts
HandlingNail trims, ear checks, mouth examination, lifting, brushing
ObjectsUmbrellas, wheelchairs, bicycles, plastic bags, balloons
WeatherRain, wind, sun, cold (briefly, safely)

Target: 100 positive new experiences before 16 weeks. That is about 3-5 new things per week.

How to socialise correctly

  1. Positive associations. Every new experience should be paired with treats, praise, and calm.
  2. Never force. Let the puppy approach at their own pace.
  3. Short sessions. 5-10 minutes per new experience is plenty.
  4. End on a good note. If the puppy is worried, back off and try again later.
  5. One new thing at a time. Do not overwhelm.
  6. Consistency. Repeat exposures daily, not once.
  7. Protect from scary experiences. A puppy frightened by a large dog or loud truck may be permanently affected.
  8. Do not take unvaccinated puppies to public places where unknown dogs walk. Carry them or use a stroller.

Handling exercises

Every puppy should be handled daily in these ways:

  • Touch all over: ears, mouth, paws, tail, belly, between toes.
  • Pick up and hold: supported, calm, in different positions.
  • Restrain gently: hold still for 5-10 seconds, release calmly. This teaches acceptance of vet handling.
  • Examine mouth: open gently, look at teeth, release.
  • Examine feet: handle each paw, press pads slightly, release.
  • Brush and comb: short sessions, gentle, positive.

    Housetraining (toilet training)

    Start at 3 weeks - by 8 weeks most puppies are ready for a routine.

    AgeBladder controlToilet frequency
    3-4 weeksNoneConstant, mother cleans up
    4-8 weeks1 hourEvery 1-2 hours
    8-12 weeks2-3 hoursEvery 2-3 hours
    12-16 weeks3-4 hoursEvery 3-4 hours
    4-6 months4-6 hoursEvery 4-6 hours
    6+ months6-8 hours3-4 times daily

    Housetraining rules:

    1. Take the puppy out every 1-2 hours, plus after waking, eating, playing.
    2. Take to the same spot every time.
    3. Praise immediately on success - within 2 seconds.
    4. If you catch the puppy mid-accident, interrupt gently and take them out.
    5. Never punish accidents. Punishment causes fear, not learning.
    6. Clean accidents with enzymatic cleaner - ordinary cleaners do not remove the smell.
    7. Feed at fixed times so you can predict toilet times.
    8. Take water up 1-2 hours before bedtime.

    Bite inhibition

    Puppies learn to control their jaws by playing with littermates. When one bites too hard, the other yelps and stops playing - the biter learns to be gentle. This is one of the most important lessons a puppy learns.

    If a puppy is separated too early (before 8 weeks), they often miss this lesson and become a mouthy adult.

    To reinforce bite inhibition:

    • Let puppies stay with littermates until at least 8 weeks.
    • When a puppy bites you too hard, yelp "ow!" and stop playing for 10-20 seconds.
    • Resume play calmly. Repeat every time.
    • Never hit or hold the puppy''s mouth shut - this creates fear and aggression.

    Play and exercise

    AgeExercise
    0-8 weeksFree play in safe area
    8-12 weeksShort walks (10-15 min, 2-3 times/day), free play
    3-6 monthsWalks of 20-30 min, twice daily
    6-12 monthsWalks of 30-45 min, twice daily

    Growing puppies should not do forced running or jumping until their growth plates close (12-18 months depending on breed). Free play and walks on soft surfaces are safe.

    Health checks before sale

    Before selling any puppy, ensure:

    • Vet check - full physical exam, clear eyes, ears, heart, lungs, joints.
    • Vaccination - at least first vaccine, ideally two.
    • Dewormed - 2-4 times.
    • Microchipped - most buyers expect it, and it is often legally required.
    • Puppy pack - feeding schedule, vaccination card, sample food, toy for familiarity, care guide.
    • Contract - health guarantee, return policy, spay/neuter agreement if applicable.
    The 16-week rule: Everything that happens (or doesn''t happen) before 16 weeks shapes the adult dog. Socialised puppies are confident, trainable, and easy to live with. That is what buyers pay for - and what they tell their friends about.
