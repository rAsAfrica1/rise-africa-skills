Okra Processing

8 lessons · 30 quiz questions · Fruit Vegetables

1 Okra varieties

Okra (Abelmoschus esculentus) is a warm-season vegetable popular across Africa, the Middle East and South Asia. It's grown for the mucilaginous (slimy) pods which thicken soups and stews.

Clemson Spineless: Most common commercial variety. Smooth pods, good yield.

Emerald: Dark green, tender pods. Premium fresh market.

Annie Oakley: Compact plant, good for small farms.

Burgundy: Red pods (turn green when cooked). Specialty market.

For drying and powder, choose Clemson Spineless or Emerald — thin pods dry quickly and evenly.

2 Washing and topping

Okra pods have fine spines (even "spineless" varieties have small bumps). Handle with care.

  1. Wash pods in cold water
  2. Cut off the stem end (top) at a shallow angle — don't cut into the pod body
  3. Leave the pointy tail intact — it holds the pod together
  4. Do not wash until just before use — wet okra spoils fast

Okra is extremely perishable. Process within 24 hours of harvest.

⚠ Okra loses quality fast. Refrigerate at 7–10°C (not colder — chilling injury) and use within 2–3 days.

3 Slicing and drying

Dried okra is a traditional product across West Africa, used in soups and as a thickener.

  1. Wash and top okra pods
  2. Slice into 5 mm rings (a mandolin gives consistency)
  3. Blanch 90 seconds (optional — reduces mucilage on drying)
  4. Cool and drain
  5. Spread on drying racks in single layer
  6. Sun-dry 3–5 days or solar-dry 1–2 days
  7. Target moisture: 8–10%
  8. Pack in airtight bags

Yield: 100 kg fresh → 10–14 kg dried.

Retail in African markets: $10–$20 per kg.

4 Okra powder

Okra powder is a thickening agent and nutrition supplement. Sold in health food markets and used in African cooking as a soup base.

  1. Make dried okra as above
  2. Mill in a hammer mill or spice grinder
  3. Sieve through 250 micron
  4. Package in dark airtight containers

Yield: 100 kg fresh → 9–12 kg powder.

Retail: $15–$35 per kg. Higher for organic.

5 Frozen okra

Frozen okra is the biggest market for okra in Western countries.

  1. Wash and top pods
  2. Leave whole or slice
  3. Blanch 2 minutes
  4. Ice water bath
  5. Drain and dry
  6. IQF freeze on trays
  7. Pack in poly bags (500 g, 1 kg)

Yield: 100 kg fresh → 90 kg frozen (small loss from blanching).

Retail: $4–$8 per 500 g bag. Bulk to food service: $2–$4 per kg.

6 Okra soup mix

Okra soup mix is a value-added dry product — big market in Nigerian and Ghanaian groceries worldwide.

Recipe per 250 g pack:

  • 150 g dried okra powder
  • 50 g dried pepper flakes
  • 30 g dried onion powder
  • 15 g salt
  • 5 g dried ground crayfish (or shrimp powder)

To make soup: add 1 litre boiling water, simmer 5 minutes.

Retail: $6–$12 per 250 g pack. Premium in diaspora markets.

7 Pickled okra

Pickled okra is popular in the Southern US and Middle East.

  1. Use small tender pods (under 8 cm)
  2. Wash and top
  3. Pack into jars with dill, garlic, chilli flakes
  4. Cover with brine: 1 cup vinegar + 1 cup water + 1 tbsp salt + 1 tsp sugar
  5. Water-bath process 10 minutes
  6. Refrigerate 2 weeks before eating

Retail: $8–$15 per jar.

8 Retail and export

Packaging by product:

  • Dried slices — foil pouches, 100 g–1 kg
  • Powder — dark jars or pouches, 100 g–1 kg
  • Frozen — poly bags, 500 g–1 kg
  • Soup mix — foil pouches, 250 g
  • Pickled — glass jars, 350 g

Export markets: US, EU, Middle East — big demand from African and South Asian diaspora. Bulk dried okra: $8–$18 per kg FOB. Powder: $12–$25 per kg.

Compliance: Phytosanitary certificate, pesticide residue test, microbial testing (Salmonella must be absent).

💰 Frozen okra and dried powder are the highest-volume products. Soup mixes are highest margin — target diaspora markets.
