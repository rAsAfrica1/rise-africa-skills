🧠 Brain Opener

Cereal and legume seed sells for less per kilogram than vegetable seed. But the volumes are much larger. A farmer with 5 hectares of maize seed can earn 3,000 dollars. Volume has its own economics.

  • What are the main cereal and legume seed crops in Africa?
  • What is the price range for these crops?
  • What is the biggest risk in cereal seed production?
🎁 Reveal a strong answer

The main crops are maize, sorghum, millet, rice, groundnut, cowpea, and soybean. Prices range from 1.50 to 5 dollars per kilogram. The biggest risk is cross-pollination with neighbouring fields - especially for maize and sorghum, which need 200 to 400 metres of isolation.

📖 Tapiwa Plants Maize Seed

Tapiwa had grown maize for eating for 15 years. He knew the crop. But he had never grown it for seed.

His neighbour, Mr Moyo, told him the difference. "For eating, you plant and harvest. For seed, you plant, isolate, rogue, select, harvest, and test. Six steps instead of two."

Tapiwa chose an open-pollinated variety, not a hybrid, because hybrids require special management he did not have. The open-pollinated variety could be multiplied by any farmer with the right technique.

He planted 1 hectare, isolated from other maize by 300 metres - his nearest neighbour planted a different variety but far enough away.

Three weeks after planting, he started roguing. He walked the field every week and removed any plant that was different from the rest - different height, different leaf colour, different tassel timing. By flowering time he had removed 80 plants.

"That is the hardest part," he told his wife. "You spend six weeks pulling out good plants because they do not fit the variety."

At harvest, he selected seed only from plants that had two cobs, good height, and healthy leaves. He harvested these separately from the rest of the crop.

He dried the cobs in a crib for three weeks. Then he shelled them by hand, keeping the middle kernels from each cob - not the top or bottom, which are less uniform.

He tested germination: 94 percent. Purity: 99 percent.

He sold 800 kilograms of certified seed at 2.50 dollars per kilogram - 2,000 dollars. His cost: 400 dollars. Net profit: 1,600 dollars.

His eating maize on the same hectare would have earned 500 dollars. Seed maize earned 3 times more.

"The work is more," he told Mr Moyo. "But the money is better."

💡 The Big Idea

Volume plus quality equals profit

Cereal and legume seed sells for less than vegetable seed but in much larger quantities. A maize seed farmer can produce 800 kilograms from one hectare, earning 2,000 dollars. The key is quality - open-pollinated varieties, correct isolation, careful roguing, and clean processing. Each step adds value.

📚 Three Deep Lessons

1

Maize seed production

Maize is the biggest seed crop in Africa. Open-pollinated varieties (OPVs) are the standard for organic and smallholder production. Hybrids require breeding lines that most farmers cannot maintain.

Isolation: 200 metres minimum from other maize. If a different variety is within 200 metres, both fields lose genetic purity. Most cooperative maize seed production blocks farms together so isolation is guaranteed.

Roguing: walk the field weekly from emergence to flowering. Remove any plant that looks different - taller, shorter, different tassel colour, different cob shape. Remove the whole plant, not just the top. Aim for less than 1 percent off-types by flowering time.

Harvest: harvest when the black layer forms at the base of the kernel - usually 40 to 50 days after flowering. Dry in a crib for 3 weeks. Shell by hand or with a small sheller. Sieve to grade. Test germination.

Yield: 800 to 1,500 kilograms per hectare. Price: 2 to 3 dollars per kilogram.

2

Sorghum, millet, and rice seed production

Sorghum and millet are traditional African cereals, drought-tolerant, and increasingly in demand. Both are cross-pollinating and need isolation of 300 to 500 metres.

Sorghum seed production is similar to maize - plant, isolate, rogue, select, harvest, dry. The main difference is the seed head. Sorghum heads must be harvested before birds take the seed. Cover with netting or harvest at 80 percent maturity.

Rice seed production requires flooded or rainfed paddy. Isolation is 30 metres for most varieties. Seed rice must be harvested at full maturity, dried to 12 percent moisture, and stored carefully - rice loses viability faster than maize.

Yields: sorghum 1,000 to 1,500 kg per hectare. Millet 500 to 800. Rice 2,000 to 3,000. Prices: 1.50 to 3 dollars per kilogram.

3

Legume seed production - groundnut, cowpea, soybean

Legumes are the easiest seed crops for beginners. They self-pollinate, so isolation distances are short - usually 10 to 50 metres. They also fix nitrogen, improving the soil for the next crop.

Groundnut seed production is the most valuable legume seed. Yield: 800 to 1,500 kg per hectare. Price: 1.50 to 2.50 dollars per kilogram. But groundnut seed is delicate - the seed coat cracks easily and the oil goes rancid. Handle gently, dry slowly, store cool.

Cowpea and soybean are similar to groundnut but with harder seed coats. Yield: 500 to 1,000 kg per hectare. Price: 1 to 2 dollars per kilogram.

The main risk with legumes is aflatoxin - a toxin from mould that grows on poorly dried seed. Dry to 8 percent moisture. Store in dry, sealed containers. Test for aflatoxin if selling to formal buyers.

🛠 Hands-On - Grow 100 Square Metres of Legume Seed

You need

  • 100 square metres of land
  • Legume seed (cowpea is easiest)
  • Compost
  • A notebook
  1. Prepare the plot with compost.
  2. Plant cowpea or groundnut seed at 30 cm spacing.
  3. Water regularly.
  4. At flowering, remove any plants that look different.
  5. Wait for pods to mature and dry on the plant.
  6. Harvest whole plants. Hang to dry further for one week.
  7. Shell the pods by hand.
  8. Dry the seed to 8 percent moisture.
  9. Test germination.
  10. Weigh and store in a sealed container.
Why it works: Legumes are the easiest seed crop for a beginner. Low isolation requirements, self-pollinating, and they improve the soil.

🌍 Real Stories from Africa

Zambia maize seed

Zambia produces over 30,000 tonnes of certified maize seed per year. Small-scale contract farmers produce about 20 percent of this. A one-hectare seed plot earns 2,000 to 3,000 dollars. Same hectare growing food maize earns 500.

Malawi groundnut seed

Malawi groundnut seed producers earn 1.50 to 2.50 dollars per kilogram for certified seed - 50 percent more than food-grade groundnut. The market is stable and demand exceeds supply every year.
