Kids Drones — Junior

Module 3 of 8: Wind and Weather — Read the Sky Before You Fly

No device needed

🧠 Brain Opener — 60 seconds

Before anything else

A bird lands before a storm. A farmer knows rain is coming by the smell of the soil. A drone pilot needs the same instinct. What does the sky look like right now? Write down three things you can see.

📚 Lessons

1. Wind is the invisible hand

Wind pushes drones the way it pushes paper. A steady 5 km/h wind is easy — you can compensate. A gust of 30 km/h hits the drone like a shove from a giant. Below 3 km/h is beginner weather. Between 3 and 15 km/h is intermediate. Above 20 km/h, stay grounded.

2. The 3-part drill

Round 1: make a wind vane — a piece of paper on a pin on a stick. Check it every hour for a day. Write the direction. Round 2: on a windy day, drop a paper from the same height three times and see where it lands each time. Round 3: draw an arrow map of wind direction around your house — one arrow per corner.

3. Where the idea breaks

Rain is not the enemy — wind shear is. Wind shear is a sudden change in wind speed or direction over a short distance. It flips drones in mid-air and takes down real planes. It happens most near buildings and trees, where wind swirls. If you stand between tall buildings and feel the wind change direction as you walk — that is a no-fly day.

🎯 Cross-Skill Links

♟️
Chess

Before every move, strong players read the position: whose king is safer, whose pieces are active, where the tension is. Same before every flight: read the wind, read the sky, read the space you will fly in.

🎹
Music & Piano

A pianist reads tempo before starting — is this piece fast or slow? — and adjusts touch. A drone pilot reads wind before takeoff — steady or gusty? — and adjusts throttle.

🀄
Chinese-Style Drill

风 (fēng) = wind. 云 (yún) = cloud. 风 tells you what is happening. 云 tells you what is coming.

🤖 Real-Time AI-Era Skill

Ask Claude: "Given a forecast of 18 km/h gusts and clear skies, is it safe to fly a toy drone with a child today? Explain in three sentences." Then compare against the manufacturer's wind rating for your drone. AI gives you a starting point — the manual gives you the truth.

🎮 Game

Do it now

Weather journal. For one week, every day at the same time, go outside and write: (1) wind strength — calm / gentle / strong / dangerous; (2) wind direction by wetting your finger and holding it up (the cool side faces the wind); (3) cloud type — clear / puffy / flat / dark. After 7 days, look back. Which day would you have flown?
