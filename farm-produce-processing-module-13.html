Melon (Sweet & Bitter) Processing

8 lessons · 30 quiz questions · Cucurbits

1 Sweet vs bitter melon

These are two completely different crops despite the shared name.

Sweet melon (Cucumis melo): Includes cantaloupe, honeydew, galia. High sugar (10–14 brix). Sweet flesh used for juice, smoothie base, fruit salads. Rind is usually discarded.

Bitter melon (Momordica charantia): Also called bitter gourd, karela, balsam pear. Warty green fruit with intense bitterness. Used in Asian cooking and traditional medicine. Reputed to lower blood sugar. Sells at premium in Asian markets and health food shops.

These need separate handling and separate markets. Pick your direction before buying seeds.

2 Washing and cutting

Sweet melon:

  1. Wash the whole melon thoroughly — soil often carries Listeria
  2. Sanitise in 100 ppm chlorine solution
  3. Cut in half, remove seeds
  4. Scoop flesh with a spoon or cube it
  5. Use immediately or chill to 4°C

Bitter melon:

  1. Wash and cut off the two ends
  2. Cut in half lengthwise
  3. Scoop out seeds and white pith (most bitter part)
  4. Slice 3 mm thick
  5. To reduce bitterness: salt slices 30 minutes, then rinse
⚠ Bitter melon seeds are toxic when eaten raw in quantity — remove fully.

3 Sweet melon juice and smoothie base

Sweet melon juice is a refreshing product with strong summer demand.

  1. Cube chilled melon flesh
  2. Blend until smooth
  3. Strain (optional — smoothies don't need straining)
  4. Add lemon juice (5%) for acidity and flavour
  5. Add 0.1% citric acid + 0.05% ascorbic acid
  6. Pasteurise at 85°C for 15 seconds
  7. Hot-fill into bottles

Yield: 100 kg melon → 60–70 litres juice.

Retail: $2.50–$5 per 500 ml bottle.

4 Dried sweet melon slices

Dried melon is a chewy, sweet snack — similar to dried mango.

  1. Peel melon and slice 5 mm thick
  2. Soak in 20% sugar syrup for 2 hours (osmotic pre-treatment)
  3. Optionally dip in 0.5% citric acid solution (prevents browning)
  4. Drain and pat dry
  5. Dry at 55°C for 10–14 hours
  6. Target moisture: 15–18% (chewy, not brittle)
  7. Cool and pack in foil pouches

Yield: 100 kg fresh → 12–18 kg dried.

Retail: $30–$50 per kg.

5 Bitter melon juice and tea

Bitter melon is a functional food — claimed health benefits drive premium pricing.

Bitter melon juice:

  1. Wash and slice bitter melon
  2. Salt for 30 minutes to reduce bitterness
  3. Rinse, blend with water (1:2 ratio)
  4. Strain through fine mesh
  5. Add lemon, honey, ginger to mask bitterness
  6. Pasteurise at 85°C
  7. Hot-fill into bottles

Bitter melon tea: Dried bitter melon slices steeped in hot water. Pack dried slices in pouches as a tea product.

Retail: $8–$20 per kg dried slices; $4–$8 per 500 ml juice.

6 Pickled bitter melon

Pickled bitter melon is popular in Filipino and Southeast Asian cuisine.

  1. Slice bitter melon 3 mm thick
  2. Salt for 1 hour, then rinse and squeeze
  3. Pack into jars
  4. Cover with brine: 1 part vinegar + 1 part water + sugar + salt + garlic + chilli
  5. Seal and refrigerate 1 week before use

Retail: $6–$12 per jar.

7 Melon seed roasting

Both sweet and bitter melon seeds can be roasted. Bitter melon seeds are especially valued in health food markets.

  1. Wash seeds thoroughly to remove pulp
  2. Soak in salted water 2 hours
  3. Drain and dry
  4. Roast at 150°C for 20–25 minutes
  5. Cool and pack

Yield: 1 melon → 30–60 g seeds. Retail $10–$25 per kg.

⚠ Bitter melon seeds are considered toxic in large raw doses. Only sell roasted.

8 Market positioning

Sweet melon products:

  • Juice — supermarkets, cafés, delis
  • Dried — health food shops, snack market
  • Frozen smoothie packs — retail and food service

Bitter melon products:

  • Juice and tea — health food shops, Asian groceries, wellness market
  • Pickled — Asian grocery stores
  • Dried slices — herbal tea market
  • Capsules (powdered) — supplement companies
💰 Bitter melon commands 3–5× the price of sweet melon in health markets. Same crop family, different positioning.
