Guava, Papaya & Passion Fruit Processing

8 lessons · 30 quiz questions · Fruits

1 Varieties and ripening

Three tropical fruits with strong African production and growing global demand.

Guava (Psidium guajava): Apple Guava, Brazilian Guava, Strawberry Guava. Very high in Vitamin C (4× oranges). Best for juice, jelly, paste.

Papaya (Carica papaya): Solo, Sunrise, Tainung. Sweet orange flesh, edible seeds. Green papaya used for salad and papain extraction. Best for juice, dried slices, and green papaya salad.

Passion fruit (Passiflora edulis): Purple and yellow varieties. Intensely aromatic. Best for juice, concentrate, and desserts.

All three ripen fast. Process within 48 hours of harvest.

2 Washing and prep

All three fruits need careful handling.

  1. Wash in cold water
  2. Sanitise in 100 ppm chlorine solution for 1 minute
  3. Rinse again
  4. For guava: cut in quarters, remove seeds (optional — seeds are edible)
  5. For papaya: cut in half, scoop seeds, peel skin
  6. For passion fruit: cut in half, scoop pulp with seeds
⚠ Passion fruit seeds are edible and add texture. Don't remove them unless making clear juice.

3 Guava juice and nectar

Guava juice is the most popular guava product globally.

  1. Cut guava into chunks
  2. Blend with water (1 part guava to 2 parts water)
  3. Strain through fine mesh (remove seeds if you don't want them)
  4. Add sugar to 12–14 brix
  5. Add citric acid to pH 3.6–3.8
  6. Pasteurise at 90°C for 30 seconds
  7. Hot-fill into bottles

Yield: 100 kg fresh → 150–180 L juice.

Retail: $2–$4 per litre. Guava nectar is very popular in Asia, Africa, Latin America.

4 Papaya juice and dried slices

Papaya is a popular juice and dried fruit in Africa.

Papaya juice:

  1. Peel and deseed papaya
  2. Blend with water (1 part papaya to 1.5 parts water)
  3. Strain
  4. Add sugar to 12 brix, citric acid to pH 3.8
  5. Pasteurise at 88°C for 30 seconds
  6. Hot-fill

Yield: 100 kg fresh → 140–160 L juice.

Dried papaya slices:

  1. Peel, deseed, slice 5 mm thick
  2. Soak in 30% sugar syrup 4 hours
  3. Dry at 55°C for 10–12 hours
  4. Target moisture: 15–18%

Yield: 100 kg fresh → 15–20 kg dried.

Retail: $25–$40 per kg (dried).

5 Passion fruit juice and concentrate

Passion fruit juice is a premium product due to intense flavour and high price.

  1. Scoop pulp from halved passion fruit
  2. Blend briefly to separate juice from seeds
  3. Strain (leave some seeds for texture, or strain fully for clear juice)
  4. Add sugar (passion fruit is acidic — 1 part juice to 1.5 parts water to 12 brix)
  5. Pasteurise at 90°C for 30 seconds
  6. Hot-fill

Yield: 100 kg fresh → 30–40 L juice (passion fruit has low juice yield).

Retail: $8–$15 per litre (juice); $20–$40 per litre (concentrate).

💰 Passion fruit concentrate is one of the highest-value fruit products — $20–40 per litre. Target beverage companies and dessert makers.

6 Jams and jellies

Guava and passion fruit make excellent jams and jellies — high pectin content means good set.

Guava jam: 1 kg guava pulp + 800 g sugar + lemon juice. Simmer to 65 brix. Retail $6–$12 per 350 g jar.

Passion fruit jelly: 1 L passion fruit juice + 800 g sugar + pectin (0.5%). Simmer to 65 brix. Retail $8–$15 per 350 g jar.

Papaya jam: 1 kg papaya pulp + 700 g sugar + lemon + ginger. Simmer to 65 brix.

7 Mixed fruit pulp blends

Blended tropical pulps are premium products sold to juice makers and food service.

Popular blends:

  • Mango + passion fruit + orange
  • Guava + pineapple + lime
  • Papaya + mango + banana (baby food base)
  • Passion fruit + mango (cocktail base)

Blends are packaged in aseptic bags or frozen drums and sold to beverage manufacturers. Price: $2–$5 per kg.

8 Seed handling and cold chain

Seeds are the main waste stream from these fruits.

Uses for seeds:

  • Passion fruit seeds — pressed for oil ($30–$80 per kg)
  • Papaya seeds — dried as a spice (peppery flavour)
  • Guava seeds — ground into flour

Cold chain:

  • Fresh fruit: 7–10°C, 3–5 days
  • Fresh juice: 4°C, 3–5 days
  • Pasteurised juice: 12 months unopened
  • Frozen pulp: −18°C, 18 months
  • Dried: cool dark, 12 months
  • Aseptic pulp: 12 months at room temp
💰 Passion fruit is the highest-value of the three — concentrate sells at $20–40 per litre. Guava is the highest volume — juice and nectar markets are huge in Africa and Asia.
