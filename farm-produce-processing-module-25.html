Bell Pepper Processing

8 lessons · 30 quiz questions · Fruit Vegetables

1 Colour varieties

Bell peppers (Capsicum annuum) are the same species as chilli — just bred for zero heat. The colour depends on ripeness stage:

  • Green: Unripe. Bitter. Cheapest.
  • Yellow: Ripe. Sweeter. Mid-price.
  • Orange: Ripe. Sweetest flavour. Premium.
  • Red: Fully ripe. Sweet, high Vitamin C. Highest price.
  • Purple/Chocolate: Specialty varieties.

Red and yellow bell peppers are the highest-value processing inputs. Green is used for pickles and low-cost products.

2 Washing and coring

Bell peppers have a hollow centre with seeds and pith.

  1. Wash whole peppers in cold water
  2. Cut off the top (stem end) with a paring knife
  3. Scoop out seeds and white pith with a spoon
  4. Discard seeds (unless saving for planting)
  5. Cut pepper into required shapes

Pith is bitter and contains most of the (tiny) heat. Remove fully for premium flavour.

💡 Save the tops and cores — they can be blended into sauce or dried for pepper flakes.

3 Roasted pepper in jars

Roasted peppers are a premium deli product — $15–$30 per kg retail.

  1. Roast whole peppers over open flame or under grill until skin is blackened
  2. Place in a covered bowl and steam 10 minutes (loosens skins)
  3. Rub skins off (they slip off easily)
  4. Remove seeds and core
  5. Pack into jars with olive oil, garlic and herbs
  6. Seal and refrigerate

Shelf life: 6 months refrigerated. Every jar is essentially peppers suspended in oil.

4 Dried pepper flakes

Dried sweet pepper flakes are used in soups, pasta, and spice blends.

  1. Wash, core and slice peppers 3 mm thick
  2. Blanch 2 minutes (preserves colour)
  3. Cool and drain
  4. Dehydrate at 60°C for 8–10 hours
  5. Target moisture: 6–8%
  6. Break into flakes by hand

Yield: 100 kg fresh → 8–10 kg dried flakes.

Bulk: $8–$15 per kg. Retail: $20–$35 per kg.

5 Pepper paste

Pepper paste is a cooking base popular in Eastern Europe and Middle East (ajvar, pindjur).

  1. Roast or grill peppers
  2. Peel and remove seeds
  3. Blend with oil, garlic, salt, citric acid
  4. Simmer 30–45 minutes until thick
  5. Hot-fill at 90°C into jars
  6. Seal and cool

Yield: 100 kg fresh → 45–55 kg paste.

Retail: $5–$12 per 500 g jar.

6 Frozen pepper mix

Frozen pepper strips are sold to supermarkets, restaurants and food service.

  1. Wash, core and slice peppers into strips (5 mm × 50 mm)
  2. Blanch 2 minutes
  3. Ice water bath
  4. Drain thoroughly
  5. IQF freeze on trays
  6. Pack in poly bags (500 g, 1 kg)

Yield: 100 kg fresh → 60–70 kg frozen.

Bulk: $2.50–$5 per kg to food service.

7 Sweet pepper sauce

Sweet pepper sauce is a mild alternative to chilli sauce — same process, zero heat.

  1. Blend roasted peppers with vinegar, sugar, salt, garlic
  2. Simmer 20 minutes
  3. Strain for smooth sauce
  4. Add 0.1% potassium sorbate
  5. Hot-fill at 90°C

Retail: $6–$12 per 250 ml bottle. Growing market.

8 Retail packaging

Packaging by product:

  • Roasted jars — 300–500 g glass jars in oil
  • Dried flakes — foil pouches, 50 g–1 kg
  • Pepper paste — glass jars, 300–500 g
  • Frozen strips — poly bags, 500 g–1 kg
  • Sauce — glass bottles, 250 ml

Shelf life:

  • Roasted (in oil): 6 months refrigerated
  • Dried flakes: 18 months in sealed foil
  • Paste: 12 months unopened
  • Frozen: 12 months at −18°C
  • Sauce: 12 months unopened
💰 Roasted peppers in jars are the highest-margin bell pepper product. Simple process, premium price.
