Eggplant (Aubergine) Processing

8 lessons · 30 quiz questions · Fruit Vegetables

1 Variety selection

Eggplant (Solanum melongena) comes in many shapes and colours. For processing, pick varieties with firm flesh, few seeds, and thin skin.

Globe (American): Large, dark purple, mild flavour. Best for roasting and baba ganoush.

Italian: Smaller, elongated, sweeter. Best for frying and pickling.

Chinese/Japanese: Long, thin, tender skin. Best for stir-fry and grilling. Fast cooking.

Thai (round green): Small, bitter, firm. Best for curries.

White eggplant: Creamy skin, mild. Specialty markets.

For baba ganoush and roasting, globe or Italian. For pickling, small Italian or Thai.

2 Washing and cutting

Eggplant skin is edible — peeling is optional and reduces yield by 10–15%.

  1. Wash thoroughly to remove soil and any field spray
  2. Cut off stem and calyx
  3. Cut into required shape: halves for roasting, cubes for drying, slices for frying
  4. For baked products, prick the skin to prevent bursting
  5. Optional: salt slices 30 minutes to remove bitterness and excess water
⚠ Eggplant oxidises fast. Cut surfaces brown within 30 minutes. Use lemon water dip if holding.

3 Roasted eggplant (baba ganoush base)

Roasted eggplant is the base for baba ganoush (Middle Eastern dip), eggplant parmigiana, and many Mediterranean products.

  1. Roast whole eggplants over open flame or under grill until skin is black and flesh soft
  2. Turn every 5 minutes to roast evenly (20–30 minutes total)
  3. Cool in a covered bowl
  4. Peel off charred skin
  5. Drain flesh in a colander 30 minutes (removes excess water)
  6. Mash or blend with tahini, garlic, lemon juice, salt for baba ganoush

Yield: 100 kg fresh → 55–65 kg roasted pulp.

Baba ganoush retail: $8–$15 per 300 g tub.

4 Dried eggplant slices

Dried eggplant is a traditional product in Mediterranean, Middle Eastern and Asian cuisines.

  1. Wash and cut eggplant into 5 mm rounds or half-moons
  2. Optional: salt 30 minutes, then pat dry
  3. Sun-dry 4–6 days or solar-dry 2 days
  4. Or dehydrate at 55°C for 8–10 hours
  5. Target moisture: 8–10%
  6. Pack in airtight bags

Yield: 100 kg fresh → 10–14 kg dried.

Retail: $12–$22 per kg. Popular with diaspora communities.

5 Eggplant pickle

Pickled eggplant is popular in North Africa, the Middle East and India.

  1. Cut eggplant into cubes or strips
  2. Salt 2 hours, then drain and squeeze
  3. Blanch 2 minutes in vinegar-water (1:1)
  4. Drain and cool
  5. Pack into jars with garlic, chilli, herbs
  6. Cover with oil or vinegar brine
  7. Seal and refrigerate 2 weeks before eating

Retail: $8–$15 per jar.

6 Frozen eggplant

Frozen eggplant is sold to food service and supermarkets. Requires blanching to prevent discolouration.

  1. Wash and slice or cube
  2. Blanch 3 minutes (or roast first for better texture)
  3. Ice water bath 2 minutes
  4. Drain thoroughly
  5. IQF freeze on trays
  6. Pack in poly bags

Yield: 100 kg fresh → 85–90 kg frozen.

Bulk price: $2.50–$4 per kg to food service.

7 Packaging

Packaging by product:

  • Baba ganoush — glass jars or tubs (200–400 g)
  • Dried slices — foil pouches (100 g–1 kg)
  • Pickled — glass jars (350–700 g)
  • Frozen — poly bags (500 g–1 kg)
  • Roasted pulp (bulk) — vacuum bags (5–10 kg)

Shelf life:

  • Baba ganoush: 3 weeks refrigerated; 6 months if pasteurised & sealed
  • Dried slices: 18 months in sealed packaging
  • Pickled: 12 months refrigerated
  • Frozen: 12 months at −18°C

8 Restaurant supply

Eggplant is a staple in Italian, Greek, Turkish and Middle Eastern restaurants.

What chefs want:

  • Consistent size (medium, uniform)
  • Glossy skin, no soft spots
  • Firm flesh
  • Delivered chilled
  • Reliable weekly supply

Direct supply to restaurants pays $2–$4 per kg. Ready-made baba ganoush pays $15–$25 per kg to delis — a 6× markup for value-add.

💰 The value-add is huge: $1/kg raw eggplant becomes $15/kg baba ganoush. Focus on value-added products, not fresh sales.
