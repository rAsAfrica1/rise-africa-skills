Traceability and Records - The Paper That Sells Seed

🧠 Brain Opener

When a buyer in Germany holds your seed packet, they want to know exactly which field it came from. That is called traceability. It is a piece of paper and a system. And it is worth thousands of dollars.

  • What is traceability?
  • Why do buyers demand it?
  • What do you need to record, and when?
🎁 Reveal a strong answer

Traceability is the ability to follow a batch of seed back through the whole chain - from the packet, to the cleaning, to the harvest, to the field, to the planting. Buyers demand it because a recall has to be traceable. You need to record inputs, dates, people, and process at every step, starting before you plant.

📖 Simba Recalls a Batch

Simba was twenty-eight. He had been producing organic kale seed for three years. He had 40 kilograms in storage, ready for a customer in Nairobi.

Then he got the call. The Nairobi buyer had a complaint. Two farmers who planted his seed had lower than expected germination - 60 percent instead of 88 percent. The buyer asked for a refund and a traceability report.

Simba was panicked. He did not have a traceability system. He stored seed in bags labelled only by weight.

He went to his aunt, an agronomist. She asked him three questions.

"When did you harvest?"

"I do not remember exactly. Maybe late September."

"From which plot?"

"Any of the three plots. I mixed the harvest."

"How did you dry it?"

"In the sun, on a mat."

His aunt shook her head. "You cannot prove anything. Without records, you cannot defend yourself. You cannot find the problem. You cannot fix it."

Simba had to refund the buyer in full - 2,400 dollars. He lost three years of building a reputation.

He started again. This time he kept records from day one. Every plot had a code. Every harvest had a batch number. Every bag had a label with variety, plot, harvest date, moisture, and germination.

Two years later, the same Nairobi buyer came back. He tested Simba new batch: 92 percent germination. He asked for a traceability report.

Simba sent him a one-page document - the exact field, the exact harvest date, the exact processing, the exact moisture, the germination test. The buyer was impressed. He ordered 200 kilograms instead of 40.

Simba learned the lesson the hard way. Records are not paperwork. Records are your defence, your proof, and your sales tool.

💡 The Big Idea

Traceability is a sales tool, not a burden

Buyers pay a premium for traceable seed because it protects them from recalls. A traceability report turns your seed from an anonymous product into a trusted one. Every hour spent on records returns 10 hours of value in the next sale.

📚 Three Deep Lessons

1

What traceability means

A traceable batch has five things written down: which field it came from (plot code), when it was planted, when it was harvested, how it was processed, and what tests it passed. If a problem is reported, you can find the exact field and the exact processing step.

A batch number is the key. Use a simple system: PLOT-YEAR-SEQUENCE. So "A1-25-03" means plot A1, year 2025, third batch. Write the number on every bag and every record.

Your traceability system does not need software. A notebook and a marker work. A simple spreadsheet is better. Digital tools like SeedTracker help for larger operations.

2

What to record and when

Before planting. Plot code, previous crop, soil test, compost used, seed source (variety, batch), planting date.

During growing. Every input (compost, sprays, water), every observation (pests, disease), every roguing, every weeding.

At harvest. Date, weather, quantity, moisture reading, method.

During processing. Drying days, threshing method, sieving sizes, water separation, final weight.

Before sale. Germination test, purity test, batch number, buyer name, delivery date.

Record daily. If you record weekly, you will forget. Fifteen minutes a day is enough.

3

What a traceability report looks like

A one-page traceability report has four sections: batch identity (number, variety, weight), origin (plot, planting date, harvest date), processing (drying, cleaning, testing), and quality (germination, purity, moisture).

Buyers love a clean, one-page report. It signals professionalism. It removes their risk. It commands premium prices.

You do not need to send it with every bag. You need to have it available. Just being able to send one within 24 hours of a request is enough to qualify you for most premium buyers.

🛠 Hands-On - Set Up a Batch Number System

You need

  • A notebook
  • A marker or pen
  • Sticky labels
  1. Decide your plot codes. Use letters and numbers: A1, A2, B1, B2.
  2. Decide your batch number format: PLOT-YEAR-SEQUENCE.
  3. For each harvest, write the batch number on every bag.
  4. Create a master log: batch number, plot, crop, date harvested, date processed, weight, quality tests.
  5. Store the log in a safe place - a fireproof box or a cloud document.
  6. Before every sale, copy the relevant row of the log into the buyer traceability report.
  7. Keep the log for at least 3 years.
Why it works: A batch number system costs nothing. It is worth thousands of dollars in buyer confidence.

🏆 Project Challenge

Build Your Traceability System

Set up a paper system for your next crop.

  1. Choose plot codes for your land.
  2. Choose your batch number format.
  3. Print or draw a master log with columns: batch, plot, crop, dates, weight, tests.
  4. For your next harvest, assign a batch number.
  5. Write the number on every bag.
  6. Fill the log daily for the whole season.
