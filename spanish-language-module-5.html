1. Directions and Asking for Locations

When you need to find a place in a Spanish-speaking country, start with 'Dnde est...?' (Where is...?). For example, 'Dnde est la estacin de autobuses?' (Where is the bus station?). If you're asking a stranger, begin with 'Disculpe' (Excuse me). To understand the answer, learn these key words: 'a la derecha' (to the right), 'a la izquierda' (to the left), 'derecho' (straight ahead), 'cerca' (close), 'lejos' (far), 'una cuadra' (one block), 'una esquina' (a corner). In many African cities, landmarks are used instead of street names. So you might hear: 'Siga derecho hasta la farmacia, luego gire a la izquierda en la escuela.' (Go straight until the pharmacy, then turn left at the school.)

Practice asking for directions to common places: 'Dnde est el mercado?' (Where is the market?), 'Dnde est el banco?' (Where is the bank?), 'Dnde est el bao?' (Where is the bathroom?). If you get lost, say 'Estoy perdido' (I'm lost). To confirm you understood, repeat: 'Entonces, voy a la derecha?' (So, I go to the right?). Numbers are useful: 'la primera calle' (the first street), 'la segunda calle' (the second street). In rural areas, directions might include 'kilmetro 5' (kilometer 5) or 'junto al ro' (next to the river).

If you're in a taxi or boda boda, you can say 'Llveme a...' (Take me to...). For example, 'Llveme al aeropuerto, por favor.' (Take me to the airport, please.) To ask the driver to stop, say 'Pare aqu' (Stop here). In many African countries, motorbike taxis are common; in Spanish, they might be called 'moto taxis' or 'boda boda' (in Equatorial Guinea). Always agree on the price before you start: 'Cunto cuesta?' (How much does it cost?).

A useful tool is a maps app like Google Maps, but if you don't have data, ask for written directions. Carry a small notebook and ask the person to write it down. Learn to say 'Puede escribirlo?' (Can you write it down?). Also, learn to read street signs: 'Calle' (Street), 'Avenida' (Avenue), 'Carretera' (Highway). In some countries, addresses include 'barrio' (neighborhood) and 'referencia' (landmark). For example, 'Barrio Centro, frente a la iglesia' (Downtown neighborhood, in front of the church). Practice these phrases with a language partner or by using apps like Duolingo or Babbel. The more you practice, the easier it gets.

2. Modes of Transport: Taxi, Bus, Boda Boda, Truck

In Spanish-speaking countries, you'll find many modes of transport. A taxi is 'taxi' or 'libre' (in some places, 'colectivo' for shared taxi). A bus is 'autobs' or 'bus'. In some countries, a minibus is 'micro' or 'combi'. A motorbike taxi is 'moto taxi' or 'boda boda' (in Equatorial Guinea). A truck is 'camin'. To ask for a taxi, say 'Dnde puedo tomar un taxi?' (Where can I take a taxi?). For a bus, 'Dnde est la parada de autobs?' (Where is the bus stop?).

When you get in a taxi, confirm the fare: 'Cunto cuesta al centro?' (How much to downtown?). In many African cities, taxis don't use meters, so you must negotiate. Start with 'Me puede llevar por 5000 francos?' (Can you take me for 5000 francs?). In Latin America, you might say 'Cunto me cobra?' (How much will you charge me?). For buses, you usually pay the conductor: 'Cunto es el pasaje?' (How much is the fare?). Keep small bills and coins handy.

For boda boda, always agree on the price first and ask for a helmet: 'Tiene casco?' (Do you have a helmet?). In some countries, boda bodas are called 'mototaxis'. To say 'slow down', say 'ms despacio'. To say 'stop here', 'pare aqu'. For trucks, if you need to transport goods, say 'Necesito un camin para llevar mercanca' (I need a truck to carry goods). Ask about capacity: 'Cuntas toneladas puede llevar?' (How many tons can it carry?).

If you're traveling between cities, you might take a 'bus interurbano' (intercity bus). Ask for the schedule: 'A qu hora sale el bus?' (What time does the bus leave?). For long trips, there may be 'bus cama' (sleeper bus) or 'bus ejecutivo' (executive bus). In West Africa, you might find 'sept-place' (seven-seater) taxis. Always keep your luggage with you or get a receipt: 'Me da un recibo?' (Can you give me a receipt?).

Safety tips: In taxis, sit in the back. For boda boda, hold on tight. For buses, keep your valuables secure. Learn to say 'Voy a bajar en...' (I'm going to get off at...). Also, 'Me avisa cuando lleguemos?' (Will you let me know when we arrive?). Practice these phrases with a friend or using language apps.

3. Buying Tickets and Reserving Seats

Buying tickets in Spanish is straightforward once you know the phrases. For a bus or train ticket, go to the 'taquilla' (ticket window) or 'boletera'. Say 'Quiero un boleto a...' (I want a ticket to...). For example, 'Quiero un boleto a Malabo, por favor.' (I want a ticket to Malabo, please.) If you want a round-trip ticket, say 'ida y vuelta'. One-way is 'solo ida'. For multiple tickets, say 'dos boletos' (two tickets).

To reserve a seat, you might say 'Quiero reservar un asiento' (I want to reserve a seat). If you prefer a window seat, 'asiento de ventana'; aisle seat, 'asiento de pasillo'. For a bus, you might ask 'Puedo elegir mi asiento?' (Can I choose my seat?). In some countries, you can buy tickets online or via mobile money. For example, in Kenya, you can use M-Pesa to pay for bus tickets. In Spanish, 'Puedo pagar con M-Pesa?' (Can I pay with M-Pesa?).

When buying a ticket, you'll need to provide your name and sometimes your ID: 'Necesita mi identificacin?' (Do you need my ID?). For international travel, you'll need a passport: 'Aqu est mi pasaporte' (Here is my passport). Ask about luggage: 'Cunto equipaje puedo llevar?' (How much luggage can I bring?). There may be an extra fee for excess luggage: 'Hay cargo extra por equipaje?' (Is there an extra charge for luggage?).

If you need to change your ticket, say 'Quiero cambiar mi boleto' (I want to change my ticket). For a refund, 'Quiero un reembolso' (I want a refund). Keep your ticket safe; you'll need it to board. At the gate, show your ticket: 'Aqu est mi boleto' (Here is my ticket). The conductor might ask 'A dnde va?' (Where are you going?). Answer clearly.

Useful vocabulary: 'andn' (platform), 'salida' (departure), 'llegada' (arrival), 'retraso' (delay), 'cancelado' (cancelled). If there's a delay, ask 'Cunto tiempo de retraso?' (How long is the delay?). Practice buying tickets with a language partner or using apps like Duolingo. In real life, start with simple transactions at local bus stations.

4. Talking About Delivery Times and Routes

If you're in logistics, you'll need to discuss delivery times and routes. Start with 'Cundo es la entrega?' (When is the delivery?). To say 'The delivery is tomorrow', 'La entrega es maana'. For a specific time, 'La entrega es a las 3 de la tarde' (The delivery is at 3 PM). If you need to delay, 'Necesito posponer la entrega' (I need to postpone the delivery). To confirm, 'Puede entregar el lunes?' (Can you deliver on Monday?).

For routes, ask 'Cul es la ruta?' (What is the route?). To describe a route, use 'pasar por' (to pass through), 'llegar a' (to arrive at), 'salir de' (to leave from). For example, 'Salimos de Accra, pasamos por Lom, y llegamos a Coton' (We leave from Accra, pass through Lom, and arrive in Cotonou). If there's a toll, 'peaje'; a border, 'frontera'. Ask 'Hay peajes en la ruta?' (Are there tolls on the route?).

Delivery times can be affected by traffic, weather, or customs. Say 'El trfico est pesado' (Traffic is heavy). 'Hay mucha lluvia' (There's a lot of rain). 'La aduana est lenta' (Customs is slow). To give an estimate, 'La entrega tomar 3 horas' (The delivery will take 3 hours). If you're late, 'Lo siento, voy retrasado' (Sorry, I'm running late).

For tracking, you might say 'Dnde est el camin?' (Where is the truck?). 'El conductor est en la frontera' (The driver is at the border). Use numbers: 'kilmetros' (kilometers), 'horas' (hours), 'das' (days). For example, 'La distancia es 200 kilmetros' (The distance is 200 kilometers). 'El viaje dura 4 horas' (The trip takes 4 hours).

If you need to change the route, 'Necesitamos cambiar la ruta' (We need to change the route). Ask for alternatives: 'Hay otra ruta?' (Is there another route?). In case of breakdown, 'El camin se descompuso' (The truck broke down). 'Necesitamos un mecnico' (We need a mechanic). Practice these phrases with a colleague or using language apps. In real logistics, clear communication saves time and money.

5. Customs and Border Crossing Phrases

Crossing borders in Africa often involves customs checks. Learn key phrases to make it smoother. At the border, you'll see 'Aduana' (Customs) and 'Migracin' (Immigration). Have your documents ready: 'Aqu est mi pasaporte' (Here is my passport). 'Aqu est mi visa' (Here is my visa). If you're transporting goods, you'll need a 'declaracin de aduana' (customs declaration). Say 'Tengo mercanca para declarar' (I have goods to declare).

The officer may ask 'Cul es el propsito de su viaje?' (What is the purpose of your trip?). Answer 'Negocios' (Business) or 'Turismo' (Tourism). For goods, 'Qu lleva?' (What are you carrying?). Answer 'Llevo productos electrnicos' (I'm carrying electronics). If you have nothing to declare, 'No tengo nada que declarar' (I have nothing to declare).

You might need to pay duties: 'Cunto tengo que pagar?' (How much do I have to pay?). Ask for a receipt: 'Me da un recibo?' (Can you give me a receipt?). If there's a delay, 'Cunto tiempo toma?' (How long does it take?). For inspections, 'Puede revisar mi equipaje?' (Can you check my luggage?).

In some borders, you might need a yellow fever certificate: 'Aqu est mi certificado de fiebre amarilla' (Here is my yellow fever certificate). For vehicles, you need 'permiso de circulacin' (circulation permit) and 'seguro' (insurance). Say 'Tengo el seguro del vehculo' (I have vehicle insurance). If you're a driver, 'Soy el conductor' (I am the driver).

If you face issues, stay calm and polite. 'Disculpe, no entiendo' (Excuse me, I don't understand). 'Puede hablar ms despacio?' (Can you speak slower?). 'Hay un intrprete?' (Is there an interpreter?). Useful words: 'frontera' (border), 'control' (checkpoint), 'documentos' (documents), 'impuesto' (tax). Practice these phrases with a language partner. Knowing them can save you hours at the border.

6. Practice: Plan a Delivery Route in Spanish

Now let's put everything together. You'll plan a delivery route from Accra, Ghana to Lagos, Nigeria, using Spanish. First, gather your team and say: 'Vamos a planificar la ruta de entrega' (Let's plan the delivery route). Start by identifying the origin and destination: 'Salimos de Accra y llegamos a Lagos' (We leave from Accra and arrive in Lagos). The distance is about 1,000 kilometers. 'La distancia es mil kilmetros' (The distance is 1,000 kilometers).

Next, choose the mode of transport. For goods, you'll use a truck: 'Usamos un camin' (We use a truck). Ask about capacity: 'Cuntas toneladas puede llevar el camin?' (How many tons can the truck carry?). Suppose it's 10 tons: 'Puede llevar 10 toneladas' (It can carry 10 tons). Plan the route: 'La ruta pasa por Lom, Coton, y Porto-Novo' (The route passes through Lom, Cotonou, and Porto-Novo). There are border crossings: 'Hay tres fronteras' (There are three borders).

Estimate delivery time: 'El viaje dura 3 das' (The trip takes 3 days). But consider delays: 'Puede haber retrasos en la aduana' (There may be delays at customs). So, 'La entrega ser el viernes' (The delivery will be on Friday). Communicate with the driver: 'Conductor, dnde est ahora?' (Driver, where are you now?). 'Estoy en la frontera de Togo' (I'm at the Togo border).

Prepare documents: 'Necesitamos pasaportes, visas, y declaraciones de aduana' (We need passports, visas, and customs declarations). For the truck: 'Necesitamos permiso de circulacin y seguro' (We need circulation permit and insurance). If there's a breakdown: 'El camin se descompuso en Benin' (The truck broke down in Benin). 'Necesitamos un mecnico' (We need a mechanic).

Finally, confirm the delivery: 'La entrega fue exitosa' (The delivery was successful). 'El cliente recibi la mercanca' (The client received the goods). Practice this scenario with a partner. Use a map and speak only Spanish. This will build your confidence for real logistics work.

<h2>Practical Reference: Navigating Transport and Logistics in Spanish-Speaking Africa</h2>

<p>For African agribusiness professionals moving goods and people across Spanish-speaking markets Equatorial Guinea, and trade corridors linking to Spanish-speaking partners fluent logistics Spanish is a commercial advantage. This Practical Reference consolidates the vocabulary, phrases, and operational frameworks you need to move cargo, negotiate transport, clear borders, and plan delivery routes with confidence. Whether you are arranging a truck from Malabo to Bata, booking a boda boda for last-mile distribution in a farming community, or negotiating customs at the CameroonEquatorial Guinea border, the language you use determines your speed, cost, and margin.</p>

<p>This lesson is business-first: every phrase is framed for commercial use. You will find ready-to-use expressions for directions, transport modes, ticketing, delivery scheduling, and customs. The reference tables, diagram, and checklist are designed to be printed or saved for field use.</p>

<h2>1. Directions and Asking for Locations</h2>

<p>When you arrive in a new market town, the first task is orientation. Spanish direction phrases are direct and practical. Use formal address (<em>usted</em>) with officials and elders; informal (<em>t</em>) with drivers and market traders once rapport is established.</p>

<ul>
<li><strong>Dnde est el mercado central?</strong> Where is the central market?</li>
<li><strong>Cmo llego al puerto / a la aduana?</strong> How do I get to the port / customs?</li>
<li><strong>Siga recto, luego gire a la derecha / izquierda.</strong> Go straight, then turn right / left.</li>
<li><strong>Est a dos kilmetros, pasando el cruce.</strong> It is two kilometres away, past the junction.</li>
<li><strong>Est lejos de aqu?</strong> Is it far from here?</li>
<li><strong>El almacn est detrs de la estacin de servicio.</strong> The warehouse is behind the fuel station.</li>
</ul>

<p>In Equatorial Guinea, major commercial hubs include Malabo (Bioko Island), Bata (mainland), and Ebebiyn (near the Cameroon and Gabon borders). Knowing how to ask for these locations precisely saves hours. For agribusiness, the key destinations are ports, customs offices, wholesale markets, and rural collection points.</p>

<h2>3. Buying Tickets and Reserving Seats</h2>

</ul>

<h2>4. Talking About Delivery Times and Routes</h2>

<p>Delivery commitments are contractual. Use precise time expressions and route descriptions.</p>

<ul>
<li><strong>La entrega est prevista para el viernes a las 14:00.</strong> Delivery is scheduled for Friday at 14:00.</li>
<li><strong>El camin saldr de Malabo a las 5:00 y llegar a Bata en ocho horas.</strong> The truck will leave Malabo at 5:00 and arrive in Bata in eight hours.</li>
<li><strong>La ruta pasa por Ebebiyn; hay un control aduanero.</strong> The route goes through Ebebiyn; there is a customs checkpoint.</li>
<li><strong>Cul es el tiempo de trnsito habitual?</strong> What is the usual transit time?</li>
<li><strong>Hay retrasos por lluvia en la carretera.</strong> There are delays due to rain on the road.</li>
</ul>

<p>In agribusiness, perishability matters. Always ask about cold chain availability: <strong>El camin tiene refrigeracin?</strong> (Does the truck have refrigeration?). For export crops like cocoa, coffee, and rubber, coordinate with the buyer on arrival windows.</p>

<h2>5. Customs and Border Crossing Phrases</h2>

<p>Border crossings in Central Africa can be slow. Prepared documentation and clear Spanish speed the process.</p>

<ul>
<li><strong>Aqu est la declaracin de aduanas.</strong> Here is the customs declaration.</li>
<li><strong>Llevo productos agrcolas para exportacin.</strong> I am carrying agricultural products for export.</li>
<li><strong>Necesito un certificado fitosanitario?</strong> Do I need a phytosanitary certificate?</li>
<li><strong>Cunto es el arancel de importacin?</strong> How much is the import duty?</li>
<li><strong>El contenedor est sellado y precintado.</strong> The container is sealed and stamped.</li>
<li><strong>Puedo hablar con el oficial de aduanas?</strong> Can I speak with the customs officer?</li>
</ul>

<p>Apply the module by planning a route from a farm collection point to an urban market. Use the template below and write your plan in Spanish.</p>

<p><strong>Template:</strong></p>
<ol>
<li><strong>Origen:</strong> Finca en [localidad].</li>
<li><strong>Destino:</strong> Mercado de [ciudad].</li>
<li><strong>Modo de transporte:</strong> Camin / boda boda / autobs.</li>
<li><strong>Ruta:</strong> Salida a las [hora], pasando por [punto intermedio].</li>
<li><strong>Aduana:</strong> Documentos necesarios: [lista].</li>

<li><strong>Presupuesto:</strong> [coste estimado].</li>

<h2>Quick Reference Checklist</h2>
<ul>
<li> Confirm destination and route in Spanish before departure.</li>
<li> Negotiate transport price and confirm in writing (receipt or message).</li>
<li> Verify ticket, seat, and luggage allowance for bus or ferry.</li>
<li> Check delivery time window and cold chain requirements.</li>
<li> Prepare customs documents: declaration, phytosanitary certificate, export permit.</li>
<li> Keep official receipts for all border payments.</li>
<li> Share route and ETA with buyer and local contact.</li>
<li> Have emergency contacts for freight forwarder and customs broker.</li>
</ul>
