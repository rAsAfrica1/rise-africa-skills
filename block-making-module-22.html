Fair-Faced (Architectural) Block

8 lessons · 30 quiz questions · Medium difficulty

1 What is fair-faced

"Fair-faced" means the block is good enough to leave unplastered. Every surface is visible, so any defect shows.

Fair-faced requirements:

  • Tight dimensional tolerance (±1 mm)
  • Smooth, uniform face — no chips, no honeycomb
  • Consistent colour across the batch
  • Sharp, square edges
  • No cracks or surface voids

Used in modern architecture — offices, schools, retail, and high-end residential where the raw concrete look is the design feature. Fair-faced walls save the cost of plaster and paint while adding visual value.

💡 Fair-faced is 3-5× the price of standard blocks. Same materials — just tighter process control and better moulds.

2 Tight-tolerance moulds

Fair-faced blocks need precision moulds. The mould determines dimensional accuracy.

Specifications:

  • Machined steel mould (not fibreglass)
  • Tolerance ±0.5 mm on all dimensions
  • Smooth mould faces (polished steel)
  • Precise alignment pins
  • Regular inspection — replace after 20,000–30,000 uses

Cost: $1,500–$6,000 per fair-faced mould. More than double a standard block mould — but the price premium on the finished block pays it back within a few hundred sales.

Worn moulds produce wavy faces, rounded corners and dimensional drift. If your "fair-faced" blocks look slightly off, it's the mould.

3 Smooth mix design

Fair-faced concrete needs a finer, richer mix than structural blocks.

Fair-faced mix by weight:

  • Cement: 1 part (increase to 1.1 for premium finish)
  • Fine sharp sand (0–2 mm): 2 parts
  • Small aggregate (2–5 mm): 2 parts
  • Water: earth-damp (never wet)
  • Optional: 0.5% plasticiser for smoother flow

Cement content: 14–18% of weight. Higher cement = smoother surface, fewer surface pores.

Avoid coarse aggregate — it creates visible stones on the face. If you want visible aggregate texture, that's a different product (exposed aggregate, Module 21 territory).

4 Vibration for smooth face

Vibration technique determines surface quality on fair-faced blocks.

Rules:

  • Fill the mould in two layers.
  • Vibrate 6–8 seconds per layer.
  • Use a vibrating table with a smooth plate — rough plates mar the face.
  • Don't over-vibrate (causes segregation — heavy particles sink, water rises).
  • Don't under-vibrate (leaves air bubbles = pockmarks on the face).

Watch for:

  • Surface bubbles: Under-vibrated or mix too stiff.
  • Sand streaks: Over-vibrated or too much sand.
  • Colour variation: Inconsistent water content between batches.

5 Releasing fair-faced blocks

Demoulding fair-faced blocks is delicate.

  1. Wait 24–36 hours (longer than standard blocks — edges are still fragile).
  2. Loosen the mould evenly — never one side first.
  3. Lift straight up — no tilt, no rotation.
  4. Place on a soft surface (foam pad or sand bed) to prevent chipping.
  5. Do not stack for 48 hours after demoulding.
  6. After 48 hours, stack face-to-back to protect faces.

If any corner chips during demoulding, it can't be sold as fair-faced. Rebate it as a standard block or scrap it.

⚠ Fair-faced blocks are unforgiving. Train operators slowly — bad demoulding wastes a full day's work.

6 Touching up blemishes

Small blemishes are inevitable. Touch-up can save 20–30% of "would-be" scrap.

Techniques:

  • Surface pinholes: Fill with a cement paste (cement + water, no sand). Rub smooth with a sponge.
  • Small chips at edges: Fill with a colour-matched mortar. Sand lightly after cure.
  • Colour variation: Wet the block evenly with a sponge before curing — colours even out as it dries.
  • Honeycomb (severe): Not fixable — reject as fair-faced, sell as standard.

Touch-up must happen within 4–6 hours of demoulding, before the block fully hardens.

7 When fair-faced beats plaster

Fair-faced walls offer several advantages over plastered walls.

Cost: Fair-faced block costs more than standard block, but the wall costs less — no plastering labour, no plaster material, no painting.

Time: Plastering a wall takes 2–3 days per section. Fair-faced block installation is immediate. Project timelines drop by weeks.

Durability: Plaster cracks over time and needs repainting. Fair-faced concrete ages naturally, often improving with time.

Aesthetic: Modern architectural look — industrial, honest, minimal. Popular in contemporary design.

Maintenance: Just dust and occasional clean. No painting, no patching.

8 Selling at a premium

Fair-faced blocks sell to a specific, high-value market.

Who buys:

  • Architects on contemporary projects
  • Commercial interior fit-outs
  • Hotel and restaurant lobbies
  • High-end residential
  • Gallery and retail spaces

Pricing (Africa, 2026):

  • Standard fair-faced: $3.50–$6.00 per block
  • Premium (near-perfect face): $6.00–$10.00 per block
  • Custom colours/shapes: $10.00–$20.00 per block

Cost to make: $0.80–$1.50 per block. Margin: 65–80%.

Selling tools:

  • Sample wall (build a 2 × 2 m panel to show visitors)
  • Full technical specs (dimensions, weight, strength, fire rating)
  • High-quality photos of finished installations
  • Written guarantee on colour consistency
💰 Fair-faced blocks have the highest margin of any block product. Best for a yard that can invest in precision moulds and skilled operators.
