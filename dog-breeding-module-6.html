6. Health, Vaccines and Parasite Control

Dogs are cheaper to keep healthy than to cure. Here is the full prevention program.

Module 6 of 12 · Dog Breeding for Profit

The seven diseases that kill dogs and puppies

DiseaseCauseSignsPrevention
ParvovirusVirus (faecal-oral)Bloody diarrhoea, vomiting, sudden death, especially in puppiesVaccinate at 6, 10, 12 weeks
DistemperVirusFever, discharge, neurological signs, twitchingVaccinate
Canine hepatitis (adenovirus)VirusFever, liver failure, vomitingVaccinate
LeptospirosisBacteria (urine)Fever, jaundice, kidney failureVaccinate
RabiesVirus (bite)Behaviour change, aggression, deathVaccinate - legally required in most countries
Kennel cough (Bordetella)Bacteria + virusHonking cough, retchingVaccinate, avoid contact with unknown dogs
CoronavirusVirusDiarrhoea, vomitingVaccinate if available

Vaccination schedule

AgeVaccine
6-8 weeksFirst core vaccine (distemper, parvo, hepatitis, parainfluenza)
10-12 weeksSecond core vaccine + leptospirosis + kennel cough
14-16 weeksThird core vaccine + rabies (legal requirement)
12 monthsBooster of all
AnnuallyBoosters of core vaccines + rabies (every 1-3 years depending on country)

Breeding females should be vaccinated before mating, not during pregnancy. Puppies get antibodies from colostrum, which protect them for the first 6-12 weeks.

Biosecurity in a kennel

  1. Quarantine new dogs for 3-4 weeks in a separate kennel.
  2. Vaccinate before mixing - 2 weeks before introducing to kennel.
  3. Disinfect between dogs. Use kennel-safe disinfectant.
  4. Footbaths at kennel entrances.
  5. Limit visitors. No visits from other dog owners without disinfection.
  6. Separate tools for quarantined animals.
  7. Do not share water or food bowls between litters.
  8. Isolate sick dogs immediately - even a suspected case.
  9. Vaccinate all adults annually to maintain herd immunity.
  10. Keep vaccination records for every dog.

Dental care

  • Brush teeth 2-3 times per week (dog toothpaste only).
  • Provide dental chews - safe, tough, digestible.
  • Check teeth monthly for tartar and gum disease.
  • Professional scaling by vet every 1-3 years.
  • Never use human toothpaste - xylitol and fluoride are toxic.

TaskFrequency

When to call the vet - routine

  • Annual check-up for all adult dogs.
  • Pre-breeding check for the female.
  • Hip and eye scoring before first breeding.
  • Pregnancy diagnosis at 25-30 days.
  • Puppy health check before sale.
  • Any change in appetite, water intake, or behaviour.
  • Any new lump, wound, or lameness.

First aid kit for a kennel

  • Digital thermometer
  • Antiseptic solution (chlorhexidine, iodine)
  • Antibiotic ointment
  • Bandages, gauze, self-adhesive wrap
  • Saline solution for eye flushing
  • Bulb syringe (for newborn puppies)
  • Puppy milk replacer and bottles
  • Dewormer (puppy and adult doses)
  • Flea and tick treatment
  • Glucose powder (for hypoglycaemia in small puppies)
  • Notebook for records
  • Emergency vet contact number
The vaccination rule: Never skip a vaccine. Distemper and parvo are endemic in Africa and kill whole litters. The $5-15 vaccine is the cheapest insurance you will ever buy.
Practical exercise 6: Set up a health calendar for your kennel. Mark vaccination dates, deworming dates, and flea/tick treatment dates. Post it on the kennel wall. Track every dog on the calendar.
