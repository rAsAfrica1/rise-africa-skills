Cucumber Processing

8 lessons · 30 quiz questions · Cucurbits

1 Cucumber varieties

Cucumbers (Cucumis sativus) fall into three groups based on their use. Picking the right variety is crucial for the product you want to make.

Pickling cucumbers: Short (5–12 cm), warty skin, firm flesh, low water. Best for pickles and gherkins. Varieties: National Pickling, Calypso, Salt and Pepper.

Slicing cucumbers: Long (20–30 cm), smooth skin, high water. Sold fresh. Not ideal for pickling (too watery).

English/Seedless: Very long, thin skin, no seeds. Premium fresh market only.

For processing, choose pickling varieties — they hold their crunch in brine.

2 Washing and grading

Cucumbers come in with soil and possible field bacteria.

  1. Soak in cold water for 15 minutes
  2. Brush gently to remove soil and spines
  3. Rinse in clean water
  4. Soak in 100 ppm chlorine solution for 2 minutes
  5. Rinse again
  6. Grade by size (small = whole pickles; larger = sliced)
  7. Remove any with yellow spots (overripe) or soft spots

The blossom end contains enzymes that soften pickles. Cut off 2 mm from the blossom end before brining.

⚠ Cucumbers spoil fast at room temp. Refrigerate between harvest and processing — max 24 hours.

3 Dill pickles (fermented)

Traditional fermented pickles use natural lactic acid bacteria — no vinegar needed.

  1. Pack washed cucumbers into a food-grade barrel or crock
  2. Add fresh dill, garlic, grape leaves (for crunch), peppercorns
  3. Cover with brine: 1 litre water + 50 g salt (5%)
  4. Weight down so cucumbers stay submerged
  5. Ferment at 18–22°C for 2–4 weeks
  6. Check daily — skim any white scum (kahm yeast, safe)
  7. When pH drops below 4.6, pickles are ready
  8. Transfer to jars with fresh brine, refrigerate

Yield: 100 kg cucumbers → 90 kg pickles (small loss). Shelf life: 12 months refrigerated.

⚠ If the brine smells rotten or cucumbers are slimy, discard — that's spoilage, not fermentation.

4 Sweet and bread-and-butter pickles

These use vinegar (not fermentation) and are ready in days.

Bread-and-butter pickles:

  1. Slice cucumbers 3 mm thick
  2. Cover with salt (100 g per kg) for 2 hours
  3. Drain and rinse
  4. Cover with brine: 1 L vinegar + 250 g sugar + 30 g salt + mustard seed + turmeric
  5. Heat to 80°C
  6. Pack in jars, hot-fill with brine
  7. Water-bath process 10 minutes (they're acidic)

Shelf life: 12 months unopened.

5 Cucumber juice and detox drinks

Cucumber juice is popular in wellness and detox markets.

  1. Peel if skin is bitter
  2. Blend or press flesh
  3. Strain through fine mesh
  4. Add lemon, mint and apple for flavour
  5. Add citric acid (0.2%) and ascorbic acid (0.05%) as preservatives
  6. Pasteurise at 85°C for 15 seconds
  7. Hot-fill into bottles

Yield: 100 kg cucumbers → 70–80 litres juice (cucumber is 96% water).

Retail $3–$6 per 500 ml bottle.

6 Cucumber chips (dehydrated)

Dried cucumber chips are a novelty snack and used in powder form for dips.

  1. Slice 3 mm thick (mandolin for consistency)
  2. Salt for 30 minutes
  3. Pat dry thoroughly
  4. Dehydrate at 55°C for 6–8 hours
  5. Cool completely
  6. Pack in airtight foil bags with oxygen absorber

Yield: 100 kg fresh → 4–6 kg chips (cucumber is 96% water).

Retail $40–$60 per kg — but check demand before scaling.

7 Cold storage

Fresh cucumbers are sensitive to temperature.

  • Best storage: 10–12°C at 95% humidity
  • Below 10°C: chilling injury — pitting, water-soaked spots
  • Shelf life fresh: 10–14 days under ideal conditions
  • Do not store with: bananas, tomatoes, apples (ethylene gas causes yellowing)
  • Pickles: 12 months in cool dark storage
  • Juice: 6 months pasteurised sealed
💡 Cucumbers are ethylene-sensitive. Store separately from ripening fruits.

8 Export standards

Cucumber pickles are exported from India, Turkey and China to Europe and North America. To compete:

  • Size grading (small pickles fetch premium — 3–6 cm for gherkins)
  • Uniform colour (dark green, no yellow)
  • Firm texture (not soft or hollow)
  • pH below 4.6 (for food safety)
  • Salt content 3–5%
  • Compliant labelling (ingredients, allergens, net weight)
  • HACCP certification for major buyers

Prices: $1.50–$4 per kg for bulk pickles FOB. Premium gherkins $6–$10 per kg.

💰 Small gherkins (3–6 cm) sell for 5–8× the price of standard cucumbers. Plant pickling varieties and harvest small.
