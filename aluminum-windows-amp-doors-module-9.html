Lessons

1. Cleaning and Polishing Aluminum Surfaces

Aluminum windows and doors look best when cleaned regularly. In Africa, dust, Harmattan sand, and coastal salt build up fast. Start by rinsing the frame with clean water to remove loose dirt. Mix a mild soap solution: 2 tablespoons of liquid dish soap per 4 liters of water. Never use abrasive cleaners, steel wool, or bleachthey scratch the anodized or powder-coated finish. Use a soft microfiber cloth or a soft-bristle brush. For stubborn stains like bird droppings or tree sap, apply the soap solution and let it sit for 5 minutes, then scrub gently. Rinse thoroughly with clean water and dry with a clean cloth to prevent water spots. For polished aluminum (mirror finish), use a commercial aluminum polish like Mothers Mag & Aluminum Polish. Apply a small amount to a soft cloth, rub in circles, then buff with a clean dry cloth. Do this every 3 months in dusty areas. For anodized or powder-coated surfaces, a silicone-based spray (e.g., WD-40 Specialist Silicone) can add shine and repel dust. Avoid polishing compounds on these finishes as they can dull the coating. In humid regions, clean more oftenevery 2 weeksto prevent mold and mildew. Always work in shade; direct sun dries the soap too quickly and leaves streaks. After cleaning, check weep holes at the bottom of frames; clear them with a paperclip or compressed air. A clean surface not only looks good but also reveals early signs of corrosion or damage. Teach your customers: clean with soap and water monthly, and never use harsh chemicals.

2. Applying Protective Coatings for Coastal Areas

Coastal areas in Africalike Mombasa, Lagos, or Durbanhave high salt and humidity, which corrode aluminum fast. The best protection is a clear coat or anodizing, but you can apply aftermarket coatings. First, clean the surface thoroughly as in Lesson 1. Let it dry completely. For a clear protective coating, use a product like Everbrite ProtectaClear or a marine-grade clear coat (e.g., Rust-Oleum Clear Enamel). Apply with a foam brush or spray in thin, even coats. Two coats are better than one thick coat. Allow 2 hours drying between coats. For powder-coated frames, you can apply a carnauba wax (e.g., Collinite 845) every 6 months; it fills micropores and repels salt. For raw aluminum, anodizing is best but expensive; a cheaper option is a conversion coating like Alodine 1201, which requires safety gear (gloves, goggles, ventilation). In coastal areas, reapply protective coatings every 6 to 12 months. Check for chalking or fading. Also, consider installing sacrificial anodessmall zinc blockson the frame, especially near the bottom, to divert corrosion. These need replacement yearly. For sliding tracks, apply a dry film lubricant (e.g., PTFE spray) instead of oil, which attracts sand. Remember: salt spray from the ocean can travel 10 km inland, so even if you are not right on the beach, protection is needed. Train customers to rinse their windows with fresh water monthly to remove salt. If you see white rust (aluminum oxide), remove it with a 50/50 mix of white vinegar and water, then rinse and coat. Never use steel brushesthey embed iron particles that cause rust.

3. Preventing Corrosion in Humid Environments

Humid environmentslike the Congo Basin, coastal West Africa, or the Niger Deltaaccelerate aluminum corrosion. The main enemy is galvanic corrosion when aluminum touches dissimilar metals like steel or copper. Always use nylon or plastic washers between aluminum and steel screws. If you must use steel, use stainless steel 316. Also, avoid contact with treated wood (which contains copper) or concrete (alkaline). Use a bitumen or zinc-chromate primer as a barrier. For humid areas, ensure proper drainage: weep holes must be clear, and frames should be installed with a slight slope (1-2 degrees) outward so water runs off. Condensation inside the frame can cause corrosion from the inside. Apply a water-repellent coating like Rain-X to glass and frames. For sliding tracks, use a silicone-based lubricant (e.g., 3-IN-ONE Silicone) every 3 months; it displaces water. Check for white rusta white powdery deposit. Remove it with a 10% citric acid solution or white vinegar, then rinse and apply a clear coat. In extreme humidity, consider using marine-grade aluminum (5052 or 6061) which resists corrosion better. If you see pitting (small holes), that's advanced corrosion; clean with a wire brush (brass, not steel), apply a zinc-rich primer, then topcoat. For long-term protection, anodizing or powder coating is best. Educate customers to keep indoor humidity below 60% using ventilation or dehumidifiers. Also, avoid storing chemicals like bleach or fertilizers near windows. Regular inspection every 3 months is key. A simple test: spray water on the frame; if it beads and runs off, protection is good; if it spreads, reapply coating.

4. Routine Maintenance for Windows and Doors

Routine maintenance keeps aluminum windows and doors working smoothly for decades. Create a schedule: monthly, quarterly, and yearly tasks. Monthly: clean tracks and frames with a vacuum brush or soft cloth. Check for debris, sand, or insects. Wipe seals with a damp cloth. Quarterly: lubricate moving partsrollers, hinges, lockswith a silicone spray or dry PTFE lubricant. Do not use oil; it attracts dust. Check screws and tighten any loose ones. Inspect weatherstripping and caulking for cracks; replace if brittle. Yearly: deep clean all surfaces, check for corrosion, and touch up paint or coating. For sliding doors, adjust rollers if they drag; most have an adjustment screw. For casement windows, check the crank mechanism; apply a little white lithium grease. Test locks and handles; if stiff, spray with graphite or silicone. In dusty regions, clean tracks every 2 weeks. Use a small brush and a vacuum. If tracks are worn, replace themthey are inexpensive. Also, check drainage holes: poke with a wire. For hinges, apply a drop of machine oil every 6 months. For screens, wash with soapy water and rinse. Inspect glass seals; if cracked, remove old sealant and apply new silicone caulk. In coastal areas, rinse frames monthly with fresh water. Keep a maintenance log for each installation. Train customers to do simple tasks: clean tracks, wipe frames, and report issues early. A well-maintained window saves energy and avoids costly repairs. Remember: prevention is cheaper than replacement.

5. Repairing Minor Dents and Scratches

Minor dents and scratches happen. For scratches on anodized or powder-coated aluminum, use a touch-up paint pen matched to the color (e.g., from the manufacturer). Clean the area with alcohol, then apply thin coats until flush. For deeper scratches that expose bare metal, first apply a zinc chromate primer, then touch-up paint. For polished aluminum, sand lightly with 2000-grit wet sandpaper, then polish with aluminum polish. For dents, you can sometimes pop them out using a suction cup dent puller. If that fails, drill a small hole in the center, insert a dent puller screw, and pull. Then fill with aluminum-filled epoxy (e.g., J-B Weld). Let it cure, then sand smooth and paint. For small dents on frames that are not visible, you can leave them. For dents on sliding tracks, they can cause sticking; use a hammer and a block of wood to gently tap them out. Always protect the surrounding area with tape. For scratches on glass, use cerium oxide polish. For deep gouges, consider replacing the section. In Africa, many hardware stores sell aluminum repair kits. Practice on scrap pieces first. After repair, apply a protective coating. If the dent is on a structural part, consult a professional. Remember: safety firstwear gloves and goggles. A good repair should be invisible. If you cannot match the finish, consider painting the entire frame a new color. Teach customers to report dents early before corrosion sets in.

6. Educating Customers on Care and Warranty

Your job does not end at installation. Teach customers how to care for their aluminum windows and doors. Provide a simple one-page care guide in English and local language. Include: clean monthly with mild soap and water; never use abrasive cleaners; lubricate moving parts every 3 months; check and clear weep holes; rinse coastal installations monthly. Explain the warranty: typically 5-10 years on finish, 1-2 years on hardware. Void warranty if improper cleaning or chemicals used. Show them how to identify problems: white rust, sticking, water leaks. Give them your contact for service. Offer a maintenance contract: annual inspection for a fee. In Africa, many customers are not used to maintaining aluminum; they think it is maintenance-free. Emphasize that salt, dust, and humidity require care. Use a checklist during handover. Demonstrate cleaning on one window. Leave a small care kit: microfiber cloth, silicone spray, and a brush. For landlords, provide a schedule. For warranty claims, keep records of installation date and materials. Train your team to be polite and clear. A well-informed customer will refer others. Also, explain that repairs due to neglect are not covered. Provide a sticker with your phone number on the frame. Follow up after 3 months to check. This builds trust and repeat business. Remember: happy customers are your best marketing.

7. Practical Reference

Practical Reference: Finishing, Protection, and Maintenance of Aluminum Windows & Doors

In African coastal and humid markets from Mombasa and Dar es Salaam to Lagos, Accra, and Durban aluminum windows and doors fail not because the extrusion was weak, but because the finish was neglected. For fabricators, installers, and facility managers, finishing and maintenance are not cosmetic afterthoughts: they are the difference between a one-off sale and a repeat contract, between a warranty claim and a referral. This practical reference consolidates the cleaning, coating, corrosion-prevention, repair, and customer-education practices that keep aluminum systems serviceable for 20+ years in tropical and coastal conditions. Treat every point as a billable service line, not just a technical instruction.

1. Cleaning and Polishing Aluminum Surfaces

Aluminum naturally forms a thin oxide layer that protects it, but in dusty, salty, or polluted air this layer becomes contaminated. The standard regime is: rinse with clean water to remove grit, wash with a mild pH-neutral detergent (never caustic soda or abrasive powders), rinse thoroughly, then dry with a soft cloth to prevent water spots. For anodized surfaces, a non-abrasive cream polish restores lustre; for powder-coated surfaces, use only soft cloths and mild soap to avoid dulling the coating. In dusty Sahelian and savannah markets, weekly rinsing during harmattan or dry season is realistic; in coastal towns, fortnightly cleaning is the minimum commercial standard.

2. Applying Protective Coatings for Coastal Areas

Within roughly 5 km of the sea, airborne chloride attacks mill-finish aluminum within months. The correct specification is a factory-applied powder coat (minimum 6080 microns) or a PVDF coating for premium projects, plus a clear protective wax or lacquer on cut edges, fixings, and any site-drilled holes. On site, apply a compatible clear protective coating to exposed cut ends and touch up scratches with approved touch-up paint. Never mix coating systems from different suppliers without a compatibility test adhesion failure is the most common coastal defect.

3. Preventing Corrosion in Humid Environments

Humidity alone is manageable; humidity plus salt, acid rain, or contact with dissimilar metals is not. Key controls:

  • Isolate dissimilar metals: use nylon or neoprene washers between aluminum and steel or stainless-steel fixings to prevent galvanic corrosion.
  • Drain correctly: ensure weep holes and drainage channels are clear so water does not pond in the frame.
  • Seal cut edges: every site cut exposes bare aluminum; seal it immediately.
  • Avoid chloride cleaners: bleach-based products accelerate pitting.
  • Ventilate interiors: in humid tropical buildings, condensation on cold frames is a hidden corrosion driver.

5. Repairing Minor Dents and Scratches

Minor damage is a service opportunity. For powder-coated surfaces: clean the area, sand lightly with 600800 grit wet-and-dry paper, apply approved touch-up paint in thin coats, and blend. For anodized surfaces, deep scratches cannot be fully restored on site use a matching touch-up pen and disclose the limitation to the customer. For dents in hollow sections, gentle panel-beating with a nylon mallet and a backing block can restore profile; never heat aluminum to reshape it, as this destroys temper and coating. Document every repair with photographs for the warranty file.

6. Educating Customers on Care and Warranty

Warranty disputes in African markets usually stem from cleaning neglect, not manufacturing defects. Provide every client with a one-page care card (in English and the local language where possible), covering: acceptable cleaners, cleaning frequency, what voids warranty (abrasive cleaners, chloride bleach, painting over powder coat, unauthorised repairs), and how to report a claim. Offer an annual paid maintenance contract this converts a one-time installation into recurring revenue and protects the warranty.

SAFETY WARNING

Never mix acids, bleach, or ammonia-based cleaners toxic gases can be released. Always wear gloves and eye protection when handling coatings, sealants, and solvents. Work in ventilated areas. On high-rise installations, use certified fall-arrest equipment and never lean out of unsecured openings. Dispose of solvent and coating waste according to local environmental regulations; do not pour into drains or soil.

Quick Reference Checklist

  • Rinse before washing never scrub dry grit into the finish.
  • Use pH-neutral detergent only; ban bleach, caustic, and abrasive pads.
  • Clear weep holes and drainage channels every visit.
  • Seal every site cut edge immediately with compatible coating.
  • Isolate aluminum from steel and stainless fixings with nylon/neoprene washers.
  • Lubricate hinges, rollers, and locks with a non-staining silicone or PTFE lubricant.
  • Photograph and log all repairs for the warranty file.
  • Give every customer a care card and offer an annual maintenance contract.
