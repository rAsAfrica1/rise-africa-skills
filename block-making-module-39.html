Sales to Builders and Contractors

8 lessons · 30 quiz questions · Business

1 Who buys in bulk

Builders and contractors are your highest-value customers. One good relationship = ongoing orders worth thousands.

Types of bulk buyers:

  • Residential builders: 5,000–50,000 blocks per house
  • Commercial contractors: 50,000–500,000 blocks per project
  • Government/council: Housing schemes, schools, clinics
  • Developers: Multi-house estates
  • Small job builders: 500–5,000 blocks per job

Builders care about three things: price, delivery reliability, and block quality. Get those right and they buy for years.

💡 10 loyal builders = guaranteed monthly production volume. Easier than chasing 1,000 one-off customers.

2 Sales call script

A simple script for the first call. Don't overthink it.

Opening: "Hi, I'm [name] from [business]. We make concrete blocks locally. Can I ask what you're currently paying per block and who you buy from?"

After listening: "We're [X]% cheaper than that, or same price with faster delivery. Can I drop off some samples so you can check the quality?"

Sample drop-off: Take 5–10 blocks to their site. Leave them. "Have a look, no rush. Call me if you want to try us on a small order."

Follow-up (3 days later): "Hi, did you get a chance to look at the samples? Any questions?"

First order: Offer to deliver free on first order. Show reliability. Once they trust you, they order again.

Average close rate: 1 in 10 sales calls. 5 calls per week = 1 new customer per fortnight.

3 Sample block offers

Nobody buys blocks without seeing them first. Samples close deals.

Sample pack for builders:

  • 3 blocks (one dense, one hollow, one whatever you want to feature)
  • Printed price list
  • Your business card
  • Optional: a small bag of cement mix so they can test

How to present:

  • Drop in person — don't post
  • Show the block's sharp edges, uniform colour, solid weight
  • Break one block in front of them (or drop from waist height)
  • Explain the strength (7 N/mm², tested)

Cost of samples: About $3 per pack. Cheap compared to the value of a new builder customer.

4 Credit terms

Credit is a sales tool — but also a risk. Get it right.

Standard credit terms:

  • Cash on delivery (COD): Safest. Use with new customers for first 3 orders.
  • 7-day terms: For builders with good payment history.
  • 30-day terms: For established, trusted contractors.
  • 50% deposit + 30 days: Compromise for large orders.

How to decide:

  • New customer + big order → COD or 50% deposit
  • New customer + small order → COD
  • Regular (3+ orders) → 7-day terms
  • Long-term trusted → 30-day terms

Never offer credit to a first-time customer. Once burned, you'll be cautious for years.

⚠ Bad debt is worse than lost sales. One customer owing $5,000 wipes out your monthly profit.

5 Invoicing and payment

Get paid on time with clear invoicing.

Invoice essentials:

  • Your business name, address, phone, email
  • Invoice number (sequential)
  • Customer name and address
  • Date of invoice and date due
  • Itemised list: block type, quantity, unit price, line total
  • Subtotal, VAT (if applicable), total
  • Payment terms and bank details
  • Thank-you note

Payment methods: Bank transfer, EcoCash, M-Pesa, cash, card. Offer 2–3 options.

Chase late payments:

  • Day 1 after due: friendly WhatsApp reminder
  • Day 7 after due: call, ask for update
  • Day 14 after due: stop new deliveries
  • Day 30 after due: formal letter, legal action warning

90% of late payers just need a reminder. The rest — cut them off.

6 Repeat order strategy

The first sale is expensive. Repeat sales are almost free.

How to drive repeat orders:

  • Call after every delivery — "How did the blocks lay? Any issues?"
  • WhatsApp on big project milestones — "Saw your wall going up, looking good!"
  • Predict their next need — if they're building a 3-bed house, they'll need roof blocks next
  • Save their details — name, block type, delivery address, delivery frequency
  • Small gift — at Christmas or after the 5th order, send a small thank-you

Builders who've bought 3+ times are 80% likely to buy again. Give them a reason.

7 Referral systems

Every happy builder knows 5 other builders. Tap that network.

Referral program:

  • "Refer a builder who places a $1,000+ order, get $50 credit on your next order"
  • Track referrals in a simple spreadsheet
  • Pay referral rewards immediately — don't make them chase
  • Thank both the referrer and the new customer personally

Other referral sources:

  • Hardware stores (they see builder traffic daily)
  • Architects (they specify for multiple projects)
  • Engineers (they recommend material suppliers)
  • Building inspectors (they know every site)

One architect relationship can be worth 20 builder relationships — they specify for many projects at once.

8 Handling complaints

Builders complain about: bad blocks, late delivery, wrong quantity, broken blocks during transport.

How to handle each:

Bad blocks: Replace within 48 hours. No arguments. Then investigate internally — was it your batch or their handling?

Late delivery: Communicate before they call. If you're going to be 2 hours late, WhatsApp them 1 hour before. Most builders are reasonable if they're kept informed.

Wrong quantity: Deliver the shortfall same day. Apologise. Note the cause (usually counting errors).

Broken during transport: Check your loading method. Are pallets strapped? Is the vehicle suitable? Fix, then refund or replace.

The 24-hour rule: Respond to every complaint within 24 hours. Even if just to say "I'm looking into it." Silence loses customers.

💰 Builders buy on trust. One bad delivery handled well keeps a customer for years. One bad delivery handled badly loses them forever — and their network.
