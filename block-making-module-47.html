1 Why records matter

Records turn daily work into useful information.

  • Track production and quality
  • Control costs and inventory
  • Prove safety compliance
  • Support loan or grant applications
  • Resolve customer complaints
  • Spot trends and problems early

2 Production records

Record what you make every day.

  • Date and shift
  • Operator name
  • Block type and size
  • Quantity produced
  • Downtime and reason
  • Raw materials used

Use a simple daily production sheet.

3 Quality control records

Quality records prove your blocks meet spec.

  • Batch number and date
  • Raw material source
  • Dimensions measured
  • Compressive strength test results
  • Non-conformance reports
  • Corrective actions

4 Maintenance logs

Maintenance logs prevent breakdowns.

  • Date and machine
  • Service or repair done
  • Parts replaced
  • Next service due
  • Mould usage count
  • Downtime hours

5 Staff and training records

Keep records for each worker.

  • Attendance
  • Training completed
  • Certifications
  • Skills matrix
  • Incident records
  • Performance notes

6 Safety and incident records

Safety records protect workers and the business.

  • Incident date and time
  • Person involved
  • Injury or near miss
  • Cause
  • Action taken
  • Toolbox talk attendance

7 Sales, inventory, and financial records

Know your money and stock.

  • Sales invoices and receipts
  • Inventory: cement, sand, aggregate, blocks
  • Expenses: fuel, parts, wages
  • Payroll records
  • Bank statements
  • Tax records

8 Using records to improve decisions

Records are only useful if you use them.

  • Analyse downtime — fix frequent problems
  • Track reject rate — improve quality
  • Compare costs — negotiate better prices
  • Plan maintenance — avoid breakdowns
  • Set targets — motivate team
