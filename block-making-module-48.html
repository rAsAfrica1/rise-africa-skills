1 Why financial management matters

Good block making without financial control leads to failure. You must know your numbers.

  • Track money in and out
  • Know your true costs
  • Price for profit
  • Plan for taxes and emergencies
  • Make informed decisions

Financial management is not just for accountants. It is a daily discipline.

2 Tracking income and expenses

Record every transaction. Use a simple cash book or spreadsheet.

Income:

  • Block sales (cash and credit)
  • Delivery charges
  • Custom orders

Expenses:

  • Cement, sand, aggregate
  • Fuel, electricity, water
  • Wages and salaries
  • Maintenance and parts
  • Transport and rent

Keep receipts. Review weekly.

3 Costing and pricing your blocks

Many block makers underprice because they don't know their costs.

Calculate cost per block:

  1. Add material cost per batch
  2. Add labour cost per batch
  3. Add overheads (rent, power, maintenance) per batch
  4. Divide by number of blocks per batch

Pricing formula: Cost per block × (1 + profit margin). Typical margin 20–50%.

Review prices when material costs change.

4 Cash flow management

Cash flow is the lifeblood of a block yard. Profit is not cash.

  • Forecast cash in and out weekly
  • Collect receivables quickly
  • Negotiate supplier terms
  • Keep a cash reserve for emergencies
  • Avoid overbuying stock

Never run out of cash to pay wages or buy cement.

5 Budgeting and forecasting

A budget is a plan for money. It helps you control spending.

  • Estimate monthly income
  • List fixed costs (rent, salaries)
  • List variable costs (materials, fuel)
  • Set a profit target
  • Compare actual vs budget each month

Adjust as needed. A budget is a guide, not a straitjacket.

6 Managing credit and debt

Credit can help growth but can also kill a business.

  • Offer credit only to trusted customers
  • Set clear credit limits and terms
  • Follow up overdue payments
  • Borrow only for productive assets
  • Avoid high-interest loans for daily expenses

Keep debt below 30% of monthly revenue.

7 Profit and loss, balance sheet basics

Understand these two reports.

Profit & Loss (P&L): Income – Expenses = Profit (or Loss). Shows performance over a period.

Balance Sheet: Assets = Liabilities + Equity. Shows what you own and owe at a point in time.

You don't need to be an accountant, but you must understand these.

8 Financial records and tax compliance

Keep records for tax and legal requirements.

  • Keep all receipts and invoices
  • Record sales and purchases
  • File tax returns on time
  • Keep payroll records
  • Store records for at least 5 years

Good records prevent fines and help you get loans.
