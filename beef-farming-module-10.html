10. Slaughter, Meat Handling and Value-Adding

Dressed and processed beef earns 1.5-3x the live price. Here is how to do it properly.

Module 10 of 12 · Beef Farming for Profit

Why slaughter and process

Selling live cattle at market earns market rate. Dressed, graded, and processed beef earns 1.5-3x more. Butcheries and restaurants pay premium for consistent quality and food safety.

A 500 kg live steer gives 260-300 kg carcass. A 400 kg indigenous steer gives 190-215 kg carcass.

The slaughter process

  1. Withdraw feed 12-24 hours before. Keep water available.
  2. Rest the animal in a quiet pen 24 hours before - stress affects meat quality (dark cutters).
  3. Weigh live weight. Record.
  4. Restrain humanely. Proper stunning (captive bolt) or ritual slaughter if religious market.
  5. Bleed fully - 3-5 minutes.
  6. Skin immediately - take care not to cut the hide.
  7. Remove head, feet, and internal organs.
  8. Split carcass into halves along the spine.
  9. Wash carcass with clean cold water.
  10. Chill to 0-4 C within 1 hour.
  11. Age the carcass 7-14 days at 0-2 C for tenderness (premium market).

Hide handling

Cattle hides are valuable - from $15-60 each when properly handled.

  1. Skin carefully - no knife cuts.
  2. Remove flesh and fat from the hide.
  3. Salt immediately with coarse salt (1 kg per hide).
  4. Roll flesh-side in, store in a cool dry place.
  5. Or air-dry stretched on a frame in the shade.
  6. Sell to tannery or leather worker.

Food safety - non-negotiable

  • Clean water only for washing carcass.
  • Clean knives and table - disinfect between animals.
  • Separate evisceration area from wash area.
  • Chill within 1 hour - never leave warm carcasses in the sun.
  • Wrap or bag all meat for transport.
  • Record batch number and date for traceability.
  • Wash hands frequently with food-grade sanitiser.
Never sell meat from a sick or dead animal. Animals that died of disease must be burned or buried. Disease (especially anthrax) can pass to humans.
The processing rule: A $1,000 steer sells for $1,000 live, or $1,500-2,500 processed and graded. The extra $500-1,500 is profit for 1-2 days of work.
Practical exercise 10: Visit a butchery that sells beef. Ask their price per kg for whole carcass vs individual cuts. Calculate the extra income if you processed your own cattle.
