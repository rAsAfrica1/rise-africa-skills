Choosing Your Poultry System
Start

Before you buy a single chick, you must decide: layers or broilers? Intensive or free-range? Your choice determines your housing, feed budget, and profit timeline.

  • Broilers reach market weight in 6-8 weeks; layers start producing eggs at 18-20 weeks.
  • Deep litter system is cheapest to start; battery cage system is best for large-scale layers.
  • Free-range (semi-intensive) reduces feed costs but needs more land and predator protection.
Pro Tip: Start with 100-200 birds. This teaches you management without catastrophic loss risk.

Key Takeaway: Match your poultry system to your capital, land, and target market (meat vs eggs).

Brooding Day-Old Chicks
Locked

The first 14 days decide your mortality rate. Chicks cannot regulate body temperature. A proper brooder house is non-negotiable.

  • Temperature: 35°C in week 1, reduce by 2.5°C weekly until ambient temperature.
  • Use infrared bulbs, gas brooders, or charcoal stoves — but ensure ventilation to prevent CO2 buildup.
  • Spread newspaper with feed for the first 3 days so chicks learn to peck.
Pro Tip: Dip each chick's beak in water on arrival. Dehydration kills more chicks than disease in the first 48 hours.

Key Takeaway: Brooding success = low mortality. Control heat, water, and feed placement religiously.

Feed Formulation & Nutrition
Locked

Feed is 70% of your production cost. Understanding what your birds need at each stage protects your profit margin.

  • Starter feed (0-4 weeks): 22-24% protein for broilers; 20% for layers.
  • Grower feed (5-10 weeks for layers): 16-17% protein to prevent early fattening.
  • Layer mash (from 18 weeks): 16-18% protein with added calcium for strong eggshells.
Pro Tip: Buy feed in bulk from reputable mills. Cheap, adulterated feed is the #1 reason for poor flock performance.

Key Takeaway: Never compromise on feed quality. Poor nutrition = poor growth, low eggs, and disease susceptibility.

Vaccination & Disease Prevention
Locked

Prevention is infinitely cheaper than treatment. A strict vaccination schedule and biosecurity protocol are your insurance policy.

  • Day 1: Marek's disease (usually done at hatchery).
  • Day 7-14: Newcastle + Infectious Bronchitis (ND/IB).
  • Week 3-4: Gumboro (IBD) — critical for immune development.
Pro Tip: Keep a vaccination logbook. One missed date can wipe out an entire flock.

Key Takeaway: Vaccinate on schedule. Biosecurity (footbaths, restricted access, quarantine) prevents 80% of outbreaks.

Housing & Ventilation Design
Locked

Your poultry house is a controlled environment. Poor design causes heat stress, respiratory disease, and cannibalism.

  • Orient the long axis east-west to minimize direct sun exposure.
  • Provide 1 square foot per broiler; 2 square feet per layer in deep litter.
  • Cross-ventilation is essential: hot air rises and exits through roof vents.
Pro Tip: Use wire mesh on windows, not solid walls. Airflow prevents ammonia buildup from droppings.

Key Takeaway: A well-ventilated, shaded house reduces stress, disease, and mortality dramatically.

Water Management & Hygiene
Locked

Birds drink twice as much as they eat. Contaminated water is the fastest route for disease transmission in a flock.

  • Provide 1 nipple drinker per 10-12 birds, or 1 bell drinker per 50 birds.
  • Clean drinkers daily. Add chlorine (2-3 ppm) or organic acids to water weekly.
  • Never let water run out. Even 1 hour without water reduces egg production for days.
Pro Tip: Raise drinkers slightly as birds grow to prevent litter contamination.

Key Takeaway: Clean, unlimited water is as important as feed. Treat it like medicine, not an afterthought.

Egg Handling & Marketing (Layers)
Locked

Eggs are fragile and perishable. How you collect, clean, grade, and price them determines your profit.

  • Collect eggs 3-4 times daily to reduce breakage and soiling.
  • Grade by size: Small (<53g), Medium (53-63g), Large (63-73g), Extra Large (>73g).
  • Sell directly to retailers, bakeries, and schools for better margins than wholesalers.
Pro Tip: Wash dirty eggs in warm water (not cold) with egg wash powder. Cold water pulls bacteria through the shell pores.

Key Takeaway: Handle eggs like cash. Clean, grade, and market directly to maximize price per crate.

Broiler Processing & Sales
Locked

The final stage: converting live birds to cash. Processing, packaging, and timing your sales correctly increases profit by 30%.

  • Target 2.0-2.5 kg live weight at 6-7 weeks for premium market price.
  • Process early morning to avoid heat stress on birds and meat spoilage.
  • Sell live, dressed, or cut-up portions. Cut-up yields highest profit per kg.
Pro Tip: Build a customer list before you stock chicks. Pre-orders eliminate the risk of unsold inventory.

Key Takeaway: Plan your market before you start. Processing adds value — never sell everything live at farm gate.

Record Keeping & Profit Analysis
Locked

Many poultry farms fail not from disease, but because the owner never calculated true costs. Records turn guessing into business.

  • Track daily mortality, feed intake, water consumption, and egg production.
  • Calculate Feed Conversion Ratio (FCR): kg feed / kg weight gain. Target <2.0 for broilers.
  • Know your break-even point: total cost / number of birds or crates.
Pro Tip: Use a simple notebook or phone notes app. Daily records take 5 minutes but save thousands in mistakes.

Key Takeaway: What gets measured gets managed. Records reveal problems before they become crises.

10
Scaling & Business Registration
Locked

You started small. Now you want to grow. Scaling requires structure: legal registration, access to credit, and professional management.

  • Register your business with CAC (Nigeria) or your country's corporate affairs body.
  • Open a business bank account — required for loans and grants.
  • Consider cooperative membership for bulk purchasing and shared cold storage.
Pro Tip: Apply for BOI (Bank of Industry) or CBN AGSMEIS loans once you have 6 months of records.

Key Takeaway: Treat poultry as a business, not a side hustle. Registration and records unlock government support and credit.
