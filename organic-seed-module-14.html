Seed Quality Testing - Proving It Works

🧠 Brain Opener

A buyer will not believe your seed is good. They will test it. If your seed fails, the sale fails. So test it yourself, before they do.

  • What are the four main seed quality tests?
  • How do you test germination at home?
  • Why does moisture matter so much?
🎁 Reveal a strong answer

The four tests are germination (will it sprout), purity (is it clean), moisture (is it dry enough to store), and vigour (will it grow strongly under stress). You can test germination at home with wet paper and a warm place. Moisture matters because wet seed rots and dry seed stores for years.

📖 Rudo Builds a Testing Table

Rudo was twenty-six. She had 40 kilograms of certified organic tomato seed ready for sale. She had dried it. She had cleaned it. She thought it was perfect.

Then her buyer, an organic exporter in Harare, asked for a germination test.

Rudo did not know what that was. She said yes anyway.

She called her aunt, who had gone to agricultural college. Her aunt explained.

"Take 100 seeds. Put them on wet paper. Keep them warm and damp for 14 days. Count how many sprout. That is your germination percentage."

Rudo did the test. Out of 100 seeds, 76 sprouted. She was pleased.

But her aunt said: "The standard for certified tomato seed is 85 percent. You are below."

Rudo was stunned. She had done everything right. Why was her germination low?

Her aunt tested the moisture next. It was 14 percent. The standard was 8 percent.

The seed was too wet. That was the problem. Wet seed has lower germination because it has already started to rot.

Rudo spread the seed on a mat and dried it for three more days. Then she ran the germination test again. 89 percent.

She delivered the seed. The buyer tested it independently. 88 percent. He paid her in full.

Rudo built a small testing table in her shed. She now tests every batch of seed before selling. No buyer has rejected her seed in two years.

💡 The Big Idea

Test your own seed before the buyer does

A germination test takes 14 days and costs almost nothing. A moisture test takes 5 minutes. These two simple tests, done before every sale, prevent nearly every rejection. Buyers expect you to know your own product. If you do not test it, you do not know it.

📚 Three Deep Lessons

1

The four seed quality tests

Germination. How many seeds sprout under ideal conditions. Standard: 85 to 95 percent for most crops. Test method: wet paper towel in a warm place for 7 to 14 days.

Purity. What percentage is the target seed, by weight. Standard: 98 to 99 percent. Test method: weigh a sample, separate non-seed, weigh again.

Moisture. How much water is in the seed. Standard: 8 to 12 percent depending on crop. Test method: a moisture meter, or a simple oven test if you have no meter.

Vigour. How strongly the seed grows under stress. Standard: measured by speed of germination and root length. Test method: cold germination, or salt stress test.

2

Home germination test - step by step

Step 1: Count out 100 seeds of your crop. Use a random sample, not the biggest ones.

Step 2: Fold a paper towel or cloth and wet it. It should be damp, not dripping.

Step 3: Spread the 100 seeds evenly on the wet towel. Space them apart.

Step 4: Cover with another wet towel. Put the whole thing in a plastic bag or container.

Step 5: Keep it warm (20 to 25 degrees Celsius) and damp. Check every 2 days and add water if dry.

Step 6: After 7 days, count how many have sprouted. After 14 days, count again. The final count is your germination percentage.

For tomatoes, count at day 10. For pepper, day 14. For brassicas, day 7. For maize, day 7.

3

Moisture testing without a meter

If you do not have a moisture meter, you can still estimate. Take 20 grams of seed. Weigh it accurately. Dry it in a low oven (not more than 130 degrees Celsius) for 1 hour. Weigh again. The weight lost is the water. If 20 grams went to 18 grams, moisture was 10 percent.

For field estimates, use the salt test. Put a tablespoon of dry salt in a glass jar with a handful of seed. Shake. If salt sticks to the seed, moisture is above 12 percent. If the salt stays free, moisture is below 12 percent.

For any commercial sale, buy an inexpensive moisture meter ($30 to $80). It pays for itself with the first rejected batch you avoid.

🛠 Hands-On - Run a Full Germination Test

You need

  • 100 seeds of your crop
  • Paper towels or a clean cloth
  • A plastic bag or container
  • Water
  • A pen and notebook
  1. Count out 100 seeds.
  2. Wet the paper towel or cloth. Squeeze out excess water.
  3. Place the seeds on the wet towel, spaced apart.
  4. Cover with another wet towel.
  5. Put in a plastic bag or container. Keep at room temperature.
  6. Check every 2 days. Add water if dry.
  7. After 7 days, count how many have sprouted.
  8. After 14 days, count again. Write down the final number.
  9. That number is your germination rate. Below 80 percent is a problem.
Why it works: A germination test is the single most useful quality check. It tells you whether your seed will actually work in the field.

🎮 Game - Germination Race

2 to 8 players - 2 weeks (checked daily)

Each player plants 50 seeds of the same crop in identical conditions. Check daily. The player with the highest germination percentage after 14 days wins. Discuss: what did the winner do differently - planting depth, moisture, temperature?

🏆 Project Challenge

Test Three Batches

Run germination, purity, and moisture tests on three different seed batches.

  1. Choose three batches of seed - from different crops or different harvests.
  2. Run germination on each (100 seeds each).
  3. Run purity on each (weigh 100 grams, remove non-seed, weigh again).
  4. Run moisture estimate on each (salt test or oven test).
  5. Write a report card for each batch: germination, purity, moisture, overall grade.
  6. Decide which batch to sell first.
  7. Test the same batches in one month to see if quality is stable.
