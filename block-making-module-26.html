Retaining Wall (Segmental) Block

8 lessons · 30 quiz questions · Medium difficulty

1 Retaining wall basics

A retaining wall holds back soil. It's used to create level ground on a slope, prevent erosion, or support a raised garden.

Types of retaining walls:

  • Gravity wall: Heavy enough to resist soil pressure alone. Made from mass concrete or large blocks.
  • Cantilever wall: Reinforced concrete, held by steel bars running into the ground.
  • Segmental wall: Made from interlocking blocks that stack without mortar. Modern standard — what this module covers.
  • Gabion wall: Wire baskets filled with rocks. Not a block product.

Segmental retaining walls use specially shaped concrete blocks. They interlock with each other and can be built without mortar or steel — the shape does the work.

2 Segmental block design

Segmental retaining wall blocks have three key features:

1. Lip or lug (shear key):

  • A small ridge at the back top of the block
  • Catches the block above it to prevent sliding forward
  • Sets a 2–5° backward lean (batter), which helps stability
  • Eliminates need for mortar

2. Pin connection (alternative to lip):

  • Plastic or fiberglass pins pushed through holes in the blocks
  • More precise than lip
  • Common in high-end systems

3. Split face or textured face:

  • Most retaining wall blocks have a decorative split face
  • Looks natural, hides soil staining

Standard block size: 400 × 200 × 200 mm (length × height × depth). Weight: 20–25 kg.

3 Heavy concrete mix

Retaining wall blocks carry soil pressure — they need strong, durable concrete.

Standard mix:

  • Cement: 1 part
  • Sharp sand: 1.5 parts
  • Crushed stone (6–10 mm): 3 parts
  • Water: earth-damp

Cement content: 15–18% of weight. Similar to paver mix.

Target strength: 25–35 N/mm² at 28 days. Higher than standard blocks because the wall is under continuous load.

For exposed locations (coastal, high rainfall), add 1% waterproofer. Reduces water absorption and freeze-thaw damage.

4 Lip and lug tooling

The lip is what makes the block interlock. It's built into the mould.

Design of the lip:

  • Position: rear top of the block, 20–30 mm from the back edge
  • Height: 15–20 mm
  • Width: full block width or two smaller lugs
  • Angle: matches the desired wall batter (2–5° backward lean)

The mould must include the lip in its top plate. This complicates the mould design — a standard block mould won't work.

Custom mould cost: $800–$2,500 for a 3-cavity retaining wall mould. Worth the investment — the blocks sell for 2–3× standard blocks.

5 Stacking for stability

Segmental walls are stacked with the lip of each block sitting behind the block below — the wall leans backward into the soil.

Stacking rules:

  • First course: laid on a compacted gravel base, fully level
  • Second course: blocks offset by half a block from the course below
  • Lip catches the top back of the block below — every course steps back slightly
  • Every 2–4 courses, add a geogrid layer (reinforcing mesh) that runs back into the soil
  • Cap the wall with a final row of capping blocks or a coping stone

The batter (backward lean) puts the wall's weight into the slope, increasing stability without needing heavy foundations for walls up to 1.5–2 m high.

6 Drainage behind walls

Water is the #1 cause of retaining wall failure. Waterlogged soil becomes heavy and pushes the wall over.

Drainage system behind every wall:

  • Gravel backfill: 300 mm of clean 20–40 mm gravel immediately behind the wall
  • Filter fabric: Between gravel and soil to prevent clogging
  • Drainage pipe: Perforated pipe at the base of the wall, running to a drainage point
  • Weep holes: Small gaps every 2 m in the wall for water to escape

Without drainage, water pressure builds up behind the wall. In heavy rain, an undrained wall can fail in hours.

⚠ Never skip drainage. A wall that fails in rain ruins reputation and creates liability.

7 Handling heavy blocks

Retaining wall blocks are heavy (20–25 kg each). Safe handling:

  • Two hands per block — no one-handed lifting
  • Lift with legs, not back
  • Deliver on pallets and use a forklift on-site if possible
  • For high walls, rent a small telehandler or skid-steer
  • Never carry a block up a ladder
  • Use gloves with grip

Block lifting injuries cost businesses thousands in claims. Train every worker.

8 Selling to landscapers

Retaining wall blocks are a premium landscaping product.

Who buys:

  • Landscape contractors (bulk)
  • Garden designers (specification)
  • Homeowners with sloped gardens (DIY)
  • Commercial site developers (roadside, car parks)
  • Municipal parks and public realm projects

Pricing (Africa, 2026):

  • Standard block: $2.50–$5.00 each
  • Premium (split face, coloured): $3.50–$8.00 each
  • Capping/coping block: $4.00–$9.00 each

Cost to make: $1.00–$1.80 per block. Margin: 55–70%.

Selling tools:

  • Design guide for common wall heights
  • Sample wall or panel
  • Photos of finished installations
  • Partner with a landscape contractor who installs and promotes your blocks
💰 Retaining wall blocks are a small-margin, high-value product. Best for yards with split-face capability and a landscaper network.
