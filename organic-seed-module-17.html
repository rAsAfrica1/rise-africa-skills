Seed Storage and Warehousing - Keeping Quality Alive

🧠 Brain Opener

Seed that stores well sells well. Seed that stores badly sells once and never again. Storage is where your quality either holds or dies.

  • What are the three enemies of stored seed?
  • What is hermetic storage and why does it work?
  • How long can you store vegetable seed?
🎁 Reveal a strong answer

The three enemies are moisture, heat, and pests. Hermetic storage is airtight - it stops moisture, stops insects from breathing, and stops fungus. Vegetable seed stored at 8 percent moisture and 15 degrees Celsius can keep germination for 3 to 5 years.

📖 Tendai Loses a Storage Season

Tendai had a good harvest - 60 kilograms of organic kale seed. He had cleaned it. He had dried it. He had tested it. Germination was 91 percent.

He stored it in sacks in the corner of his shed. The shed had a tin roof. It faced south.

He planned to sell the seed over the next three months as buyers came.

Three months later, he opened the first sack. The smell was wrong - musty, thick. He put 100 seeds on wet paper. Germination was 62 percent.

He opened the second sack. Germination was 48 percent.

By the third sack, germination was under 30 percent. Half his harvest was worthless.

His aunt, who had been storing seed for twenty years, came to see what had happened.

She put her hand inside the sack. It was warm.

"Your shed is a cooker," she said. "The tin roof heats up all day. The seed is at 30 degrees Celsius. At that temperature, germination drops 10 percent every month."

She showed him her own storage - a cool room at the back of her house, with ventilated shelves, in sealed plastic drums with a small packet of ash inside each one.

Her seed, harvested the same week as Tendai, had 89 percent germination after three months.

Tendai rebuilt. He moved his seed to a cool interior room. He bought five 20-litre sealed drums. He put a small cloth bag of wood ash inside each drum to absorb moisture.

His next harvest stored at 88 percent germination for six months.

The cost of the new storage: 40 dollars. The value of the seed saved: 1,200 dollars.

💡 The Big Idea

Storage is not passive - it is a decision

Every day of storage is a day your seed is being tested by moisture, heat, and pests. The three enemies work slowly, then suddenly. Cool, dry, sealed, dark storage holds germination for years. Warm, humid, open storage destroys it in months.

📚 Three Deep Lessons

1

The three enemies of stored seed

Moisture. Above 12 percent, fungus grows. Above 14 percent, seed heats up and rots. Every 1 percent above 8 percent cuts storage life by half. Moisture is the biggest enemy.

Heat. Every 5 degrees above 20 Celsius cuts storage life by half. A tin shed in the sun can reach 40 Celsius - that is 4 times faster degradation than a cool room.

Pests. Weevils, beetles, and moths lay eggs in stored seed. Their larvae eat the seed from inside. A single weevil can destroy 30 seeds in its lifetime.

2

The four-part storage solution

Cool location. Store on the south side of a building (shaded in the northern hemisphere) or in a basement or inner room. Target 15 to 20 Celsius.

Low humidity. Dry seed to 8 percent. Store with a desiccant - wood ash, dry rice, or silica gel. Change the desiccant every 3 months.

Sealed container. Hermetic drums, thick plastic bags sealed with heat, or glass jars. The container must be airtight. Oxygen and moisture must not enter.

Dark and stable. No sunlight. No temperature swings. Stable conditions are better than a cool room that warms and cools daily.

3

How long can you store different seeds

Vegetable seed (tomato, pepper, brassicas). 3 to 5 years in good storage. Pepper is shorter - 2 to 3 years.

Cereal seed (maize, sorghum, millet). 2 to 3 years. Maize loses vigour fastest - store no more than 18 months.

Legume seed (groundnut, cowpea, beans). 1 to 2 years. Groundnut has high oil content and goes rancid.

Tree and agroforestry seed. Highly variable. Some tree seed loses viability in weeks. Others keep for years. Check each species.

The rule: sell your seed within the safe window. Do not store it hoping for a higher price. The price rarely rises faster than germination falls.

🛠 Hands-On - Build a Seed Storage Trial

You need

  • 4 small bags of the same seed
  • 4 containers with different conditions
  • Wood ash or dry rice
  • A notebook
  1. Dry 100 grams of seed to 8 percent moisture.
  2. Divide into 4 equal bags of 25 grams each.
  3. Bag 1: paper bag, stored on a hot shelf.
  4. Bag 2: paper bag, stored in a cool room.
  5. Bag 3: sealed jar with wood ash, on a hot shelf.
  6. Bag 4: sealed jar with wood ash, in a cool room.
  7. Test germination on day 0, day 30, day 60, day 90.
  8. Write down the germination for each bag on each day.
  9. Compare the four after 90 days. The lesson is clear.
Why it works: This experiment shows you with your own eyes what different storage conditions do. You will never forget the result.

🌍 Real Stories from Africa

Norway seed vault

The Svalbard Global Seed Vault stores seed at minus 18 Celsius, sealed in foil packets, inside a mountain in Norway. It holds over 1 million seed samples. The principle is the same as a small African farmer with a sealed drum - cool, dry, dark, sealed.

Zimbabwe seed bank

A Zimbabwean community seed bank stores traditional maize and sorghum varieties in sealed drums with wood ash. Germination stays above 85 percent for 3 years. The cost: 15 dollars per drum. The alternative, refrigeration, costs 500 dollars.

🏆 Project Challenge

Set Up Proper Storage

Convert your current storage into a proper seed storage room or cabinet.

  1. Measure the temperature of your current storage at 6am, noon, and 6pm for one week.
  2. Find the coolest room or cabinet available to you.
  3. Buy or find 3 sealed containers - drums, glass jars, or thick plastic bags.
  4. Dry 500 grams of wood ash or rice as desiccant.
  5. Put a cloth bag of desiccant in each container.
  6. Move your seed into the sealed containers.
  7. Record the temperature and humidity every week for 3 months.
  8. Retest germination at 3 months.
