9. Advanced Breeding and Genetics

The right ram doubles your lamb crop value. Here is how to build a productive flock over 5-10 years.

Module 9 of 12 · Sheep Farming for Profit

Why genetics compound over time

Unlike feed or labour, genetics improvements compound year after year. A better ram this year improves every lamb born for the next 3-4 years. After 5 years of good selection, a flock can double its productivity per ewe.

The four selection criteria

  1. Weaning weight: The best single indicator of future growth. Weigh every lamb at weaning (8-12 weeks). Keep daughters of the heaviest.
  2. Mothering ability: Watch ewes at lambing. Cull any that abandon, reject, or smother lambs.
  3. Lambing ease: Cull ewes that need assistance.
  4. Parasite resistance: Use FAMACHA scores. Keep daughters of ewes that maintain low worm counts.

The selection index

Score each ewe and ram on a simple 100-point scale:

CriteriaPoints
Lambs weaned per year (twins = 30, singles = 15)30

Keep the top 30% of ewes for breeding. Sell the bottom 30% to the market.

Ram selection - the multiplier

One ram serves 25-40 ewes. His genetic contribution per year is 25-40 times more impactful than any single ewe. Invest heavily in the ram.

Inbreeding and line breeding

Inbreeding occurs when related animals mate. It causes:

  • Smaller, weaker lambs
  • Higher mortality
  • Reduced fertility
  • Genetic defects
  • Poor mothering

Rule: Never breed father-daughter, brother-sister, mother-son. Change your ram every 2-3 years. Source rams from unrelated flocks.

Replacement ewe selection

  1. Retain 20-30% of ewe lambs as replacements for culled ewes.
  2. Choose from twins or triplets. Prolific mothers produce prolific daughters.
  3. Weigh at weaning. Keep the heaviest 50%.
  4. Assess at 12 months. Sound structure, correct size, good body condition.
  5. Cull any that show problems - foot issues, poor mothering, chronic disease.

The genetics rule: Invest in the best ram you can afford every 2-3 years. Genetics improvements compound - they are the highest-return investment on a sheep farm.
