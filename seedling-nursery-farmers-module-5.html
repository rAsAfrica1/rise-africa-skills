5. Pest and Disease Control in the Nursery

Young seedlings are more vulnerable to pests and diseases than mature plants. One outbreak can wipe out a whole tray in 48 hours. This module teaches prevention, early detection, and safe treatment.

Module 5 of 7 · 6 lessons · ~50 minutes · 6 practical exercises · quiz at the end

Lesson 5.1 — Damping-off — the #1 killer of seedlings

Damping-off is a fungal disease that kills seedlings at or just after emergence. It is the single biggest cause of nursery losses worldwide.

Symptoms:

  • Seedlings collapse at the soil line
  • Stem base looks pinched, water-soaked, or dark
  • Whole tray dies in days
  • Often spreads in circular patches, starting from one spot

What causes it:

  • Overwatering. #1 cause. Constant wet media promotes fungus.
  • Poor drainage. Water sits in trays.
  • Contaminated media. Soil from a field with disease history.
  • Poor airflow. Stagnant air near soil surface.
  • Cool, wet weather. Fungus loves cold and damp.
  • Overcrowding. Seedlings touching each other spread disease.

Prevention (all four together):

  1. Use sterile media. Sterilise soil with heat, or use clean coir/compost blend.
  2. Water carefully. Never soak. Let media surface dry between waterings.
  3. Good airflow. Space trays apart, keep nursery ventilated.
  4. Good drainage. Trays must have holes. Benches must be mesh, not solid.

Treatment if it starts:

  • Remove and destroy affected seedlings immediately.
  • Reduce watering drastically.
  • Increase airflow.
  • Apply a fungicide if available (copper-based or biological).
  • Isolate affected tray from others.
Once damping-off starts, it spreads within 24 hours. Act immediately — remove affected cells, dry out the tray. Do not wait to see if it gets better. It will not.
Practical exercise 5.1: Sterilise 100 litres of soil media by baking or steaming. Compare germination and survival with an unsterilised batch. The difference will be obvious.

Lesson 5.2 — Common pests in the nursery

PestDamageSigns

WhitefliesSap-sucking, virus spreadSmall white flying insects when plant is disturbed

Early detection is everything:

  • Inspect daily. Walk the nursery every morning. Look at leaf undersides, stem bases, media surface.
  • Check at dawn. Many pests are active at night. Look before the sun is fully up.
  • Look for frass (droppings). Pests leave evidence.
  • Use yellow sticky traps. Cheap way to monitor flying insects.
Practical exercise 5.2: Buy 10 yellow sticky traps. Place around the nursery. Check daily for captured insects. This tells you what pests are active before they damage plants.

Lesson 5.3 — Common diseases and prevention

DiseaseSymptomsCausePrevention
Damping-offCollapse at soil lineFungus (Pythium, Rhizoctonia)Sterile media, less water
Root rotBrown, mushy roots, wiltingOverwatering, poor drainageImprove drainage, less water
Bacterial leaf spotDark spots with yellow halosBacteria from contaminated seedCertified seed, avoid wet foliage

Mosaic virusMottled leaves, distortionVirus spread by aphids/thripsControl insects, remove infected plants
Wilt (Fusarium, Verticillium)One-sided wilting, yellowingSoil fungus, contaminated mediaSterilise media, resistant varieties

Prevention is the only real strategy for disease in seedlings:

  • Sterilise media — heat to 70°C for 30 minutes, or use coir/compost blend.
  • Certified seed — never use seed from suspect sources.
  • Water on schedule — never overwater.
  • Airflow — space trays, ventilate.
  • Remove sick plants fast — one diseased plant ruins a tray.
  • Disinfect tools — between trays and batches.
The prevention rule: 95% of disease in a nursery is caused by poor conditions, not by pathogens arriving from outside. Get the environment right and disease is rare.
Practical exercise 5.3: Read the disease table once a week for the first 3 months. You will learn to identify early-stage problems before they become serious.

Lesson 5.4 — Integrated Pest Management (IPM)

IPM is a strategy that combines methods — not just one approach. It reduces pesticide use and produces healthier plants.

The four IPM layers:

  1. Prevention (cultural). Sterile media, correct spacing, proper watering, good airflow, resistant varieties. This alone solves most pest problems.
  2. Monitoring. Daily inspection, yellow sticky traps, note-taking. Find pests early before damage starts.
  3. Physical/mechanical control. Hand-remove insects, spray water to knock off aphids, use mesh screens, remove affected leaves. No chemicals.
  4. Biological control. Introduce natural predators — ladybugs for aphids, predatory mites for spider mites. Works in enclosed nurseries.
  5. Chemical control (last resort). Only when other methods fail. Use the safest products first (soap, neem, pyrethrin) before stronger chemicals.
The IPM rule: Start with cultural controls. Escalate only if needed. A nursery that jumps to strong chemicals creates pesticide-resistant pests and unsafe plants.
Practical exercise 5.4: Try the physical controls first for one month — hand-removal, water spray, mesh screens. If any pest problem remains, escalate slowly. Note results.

Lesson 5.5 — Safe pesticide use in the nursery

If you must use pesticides, use them safely. This protects you, your workers, your customers, and the environment.

TypeSafetyUse
Insecticidal soapVery safe, gentleAphids, whiteflies, thrips
Neem oilVery safe, organicBroad range of soft-bodied insects
PyrethrinSafe, short residualFast knockdown of many insects
Copper fungicideModerately safeBacterial and fungal diseases
Bacillus thuringiensis (Bt)Very safe, specificCaterpillars only
ImidaclopridStronger, systemicOnly when others fail
ChlorpyrifosHighly toxic — banned in many countriesDo not use

Safe pesticide rules:

  • Read the label. Every product. Every time.
  • Wear protection. Gloves, mask, long sleeves, eye protection.
  • Apply in the evening. Fewer pollinators, less evaporation.
  • Do not spray in wind. Pesticide drift damages neighbours.
  • Observe the pre-harvest interval. Do not sell plants for days after spraying.
  • Never mix chemicals. Combinations can become more toxic.
  • Store safely. Locked cabinet, away from food, water, and children.
Never spray seedlings the day before selling them. Customers will plant them in gardens and homes. Any chemical residue is a hazard. Stop all chemical sprays at least 7 days before sale.
Practical exercise 5.5: Buy one bottle of insecticidal soap and one of neem oil. Learn to use them. Avoid all stronger chemicals in your first year.

Lesson 5.6 — Disease prevention through hygiene

Hygiene is the cheapest disease prevention there is.

PracticeFrequency
Wash hands before handling seedlingsEvery time
Disinfect tools between batchesEvery batch
Remove dead or diseased seedlingsDaily
Clean bench surfaceWeekly
Replace media if disease appearsImmediately
Sterilise trays between usesEvery cycle
Quarantine new seed or plantsAlways

Tray sterilisation:

  1. Soak used trays in a 1% bleach solution for 10 minutes.
  2. Scrub with a brush to remove media residue.
  3. Rinse thoroughly with clean water.
  4. Dry in the sun.
  5. Store dry until next use.
The hygiene rule: Assume everything is contaminated until you clean it. Hands, tools, trays, benches, storage containers. Cleaning takes minutes — losing a batch of 5,000 seedlings costs weeks.
Practical exercise 5.6: Wash all your trays this week using the bleach method. Then use them for the next cycle. Compare results to a previous cycle where trays were just rinsed.
