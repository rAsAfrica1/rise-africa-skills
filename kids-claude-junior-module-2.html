Talking to Claude — Junior

Module 2 of 8: The Complaint Walk

No device needed

🧠 Brain Opener — 60 seconds

Before anything else

Walk down your street with a notebook. Every time someone sighs and says "ehh, this thing…" — write it down. Do this for 20 minutes. When you get home, count your list. You have just counted businesses.

📚 Lessons

1. Complaints are payment waiting to happen

"The borehole water tastes bad" is not just a complaint. It is a market. Somebody will pay to fix it. Somebody will pay to filter it. Somebody will pay to deliver clean water on a bicycle. The complaint is the map.

2. The 3-part drill

Round 1: hear a complaint. Write it down word for word. Round 2: rewrite it as a problem — "water tastes bad" → "families need clean drinking water". Round 3: rewrite it as a service — "I deliver 20 litres of filtered water for $1 a day".

3. Where it breaks

Some complaints cannot become a business: the weather, football results, other people's manners. Ask: "Could I actually change this for someone?" If no — it is only a complaint.

🎯 Cross-Skill Links

♟️
Chess

Every weakness on the board is a complaint. A hole in the pawn structure. A pinned piece. A king stuck in the centre. Find the complaint, find the attack.

🎹
Music & Piano

A sour note is a complaint. The musician's job is to resolve it — usually by stepping one note up or down. Business is the same: the complaint resolves into the service.

🀄
Chinese-Style Drill

抱怨 (complaint) and 服务 (service) share one meaning: something is not right, someone fixes it. Two characters, one business.

🤖 Real-Time AI-Era Skill

Ask Claude: "List 10 complaints common in a small town in Zimbabwe in 2026 — grocery, phone, laundry, transport, school runs." Rank them by "which would someone pay $1 to fix today?"

🎮 Game

Do it now

60-second complaint relay. Sit in a circle. Each person adds one complaint they have heard this week. No repeats. After 60 seconds, the group picks the top one and names the service that would fix it.
