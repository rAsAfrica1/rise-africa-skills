Refractory (Firebrick) Block

8 lessons · 30 quiz questions · Hard difficulty

1 What is refractory

"Refractory" means resistant to very high heat — above 1,000°C. Standard concrete breaks down at 400–600°C. Refractory materials handle 1,200–1,800°C.

Where refractory bricks are used:

  • Fireplace linings
  • Pizza ovens
  • Pottery and ceramic kilns
  • Furnaces (metal smelting, glass)
  • Boiler linings
  • Chimneys and flues
  • Barbecues (commercial)

Refractory bricks are not the same as fire-resistant blocks (Module 28). Fire-resistant = retards fire spread. Refractory = withstands direct contact with fire/flame at high temperature.

⚠ Refractory is a specialist product. High materials cost, technical know-how, and a niche market — not for a general block yard.

2 Fireclay vs high-alumina

Two main types of refractory material:

Fireclay (chamotte) refractories:

  • Made from fireclay (alumina-silicate)
  • Service temperature: 1,200–1,400°C
  • Cheaper
  • Standard for fireplaces, pizza ovens, small kilns
  • Cost: $300–$800 per tonne

High-alumina refractories:

  • Made from bauxite + fireclay
  • Service temperature: 1,500–1,800°C
  • Expensive
  • Standard for industrial furnaces, steel plants
  • Cost: $800–$3,000 per tonne

For small-scale firebrick production, use fireclay. It's affordable and works for 95% of applications.

3 Sourcing specialist cement

Refractory bricks use calcium aluminate cement (CAC) — not ordinary Portland cement.

Why CAC:

  • Withstands high temperature — doesn't dehydrate and lose strength like OPC
  • Sets fast, gains strength in 24 hours
  • Chemically resistant to many industrial environments

Where to source:

  • Specialty cement suppliers (Imerys, Kerneos, Almatis)
  • Industrial chemical traders
  • Refractory suppliers
  • Imported from Europe, China, India

Cost: $800–$1,800 per tonne. 5–10× the price of ordinary cement. This is the biggest cost in firebrick production.

💡 Substitutes like high-alumina cement exist but are still specialist. Never use ordinary Portland cement in firebricks — it fails within weeks at high temperature.

4 Mixing refractory concrete

Refractory mix by weight:

  • Calcium aluminate cement: 15–25%
  • Fireclay or crushed firebrick (chamotte): 60–75%
  • Fine alumina or fine fireclay: 10–15%
  • Water: 8–12% (very low — refractory must be water-poor)

The mix is dry and crumbly-looking — this is correct. It's compacted, not poured.

Mixing time: 5 minutes maximum. Longer mixing is not harmful but unnecessary. Do not add more water "to help flow" — extra water = more shrinkage = cracking at high temperature.

Strength at 24 hours: 20–30 N/mm². After firing to 1,000°C: 40–60 N/mm².

5 Casting firebricks

Firebricks are cast in steel or timber moulds, using vibration or tamping.

Standard brick size: 230 × 115 × 75 mm (UK/EU) or 230 × 114 × 64 mm (US).

Casting process:

  1. Pre-wet the mould (spray with water — prevents moisture loss from mix)
  2. Fill in two layers
  3. Vibrate each layer 3–5 seconds, OR tamp firmly 20 times per layer
  4. Level the top with a straight edge
  5. Cover with damp cloth or plastic
  6. Leave in mould 24 hours
  7. Demould carefully
  8. Air-cure for 7 days
  9. Slow-dry (heat gently to 100°C over 24 hours to remove remaining water)
  10. Ready for use

Casting cost: about $4–$8 per brick (mostly the CAC and chamotte).

6 Controlled curing

Refractory curing is different from standard concrete — you must remove all free water before high-temperature use.

Curing schedule:

  • Day 1: Leave in mould, covered, at 20–25°C
  • Days 2–7: Demould, air-cure in shade
  • Days 8–9: Slow-dry at 60°C (oven)
  • Days 10–11: Slow-dry at 110°C
  • Day 12: Ready to ship or use

Never put a wet firebrick into a hot furnace — the trapped water turns to steam and shatters the brick. Always pre-dry in a low oven.

This is why firebricks are usually shipped pre-cured by the producer, not raw like regular concrete.

7 Testing at high temperature

Standard firebrick tests:

Refractoriness (Pyrometric Cone Equivalent, PCE): A small cone-shaped sample is heated until it bends over. The temperature at which it bends indicates service rating.

Cold crushing strength: Compressive strength at room temperature, before firing. Should be 20–30 N/mm².

Hot modulus of rupture: Strength at high temperature (e.g. 1,200°C). Should be 5–15 N/mm² for good fireclay brick.

Thermal shock resistance: Cycles between hot and cold. Should survive 20+ cycles without cracking.

Test costs: $500–$3,000 per test. Only needed for industrial certification.

For most applications (fireplaces, pizza ovens, small kilns), simple visual + air-cure testing is enough.

8 Selling to kiln builders

Refractory bricks sell to a specific customer base:

Who buys:

  • Kiln builders (pottery, ceramics, brick factories)
  • Pizza oven makers
  • Blacksmiths and foundries
  • Bakery and commercial oven manufacturers
  • Furnace and boiler manufacturers
  • Glass works
  • Small metal smelters

Pricing (Africa, 2026):

  • Standard fireclay brick: $5–$12 each
  • Premium (higher alumina): $15–$30 each
  • Special shapes: $30–$80 each

Cost to make: $3–$6 per brick (mostly materials). Margin: 40–60%.

Selling tools:

  • Refractoriness certificate (PCE rating)
  • Sample bricks for customers to test
  • Custom shapes to match kiln designs
  • Relationship with kiln builders and potters
💰 Refractory is a low-volume, high-value specialist market. Best for a small producer with access to fireclay deposits, targeting pottery and small industrial kilns. Not for a general block yard.
