Battery and Power Systems - The Limits of Flight Time

🧠 Brain Opener

A quadcopter can only fly for 20 to 30 minutes on one battery. This is the single biggest limitation of drone work. Understanding batteries means understanding why every commercial drone operation is planned around them.

  • Why can a drone only fly 20 to 30 minutes?
  • What is a lithium-polymer battery?
  • What happens if you over-discharge a drone battery?
🎁 Reveal a good answer

A drone battery stores chemical energy. Every second of flight uses energy to spin four motors and power cameras. The energy available is limited by weight - a bigger battery weighs more and makes the drone work harder. Lithium-polymer batteries can hold a lot of energy for their weight, but they degrade if over-discharged or overcharged.

📖 Rudo Plans the Clinic Route

Rudo was sixteen and volunteered at a rural clinic in eastern Zimbabwe. The clinic was 40 kilometres from the nearest hospital. Blood samples had to be driven there every morning - a two-hour trip each way.

She heard that some clinics in Rwanda were using drones to fly samples instead. She wanted to try it at her clinic.

She wrote down the numbers. Distance: 40 kilometres. Drone speed: 15 metres per second. Battery life: 25 minutes. So the round trip would take 89 minutes of flight time. Impossible on one battery.

She rethought. What if the drone only flew one way? The clinic could buy two drones. One at each end. Each drone flies one way, lands, battery swapped, flies back.

That cut flight time per drone to 45 minutes. Still not enough.

She looked at the specs again. Fixed-wing drones fly further. A small fixed-wing could do 40 kilometres in 35 minutes. Still not enough for a round trip, but enough for a one-way trip with a spare battery landing pad at the hospital.

So the plan became: clinic drone flies east with samples. Hospital drone flies west with medicine. They meet in the middle, swap cargo, and return. Each drone flies 20 kilometres. 18 minutes. Comfortably within battery life.

Rudo wrote the plan into a one-page report and sent it to a drone delivery company in Harare. They replied three weeks later, interested.

Her clinic did not get a drone program that year. But two years later, they did. Rudo was one of the consultants.

💡 The Big Idea

Battery life decides the mission

Every drone mission is designed around battery life. A drone that flies 25 minutes cannot do a 60-minute mission. A drone that flies 25 minutes can do a 20-minute mission with 5 minutes of safety margin. Before you plan anything, find out the flight time. Then design backwards from there.

📚 Three Deep Lessons

1

What is inside a drone battery

Almost all drone batteries are lithium-polymer (LiPo). They store energy in chemical cells. Each cell produces 3.7 volts. Drone batteries usually have 3, 4, or 6 cells in series - giving 11.1, 14.8, or 22.2 volts. More cells mean more power, but also more weight.

A LiPo battery can deliver high current very quickly - which is exactly what four spinning motors need. But LiPo batteries are also delicate. They do not like being fully drained. They do not like being stored fully charged for weeks. They do not like being left in the sun.

2

The three rules of LiPo batteries

Rule one: never drain below 3.3 volts per cell. If a 4-cell battery drops below 13.2 volts total, the cells are damaged. The drone should land when the battery hits 3.5 volts per cell, giving a safety margin.

Rule two: store at half charge. A fully charged LiPo left on a shelf for a month loses capacity permanently. Store at 3.8 volts per cell - about 50 percent. This is called "storage charge."

Rule three: never puncture or overheat. A swollen or hot LiPo can catch fire. Lithium fires cannot be put out with water. If a battery is puffy, damaged, or hot, retire it and take it to a proper disposal point.

3

Why batteries limit flight time

A bigger battery would fly longer - but it also weighs more. The heavier the drone, the more energy each motor uses. There is a point where adding battery makes the drone fly shorter, not longer. This is called the point of diminishing returns.

Real drone engineers calculate the ideal battery size for their mission. Sometimes the answer is a smaller drone with two batteries and a mid-flight landing. Sometimes it is a fixed-wing design that uses less energy per kilometre. The engineering is about matching battery to mission.

🛠 Hands-On - Map a Drone Mission on Battery Life

You need

  • A notebook
  • A map of your area (or a drawing)
  • A calculator (or a phone)
  1. Pick a target - a farm, a school, a clinic - that you want to fly to.
  2. Measure or estimate the distance from your launch point.
  3. Look up the flight time of a drone you can access (or use 25 minutes as standard).
  4. Calculate how far the drone can fly at 15 metres per second in 20 minutes (leaving 5 min safety).
  5. Write down whether the round trip is possible in one battery.
  6. If not, redesign: mid-point landing, spare battery, or one-way trip with a return drone.
  7. Write your final plan in 5 sentences.
Why it works: Every commercial drone company plans missions this way. Battery first, everything else second.

🎮 Game - Battery Math Race

2 to 10 players - 15 min

Each player is given a mission distance and a drone spec. On paper, calculate: will this mission succeed in one battery, or does it need a landing point? Fastest correct answer wins. Repeat with new numbers. Best three out of five wins.

🤖 AI-Era Skill

AI can help you calculate mission feasibility. It can also make mistakes - always check the maths.

Try this prompt: "A drone flies at 15 metres per second and has a 25-minute battery. I want to fly from point A to point B, 12 kilometres away, and return. Can I do this on one battery? Walk me through the calculation."

Do the maths yourself. 12 km at 15 m/s = 800 seconds = 13.3 minutes one way. Round trip = 26.6 minutes. That exceeds 25 minutes. The answer should be no, unless a landing point exists in the middle.

🌍 Real Stories from Africa

Zipline Rwanda batteries

Zipline drones use lithium-polymer batteries and a fixed-wing design. Their range is about 160 kilometres round trip. They achieve this by flying efficiently, not by carrying huge batteries. The mission is designed around the battery, not the other way around.

Kenyan coffee farm mapping

A Kenyan coffee farm uses quadcopters to map 200 hectares. The drone battery lasts 22 minutes. The pilot flies four separate 20-minute missions with a spare battery. Over the course of a day, the whole farm is mapped with four battery cycles and one drone.
