9. Marketing, Selling and Value-Adding

Horses are sold, not bought. Presentation, reputation and timing decide your price.

Module 9 of 12 · Horse Breeding for Profit

Why horse marketing is different

Horses are a high-value, low-volume sale. You might sell 5-20 horses per year, not hundreds. Each sale matters. Buyers are emotional and pay for pedigree, performance, and trust. Marketing is personal, reputation-driven, and relationship-based.

Presenting a horse for sale

  1. Condition: body score 3-4, shiny coat, good feet, trimmed, well-fed.
  2. Clean: groomed, hooves painted (optional), mane and tail tidy, no mud.
  3. Tack: clean, well-fitted, appropriate for the discipline.
  4. Presentation: show the horse at walk, trot, canter (ridden or in-hand).
  5. X-rays / vet records: if you have them, share them.
  6. Videos: 3-5 minutes showing all gaits, jumping, hacking.
  7. Photos: conformation shots (side, front, hind), ridden, and action.
  8. Vet check: be ready for the buyer's vet to inspect.

Pricing a horse for sale

StepWhat to do
1. ResearchCheck prices of similar horses in your region
2. Cost-based floorWhat you have invested (feed, training, vet, farrier)
3. Market-based rangeWhat buyers are paying for this type
4. Ask priceStart at the top of the range - you can always come down
5. Floor priceDecide below which you will not sell

Sales contract essentials

  • Horse identification (name, age, breed, markings, microchip or brand).
  • Purchase price and payment terms.
  • Delivery / collection date.
  • Veterinary examination clause.
  • Warranty on soundness (or sold "as seen").
  • Trial period (if offered) - max 14 days.
  • Registration papers transfer (if registered).
  • Return policy - usually "sold as seen" unless a vet issue is found.
  • Signatures of seller, buyer, witness.

Building reputation

In the horse world, reputation is everything. Buyers talk. Good breeders are known for:

  • Honesty - accurately describing horses, disclosing vices.
  • Consistent quality - a type of horse they are known for.
  • After-sales support - helping buyers, offering advice.
  • Reliability - on time, contactable, keeps records.
  • Performance - their horses winning or working well.
  • Welfare - horses well-cared for, not rushed through systems.

Online marketing

  • Professional photos: good light, clean background, all four sides plus action shots.
  • Videos: 3-5 minutes, clear and stable footage. Walk, trot, canter, jump (if applicable).
  • Description: honest, detailed, includes age, height, training, temperament, vet history.
  • Platforms: Facebook groups, horse-specific websites, WhatsApp groups, YouTube.
  • Response: answer messages quickly and honestly. Bad communication loses sales.

After-sales follow-up

Follow up with every buyer after 30 and 90 days:

  • How is the horse settling in?
  • Any issues we can help with?
  • Offer advice on feeding, training, or veterinary care.
  • Ask if they would recommend you to friends.

Repeat buyers and referrals become 60-80% of your business over time.

The reputation rule: In horses, your name is worth more than any single horse. One dishonest sale destroys the reputation it took 10 years to build. Disclose everything, stand behind your horses, and never oversell.
Practical exercise 9: Prepare a sales listing for one of your horses. Include professional photos, video, and an honest description. Set an asking price based on research. Post it and follow up with interested buyers.
