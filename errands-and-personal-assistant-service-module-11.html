1. Pet Care and Dog Walking Errands

Pet care errands include dog walking, feeding, grooming runs, and vet visits. In many African cities, busy professionals own dogs but lack time for midday walks. Charge per walk: in Nairobi, KES 500800 for a 30-minute walk; in Lagos, NGN 3,0005,000. For feeding visits, charge KES 1,000 per visit. Always carry a leash, waste bags, and fresh water. Build a route with 34 dogs in the same neighborhood to maximize earnings. For vet errands, confirm appointment times, transport the pet safely in a crate or with a pet taxi, and keep a record of vaccinations. Use WhatsApp to send photo updates to owners after each walk. Insurance is rare, so create a simple liability waiver. Start by offering free trial walks to friends' pets to get testimonials. Then advertise on local Facebook groups, vet clinics, and apartment notice boards. Key safety: never walk more than two dogs at once unless trained; avoid hot pavement during midday; and learn basic first aid for pets. For overnight pet sitting, charge a daily rate (KES 2,5004,000). Keep a pet first-aid kit with bandages, antiseptic, and tick remover. In Africa, be aware of ticks and snakes in some areas. Always ask owners about allergies, medications, and emergency vet contacts. Reliable pet care builds loyal clients who travel frequently, giving you recurring income.

2. Elderly Care Errands and Companion Services

Elderly care errands involve grocery shopping, pharmacy pickups, bill payments, and accompanying seniors to medical appointments. In many African communities, extended family may live far away, so paid companions fill a gap. Charge hourly: KES 400600 in Kenya, NGN 2,5004,000 in Nigeria. Services include reading mail, writing letters, and helping with mobile banking. For medical visits, arrive 30 minutes early, bring a list of medications, and take notes for family. Build trust by being punctual, patient, and respectful. Learn basic signs of stroke, dehydration, and low blood sugar. Keep an emergency contact card. For companionship, play cards, take walks, or assist with video calls to relatives. Always get permission before moving furniture or handling finances. A simple contract outlining tasks, hours, and payment terms is essential. Advertise through churches, mosques, community centers, and retirement homes. Many clients prefer a weekly schedule: e.g., Monday and Thursday, 9am1pm. Use a notebook to log each visit: what was done, what was purchased, and any concerns. Payment can be cash or mobile money (M-Pesa, Airtel Money). Avoid handling large sumsuse receipts. For grocery runs, ask for a list and a budget cap. In some cultures, it's polite to remove shoes and accept a cup of tea. Build a reputation for honesty and kindness; word-of-mouth referrals are powerful. Consider taking a short first-aid course. This service is rewarding and in high demand as populations age.

3. Child-Related Errands: School Pickups, Activities

Child-related errands include school pickups, dropping kids at music lessons or sports, buying school supplies, and waiting during extracurriculars. Parents pay well for reliability. In South Africa, charge R80R150 per trip; in Kenya, KES 500800. You must be vetted: provide a police clearance certificate (costs about KES 1,000 in Kenya, R150 in South Africa). Always carry your ID and a letter from the parent. For school pickup, arrive 15 minutes early, park legally, and wait at the designated gate. Confirm the child's full name, class, and teacher. Never let a child go with anyone else without written permission. For activities, keep a schedule with times, addresses, and instructor contacts. Use a checklist: water bottle, snack, sports kit, homework folder. After pickup, send a WhatsApp message to the parent: 'Picked up Aisha at 3:15pm, heading to ballet.' For supply runs, use a shopping list app like Google Keep. Buy exact itemsbrands matter. Keep receipts. For waiting during lessons, use the time to run nearby errands or read. Charge extra for waiting beyond 30 minutes. Safety: never leave a child unattended in a car; use child locks; and avoid discussing family matters with strangers. Build a portfolio of 35 families for steady weekday income. Advertise at school gates (with permission), parenting groups on Facebook, and local libraries. In some areas, you may need a background check from a recognized agency. Always have a backup plan if a child is sick or school closes early. This service requires patience, punctuality, and a genuine love for children.

4. Corporate Errand Services for Businesses

Corporate errands serve small and medium businesses: bank deposits, document delivery, office supply restocking, lunch runs, and queue-standing at government offices. Charge per errand or a monthly retainer. In Lagos, a bank deposit run costs NGN 5,00010,000; a monthly retainer for 2 hours daily is NGN 80,000150,000. In Nairobi, similar services range KES 1,5003,000 per errand. You need a professional look: clean shirt, closed shoes, and a folder for documents. Always get a signed receipt for documents delivered. For bank deposits, count cash twice, use a deposit slip, and get a stamped acknowledgment. For government queues (e.g., KRA, NHIF, licensing), arrive by 6am, carry a book and power bank, and update the client via WhatsApp every hour. For office supplies, use a shared spreadsheet to track inventory and reorder points. For lunch runs, collect orders by 10am, use a food delivery app or call ahead, and deliver by 12:30pm. Keep a cooler bag for hot food. Invoicing: use a simple template with your business name, date, description, and amount. Payment via bank transfer or mobile money. Build relationships with receptionists and security guardsthey can speed up access. Never sign for anything you don't understand. For confidential documents, use a sealed envelope and hand-deliver only to the named recipient. Consider a non-disclosure agreement for sensitive work. Advertise by visiting offices with a one-page flyer listing services and rates. Offer a free trial errand for the first client. Reliability and discretion are your selling points. Many corporates prefer a single trusted runner over multiple gig workers.

5. Concierge Services for Hotels and Events

Concierge errands for hotels and events include airport pickups, restaurant reservations, ticket purchases, gift shopping, and last-minute supplies. Hotels often outsource to freelance concierges. Charge per task: airport pickup $20$50 in major cities; restaurant booking $10; gift shopping $15 plus cost. For events, you might handle guest check-in, luggage storage, or vendor runs. Build a contact list of reliable taxi drivers, florists, and caterers. For airport pickups, track flight numbers, arrive 30 minutes early, and hold a sign with the guest's name. Use a car with working AC and a phone charger. For restaurant reservations, call ahead, confirm dietary needs, and send confirmation to the guest. For gift shopping, ask for budget and preferences, send photos of options, and get approval before buying. Keep receipts and a small float for emergencies. In hotels, dress smartly (black trousers, white shirt) and never enter guest rooms without permission. For events, create a checklist: name tags, pens, water, extension cords, and backup power. Charge an hourly rate for event days (KES 5001,000 per hour). Use a WhatsApp group with the event planner for real-time updates. Payment: 50% deposit, 50% on completion. Advertise by dropping business cards at hotel front desks, event venues, and wedding planners. Offer a 10% commission for referrals. Important: never share guest information. Sign a confidentiality agreement. For hotel concierge, learn basic local attractions and transport options. This niche pays well if you are polished, discreet, and fast. Start with small guesthouses and build up to 5-star hotels.

6. Seasonal and Holiday Errand Planning

Seasonal and holiday errands spike during Christmas, Ramadan, Easter, and school holidays. Plan ahead to maximize income. In December, demand for gift shopping, party supplies, and airport runs doubles. Charge 2030% more during peak weeks. For Ramadan, errands include food deliveries at sunset, prayer mat purchases, and charity distributions. For Easter, focus on church events and family gatherings. For school holidays, offer childcare errands and activity drop-offs. Create a holiday calendar: list key dates (e.g., Dec 15Jan 5, Ramadan dates, Easter week). Two weeks before, send a WhatsApp broadcast to past clients: 'Booking now for holiday errandslimited slots.' Offer packages: e.g., 'Christmas Shopping Package: 5 errands for KES 5,000.' For gift shopping, use a shared spreadsheet with names, budgets, and ideas. Buy in bulk from markets (e.g., Gikomba in Nairobi, Balogun in Lagos) for better prices. For food deliveries, partner with a caterer or restaurant. For airport runs, book a reliable driver in advance. Keep a cooler and thermal bags for perishables. For charity distributions, coordinate with local NGOs and get receipts. Safety: avoid carrying large cash; use mobile money. Keep a backup of your phone. During holidays, traffic is heavyadd 30 minutes to travel time. Charge a surcharge for late-night errands. After the season, send thank-you notes and ask for testimonials. Save 30% of peak earnings for the slow months (JanuaryFebruary). Advertise on community WhatsApp groups and church bulletins. This planning turns seasonal chaos into reliable profit.

7. Practical Reference

Practical Reference: Specialized Errand Services

Specialized errands are where an ordinary runner becomes a trusted, paid professional. In most African cities Lagos, Nairobi, Accra, Johannesburg, Kampala, Kigali the demand for pet care, elderly companionship, child logistics, corporate dispatch and concierge support is rising faster than the supply of reliable, vetted providers. This reference module gives you the operational standards, pricing logic and risk controls you need to run these services as a business, not a favour. Treat every lesson here as a service line you can quote, schedule and invoice.

1. Pet Care and Dog Walking Errands

Pet ownership is growing among urban African professionals who travel and work long hours. Your service includes feeding, walking, kennel drop-offs, vet runs and medication. Charge per visit or per walk, with a premium for public holidays. Always confirm the animal's vaccination records and temperament before accepting a booking. Keep a written incident log bites, escapes and illnesses must be documented the same day.

2. Elderly Care Errands and Companion Services

This is a high-trust, recurring revenue line. Typical tasks: grocery runs, pharmacy pickups, hospital accompaniment, bill payments, and companionship visits. Never present yourself as a medical professional. Your role is logistical and social. Build a family contact sheet, agree on emergency escalation steps, and log every visit with a timestamp and note. In many African households, elders live with extended family, so coordinate with a named family member rather than acting alone.

3. Child-Related Errands: School Pickups and Activities

School pickup is the most sensitive errand you will ever run. Requirements: verified identity, a signed authorisation letter from the parent, the school's approved pickup list, and a photo check-in at collection. Never hand a child to an unauthorised person. Use a two-adult rule where possible and keep a live location share with the parent. Charge per trip; offer a monthly retainer for daily school runs.

4. Corporate Errand Services for Businesses

Companies buy reliability, not favours. Offer document dispatch, bank runs, procurement pickup, visa and immigration errands, and meeting-room setup. Price on a retainer plus per-trip rate. Provide a monthly SLA: response time, delivery window, and a named account manager. Corporate clients pay on invoice, so register your business, issue proper invoices and keep records for tax.

5. Concierge Services for Hotels and Events

Hotels, short-let apartments and event planners need last-minute errands: SIM cards, currency exchange runs, laundry, gift purchases, airport pickups and guest requests. Negotiate a standing agreement with a fixed commission or hourly rate. Discretion is the product never disclose guest details. Keep a 24-hour on-call rota during peak seasons.

6. Seasonal and Holiday Errand Planning

Demand spikes around Christmas, Eid, Easter, school holidays and end-of-year corporate closures. Plan capacity two months ahead, pre-book transport, and publish a holiday surcharge schedule. Use the quiet weeks to train, restock and renew client contracts.

SAFETY WARNING Child, Elderly and Pet Errands

Never transport a child without written parental authorisation and school confirmation. Never administer medication to an elder or pet unless instructed in writing by the owner or clinician. Never enter a client's home alone for the first time without a verified reference and a check-in call. Any suspected abuse, neglect or medical emergency must be escalated immediately to the family contact and, where required, to local authorities. Document everything the same day.

Quick Reference Checklist

  • Confirm the client's identity, address and emergency contact before the first errand.
  • Get written authorisation for child pickups and written instructions for elder or pet medication.
  • Quote a fixed price in writing before starting; state what is excluded.
  • Share live location or check in at start, midpoint and completion.
  • Take a dated photo or signed docket as proof of delivery.
  • Log every incident bite, fall, delay, refusal the same day.
  • Invoice corporate clients monthly; keep records for tax and dispute resolution.
  • Review your holiday capacity and surcharge schedule two months in advance.
