1. Identifying Clutch Plate, Pressure Plate, and Release Bearing

When you open a manual gearbox clutch housing, you will see three main parts: the clutch plate (also called friction plate), the pressure plate, and the release bearing (throw-out bearing). The clutch plate is a round disc with a hole in the centre and friction material on both sides. It has a splined hub that slides onto the gearbox input shaft. A new clutch plate for a Toyota Hilux measures about 225mm in diameter and 8mm thick. The pressure plate is a heavy metal cover with a diaphragm spring inside. It bolts to the flywheel with 6 to 8 bolts, usually 12mm or 14mm heads. The release bearing is a round bearing that slides on the gearbox input shaft sleeve. It has a smooth face that presses against the pressure plate fingers. In Africa, you will often see worn clutch plates with rivets exposed. If the friction material is less than 1mm above the rivets, replace it. The pressure plate fingers should be flat; if they are worn or bent, replace the pressure plate. The release bearing should spin smoothly without noise. Spin it by hand; if it grinds or feels rough, replace it. Always replace all three parts together as a kit. A clutch kit for a Nissan Hardbody costs around 1500 to 2500 rand in South Africa or 8000 to 12000 naira in Nigeria. Buying separate parts is false economy because a worn release bearing will quickly damage a new pressure plate. When identifying parts, check for part numbers stamped on the pressure plate and clutch plate. Take a photo with your phone before removal. Clean the parts with brake cleaner, not petrol, because petrol leaves oil residue that causes slipping. Store new clutch parts flat and dry. Never touch the friction surface with oily hands. In many African workshops, you can find reconditioned clutch plates, but they are risky. A reconditioned plate may have new friction material glued on, but the hub springs may be weak. For a daily driver, buy new. For a farm truck that does low speed work, a reconditioned kit might last, but check the rivets and springs carefully.

2. Removing and Replacing a Clutch on a Manual Transmission

To remove a clutch, first disconnect the battery negative terminal. Raise the vehicle on jack stands and remove the gearbox drain plug to drain oil into a clean pan. You will need a 10mm, 12mm, 14mm, and 17mm socket set, a breaker bar, a universal joint, extensions, and a clutch alignment tool. On a rear-wheel-drive pickup like a Toyota Hilux, remove the propeller shaft bolts (usually 14mm) and tie the shaft to the side. Disconnect the speedometer cable, reverse light switch wires, and clutch slave cylinder (two 12mm bolts). Support the gearbox with a trolley jack. Remove the crossmember bolts (17mm) and lower the gearbox slightly. Remove the bellhousing bolts (14mm or 17mm). Pull the gearbox back until the input shaft clears the clutch. It is heavyâget a helper. With the gearbox out, remove the pressure plate bolts in a star pattern, one turn at a time, to avoid warping the flywheel. The clutch plate and pressure plate will come off. Inspect the flywheel face for cracks, deep grooves, or hot spots. If the flywheel is bad, have it machined at a machine shop for about 300 to 500 rand. Replace the release bearing and pilot bearing (if fitted). Clean the bellhousing and input shaft splines. Apply a thin smear of high-temperature grease on the input shaft splines and release bearing sleeveânot too much. Install the new clutch plate and pressure plate using the alignment tool. Tighten pressure plate bolts to 25-30 Nm in a star pattern. Install the release bearing on the fork. Lift the gearbox back in, align the input shaft with the clutch splines, and push forward. Install bellhousing bolts and tighten to 50-60 Nm. Reconnect everything, fill gearbox with 75W-90 oil (usually 2.5 to 3 litres), and bleed the clutch slave cylinder. Test the pedal: it should have about 25mm free play. Start the engine and check for noise. A properly installed clutch should last 80,000 to 120,000 km. In dusty African conditions, avoid riding the clutch in trafficâit wears quickly.

3. Checking Gearbox Oil and Replacing Seals

Gearbox oil lubricates gears and bearings. Check it every 10,000 km or before a long trip. Park on level ground. Find the filler plug on the side of the gearboxâusually a 17mm or 24mm hex plug. Clean around it with a wire brush. Remove the filler plug. If oil drips out, the level is fine. If not, stick your little finger in the hole; the oil should be at the bottom of the hole. If low, add 75W-90 or 80W-90 gear oil (GL-4 or GL-5) using a hand pump. Do not overfill. For a Toyota Hilux, the gearbox takes about 2.6 litres. For a Nissan Hardbody, about 3.0 litres. If the oil is milky or black, drain it by removing the drain plug at the bottom. Let it drain fully, then refit the drain plug with a new washer. Fill with new oil until it runs out of the filler hole. Refit the filler plug. Seals leak over time. The common leaks are the input shaft seal (behind the release bearing), output shaft seal (at the propeller shaft), and selector shaft seal. To replace the output seal, remove the propeller shaft, use a seal puller or pry bar to remove the old seal, clean the housing, and tap in a new seal with a socket. For the input shaft seal, you must remove the gearbox and clutch. Use a seal puller. Lubricate the new seal lip with gear oil before installation. In Africa, dust and heat make seals hard and brittle. Replace seals every time you have the gearbox out. A seal kit for a Hilux costs about 200 to 400 rand. If you see oil dripping under the bellhousing, it is likely the input shaft seal or the rear main seal of the engine. Check the oil smell: gear oil smells like sulphur; engine oil smells different. Always use new gaskets and sealant on the drain and filler plugs. Never use silicone on the seal lip. After replacing seals, top up the oil and check for leaks after a short drive. A dry gearbox runs cool and lasts longer.

4. Diagnosing Clutch Slipping and Juddering

Clutch slipping means the engine revs but the vehicle does not accelerate properly. You will notice it most in 4th or 5th gear when climbing a hill or accelerating hard. To test, drive at 30 km/h in 3rd gear, then press the accelerator hard. If the engine rpm rises quickly but the speed does not increase, the clutch is slipping. Another test: engage handbrake, put gearbox in 3rd gear, and slowly release the clutch while pressing the accelerator. If the engine does not stall, the clutch is slipping. Common causes: worn friction material, oil on the clutch plate from a leaking rear main seal or gearbox input shaft seal, or a faulty pressure plate. If oil is the cause, you must fix the leak and replace the clutch plate. A slipping clutch will eventually fail completely. Do not delay repair. Juddering (shaking) when you release the clutch is another problem. It feels like the vehicle hops or vibrates. Causes include: worn or warped flywheel, loose engine mounts, worn clutch plate hub springs, or a bent release fork. Check engine mounts by prying with a barâthey should not move much. Check the flywheel for hot spots or cracks. If the flywheel is warped, it must be machined or replaced. A worn clutch plate with broken springs will cause judder. Also, if the release bearing is not sliding smoothly on the input shaft sleeve, it can cause judder. In Africa, dusty roads accelerate wear. Always use genuine or good quality clutch kits. A cheap clutch may slip within 10,000 km. When diagnosing, ask the driver about driving habits: riding the clutch, resting foot on the pedal, or holding the vehicle on a hill with the clutch instead of the handbrake. These habits cause premature wear. If the clutch pedal feels spongy or has no free play, the clutch may not fully engage, causing slipping. Adjust the clutch cable or bleed the hydraulic system. For hydraulic clutches, check the master and slave cylinders for leaks. A leaking slave cylinder will lose fluid and cause poor clutch operation. Fix the root cause, not just the symptom.

5. Understanding Automatic Transmission Basics

An automatic transmission (autobox) shifts gears without a clutch pedal. It uses a torque converter, planetary gearsets, clutches, and bands. The torque converter is a fluid coupling that connects the engine to the gearbox. It has a turbine, impeller, and stator. The impeller spins with the engine, throwing transmission fluid onto the turbine, which turns the gearbox input shaft. The stator multiplies torque at low speeds. Automatic transmission fluid (ATF) is the lifeblood. Check the ATF level with the engine running in Park or Neutral, on level ground. The dipstick has marks for cold and hot. Use only the specified ATF typeâusually Dexron III or Toyota Type T-IV. Never mix types. For a Toyota Hilux automatic, the gearbox takes about 7 to 8 litres. If the fluid is dark brown or smells burnt, change it. A simple drain and refill replaces only 3 to 4 litres, so repeat three times with a short drive between to exchange most fluid. Common problems: slipping, harsh shifting, or no movement. Slipping in an automatic often means low fluid, worn clutches, or a faulty solenoid. Harsh shifting can be caused by a bad vacuum modulator or dirty valve body. In Africa, many automatic gearboxes fail due to overheating in traffic and lack of fluid changes. If the gearbox has a cooler, check for leaks. If you are not trained, do not open an automatic gearboxâit is precise and clean work. For basic maintenance, you can change the fluid and filter (if accessible). The filter is usually inside the pan; remove the pan (10mm bolts), replace the filter, and refit with a new gasket. Torque pan bolts to 8-10 Nm. If the vehicle has a CVT (continuously variable transmission), use only CVT fluid. Never tow a heavy load with a slipping automatic. If the transmission has a limp mode (stuck in one gear), scan for codes with an OBD2 scanner. Common codes: P0730 (incorrect gear ratio), P0740 (torque converter clutch circuit). Many African mechanics avoid automatics, but with basic care they can last 200,000 km. Learn to check fluid and recognize symptoms. If in doubt, refer to a specialist.

6. Sourcing Reconditioned Gearboxes

A reconditioned gearbox (recon box) is a used gearbox that has been stripped, cleaned, and fitted with new bearings, seals, and synchros. In Africa, recon boxes are common because new gearboxes are expensive. A new Toyota Hilux gearbox costs 25,000 to 40,000 rand; a recon unit costs 8,000 to 15,000 rand. But quality varies. When sourcing a recon gearbox, buy from a reputable shop that gives a warrantyâat least 6 months. Ask for the rebuild invoice or tag. Check the gearbox visually: clean casing, new seals, no cracks, and all sensors and switches present. Turn the input shaft by hand; it should turn smoothly with no grinding. Check the output shaft for playâthere should be almost none. If you can, remove the top cover and inspect the gears for chipped teeth or worn synchro rings. A good recon box will have new synchro rings, bearings, and seals. Avoid buying from a scrapyard without a warranty. In many African markets, you can find imported used gearboxes from Japan (JDM) or Europe. These may be low mileage but check for rust and accident damage. Ask for the mileage of the donor vehicle if possible. For a recon box, ensure it matches your engine and vehicle model. Gear ratios differ between petrol and diesel, and between 2WD and 4WD. Take your old gearbox as a sample. Check the bellhousing pattern, input shaft spline count, and output shaft flange. When installing, replace the clutch and release bearing at the same time. Fill with correct oil. Test drive all gears, including reverse. Listen for whining in 1st, 2nd, and 3rdâworn gears whine. A good recon box should be quiet. In West Africa, you can order from Lagos or Accra; in East Africa, from Nairobi or Kampala. Always pay after inspection if possible. If buying online, use a trusted platform with buyer protection. Remember: a cheap recon box may last only a few months. Spend a bit more for quality. Also consider rebuilding your own gearbox if you have the skills and tools. A rebuild kit costs 2,000 to 4,000 rand and includes bearings, seals, and synchros. That way you know exactly what you have.

Practical Reference: Transmission and Clutch Assembly

1. Identifying Clutch Plate, Pressure Plate, and Release Bearing

Before ordering parts, confirm the exact assembly. A manual clutch consists of three core components:

  • Clutch disc (friction plate): The driven plate splined to the gearbox input shaft. Check friction material thickness, rivet depth, and torsional damper springs.
  • Pressure plate (cover assembly): Bolts to the flywheel and applies clamping force via a diaphragm spring or coil springs.
  • Release bearing (throw-out bearing): Slides on the input shaft guide tube and disengages the pressure plate when the pedal is pressed.

In African markets, counterfeit and mismatched kits are common. Always match the disc diameter, spline count, and pressure plate bolt pattern to the vehicle's VIN or engine code. For popular models such as Toyota Hilux, Nissan NP200, and Isuzu KB, kits are widely available, but cheap imports often use inferior friction material that fails within 10,000â20,000 km on dusty or hilly routes.

2. Removing and Replacing a Clutch on a Manual Transmission

Book this job at 4â6 hours for a rear-wheel-drive pickup and 6â9 hours for a front-wheel-drive car. On African roads, seized and corroded fasteners are the biggest time risk â apply penetrating oil the day before.

  1. Disconnect the battery and raise the vehicle safely on stands or a hoist.
  2. Remove the propeller shaft (RWD) or drive shafts (FWD), then support the gearbox with a jack.
  3. Unbolt the bellhousing and slide the gearbox back. Inspect the input shaft splines and guide tube.
  4. Mark the pressure plate to flywheel alignment, then loosen bolts in a criss-cross pattern.
  5. Remove the pressure plate and clutch disc. Inspect the flywheel face for heat cracks and scoring; machine or replace if needed.
  6. Clean the bellhousing, lubricate the input shaft splines sparingly, and fit the new release bearing and pilot bearing.
  7. Use a clutch alignment tool, torque the pressure plate to manufacturer spec, and refit the gearbox.
  8. Bleed the hydraulic release system and adjust cable free play if applicable. Road-test through all gears.

Business note: Always quote for a complete kit (disc, pressure plate, release bearing, pilot bearing, alignment tool) rather than individual parts. This reduces comebacks and increases average job value by 20â35%.

5. Understanding Automatic Transmission Basics

Automatic transmissions are growing in African markets, especially in urban fleets, ride-hailing, and luxury imports. Key components include the torque converter, planetary gear sets, clutches and bands, valve body, and the Transmission Control Module (TCM).

Common faults include delayed engagement, harsh shifting, slipping, and torque converter shudder. Most are caused by degraded ATF, blocked valve body passages, or faulty solenoids. In many African markets, specialist ATF and diagnostic tools are scarce, so workshops that invest in a scan tool and genuine fluid can capture high-margin work that competitors must outsource.

Business tip: Offer a transmission fluid and filter service as a standalone product. It is quick, low-risk, and builds customer trust before larger repairs.

6. Sourcing Reconditioned Gearboxes

Reconditioned gearboxes are a major opportunity in Africa, where new units are often too expensive and import lead times are long. Source from reputable importers with warranty documentation, and verify:

  • Matching part number and gear ratios to the vehicle VIN.
  • Written warranty (minimum 6 months) and return policy.
  • Evidence of new bearings, seals, and synchros.
  • Test report or bench-run confirmation.

Build relationships with two or three reliable suppliers rather than chasing the cheapest price. A failed reconditioned unit costs more in labour, towing, and reputation than the saving on purchase. Consider rebuilding popular gearboxes in-house â Toyota Hilux, Nissan Hardbody, and Isuzu KB units have strong demand and predictable rebuild costs.
