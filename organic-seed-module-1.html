What Is Organic Seed - And Why It Is a Real Business

🧠 Brain Opener

Two farmers plant the same crop on the same day. One uses ordinary seed from the market. The other uses certified organic seed. Four months later, the second farmer earns 20 to 30 percent more for the same harvest. The difference is not the land. It is the seed.

  • What exactly makes a seed organic?
  • Why do buyers pay more for organic?
  • Who in Africa is already making money from organic seed?
🎁 Reveal a strong answer

Organic seed comes from plants grown without synthetic chemicals, in soil that has been chemical-free for 2 to 3 years. It is non-GMO. It is traceable back to the field it grew in. Buyers pay more because organic produce can be sold to Europe, America, and the Middle East at premium prices - and the premium starts with the seed.

📖 Rudo Plants Two Rows

Rudo was twenty-four and had inherited a small plot outside Marondera from her grandmother. She had two hundred dollars and no experience.

She planted her first crop with ordinary kale seed from the local agro-dealer. It grew. She sold it at Mbare Musika. After transport, market fees, and the day spent selling, she cleared forty dollars.

She asked her neighbour, Mr Chikafu, why her profit was so small.

He shook his head. "You grew the same thing as everyone else. Same seed, same variety, same market. When everyone has the same thing, the price goes down."

"What should I grow?"

"Different seed. Organic kale. Certified. Sold not at the market but to a buyer in Harare who ships to Europe."

Rudo did not believe him. There was no organic seed in her village.

But she wrote to a seed company in Nairobi. Two weeks later a reply came with a price list. Organic kale seed cost four times what she had paid. She almost stopped reading.

Then she saw the price they would pay for the harvest. Nine times the market rate.

She bought half a kilogram of organic kale seed with her last eighty dollars. She planted. She did not use a single chemical. She kept records - every input, every day, every bag harvested.

Four months later, she sold her crop for six hundred and forty dollars. It was a small first year. But the next season, she planted one full hectare. And the season after that, three.

By year four, Rudo was multiplying organic seed herself and selling it to ten other farmers.

💡 The Big Idea

Organic seed is a premium product with a growing market

The Middle East and Africa organic seed market is currently worth 0.23 billion dollars and is growing at over 15 percent a year. It will reach 0.83 billion by 2034. Africa holds 60 percent of the world uncultivated arable land. The buyers already exist. They are waiting for African growers to supply them.

📚 Three Deep Lessons

1

What "organic" actually means for seed

Organic seed has three requirements. First, the parent plant was grown without synthetic pesticides, herbicides, or fertilisers for at least one full generation. Second, the land it grew on has been free of banned chemicals for 2 to 3 years. Third, the seed is non-GMO and traceable - the buyer can look up exactly which field and which farmer produced it.

For a farmer, this means you cannot just buy ordinary seed and call the harvest organic. The organic chain starts with the seed. If the seed is conventional, the produce cannot be certified organic. This is the whole business opportunity - organic seed is scarce, and organic farmers need it.

2

The 20 to 30 percent premium

Organic seed sells for 20 to 30 percent more than conventional seed of the same variety. But the real premium is on the harvest. Organic produce can be sold to European, American, and Middle Eastern markets where supermarkets pay 2 to 9 times the local African market price.

A farmer who sells organic kale at Mbare Musika gets the local price. A farmer who sells organic kale through a certified exporter gets 3 to 9 times more. The exporter only buys from farmers who used certified organic seed. So the seed choice decides the market.

3

The African opportunity in numbers

Africa imports over 90 percent of its vegetable seed. Almost all of it is conventional. Organic seed production in Africa is still in its early stages. This means two things: the market is wide open, and the first movers will build the reputation.

Contract growing for seed companies delivers profit margins above 50 percent for contracted farmers. Organic amaranth yields around 70 kilograms per hectare and sells for about 1.8 dollars per 25 grams - that is 72 dollars per kilogram, or just over 3,000 dollars of gross income per hectare. Those are real African numbers, not projections.

🛠 Hands-On - Map Your Local Seed Market

You need

  • A notebook
  • A pen
  • Two hours and a bicycle or bus fare
  1. Visit three agro-dealers in your nearest town or market.
  2. Write down every seed variety they sell. Note the price per packet and the country it was imported from.
  3. Ask the dealer which seeds sell fastest and which are most expensive.
  4. Visit one or two smallholder farmers and ask where they buy seed and why.
  5. Write down one paragraph: what seed is missing from this market that you could produce?
  6. Note the names and phone numbers of anyone who might buy organic seed from you.
Why it works: You cannot sell to a market you have not visited. Two hours of walking around teaches you more than two weeks of reading.

🌍 Real Stories from Africa

Better Seed in Kenya

A Kenyan seed company called Better Seed sells organic amaranth seed at 1.8 dollars per 25 grams. That is 72 dollars per kilogram. Farmers who multiply the seed for Better Seed earn over 3,000 dollars per hectare of gross income. The company now supplies organic seed to farmers across East Africa.

Nigeria organic seed imports

Nigeria still imports most of its organic vegetable seed from Europe and India. Local production cannot keep up with demand. This means every kilogram of organic seed produced in Nigeria today has a guaranteed buyer - either a farm, an NGO, or an exporter.
