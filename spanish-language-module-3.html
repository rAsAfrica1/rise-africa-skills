1. Asking What the Customer Needs

In a busy market in Accra or a shop in Nairobi, the first step to making a sale is finding out what the customer wants. Start with a friendly greeting: "Buenos das! En qu puedo ayudarle?" (Good morning! How can I help you?). This opens the conversation politely. If the customer looks lost, ask: "Qu est buscando?" (What are you looking for?). For a specific item, say: "Busca algo en particular?" (Are you looking for something in particular?). Listen carefully to their answer. They might say: "Busco un telfono" (I'm looking for a phone) or "Necesito zapatos" (I need shoes). If you don't understand, politely ask them to repeat: "Puede repetir, por favor?" (Can you repeat, please?). To clarify, you can ask: "Para quin es?" (Who is it for?) or "Qu talla?" (What size?). For example, if they want a shirt, ask: "Qu talla de camisa necesita?" (What shirt size do you need?). If they are just browsing, say: "Si necesita algo, avseme" (If you need anything, let me know). Always maintain eye contact and smile. In many African markets, building rapport is key. You might ask: "Es la primera vez que compra aqu?" (Is this your first time buying here?). This shows interest. If the customer speaks English or French, try to use simple Spanish. Practice these phrases daily with a partner. Role-play scenarios: one person is the customer, the other the seller. Use real items around you. For example, set up a small stall with fruits and practice asking: "Qu fruta desea?" (Which fruit do you want?). The more you practice, the more natural it becomes. Remember, asking the right questions helps you serve better and close more sales. Keep a notebook with new words you learn from customers. In no time, you'll be comfortable asking what the customer needs.

2. Offering Help and Recommendations

Once you know what the customer wants, offer help and suggest products. Use phrases like: "Le puedo mostrar..." (I can show you...). For example, if they want a phone, say: "Le puedo mostrar este modelo" (I can show you this model). If they need a recommendation, say: "Le recomiendo este" (I recommend this one). Explain why: "Es de buena calidad" (It's good quality) or "Est en oferta" (It's on sale). In African markets, customers often trust your opinion, so be honest. If a customer asks for a cheaper option, say: "Tenemos esta opcin ms econmica" (We have this cheaper option). If they want something specific, like a durable bag, say: "Este es muy resistente" (This is very durable). Use numbers: "Cuesta 5000 francos" (It costs 5000 francs) or "Son 200 birr" (It's 200 birr). If you have a promotion, mention it: "Si compra dos, le doy un descuento" (If you buy two, I'll give you a discount). When offering alternatives, say: "Tambin tenemos..." (We also have...). For example: "Tambin tenemos este en azul" (We also have this in blue). If the customer hesitates, ask: "Le gusta este?" (Do you like this one?). If they say no, show another: "Pruebe este otro" (Try this other one). Always be patient. In many African cultures, bargaining is expected, so offering help is part of the dance. If you don't have what they want, say: "Lo siento, no tenemos eso" (Sorry, we don't have that) and suggest an alternative: "Pero tenemos algo similar" (But we have something similar). If they need help with size, say: "Prubeselo" (Try it on). For electronics, demonstrate: "Mire, as funciona" (Look, it works like this). Use gestures and show the product. Practice with a friend: set up a mock shop and take turns offering help. Record yourself to improve pronunciation. Remember, offering help is not just about selling; it's about solving the customer's problem. That builds trust and repeat business.

3. Handling Bargaining and Discounts

Bargaining is common in many African markets, so you need Spanish phrases to negotiate. Start with a firm price: "El precio es 10,000 shillings" (The price is 10,000 shillings). If the customer says "Es muy caro" (It's too expensive), respond politely: "Entiendo, pero es de buena calidad" (I understand, but it's good quality). If they ask for a discount: "Me puede dar un descuento?" (Can you give me a discount?), you can say: "Le puedo dar un pequeo descuento" (I can give you a small discount). For example, if the original price is 10,000, offer 9,000: "Se lo dejo en 9,000" (I'll let you have it for 9,000). If they push for more, say: "No puedo bajar ms" (I can't go lower). Use phrases like: "Es mi precio final" (It's my final price). If you want to encourage a bigger purchase, say: "Si lleva dos, le hago un descuento" (If you take two, I'll give you a discount). For example: "Si compra tres, le doy un 10% de descuento" (If you buy three, I'll give you 10% off). If the customer walks away, you can call them back: "Espere, se lo dejo en 8,500" (Wait, I'll let you have it for 8,500). But don't go below your cost. Know your limits. In some cultures, bargaining is a social game; be friendly and smile. If they say "No tengo suficiente dinero" (I don't have enough money), you can suggest a smaller size or a cheaper alternative: "Lleve este ms pequeo" (Take this smaller one). If they insist on a lower price, you can say: "Lo siento, no puedo" (Sorry, I can't). Sometimes, offering a free item helps: "Le regalo una funda" (I'll give you a case for free). Practice with a partner: one bargains, the other holds firm. Use real currency like naira, cedi, or rand. Keep a calculator handy to show numbers. Remember, the goal is a win-win: you make a sale, and the customer feels they got a good deal. Never be rude; always stay calm and polite.

4. Closing a Sale and Making Change

Closing a sale means getting the customer to pay and giving them their item. Use phrases like: "Va a llevar esto?" (Are you going to take this?). If they say yes, say: "Perfecto, son 7,000 francos" (Perfect, it's 7,000 francs). If they agree, ask: "Efectivo o tarjeta?" (Cash or card?). In many African markets, cash is common, but mobile money is growing. If they pay with mobile money, say: "Puede pagar con M-Pesa" (You can pay with M-Pesa). For cash, take the money and count it. Then give change: "Aqu tiene su cambio" (Here's your change). Count the change out loud: "Tres mil francos" (Three thousand francs). If you need to give coins, say: "Y 500 en monedas" (And 500 in coins). Always double-check the amount. If the customer gives a large bill, say: "Tiene algo ms pequeo?" (Do you have something smaller?). If not, give change carefully. After payment, thank them: "Gracias por su compra" (Thank you for your purchase). Offer a bag: "Quiere una bolsa?" (Do you want a bag?). If they need a receipt, say: "Le doy un recibo" (I'll give you a receipt). For electronics, show them how to use it: "Mire, as se enciende" (Look, this is how you turn it on). If they bought clothes, remind them: "Puede cambiarlo si no le queda" (You can exchange it if it doesn't fit). If you have a loyalty program, mention it: "Si vuelve, tiene un descuento" (If you come back, you have a discount). Finally, say goodbye: "Que tenga un buen da" (Have a good day). Practice with a partner: simulate a transaction with fake money. Use a calculator to make change quickly. In busy markets, speed matters, so practice counting change in Spanish. For example, if the item is 4,500 and they give 10,000, you say: "Su cambio es 5,500" (Your change is 5,500). Keep a small notebook to record sales. This helps you track inventory and know what sells best. Closing a sale is the moment of truth, so be efficient and friendly.

5. Dealing with Returns and Complaints Politely

Sometimes customers want to return an item or complain. Handle it politely to keep their trust. Start by listening: "Cul es el problema?" (What's the problem?). If they say the item is defective, respond: "Lo siento mucho" (I'm very sorry). Ask for details: "Qu pas?" (What happened?). If they want a refund, say: "Podemos devolverle su dinero" (We can refund your money). If they want an exchange, say: "Podemos cambiarlo" (We can exchange it). For example, if a shirt doesn't fit, say: "Quiere cambiarlo por otra talla?" (Do you want to exchange it for another size?). If they complain about quality, say: "Tiene razn, lo sentimos" (You're right, we're sorry). Offer a solution: "Le damos uno nuevo" (We'll give you a new one) or "Le devolvemos el dinero" (We'll refund your money). If the item is damaged, ask: "Tiene el recibo?" (Do you have the receipt?). If they don't, you can still help: "No hay problema, podemos ayudarle" (No problem, we can help you). In some cases, you might offer a discount on a future purchase: "Le ofrecemos un 20% de descuento en su prxima compra" (We offer you a 20% discount on your next purchase). If the customer is angry, stay calm: "Entiendo su frustracin" (I understand your frustration). Never argue. If you can't solve it, call a manager: "Djeme llamar a mi supervisor" (Let me call my supervisor). After resolving, thank them: "Gracias por su paciencia" (Thank you for your patience). In African markets, word of mouth is powerful, so a happy customer brings more business. Practice role-plays: one person complains, the other responds politely. Use phrases like: "Cmo podemos solucionarlo?" (How can we fix it?). Keep a log of complaints to improve your service. Remember, a complaint is an opportunity to show you care. Always follow up: "Est satisfecho ahora?" (Are you satisfied now?). This builds loyalty.

6. Practice: Simulate a Full Sales Transaction

Now it's time to put everything together in a full sales transaction. Practice with a partner, taking turns as seller and customer. Start with a greeting: "Buenos das! En qu puedo ayudarle?" (Good morning! How can I help you?). The customer responds: "Busco un telfono" (I'm looking for a phone). You ask: "Qu tipo de telfono?" (What type of phone?). They say: "Uno bueno y barato" (A good and cheap one). You show options: "Le recomiendo este. Cuesta 50,000 francos" (I recommend this one. It costs 50,000 francs). They say: "Es muy caro. Me puede dar un descuento?" (It's too expensive. Can you give me a discount?). You respond: "Le puedo dar un 10% de descuento. Se lo dejo en 45,000" (I can give you a 10% discount. I'll let you have it for 45,000). They agree: "Est bien, lo llevo" (Okay, I'll take it). You ask: "Efectivo o mvil?" (Cash or mobile?). They say: "Efectivo" (Cash). You say: "Son 45,000. Me da el dinero?" (It's 45,000. Can you give me the money?). They give 50,000. You count change: "Su cambio es 5,000" (Your change is 5,000). You thank them: "Gracias por su compra" (Thank you for your purchase). They say: "Gracias" (Thank you). You say: "Que tenga un buen da" (Have a good day). Now, practice a return scenario: The customer comes back: "Este telfono no funciona" (This phone doesn't work). You respond: "Lo siento mucho. Qu pas?" (I'm very sorry. What happened?). They explain. You say: "Podemos cambiarlo por otro" (We can exchange it for another). They agree. You handle the exchange politely. Repeat this simulation several times, varying the products (clothes, food, electronics) and the issues (discounts, complaints). Use real money or tokens. Record yourself to improve fluency. In a real market, you might have only a few seconds to make a good impression, so practice until you're confident. Try to involve a third person as an observer to give feedback. After each simulation, note new words or phrases you struggled with. Study them. With regular practice, you'll be ready to handle any sales conversation in Spanish. This skill can boost your business and open doors to new customers.

7. Practical Reference

Practical Reference: Customer Service and Sales Conversations in Spanish for African Agribusiness

This practical reference supports the RISE AFRICA Skills module Customer Service and Sales Conversations. It is written for African agribusiness owners, agro-dealers, market traders, and sales agents who serve Spanish-speaking customers, buyers, or partners. The focus is on real transactions: greeting a customer, identifying needs, recommending products, negotiating price, closing a sale, giving change, and handling complaints. In African markets, trust, relationship, and clear communication often matter more than a perfect accent. This reference gives you the exact phrases, structures, and cultural notes you need to sell confidently in Spanish.

Across Africa, Spanish is used in Equatorial Guinea, and it is increasingly important in trade with Latin America, Spain, and international buyers. Whether you are selling cocoa in Ghana, coffee in Ethiopia, or fresh produce in Kenya, a Spanish-speaking customer expects courtesy, clarity, and fair dealing. The language patterns below are designed for business-first use, not academic grammar. Each section includes ready-to-use phrases, tables, and a checklist you can print or save on your phone.

1. Asking What the Customer Needs (Detectar la necesidad)

Start with a warm greeting and an open question. In many African markets, customers expect a personal greeting before business. In Spanish, use usted with older or unfamiliar customers, and t with peers or regulars.

2. Offering Help and Recommendations (Ofrecer ayuda y recomendar)

Recommendation is where you add value. Instead of listing products, connect the product to the customer's need. Use Le recomiendo... (I recommend), Este producto le conviene porque... (This product suits you because...). In African agribusiness, buyers often appreciate a seller who explains origin, quality, and reliability.

3. Handling Bargaining and Discounts (Negociar y descuentos)

Bargaining is normal in many African and Latin American markets. In Spanish, avoid saying a flat "no". Instead, offer conditions: larger quantity, cash payment, or a small discount. Use Si lleva ms cantidad, puedo hacer un descuento (If you take more, I can give a discount).

  • Customer: Me puede hacer un descuento? Can you give me a discount?
  • You: Si compra diez unidades, le doy un cinco por ciento. If you buy ten units, I give you five percent.
  • You: Ese es el precio final, pero puedo incluir el transporte. That is the final price, but I can include transport.
  • You: Le entiendo, pero no puedo bajar ms. I understand, but I cannot go lower.
SAFETY WARNING Aviso importante:

Never confirm a price, discount, or delivery date in Spanish unless you are certain of the numbers. In agribusiness, a misunderstood figure can cause financial loss or a broken contract. Always repeat the final price and quantity back to the customer: "Confirmamos: 50 kilos a 2 dlares el kilo, total 100 dlares." If you are unsure, say "Permtame confirmarlo con mi supervisor" (Let me confirm with my supervisor). Do not sign or accept written Spanish contracts without translation or legal review.

4. Closing a Sale and Making Change (Cerrar la venta y dar cambio)

Closing requires clear, simple language. Confirm the order, state the total, and handle payment. In many African markets, mobile money is common; learn the Spanish terms for payment methods.

  • Close: Lo llevamos? Shall we take it?
  • Total: El total son 45 dlares. The total is 45 dollars.
  • Payment: Paga en efectivo o con tarjeta? Are you paying cash or by card?
  • Mobile money: Puede pagar por transferencia mvil? Can you pay by mobile transfer?
  • Change: Aqu tiene su cambio: 5 dlares. Here is your change: 5 dollars.
  • Receipt: Necesita factura o recibo? Do you need an invoice or receipt?

5. Dealing with Returns and Complaints Politely (Devoluciones y quejas)

Complaints are opportunities to build trust. Never argue. Listen, apologise for the inconvenience, and offer a solution. In agribusiness, complaints often concern quality, weight, or delivery delay.

  • Listen: Entiendo su molestia. Cunteme qu pas. I understand your frustration. Tell me what happened.
  • Apologise: Lamento mucho el inconveniente. I am very sorry for the inconvenience.
  • Solution: Podemos reemplazar el producto o devolverle el dinero. We can replace the product or refund your money.
  • Escalate: Voy a consultar con mi supervisor y le respondo hoy. I will check with my supervisor and reply today.
  • Follow up: Est satisfecho con la solucin? Are you satisfied with the solution?

6. Practice: Simulate a Full Sales Transaction (Prctica)

Work in pairs. One person is the agro-dealer, the other is a Spanish-speaking buyer. Use the five-stage cycle from the diagram. Record yourselves and check the checklist below.

  1. Greet and ask what the customer needs.
  2. Recommend one product and justify it.
  3. Negotiate a discount based on quantity.
  4. Confirm the total, take payment, give change.
  5. Handle one complaint or return politely.

Quick Reference Checklist

  • I greeted the customer and asked an open question about their need.
  • I used usted or t appropriately for the customer.
  • I recommended a product and explained why it fits the need.
  • I negotiated a discount with a condition (quantity, cash, transport).
  • I repeated the final price and quantity before closing.
  • I stated the total clearly and gave the correct change.
  • I offered a receipt or invoice.
  • I handled any complaint with an apology and a solution.
