2. Rice Ecosystems and Varieties

The right variety for the right ecosystem decides 50% of your yield before you plant.

Module 2 of 12 · Rice Farming for Profit

The three ecosystems in detail

Irrigated lowland

  • Water is controlled by irrigation (canal, pump, or gravity from a dam)
  • Yields: 6-10 t/ha with proper management
  • Requires: bunded fields, inlet/outlet, reliable water source
  • Investment: $1,000-3,000 per hectare for infrastructure
  • Two crops per year possible in most tropical regions

Rain-fed lowland

  • Water comes from rain and runoff collected in valley bottoms
  • Yields: 3-5 t/ha
  • Requires: bunds to hold water, drainage to prevent flooding
  • Investment: $300-800 per hectare
  • One crop per year (or two in good rainfall areas)

Upland rice

  • No standing water - grows like maize on hillsides
  • Yields: 1.5-3 t/ha
  • Requires: good rainfall, well-drained soil
  • Investment: $200-500 per hectare
  • One crop per year
  • Uses land unsuitable for lowland rice

For most smallholders, NERICA or certified OPV varieties are the best balance of cost and yield. Hybrids pay off for commercial irrigated systems.

Seed quality

  • Buy certified seed from a licensed supplier or research station.
  • Check for the seed certification tag and lot number.
  • Look for uniform grain size, no discolouration, no insects.
  • Do a germination test: 20 seeds in damp paper, count sprouts at 5-7 days. Target 90%+.
  • Seed rate: 40-60 kg/ha for transplanted rice, 60-100 kg/ha for direct-seeded.

The variety rule: Match variety to your ecosystem and water supply. A long-duration irrigated variety in a rain-fed upland field will fail. Talk to your extension officer or research station before buying seed.
Practical exercise 2: Get 3 rice varieties from a research station or certified supplier. Compare cost, maturity, and yield potential. Choose the best fit for your plot.
