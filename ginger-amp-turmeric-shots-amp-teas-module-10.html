Lessons

1. Setting up a stall at local markets and fairs

Start by visiting your local market or fair to see what sells and where you can fit in. Talk to the market master or organiser about stall fees. In many African markets, a daily stall fee ranges from 500 to 2,000 shillings (or equivalent) depending on location and day. For a first try, book a smaller stall or share with another vendor to keep costs low. Choose a spot with foot trafficnear entrances, food areas, or busy walkways. Avoid corners where people pass quickly.

Your stall must look clean and inviting. Use a simple table covered with a bright, clean cloth. Display your ginger and turmeric shots in clear bottles or small cups so customers see the golden colour. Put up a hand-painted sign with big letters: GINGER SHOTS BOOST YOUR IMMUNITY and prices. Have a small cooler or bucket with ice to keep shots cold. For teas, bring a thermos with hot water and sample cups. Keep a waste bin for used cups.

Price clearly. For example, sell a 100ml shot for 1,000 shillings or 3 for 2,500. Offer a small discount for bulk orders. Prepare a simple menu board listing: Ginger Shot, Turmeric Shot, Ginger-Turmeric Mix, and Hot Ginger Tea. Train yourself to greet every person who passes: Hello! Try our fresh ginger shot it fights colds and gives energy. Smile and make eye contact.

Pack enough stock: for a busy market day, bring at least 50 shots and 100 tea servings. Keep a notebook to record sales and popular items. Bring change in small notes and coins. At the end of the day, count your money, subtract stall fees and transport, and note your profit. Repeat at different markets to find your best location.

2. Using WhatsApp and social media to take orders

WhatsApp is the easiest tool to start selling online. Create a WhatsApp Business account (free) and set your profile name to something clear like Aminas Ginger Shots Nairobi. Add a profile photo of your product and a status message: Fresh ginger & turmeric shots delivered. Order now! Save your customers numbers and label them (e.g., Customer Westlands).

Post daily status updates showing your shots being made, packed, or delivered. Use short videos: pour the shot, show the ginger root, or a happy customer drinking. Include prices and how to order: Send a message to 0712 345 678. Delivery in Nairobi for 200 shillings. In your status, you can also ask questions: Which flavour do you prefer ginger or turmeric? This gets replies and keeps you visible.

For orders, use a simple system. When someone messages, reply quickly: Hello! Thanks for your interest. We have 100ml shots at 1,000 each or 3 for 2,500. Where are you located? Confirm the order, total price, and delivery time. Ask for payment via M-Pesa or mobile money before delivery, or cash on delivery for regular customers. Keep a list of orders in a notebook: name, number, items, amount, location, and time. This helps you avoid mistakes.

Also use Facebook Marketplace and Instagram if you have data. Post clear photos with prices and your WhatsApp number. Join local community groups on Facebook and WhatsApp (e.g., Nairobi Buy & Sell) and post your offer once a week. Do not spam; instead, offer a free sample to the group admin. For deliveries, use boda boda or a friend with a motorbike. Charge a small delivery fee (e.g., 200500 shillings) or offer free delivery for orders above 5,000 shillings. Always ask customers to refer friendsgive them 10% off their next order if they do.

3. Partnering with health shops and gyms

Health shops and gyms are goldmines for repeat customers. Walk in during quiet hours (mid-morning or early afternoon) and ask to speak to the owner or manager. Dress neatly and carry a small cooler with free samples. Introduce yourself: Hello, I make fresh ginger and turmeric shots. Id like to supply your shop/gym. Can I leave a few samples for you and your customers to try?

Offer a simple wholesale deal. For example, you sell a 100ml shot to them at 700 shillings, and they sell it at 1,200. For a gym, suggest a commission: they display your shots at the counter, and you give them 20% of each sale. Or they buy at wholesale and keep the profit. Provide a small branded fridge or cooler with your logo and price list. Agree on a delivery scheduletwice a week, for instance, on Monday and Thursday mornings.

For health shops, highlight the health benefits: ginger reduces inflammation, turmeric is good for joints, and both boost immunity. Bring a one-page flyer with ingredients, no preservatives, and your contact. Offer to do a free tasting session on a Saturday morning. Many shops will agree if you handle the setup and cleanup. During the tasting, give out small cups and collect phone numbers for your WhatsApp list.

For gyms, target personal trainers and fitness instructors. Offer them a free shot after their workout. Ask them to recommend your product to clients. You can also sponsor a small gym eventprovide 50 free shots in exchange for your banner being displayed. Keep records of each partner: name, contact, order size, delivery days, and payment terms. Follow up with a thank-you message after each delivery. If a partner sells well, offer them a bonus (e.g., one free pack for every 10 sold). This builds loyalty and steady income.

4. Offering free samples to attract new customers

Free samples are the fastest way to turn a stranger into a buyer. People are more likely to buy after they taste. Plan your sampling session carefully. Choose a busy location: outside a market, near a gym, at a church event, or during a sports match. Get permission from the venue owner or local authority. Prepare small cups (3050ml) and fill them just before the event to keep them fresh. Keep them in a cooler with ice.

Make your sample station attractive. Use a small table with a bright cloth, a sign that says FREE TASTE GINGER & TURMERIC SHOTS, and a tip jar or price list nearby. As people pass, smile and say: Would you like to try a free energy shot? Its good for immunity. Hand them a cup and watch their reaction. If they like it, immediately offer to sell a full bottle: That was a sample. A full 100ml bottle is 1,000 shillings, or 3 for 2,500. Would you like one?

Keep track of how many samples you give and how many sales you make. For example, if you give 100 samples and sell 30 bottles, thats a 30% conversion ratevery good. If sales are low, try a different location or time. Always collect phone numbers: Can I add you to my WhatsApp list for weekly specials? Offer a small incentive: If you buy now, Ill give you a free sample for a friend.

Do not give unlimited free samples to the same person. Limit to one per person. Also, target people who look like they care about healthrunners, gym-goers, mothers with children. Avoid giving samples to small children without asking a parent. After the event, send a WhatsApp message to everyone who gave their number: Thanks for trying our shot! Heres a 10% discount on your first order. Reply YES to order. This turns a one-time taster into a regular customer.

5. Handling cash and mobile money payments

You must be able to accept both cash and mobile money. In most African countries, M-Pesa, Airtel Money, MTN Mobile Money, or Orange Money are common. Register for a merchant account if possible, or use your personal number. Display your mobile money number clearly on your stall and on your WhatsApp status. For cash, always carry small notes and coins for change. Never keep all your money in one pocketuse a money belt or a small locked box under the table.

When a customer pays by mobile money, ask them to show you the confirmation message before you hand over the product. Check the amount and the recipient number. If you have a smartphone, you will get an SMS or notification. Do not rely on a customers screenshot aloneit can be faked. For large orders, wait for the money to reflect in your account. If you are unsure, call your mobile money providers customer care to confirm.

For cash payments, count the money in front of the customer. Say the amount: You gave me 5,000. Your total is 3,000. Here is your 2,000 change. This avoids disputes. Keep a small notebook to record every sale: date, time, items sold, amount, payment method (cash or mobile money), and customer name or number. At the end of the day, balance your cash and mobile money totals against your notebook. If there is a difference, find out why.

Security is important. Do not display large amounts of cash. If you are at a market, make friends with nearby vendors and watch each others stalls. For mobile money, set a PIN that is hard to guess and never share it. If you accept payments online, use a separate phone or SIM for business. Always thank the customer and give them a receipt (a handwritten note is fine). This builds trust and makes them more likely to return.

6. Keeping simple sales records in a notebook

A notebook is your most important business tool. Buy a hardcover exercise book and a pen. On the first page, write your business name, your name, and the date you started. Then create columns for each day: Date, Item, Quantity, Unit Price, Total, Payment Method, Customer Name/Number, and Notes. For example: 12/06/2025, Ginger Shot 100ml, 5, 1,000, 5,000, M-Pesa, Mary 0712..., delivered.

At the end of each day, total your sales. Add up all the Totals. Then subtract your expenses for that day: transport, market fees, ingredients, packaging, and any helpers pay. Write the profit at the bottom. For example, if you sold 5,000 and spent 1,500, your profit is 3,500. Do this every day, even if you are tired. It takes only 5 minutes and shows you if you are making money or losing it.

Every week, review your notebook. Which product sells most? Which market or day is best? Who are your repeat customers? Use this to plan: buy more ginger if shots sell fast, or try a new location if sales are low. Also track your debts: if a customer promises to pay later, write it down and follow up. If you buy ingredients on credit, record that too.

Keep your notebook safedo not leave it at the stall. At the end of the month, add up all profits. Compare with previous months to see growth. If you want a loan or to partner with a shop, your notebook is proof that you are a serious business. You can also use a simple phone app like M-Pesa statement or Excel if you have a smartphone, but a notebook works even without electricity or data. Always write in pen, not pencil, so records cannot be erased.

<h2>Practical Reference: Selling Ginger &amp; Turmeric Shots and Teas at Markets, Events, and Online</h2>

<p>This practical reference pulls together the six lessons in this module into one working document you can print, pin to your stall, or save on your phone. It is written for small-scale African producers of ginger and turmeric shots and teas whether you are selling at a weekly market in Kumasi, a church fair in Nairobi, a gym in Lagos, or taking orders through WhatsApp in Kampala. The goal is simple: turn a good product into repeatable, recorded, profitable sales.</p>

<h3>1. Setting Up a Stall at Local Markets and Fairs</h3>
<p>A market stall is your shop for the day. Treat it like one. Choose a position with foot traffic and shade direct sun degrades fresh juice and melts ice fast. Use a folding table with a clean cloth, a branded banner or chalkboard with your prices, a cooler box for chilled shots, and a small bin for waste. In many African markets, the best spot is near a busy entrance or next to a food vendor, not in a quiet corner. Arrive early; regulars buy from the same stall every week.</p>
<p>Price visibly. Buyers hesitate when they must ask. Have three clear options: single shot, pack of three, and a family bottle of tea. Keep a small tasting cup for samples (see Lesson 4).</p>

<h3>2. Using WhatsApp and Social Media to Take Orders</h3>
<p>WhatsApp is the most important sales channel for most African small food businesses because it is free, familiar, and works on low data. Set up a WhatsApp Business profile with your name, location, operating hours, and a catalogue of your shots and teas. Post a daily "status" showing fresh stock, delivery days, and a price list image. Use a simple order format: name, quantity, flavour, pickup or delivery, payment method.</p>
<p>On Facebook and Instagram, post short videos of you preparing the shots the colour of fresh turmeric and ginger is your best advert. Tag your location and use local hashtags like #NairobiEats or #AccraMarket. Always reply within a few hours; slow replies lose sales.</p>

<h3>3. Partnering with Health Shops and Gyms</h3>
<p>Health shops, pharmacies, gyms, and juice bars already have your customers walking through their doors. Approach the owner with a simple offer: you supply chilled shots on consignment or at wholesale, they earn a margin, and you collect unsold stock weekly. Start with a small trial of 2030 bottles. Provide a branded cooler or shelf sticker so customers know the product. Gyms are especially strong for post-workout ginger shots; agree a fixed delivery day and a WhatsApp reorder line.</p>

<h3>4. Offering Free Samples to Attract New Customers</h3>
<p>A 30 ml sample cup converts far better than a poster. Offer one small taste with a short line: "Fresh ginger and turmeric, no sugar, made this morning." Do not give samples to everyone target people who stop and look. Keep samples in a sealed cooler, use disposable cups, and never re-use a cup. Track how many samples you give and how many sales follow; a good ratio is one sale for every three to five samples.</p>

<h3>5. Handling Cash and Mobile Money Payments</h3>
<p>Accept both cash and mobile money. In most African markets, M-Pesa, MTN MoMo, Airtel Money, and similar services are now the default. Display your till number or QR code clearly. Keep a small float of change, a lockable cash box, and a separate phone for receiving payments if possible. Confirm each mobile payment on your own phone before handing over the product screenshots can be faked.</p>

<h3>6. Keeping Simple Sales Records in a Notebook</h3>
<p>You cannot grow what you do not measure. A single exercise book with six columns is enough: date, location, product, quantity sold, unit price, total, payment method. Add a weekly total at the bottom. This record tells you which market, flavour, and price works best, and it is the first thing a lender or supplier will ask for.</p>

<strong>SAFETY WARNING Food Safety and Payment Security:</strong>
Ginger and turmeric shots are unpasteurised, low-acid juices. If they are not kept cold (below 5C) and sold within 48 hours, harmful bacteria can grow and cause serious illness. Never sell a bottle that has been left in the sun, has a swollen lid, or smells off. Use clean, food-grade bottles, wash hands and equipment with safe water, and label every bottle with the production date. On payments: always confirm mobile money on your own phone before releasing goods fake SMS confirmations and screenshots are common. Keep cash in a locked box and never count money in the open. If you handle large sums, bank or deposit the same day.
