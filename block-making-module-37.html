Block Testing for BS EN 771-3

8 lessons · 30 quiz questions · Business

1 What is BS EN 771-3

BS EN 771-3 is the European standard for aggregate concrete masonry units — i.e. concrete blocks. It defines:

  • How blocks are classified by size, strength, density, and use
  • Required tests
  • Tolerances (size, shape)
  • Labelling and Declaration of Performance (DoP)

Every block sold as "construction grade" in the UK and EU must comply. Even outside the EU, specifiers often require it as a quality benchmark — South Africa, Kenya, and Nigeria all have equivalent or derivative standards.

Complying to BS EN 771-3 opens doors to:

  • Commercial projects (schools, hospitals, offices)
  • Government work
  • Export markets (especially to EU)
  • Architects and engineers who need documentation
💡 Compliance is not just for Europe. It's a quality badge that wins business anywhere.

2 Compressive strength classes

BS EN 771-3 classifies blocks by compressive strength. Common classes:

  • Class 3: 3 N/mm² minimum — non-load-bearing, interior partition
  • Class 5: 5 N/mm² — single-storey non-load or light-load
  • Class 7: 7 N/mm² — single-storey load-bearing (most residential)
  • Class 10: 10 N/mm² — two-storey, foundations, exterior
  • Class 15: 15 N/mm² — commercial buildings
  • Class 20+: Heavy structural

Blocks must average at least the class value across the sample, with no individual result below 80% of the value.

Standard dense concrete blocks usually reach 7–10 N/mm². Hollow blocks reach 5–7 N/mm². CSEB reaches 3–5.

3 Dimensional tolerance rules

BS EN 771-3 defines three tolerance categories:

Category D1 (normal):

  • Dimensions 0–500 mm: ±4 mm
  • Dimensions 500–1000 mm: ±5 mm
  • Dimensions 1000+ mm: ±6 mm

Category D2 (fine):

  • Dimensions 0–500 mm: ±2 mm
  • Dimensions 500–1000 mm: ±3 mm
  • Dimensions 1000+ mm: ±4 mm

Category D3 (very fine):

  • All dimensions: ±1 mm (only achievable with precision moulds)

Most producers aim for D1. Fair-faced or thin-joint blocks need D2 or D3. The category determines what mortar you can use (thin joint = tighter tolerance).

4 Density categories

BS EN 771-3 groups blocks by gross dry density:

  • Lightweight (D1): Under 1,000 kg/m³ — insulation blocks
  • Lightweight (D2): 1,000–1,400 kg/m³ — CSEB, LECA
  • Medium: 1,400–1,700 kg/m³ — most concrete blocks
  • Dense (D3): Over 1,700 kg/m³ — structural blocks, acoustic blocks

Density is tested by weighing a dry block and dividing by its volume. Density affects:

  • Fire resistance (denser = better)
  • Thermal insulation (lighter = better)
  • Sound insulation (denser = better)
  • Structural use (denser = stronger)

Both light and heavy blocks have markets. Density is a specification, not a quality judgement.

5 Testing at an accredited lab

BS EN 771-3 testing requires an accredited lab — you can't test it yourself.

Tests required:

  • Compressive strength (EN 772-1)
  • Dimensions (EN 772-16)
  • Density (EN 772-13)
  • Water absorption (EN 772-11, optional)
  • Fire resistance (EN 13501-1, if required)
  • Sound insulation (if required)

Where to test in Africa:

  • SABS (South Africa)
  • SAZ (Zimbabwe)
  • KEBS (Kenya)
  • SON (Nigeria)
  • Or EU labs if you're exporting

Cost: $2,000–$8,000 for a full initial test set. Ongoing testing: $500–$1,500 quarterly.

⚠ Test labs can be slow. Book in advance — the queue can be 2-4 weeks.

6 UKCA and CE marking

In the UK and EU, blocks sold for construction must be marked:

  • CE mark: EU conformity. Required to sell in EU.
  • UKCA mark: UK Conformity Assessed. Required to sell in the UK (post-Brexit).

To use these marks you need:

  • Factory Production Control (FPC) system
  • Regular testing at an accredited lab
  • Documentation of every batch
  • A Declaration of Performance (DoP)
  • An audit by a certified body (for CE/UKCA)

You cannot self-certify CE/UKCA marks — you need external audits. Cost: $10,000–$40,000 for initial certification, plus annual audits.

Certification is worth it only for major export or commercial projects.

7 Documentation trail

Every block shipped under BS EN 771-3 must be traceable.

Required documents:

  • Declaration of Performance (DoP) — issued per product
  • Batch records — mix design, raw materials, production date
  • Test results — strength, dimensions, density
  • Non-conformance records — what failed and what was done
  • Complaint records — customer issues and resolutions
  • Raw material certificates — cement, aggregate sources

Records kept for 10 years (product liability). Accessible within 24 hours if a regulator or major customer requests.

Software: From simple Excel to specialist systems like Qualtrax or MasterControl. Start with Excel — you don't need fancy software to begin.

8 Cost of testing

Testing is expensive. Know the numbers before committing.

Initial costs (one-off):

  • Full BS EN 771-3 test set: $2,000–$8,000
  • FPC system documentation: $3,000–$8,000 (or DIY if you have time)
  • CE/UKCA certification audit: $10,000–$25,000
  • Total initial: $15,000–$40,000

Ongoing costs:

  • Quarterly testing: $2,000–$6,000 per year
  • Annual audit: $2,000–$5,000 per year
  • Staff time (QC): 0.5 FTE = $3,000–$6,000 per year
  • Total ongoing: $7,000–$17,000 per year

Who should certify: Only producers targeting EU/US export, large government contracts, or high-rise commercial projects. For a local African market, non-certified blocks with documented QC are usually enough.

💰 Certification pays back within 6-12 months if it lands you even one major contract. Otherwise, keep QC in-house and skip the formal certification.
