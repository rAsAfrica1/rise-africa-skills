Practical Reference

Guinea fowl (Numida meleagris) are a business asset, not a hobby bird. They are hardier than chickens, tolerate heat and poor housing better, scavenge a large share of their own feed, and clear ticks, beetles and snails from pasture and orchards. The trade-off is that they are semi-wild: they fly, they wander, they lay seasonally, and keets (chicks) are fragile in the first three weeks. Everything below is built around the numbers you need to price your product, plan your housing, and protect your margin.

Quick Reference Checklist

  1. Price check first. Call three suppliers for keets, feed and vaccines, and three buyers for live birds and eggs. Write the numbers down. No price check, no purchase.
  2. Build the brooder before the keets arrive. Dry floor, draught-free, 32â35 Â°C in week 1, dropping about 3 Â°C per week. Keets chill and die fast.
  3. Secure the run. Guinea fowl fly and wander. Use 1.8 m netting or a covered run, and clip one wing only if local practice allows. Confirm with your extension officer.
  4. Vaccinate on schedule. Newcastle at day 7â10 (and boosters per label), fowl pox at 6â8 weeks. Record batch numbers in your record book.
  5. Weigh 10 birds every two weeks. If average weight is below the benchmark in Table 1, fix feed or health before it compounds.
  6. Track every cost in the record book. Feed, fuel, labour, losses. Your gross margin is the only number that matters, not the bird count.
  7. Line up buyers before week 18. Restaurants, lodges, butcheries and festive markets. A bird with no buyer is a cost, not revenue.
  8. Reinvest a fixed share. Move at least 30% of gross margin into the next cycle's keets and feed so you are never starting from zero.
