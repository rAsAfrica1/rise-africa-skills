â  SAFETY WARNING â Chemical and Health Records

If your farm uses pesticides, herbicides, veterinary drugs or fumigants, record keeping is a health and legal matter, not just an accounting one. You are legally required in most African jurisdictions to keep application records, and you are ethically required to protect workers and buyers.

  • Always record: product name, active ingredient, batch number, rate applied, date, time, plot, applicator name, and the pre-harvest interval (PHI) observed.
  • Never sell produce before the labelled pre-harvest interval has elapsed. Residue violations can destroy an export contract and create liability.
  • Store chemicals in a locked, ventilated, labelled cabinet away from food, feed and water sources. Keep the store inventory as a record.
  • Keep personal protective equipment (PPE) issue records. If a worker is injured or poisoned, your records are your legal defence and their medical history.
  • Retain records for at least 3 years (longer where export certification or GlobalG.A.P. is involved). Buyers and regulators audit retrospectively.
  • If you handle veterinary medicines, record withdrawal periods for meat, milk and eggs. Residues in milk and eggs are a common cause of rejected deliveries.

Quick Reference Checklist

Print this and pin it inside your store room door. Tick it weekly.

  1. Capture daily, post weekly. Every sale, purchase, wage payment and input use goes into the book the same day it happens â not at month end from memory.
  2. Keep every receipt. A record without a supporting document is an opinion. File receipts by month in a lockable box or photograph them immediately.
  3. Separate business and household money. Open a dedicated business account or wallet. Mixing the two is the single biggest cause of false profit figures.
  4. Reconcile stock monthly. Count what is physically in the store against the register. Unexplained shortages are either theft, spoilage or a recording error â all three matter.
  5. Produce a monthly profit and loss. Even a one-page handwritten statement beats no statement. Segment by enterprise (crop, livestock, processing).
  6. Update the asset register on every acquisition. Include date, cost, expected life and depreciation. Lenders value this more than you think.
  7. Back up digital records. Cloud sync, email to yourself, or a WhatsApp message to a second phone. A single phone loss should not erase a season of data.
  8. Reconcile debtors and creditors before every loan application. Know exactly who owes you and whom you owe, with dates and amounts.
