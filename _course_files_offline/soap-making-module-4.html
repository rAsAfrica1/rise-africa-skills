4. Equipment Setup Under 50 Dollars

Soap making needs very little equipment. A home kitchen with 8 simple items is enough for 100 bars per week. Do not buy expensive tools until you have sold your first batch.

Module 4 of 12 · Soap Making · practical exercises · quiz at the end

Lesson 4.1 - The essential kit

ItemCostNotes
Digital kitchen scale (1 g precision)$8-15Most important tool. Do not use a mechanical scale.
Stainless steel or heavy HDPE bowl$3-6For mixing lye
Large plastic jug (2 L)$2-4For mixing lye with water
Stick blender (hand blender)$15-30Reaches trace in 2 minutes instead of 20
Silicone spatula$2-3For scraping
Silicone or wooden soap mould$5-20Basic loaf mould
Thermometer (digital instant-read)$5-10For temperature control
Chemical splash goggles + gloves$6-13Non-negotiable

Lesson 4.2 - What never to use

Never useWhy
Aluminium pots, bowls, spoonsReacts violently with lye, releases hydrogen gas
Glass mixing bowlsCan shatter from the heat of the lye reaction
Teflon or non-stick pansCoating degrades with lye
Copper or brassReacts with lye, ruins the soap
Wooden spoons that hold moistureLye soaks in and can burn later
Kitchen blender used for foodOnce used for soap, never use for food again

Lesson 4.3 - Mould options

Mould typeCostBest for
Wooden loaf mould with silicone liner$15-40Best quality, reusable for years
Only silicone loaf mould$8-15Cheap, easy to unmould, lasts 2-3 years
Individual silicone bar moulds$5-12No cutting needed, fun shapes
Plastic milk carton or PVC pipe$0-3Emergency use, ugly bars

For a starting business, get one good wooden loaf mould. It makes 8-10 bars per batch and will last for years.

Lesson 4.4 - Setting up your work area

  1. Choose a dedicated space - a kitchen counter or outdoor table that is never used for food preparation.
  2. Ventilation first - a window, a door, or work outdoors.
  3. Clean, flat, heat-resistant surface - stainless steel or a thick wooden board.
  4. Water source nearby - for the inevitable spills and rinsing.
  5. Separate storage - for lye, separate from any food or animal feed.
  6. Lockable container for lye - keeps children and animals safe.
  7. Fire extinguisher or sand bucket - for emergencies.
  8. Notebook and pen - for recording recipes and results.
The one-space rule: Once a container, spoon, or mould touches lye or raw soap batter, it never goes back to food use. Buy dedicated soap-making equipment and label it.
Practical exercise 4.1: Set up your work area this week. Photograph it. Check the ventilation and PPE before you mix anything.
Practical exercise 4.2: Test your scale. Weigh 10 g of salt. If your scale reads 9 or 11 g, get a better scale before making soap.
