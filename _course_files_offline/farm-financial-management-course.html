â  Safety Warning: Chemical and Financial Record Keeping

Agrochemicals and veterinary medicines are a major variable cost â and a major health and financial risk. Never store pesticide receipts, invoices, or product labels in the same place as food, seed for consumption, or animal feed. Many African farm stores double as family food stores; organophosphate and pyrethroid residues can contaminate grain and cause acute poisoning. Keep a separate, locked, ventilated chemical store with a concrete floor and a bund to contain spills. Record every purchase, batch number, application date, and dose in your chemical log â this is both a legal requirement in most African countries (e.g., Kenya's Pest Control Products Act, Zimbabwe's Hazardous Substances Act) and your evidence trail if a buyer rejects produce for residue exceedance. Wear gloves, boots, and a face mask when handling concentrates, and never re-use pesticide containers for water or food. If you or a worker shows symptoms of poisoning â headache, blurred vision, vomiting, sweating â stop work immediately, remove contaminated clothing, wash with soap and water, and seek medical care. Report suspected poisoning to your national poison information centre.

Quick Reference Checklist

  1. Separate business and household cash. Open a dedicated mobile-money wallet or bank account for the farm. Pay yourself a fixed monthly draw. Never mix.
  2. Cost every enterprise separately. If you grow maize, beans, and keep chickens, each must carry its own gross margin. Subsidised enterprises hide losses.
  3. Record daily, reconcile weekly. Use a simple cash book or a free app. A record written from memory at month-end is a guess, not a record.
  4. Calculate your break-even before planting. Know the yield and price at which you neither make nor lose money. If the market is below break-even, change the plan.
  5. Build a 3-month working capital reserve. The cash-flow gap in the diagram above is where most African farms fail â not at harvest, but mid-season.
  6. Price for profit, not for popularity. Add a margin for spoilage, transport, and credit sales. Selling below full cost is a slow bankruptcy.
  7. Track debt-to-asset ratio quarterly. Keep it under 40%. Above that, one bad season wipes out equity.
  8. Review your numbers against benchmarks each season. Compare your gross margin ratio and operating expense ratio to Table 2. If you are outside the healthy range, fix it before scaling.
