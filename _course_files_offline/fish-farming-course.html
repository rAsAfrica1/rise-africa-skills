â  SAFETY WARNING: Aflatoxin and Agro-Chemical Handling

Aflatoxin contamination in maize, groundnuts, and other staples is a serious health risk and a major cause of export rejection across Africa. Never mix mouldy grain with clean grain, and never sell visibly moulded produce for human consumption. Test with a rapid aflatoxin kit before storage or sale, and dry grain to safe moisture levels (maize: 13.5% or below).

When handling pesticides or fertilisers, always wear gloves, a mask, long sleeves, and boots. Store agro-chemicals in a locked, ventilated area away from food, feed, and children. Observe the pre-harvest interval (PHI) on every label â selling produce before the PHI expires exposes consumers to residues and can cost you your market access. In case of poisoning, contact your local poison control centre or nearest health facility immediately.

Quick Reference Checklist

  1. Test your grain for moisture and aflatoxin before storage or sale â use a calibrated moisture meter and rapid test kits.
  2. Grade and sort produce at harvest, not at the market; uniform quality earns premium prices and reduces transport losses.
  3. Secure an off-taker contract before planting where possible, so you know your price and volume before you invest in inputs.
  4. Use hermetic storage bags or silos for maize, beans, and other dry staples to cut insect and mould losses.
  5. Keep records of input costs, yields, and sales per plot â accurate records are required for credit, insurance, and certification.
  6. Train your team on safe agro-chemical handling and keep a written log of every application, including date, product, and PHI.
  7. Plan logistics before harvest â book transport, cold chain, or shared storage early to avoid peak-season price collapse.
  8. Review your gross margin per hectare each season and compare it to the benchmarks in Table 2 to spot where you are losing money.
