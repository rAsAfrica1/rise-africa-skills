Practical Reference

Turkey production in Africa sits at the intersection of two profitable realities: strong festive demand (Christmas, Easter, weddings, and urban specialty markets) and relatively low competition compared to broiler chicken. The numbers below are drawn from FAO and industry sources and are intended to help you price your birds, plan feed, and benchmark performance. Always confirm current local prices with at least three suppliers before committing capital.

⚠ SAFETY WARNING — Health, Food & Chemicals
  • Always wear gloves, mask, and eye protection when handling vaccines, disinfectants, or medicated feed.
  • Observe withdrawal periods for all drugs and vaccines before slaughter — selling medicated birds is illegal and dangerous.
  • Store chemicals away from feed, water, and children. Never use feed sacks for chemical storage.
  • Wash hands and change clothes after handling birds, especially before handling food.
  • Report unusual mortality or illness to a vet immediately; do not sell sick birds.

Quick Reference Checklist

  1. Confirm local poult price and availability from at least three hatcheries before ordering.
  2. Set brooder temperature to 32°C at day one, reducing by 3°C weekly.
  3. Vaccinate against Newcastle disease and fowl pox on schedule; keep records.
  4. Weigh a sample of birds weekly and calculate feed conversion ratio.
  5. Lock in a buyer or market date before week 14 to avoid holding costs.
  6. Keep a simple record book: feed in, birds out, cash in, cash out.
  7. Check withdrawal periods for every drug before any bird leaves the farm.
  8. Compare your final cost per bird against Table 2 and adjust next cycle.
