â  SAFETY WARNING â Pesticides, Agrochemicals and Food Safety

Agrochemicals and food-safety failures can kill, cause chronic illness, or destroy a business overnight through product recalls and export bans. Non-negotiable rules:

  • Never use unregistered or banned pesticides. In Africa, only use products registered by your national regulator (e.g. Kenya's PCPB, Nigeria's NAFDAC/NAQS, South Africa's DALRRD, Ghana's EPA).
  • Observe the pre-harvest interval (PHI) and maximum residue limits (MRLs) for your target market. EU MRLs are often stricter than local ones â check before exporting.
  • Wear full personal protective equipment (PPE): gloves, mask, goggles, coveralls. Never eat, drink, or smoke during application.
  • Store chemicals in a locked, ventilated, clearly labelled store away from food, feed, and children. Keep a register of use.
  • Do not use empty pesticide containers for water or food. Triple-rinse and puncture them, then dispose of them per local regulations.
  • For food handling: maintain cold chain, potable water, hand hygiene, and traceability records. One contamination event can end a buyer relationship permanently.
  • If poisoning is suspected, contact a poison control centre or nearest health facility immediately and bring the product label.

Quick Reference Checklist

Run through this checklist before every production cycle, buyer negotiation, or investment decision. Eight points, each one a common point of failure in African agribusiness.

  1. Market first, produce second. Confirm buyer, volume, quality spec, price formula, and payment terms in writing before planting or procuring.
  2. Cost the full chain. Include inputs, labour, transport, storage, processing, packaging, compliance, finance cost, and post-harvest loss â not just farm-gate cost.
  3. Lock in quality standards. Know the grade, moisture, residue, and packaging requirements of your target market; test before delivery.
  4. Secure working capital. Match loan tenor to the production cycle; keep 10â15% contingency for weather, price, and FX shocks.
  5. Manage post-harvest loss actively. Invest in storage, cold chain, or processing where loss exceeds 10% â the payback is usually under two seasons.
  6. Comply on chemicals and food safety. Registered products only, correct PHI, full PPE, traceability records, and a locked chemical store.
  7. Diversify buyers and markets. No single buyer should exceed 50% of revenue; explore regional and export markets where margins justify compliance cost.
  8. Track and record. Keep simple records of yields, costs, losses, and sales. Data is the basis for credit, insurance, and better contracts.
