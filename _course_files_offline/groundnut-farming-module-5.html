Lesson 1

The Shell Test — Judging Harvest

In this lesson
  • Judge correct harvest by the shell test
  • Understand why harvest timing matters for aflatoxin
  • Plan harvest for market, not for convenience

Groundnut is judged by the shell test. Dig 3–5 plants at random from the field and open several pods:

  • Immature: the inner shell is white or pale yellow. The seed is soft. Wait 1–2 weeks.
  • Mature: the inner shell has dark brown or black markings. The seed is firm and the skin does not slip off. Harvest now.
  • Over-mature: the peg has begun to break and some pods are already on the ground. Harvest immediately — losses are rising.

Another indicator: leaves turn yellow and start to drop. This happens alongside the shell test but is less reliable. Use both.

Why timing matters for aflatoxin

Every day past maturity increases the risk of pod cracking, termite attack and drought stress — the three triggers of aflatoxin. A field left 2 weeks too long can go from 5 ppb to 50 ppb in aflatoxin.

Lesson 2

Lifting Without Damaging Pods

In this lesson
  • Lift groundnut with minimal pod loss
  • Time lifting for moist soil
  • Handle pods carefully to avoid damage

Timing

  • If the soil is very dry, water the field 1–2 days before lifting. Soft soil releases pods cleanly.
  • Do not lift immediately after heavy rain — the pods are muddy and stick.

Lifting technique

  • Use a fork or hoe to loosen the soil under the plants.
  • Lift the whole plant gently, with the pods attached.
  • Shake off excess soil — do not scrub or wash.
  • Lay plants in the field in small stooks or windows to dry for 2–5 days.

Pod loss

Every pod left in the ground is lost income. In dry soil without pre-watering, up to 15% of the crop can stay in the ground. Lifting technique accounts for the difference.

Lesson 3

Drying to Aflatoxin-Safe Moisture

In this lesson
  • Dry groundnut pods and kernels to correct moisture
  • Prevent aflatoxin during drying
  • Test moisture without a meter

Two-stage drying

  1. Field drying (2–5 days): plants in stooks or windows, pods attached. This reduces pod moisture from 40–50% to 20–25%.
  2. Sun drying pods (5–7 days): after stripping pods from plants, spread in a 5 cm layer on tarpaulin or concrete. Turn every hour. Cover at night.

Target moisture

  • Pods: 9–10% moisture
  • Kernels: 8–9% moisture

Testing without a meter

  • Rattle test: shake a pod next to your ear. Dry pods rattle, and the seed moves freely.
  • Bite test: bite a kernel. Dry kernels crack cleanly; moist kernels squash.
  • Skin test: rub the kernel. Dry kernels shed the skin easily; moist kernels hold it.

Aflatoxin prevention during drying

  • Never pile pods wet. Piles heat and mould.
  • Dry on tarpaulin or concrete — never bare soil.
  • Do not dry on hot metal or tarmac — cracks the kernels.
  • Cover at night to prevent dew re-wetting.
Lesson 4

Shelling, Grading and Sorting

In this lesson
  • Shell without damaging kernels
  • Grade for the market
  • Remove damaged and discoloured kernels

Shelling

Hand shelling is gentle but slow. Small mechanical shellers damage fewer kernels than you might expect and are much faster. Whatever the method, the goal is intact kernels.

  • Shell pods at 9% pod moisture — drier and the kernels break, wetter and the shells stick.
  • Do not let shelled kernels sit in the sun for hours — they turn rancid faster once the shell is off.

Grading

GradeDescriptionUse
Grade 1Uniform, intact, no blemishIn-shell market, premium kernels, export
Grade 2Minor blemish or slight variationRetail kernels, peanut butter
Grade 3Blemished, small, splitOil extraction, feed
RejectDiscoloured, mouldy, damagedDo not sell for food use

Sorting

Remove:

  • Discoloured kernels — highest aflatoxin load
  • Shrivelled kernels
  • Mouldy kernels
  • Stones, dirt, and other foreign matter

A 10-minute sort per bag can turn a load that fails the aflatoxin test into one that passes. It is the cheapest quality improvement available.

Lesson 5

Storage and Seed Saving

In this lesson
  • Store groundnut for 6+ months without loss
  • Select seed for next season
  • Understand hermetic storage for groundnut

Storage methods

  • Hermetic bags: best for kernels. 12+ months of protection, chemical-free. $3–8 per 100 kg.
  • Jute or woven bags: acceptable if the storage is cool, dry, and rodent-free. Use within 6 months.
  • Metal silos: good for pods, rodent-resistant.
  • Cool room: commercial scale only.

Storage discipline

  • Moisture below 9% before storage
  • Cool, dry, well-ventilated room
  • Off the ground on pallets
  • Away from walls
  • Never mix old and new stock
  • Check monthly for mould, insects, rodents

Seed selection for next season

If you are not buying certified seed, select from the best plants in the field:

  • Healthy, no disease symptoms
  • Well-filled pods with uniform kernels
  • Matching the variety's characteristics

Keep selected pods in-shell for storage — the shell protects the seed from weevil and mould. Shell just before planting.

Knowledge check — Module 5

1. What is the shell test?

Correct: inner shell colour. Dark brown or black = mature.

2. What is the correct kernel moisture for storage?

Correct: 8–9%. Lower than other crops because of the aflatoxin risk.

3. Why shell groundnut at 9% pod moisture?

Correct: intact kernels. Too dry and kernels crack; too wet and the shell sticks.

4. What is the cheapest quality improvement before sale?

Correct: sorting. 10 minutes per bag can turn a failing load into a passing one.

5. How should seed be stored for next season?

Correct: in-shell, dry, sealed. The shell protects the seed from weevil and mould.
