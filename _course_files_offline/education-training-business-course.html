â ï¸ SAFETY & COMPLIANCE WARNING â READ BEFORE YOU TEACH

  • Child protection is non-negotiable. If you teach minors, you must have a written safeguarding policy, a register of attendance, and a second adult present during all sessions. Never teach a child alone behind closed doors. In Kenya, this is required under the Children Act (2022); in South Africa, the Children's Act 38 of 2005; in Nigeria, the Child Rights Act 2003.
  • Do not make false accreditation claims. Advertising "certified" or "accredited" courses without registration with your national qualifications authority (e.g., KNEC, NAB, SAQA, NUC) is a criminal offence in most African jurisdictions and will destroy your reputation.
  • Chemical, electrical, or workshop training: If you teach welding, hairdressing with chemicals, or lab skills, you must provide PPE (gloves, goggles, ventilation) and a first-aid kit on site. Document every incident. Do not allow learners to handle hazardous materials without supervision.
  • Data protection: Learner records, ID numbers, and payment details are personal data. Under Kenya's Data Protection Act (2019), Nigeria's NDPR (2019), and South Africa's POPIA (2013), you must store them securely and never share them without consent.
  • Refund and fee policy: Publish your refund policy in writing before taking payment. Disputes over fees are the number one cause of reputational damage for small academies.
