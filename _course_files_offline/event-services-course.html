â  SAFETY WARNING â Electrical, Structural and Chemical Hazards at Events

Event setup combines three of the highest-risk activities in small business: temporary electrical work, load-bearing structures and chemical handling. Treat every one of these as life-critical.

  • Electrical: Never run mains cable across a walkway without cable ramps. All outdoor power must come through a residual current device (earth leakage) and be earthed. Generators must be sited outdoors, minimum 5 metres from tents and guests, and never refuelled while running.
  • Structures: Tents, stages and trusses must be pegged or ballasted to the manufacturer's specification. Check wind forecast 48 and 24 hours before setup. If gusts exceed the tent rating, postpone or dismantle â no client relationship is worth a collapsed structure.
  • Chemicals: Cleaning agents, floral preservatives, pest sprays and fog or haze fluids must be stored in labelled original containers, never decanted into drink bottles. Keep material safety data sheets on site. Wear gloves and eye protection when handling.
  • Fire: One 9kg dry-powder extinguisher per 50 guests, positioned at exits. Candles and open flame must be enclosed in glass. Confirm the venue's fire certificate is current before you sign the contract.
  • Food: If you handle or serve food, cold chain must hold at or below 5Â°C and hot food at or above 60Â°C. Any food held between 5Â°C and 60Â°C for more than 2 hours must be discarded, not served.

Quick Reference Checklist

  1. Qualify before you quote. Confirm date, guest count, venue, total budget and who signs the contract. If the budget is undefined, give a band, not a number.
  2. Visit the site in person. Photograph power points, access routes, ablutions, parking and shade. Measure the actual setup footprint â do not trust a verbal description.
  3. Call three suppliers for every major line. Record name, price, stock availability, delivery cost and lead time in your record book. Prices move by season and fuel cost.
  4. Quote itemised, never lump-sum. A lump sum hides your margin and invites the client to negotiate the whole figure instead of one line.
  5. Take 40â50% deposit on signature. No deposit, no booking, no exceptions. A verbal promise is not a booking.
  6. Lock vendor deposits 14 days out. Pay your rentals and caterers from the client's sourcing milestone, not from your own pocket.
  7. Build a written run sheet. Hour-by-hour from load-in to load-out, with named crew, contact numbers and a backup for every critical role.
  8. Reconcile within 72 hours. Count stock in, count stock out, settle vendors, invoice the balance, and request a written review and two referrals while the event is still fresh.
