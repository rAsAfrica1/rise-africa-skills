7. Goat Milk Production

Dairy goats produce 2-4 litres per day. In many markets, milk earns more than meat per goat.

Module 7 of 12 · Goat Farming for Profit

Why dairy goats

A good dairy doe produces 2-4 litres of milk per day for 8-10 months of the year. At $0.60-1.20 per litre, that is $1.50-4.50 per day. Over 250 days of lactation, that is $375-1,100 per doe per year - often more than meat sales.

Selecting a dairy doe

  • Well-formed udder - attached high, not pendulous
  • Two good teats - not too big, not too small, no lumps
  • Long body - capacity for feed intake
  • Good body condition - not too thin, not too fat
  • Teeth intact - dairy goats need to eat well
  • Feet sound - no limping, no overgrown hooves
  • Milk record - if available, choose does from high-producing lines

Dairy goat concentrate should be 16-18% protein with good energy. Milking takes calories - a poorly fed doe drops milk fast.

Milking routine

  1. Twice daily - 12 hours apart. Same time every day.
  2. Wash hands first. Clean udder with warm damp cloth.
  3. Discard first 2-3 squirts - this milk has high bacteria.
  4. Milk with full hand grip - not thumb and forefinger (damages teats).
  5. Strain milk immediately through a filter into a clean container.
  6. Cool quickly to 4 C if possible. Or sell within 2 hours.
  7. Clean all equipment with hot water and food-safe sanitiser.
Never milk a goat with mastitis into the main container. Mastitis milk is stringy, bloody, or lumpy. Isolate and treat the doe. Discard her milk until cured.

Breeding for milk

To increase milk yield in your flock:

  • Keep daughters of high-yielding does as breeding stock
  • Sell or cull does with chronic mastitis or low yield
  • Use a proven dairy buck (Saanen or Toggenburg)
  • Cross local hardy does with dairy bucks for dual-purpose offspring
  • Feed heifers well from birth - a stunted doeling never produces full milk
The milk rule: Milk is a daily income. Every litre you sell pays feed for the whole flock. Never let a lactating doe miss a milking - it cuts production permanently.
Practical exercise 7: Visit a dairy goat farm. Ask about milk yield per doe, price per litre, and what buyers they supply. Calculate the extra monthly income from 10 milking does.
