Lithium polymer (LiPo) batteries can enter thermal runaway and cause fires that are difficult to extinguish â never charge unattended, never charge damaged cells, and store in a fireproof LiPo bag. If you spray agricultural chemicals by drone, treat the operation as a pesticide application: wear PPE (gloves, respirator, goggles, coveralls), observe pre-harvest intervals, never spray in wind above 10 km/h, and keep a buffer zone from homes, schools, and water sources. Drone spraying is regulated separately from photography in most African countries â confirm your permit class before you fly. Never fly within 5 km of an aerodrome, over crowds, or beyond visual line of sight (BVLOS) without written authorisation. In an emergency, land immediately â do not attempt to catch a descending drone by hand.

Quick Reference Checklist

  • Pre-flight: Check propellers for cracks, battery voltage above 3.7 V per cell, GPS lock (â¥ 8 satellites), and compass calibration in an open area away from metal.
  • Airspace: Confirm your operating class (recreational, commercial, or BVLOS) and carry your registration certificate and pilot licence on every flight.
  • Weather: Do not launch if wind exceeds your platform's rated limit, if rain is forecast, or if visibility is below 3 km.
  • Battery discipline: Charge to storage voltage (3.8 V/cell) if not flying within 48 hours; never leave a battery in a hot vehicle.
  • Data capture: Set overlap (75% forward, 65% side) for mapping; verify image count and geotags before leaving the site.
  • Client deliverable: Agree scope, resolution, and file format in writing before flying â deliver orthomosaic, DSM, and report as separate line items.
  • Maintenance: Clean motors and gimbal after every dusty flight; log flight hours and battery cycles in a maintenance book.
  • Insurance & liability: Confirm third-party liability cover and keep a signed site-access agreement for every commercial job.
