5. Breeding and Lambing

Good breeding turns 50 ewes into 75 lambs a year. Bad breeding wastes feed on empty animals.

Module 5 of 12 · Sheep Farming for Profit

Sheep breeding basics

ParameterValue
Age at puberty6-9 months (varies by breed)
Age at first breeding12-18 months
Oestrus cycleEvery 16-17 days
Heat duration24-36 hours
Gestation145-150 days (about 5 months)
Lambing interval (accelerated)8 months (3 lambings in 2 years)
Lambing interval (standard)12 months

In tropical Africa, most hair sheep breeds breed year-round. Wool breeds are more seasonal (autumn mating for spring lambing).

Signs of heat in ewes

  • Restlessness, pacing
  • Tail wagging
  • Seeking the ram, standing to be mounted
  • Vulva slightly swollen
  • Bleating more than usual
  • Reduced feed intake
  • Small mucus discharge

Heat lasts 24-36 hours. Mating should happen 12-24 hours after heat begins.

Breeding methods

MethodHowBest for
Hand matingBring ewe to ram when she is in heatSmall flocks, controlled pedigrees
Pen matingRam and 10-15 ewes in a penSmall groups
Flock matingRam runs with the whole flockStandard commercial
Artificial inseminationControlled by technicianElite genetics, breeding programs

Most smallholders use flock mating with a ram ratio of 1:25-40 ewes. Hand mating is best if you want to track individual pedigrees.

Preparing for mating

  1. Flush ewes 3 weeks before mating - increases twins and conception.
  2. Deworm ewes 3-4 weeks before mating.
  3. Vaccinate against enterotoxaemia and tetanus 4-6 weeks before mating.
  4. Trim hooves - a lame ewe conceives poorly.
  5. Check ram fertility - two firm, equal testicles, good body condition.
  6. Mark mating dates - writing the date on a calendar.

Pregnancy care

StageFeedCare
Early (first 3 months)0.3-0.4 kg concentratePrevent stress and rough handling
Mid (3-4 months)0.4-0.6 kgWatch for toxaemia in fat ewes
Late (last 6 weeks)0.6-1.0 kg70% of foetal growth. Increase feed gradually. Prepare lambing pens.
Never suddenly change feed in late pregnancy. Abrupt changes cause pregnancy toxaemia (twin lamb disease). Increase concentrate gradually over 2 weeks.

Lambing

Normal lambing takes 1-2 hours. Most ewes lamb without help. Signs of lambing:

  • Restless, isolating from the flock
  • Pawing at the ground
  • Frequent lying down and getting up
  • Swollen vulva with discharge
  • Water bag appears (first stage)

Assistance

Only intervene if:

  • Ewe strains for over 1 hour with no progress
  • Water bag broke over 1 hour ago with no lamb
  • Only feet appear but no head (or vice versa)

Wash hands, use gloves, lubricate, gently reposition. If in doubt, call a vet.

Care of the newborn lamb

  1. Clear nostrils and mouth of mucus.
  2. Let the ewe lick the lamb dry - the ewe-lamb bond happens fast.
  3. Ensure the lamb nurses within 1 hour - colostrum is critical for immunity.
  4. Dip the umbilical cord in iodine.
  5. Weigh and record birth weight.
  6. Move mother and lamb to a lambing pen for 1-2 days.
  7. Introduce to flock after bonding is solid.

Raising orphan lambs

If the ewe dies or rejects her lamb, or has triplets she cannot feed:

AgeFeedAmount
0-24 hoursColostrum (from another ewe or powdered)200 ml in first 6 hours
Day 1-3Colostrum / milk replacer4-6 feeds/day, 100-200 ml each
Week 1-3Warm milk replacer4 feeds/day, 200-300 ml each
Week 3-6Milk replacer + creep feed3 feeds + free creep
Week 6-8Creep feed + grassWeaned by week 8

Warm milk to body temperature (38 C). Feed at the same times daily. Keep everything clean.

Multiple births

Twins and triplets are more common with good flushing and in some breeds (Finnsheep, Romanov). Handling tips:

  • Watch for mismothering - the ewe may reject one lamb.
  • Feed the ewe well - she must produce enough milk for 2-3.
  • Cross-foster extra lambs onto ewes with singles (rub the lamb with the receiving ewe's afterbirth).
  • Hand-rear if the ewe cannot manage.

The 3-in-2 rule: The best sheep farms get 3 lambings per ewe in 2 years. Achieved by breeding back 60-90 days after lambing - only possible with good nutrition.
Practical exercise 5: Record the breeding dates of your ewes. Mark expected lambing dates on a calendar (150 days after mating). Prepare lambing pens 1 week before the first due date.
