Reinvestment priorities

  1. Water infrastructure - reliable water is the foundation
  2. Land levelling - laser or manual, best single productivity gain
  3. Storage - proper storage captures off-season price premiums
  4. Small mill - captures milling margin (2x revenue per kg)
  5. Pump or canal upgrade - dry season cropping
  6. Transport - own vehicle or shared transport reduces cost
  7. Labour - hired staff for larger operations
  8. Marketing - packaging, brand, contracts

Market expansion strategy

  1. Stage 1: Local market, informal sales, immediate cash
  2. Stage 2: Local mills, regular supply agreements
  3. Stage 3: Urban markets and supermarkets, packaged rice
  4. Stage 4: Institutional contracts (schools, hospitals)
  5. Stage 5: Own brand, distribution through multiple outlets
  6. Stage 6: Regional export (cross-border)
  7. Stage 7: Out-grower scheme (buy from smallholders, process, sell)

When to stop scaling

Scale only when:

  • Current area profitable for 3 consecutive seasons
  • Yield per hectare above 4 t (rain-fed) or 6 t (irrigated)
  • Water infrastructure reliable
  • Market buyers confirmed for expected output
  • Working capital covers the whole season
  • Labour and equipment capacity ready

The patience rule: Rice farming rewards those who invest in water, land, and quality. From 1 to 10 hectares takes 10 years - but each step is profitable. Do not overreach. Build the base, then expand.
