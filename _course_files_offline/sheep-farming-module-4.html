4. Feeding Sheep for Growth

Sheep graze closer than cattle and browse less than goats. Here is how to feed them properly.

Module 4 of 12 · Sheep Farming for Profit

What sheep eat

Sheep are grazers - they eat close to the ground. They prefer short, leafy pasture and legumes. They browse less than goats but will eat some shrubs.

Flushing - the pre-mating boost

Flushing = increasing nutrition 3 weeks before mating. It increases:

  • Ovulation rate (more twins and triplets)
  • Conception rate
  • Lamb birth weight

Flush with 0.4-0.6 kg of concentrate plus good pasture. Stop flushing 3 weeks after mating to prevent oversized single lambs.

Creep feed: 18-20% protein, high energy, palatable. Place in an area lambs can reach but ewes cannot (creep gate).

Pasture management

  • Rotational grazing: 4-6 paddocks, move every 3-7 days. Best for worm control and pasture health.
  • Rest period: Each paddock rests 21-30 days.
  • Stocking rate: 5-10 sheep per hectare on natural pasture, 15-25 on improved pasture.
  • Legume oversowing: Adds nitrogen and protein.
  • Never let pasture overgraze. Overgrazing kills plants and exposes sheep to worm larvae.

Water quality

  • Clean water always available - 24 hours a day.
  • Clean troughs weekly.
  • Water consumption doubles in hot weather - 8-10 L per ewe per day.
  • Mounted trough on concrete base to avoid mud.
The flushing rule: Feed ewes well 3 weeks before mating. It is the single cheapest way to increase twins and triplets - which doubles your lamb crop.
Practical exercise 4: Weigh 5 ewes this week. Body condition score them 1-5. Adjust feed so all reach score 3 before breeding.
