5. Pests and Diseases

Late blight can destroy a potato field in 7 days. Learn the preventive program that saves the crop.

Module 5 of 6 · Potato Farming for Profit

The five major pests

PestDamageControl
Potato tuber mothTunnels in leaves and tubers, storage lossesBt spray, cover tubers with soil, storage hygiene
AphidsCurled leaves, virus spread (yield loss for future crops)Soap spray, ladybirds, neem, mineral oil
CutwormSeedlings cut at soil line overnightCollars, handpick at night
Colorado beetleLeaves stripped to veinsHandpick, Bt tenebrionis, targeted insecticide
NematodesStunted plants, galls on roots, poor yieldRotation with non-solanaceous crops, resistant varieties

Late blight - the biggest killer

Late blight (Phytophthora infestans) can destroy a potato field in 7 days. It is the same disease that caused the Irish potato famine.

SignsConditions
Water-soaked grey-green patches on leavesCool (15-20 C), humid, wet weather
White fuzzy growth on leaf undersides in the morningNight temperatures 10-15 C with high humidity
Rapid browning and collapse of foliageProlonged wet spells
Brown rot of tubers, may smellSpores washing into soil

Preventive spray schedule for blight

WeatherSpray intervalProduct
Dry, hotEvery 14 daysMancozeb or chlorothalonil
Cloudy, humidEvery 10 daysMancozeb + metalaxyl
Wet, cool (blight weather)Every 7 daysMetalaxyl + mancozeb, rotate actives
After rain, if disease presentImmediatelyCurative + protectant mix
Rotate actives. Late blight quickly develops resistance. Never use the same chemical twice in a season.

Other diseases

DiseaseSignsControl
Early blightBrown concentric ring spots on older leavesMancozeb, rotation, mulch
Bacterial wiltSudden wilting, brown ring in stemNo cure. Remove plant. 4-year rotation.
Common scabRough brown patches on tubersAcidic soil (pH 5.0-5.5), rotation
Black scurfBlack warty growths on tuber skinCertified seed, rotation, fresh soil
Viral diseases (PVY, PLRV)Mottled leaves, rolled leaves, stuntedCertified seed, control aphids

IPM order of operations

  1. Prevent - certified seed, rotation, resistant varieties, healthy soil
  2. Monitor - walk fields twice a week, check leaf undersides
  3. Cultural - remove infected foliage immediately, destroy cull piles
  4. Biological - Bt, neem, mineral oils
  5. Chemical - preventative program for blight, targeted only for other pests
The blight rule: Never wait to see blight before you spray. Spray preventively from when the canopy closes. Once blight is visible, yield has already dropped 20%.
Practical exercise 5: Walk your potato plot twice this week. Look for blight on leaf undersides early morning. Start preventive spray program once canopy closes.
