4. Feeding, Watering and Weed Control

Potatoes are heavy feeders with shallow roots. Precision irrigation and split feeding give you full-size tubers.

Module 4 of 6 · Potato Farming for Profit

Feeding schedule

StageProductRate
At plantingDAP (18-46-0)300-400 kg/ha
3 weeks after emergenceCAN200 kg/ha
6 weeks (before flowering)CAN150 kg/ha
At tuber initiationSOP or potassium sulphate100 kg/ha
Never top-dress nitrogen after flowering. It delays maturity and lowers tuber quality.

Signs of nutrient deficiency

NutrientSymptomFix
NitrogenPale yellow older leaves, thin stemsCAN top dress
PhosphorusPurple leaf undersides, weak growthDAP at planting
PotassiumBronze leaf edges, poor tuber fillSOP or wood ash
MagnesiumYellow between veins on older leavesEpsom salt foliar
CalciumInternal brown spot in tubersLime at prep, calcium foliar
BoronHollow stem, cracked tubersBoron foliar - narrow margin

Watering

StageFrequencyNotes
Planting to emergenceSkip unless dryDo not waterlog
Emergence to tuber initiationEvery 3-4 daysSteady moisture
Tuber bulking (weeks 6-12)Every 2 days, deepPeak need. Critical.
Final 2 weeks before harvestStop wateringLets skins set
The tuber bulking rule: Watering during tuber bulking decides your yield. Do not let the crop dry out from week 6 to week 12.

Weed control

  • Critical period: First 4-6 weeks after emergence.
  • First weeding: 2 weeks after emergence, combined with first earthing.
  • Second weeding: 4-5 weeks, combined with second earthing.
  • Mulch: 5-8 cm of dry grass between ridges suppresses weeds.
  • No deep hoeing - potato roots are shallow and tuber damage is easy.

Earthing up (reminder)

Earth up at 15-20 cm plant height and again before flowering. This:

  • Prevents tuber greening (green tubers are toxic)
  • Increases tuber number per plant
  • Improves drainage
  • Controls weeds
Practical exercise 4: Mulch half your potato plot. Compare weed pressure, soil moisture, and yield between mulched and unmulched rows at harvest.
