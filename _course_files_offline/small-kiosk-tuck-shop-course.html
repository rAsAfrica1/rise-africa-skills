â  SAFETY WARNING â Food, Chemicals & Cash Handling

This business handles food, drinks, and potentially hazardous household chemicals (paraffin, bleach, pesticides). Read before you stock.

  • Never store food and chemicals on the same shelf. Paraffin, bleach, rat poison and pesticides must be kept on a separate, low shelf â never above or beside bread, eggs, or drinks.
  • Check expiry dates every Monday. Rotate stock (first in, first out). Selling expired food is illegal in most African jurisdictions and destroys customer trust permanently.
  • Keep cold drinks cold and hot food hot. If you sell cooked food, hold it above 60Â°C or below 5Â°C. Never reheat food more than once.
  • Wash hands before handling food. Keep a covered water container and soap at the kiosk. This is non-negotiable if you sell any unpackaged food.
  • Never sell loose paraffin in a soft-drink bottle. Use clearly labelled, dedicated containers. Children have died from drinking paraffin stored in drink bottles.
  • Keep cash out of sight. Use a lockable tin, bank or mobile-money float daily, and never count large sums in public view. Robbery risk rises with visible cash.

Quick Reference Checklist

  1. Quote three suppliers before every major stock purchase â prices vary by up to 40% between wholesalers in the same city.
  2. Mark your prices clearly on every item. Unmarked goods invite haggling and slow down service at peak hours.
  3. Count the till at close every day and record it in a book. If you cannot explain the difference between yesterday's cash and today's, you are losing money somewhere.
  4. Separate business money from personal money. Pay yourself a fixed weekly drawing â never dip into the stock float.
  5. Track your top 10 sellers weekly. Restock those first; drop any line that sits for more than 14 days.
  6. Check expiry dates every Monday and rotate stock so oldest sells first.
  7. Keep chemicals on a separate shelf from food and drinks â always.
  8. Bank or convert to mobile money daily. Cash on the premises is the single biggest robbery risk for a kiosk.
