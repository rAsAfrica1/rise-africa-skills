â  SAFETY WARNING: Pesticides, Aflatoxins, and Food Handling

Read this before any chemical or post-harvest handling activity. Agribusiness work in Africa involves real health hazards. Non-compliance can kill, poison your customers, or destroy your export licence.

  • Pesticides: Always wear gloves, boots, eye protection, and a respirator when handling or spraying. Never eat, drink, or smoke during application. Observe the pre-harvest interval (PHI) on the label â violating it leaves residues that fail export tests and harm consumers.
  • Aflatoxins: Maize, groundnuts, and other staples can carry aflatoxin, a carcinogen. Do not sell or consume visibly mouldy grain. Dry to below 13% moisture and store on pallets away from walls and floors. Test before selling to formal buyers.
  • Food safety: For dairy, meat, and fresh produce, maintain the cold chain and clean water. Wash hands and equipment. Never use banned veterinary drugs; respect withdrawal periods for milk and meat.
  • Storage chemicals: Fumigants such as phosphine are lethal. Only trained, licensed operators should fumigate. Never enter a fumigated store without proper testing and clearance.
  • Reporting: If poisoning occurs, contact your national poison centre or nearest health facility immediately. Keep the product label for the medical team.

Business consequence: One aflatoxin rejection or pesticide residue scandal can close your export channel permanently. Compliance is not optional â it is your licence to trade.

Quick Reference Checklist

Print this. Tick it before every planting season, loan application, or buyer negotiation.

  1. Know your numbers: Calculate cost of production per hectare or per animal, and your break-even price, before you plant or buy stock.
  2. Secure the market first: Get a written or verbal commitment from a buyer before scaling production. No market, no margin.
  3. Control post-harvest loss: Invest in drying, storage, and handling. Every 1% of loss recovered is pure margin.
  4. Keep records: Maintain a simple cash book, input log, and sales record. Lenders and buyers trust documented businesses.
  5. Comply with safety and quality standards: Follow PHI, moisture, and hygiene rules. Test for aflatoxin and residues before selling to formal markets.
  6. Match finance to purpose: Use short-term credit for inputs and working capital; use long-term finance or leasing for equipment and assets.
  7. Insure what you cannot afford to lose: Index-based crop insurance, livestock insurance, or at minimum a contingency fund of 10% of turnover.
  8. Join a collective: Cooperatives and aggregators reduce input costs, improve market access, and unlock group financing.
