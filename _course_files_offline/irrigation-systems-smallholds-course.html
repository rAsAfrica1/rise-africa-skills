Practical Reference

This reference block gives you the numbers, benchmarks and field rules that turn an irrigation idea into a working business. Use it alongside the six modules above. Everything here is designed for smallholder-scale operations in Sub-Saharan Africa â 0.1 to 2 hectares, low-pressure systems, and equipment that can be sourced, repaired and replaced locally.

Business-first rule: irrigation only pays when the value of the extra crop you grow exceeds the cost of the water, the pipe, the pump and your time. Before you buy anything, calculate the gross margin per cubic metre of water applied. If that number is thin, change the crop, not the pump.

â  SAFETY WARNING â Water, Chemicals and Health

Never mix irrigation water with untreated sewage, industrial effluent or pesticide concentrates in open channels. Contaminated water is the single largest cause of crop-borne diarrhoeal disease in smallholder vegetable supply chains and will destroy your market access if buyers test your produce.

  • Wait the full pre-harvest interval (PHI) on every pesticide label before irrigating or harvesting. Common PHIs range from 3 to 21 days.
  • Wear gloves, boots and a face mask when handling any agrochemical. Store chemicals locked, away from food, feed and children.
  • Test water sources annually for E. coli and salinity. Saline water above 2 dS/m will reduce tomato and onion yields sharply.
  • Never drink from, or allow children to play in, irrigation canals, tanks or reservoirs. Drowning and waterborne infection risks are real.
  • Dispose of empty pesticide containers by triple-rinsing and puncturing â never reuse them for drinking water or food storage.

If you supply supermarkets, hotels or export markets, keep a written water-test record and a spray diary. Buyers increasingly demand this as a condition of contract.

Quick Reference Checklist

  • 1. Measure before you buy. Record your water source yield (litres/hour), plot size and soil type. Size the system to the source, not the other way round.
  • 2. Calculate gross margin per mÂ³. Only proceed if the crop's gross margin per cubic metre exceeds your total water cost per cubic metre by at least 3:1.
  • 3. Choose the cheapest system that works. Gravity drip beats solar pump if your tank sits 2 m above the field and the plot is under 0.5 ha.
  • 4. Filter everything. A sand filter plus screen filter prevents emitter clogging â the number one cause of drip system failure in Africa.
  • 5. Install a water meter and a pressure gauge. Without them you cannot detect leaks, over-irrigation or pump wear.
  • 6. Train one operator per system. Emitter cleaning, filter backwashing and valve setting must be a named person's weekly task.
  • 7. Keep a spray and water-test log. This is your passport to higher-value buyers and to any future certification.
  • 8. Plan the maintenance budget. Set aside 5â8% of system capital cost per year for filters, emitters, seals and pump servicing.
