4. Pruning, Training and Orchard Management

A well-pruned avocado tree produces 3x the fruit of a neglected one. Here is the shape that works.

Module 4 of 6 · Avocado Farming for Profit

Why prune avocado

  • Controls tree height - keeps fruit within reach (max 4-5 m)
  • Improves airflow - less disease
  • Opens the canopy to sunlight - more flowers
  • Removes dead, diseased, or crossing branches
  • Maintains a strong structure that carries heavy fruit loads

The ideal tree shape

Avocado should be trained to a central leader or modified central leader:

  1. Year 1: Remove lower branches below 60 cm. Leave 3-4 well-spaced main branches.
  2. Year 2: Remove branches that cross or crowd. Keep the central trunk going up.
  3. Year 3: Remove the top of the central leader to force outward growth.
  4. Year 4+: Maintain. Remove water shoots, dead wood, and any branch growing inward.

Pruning calendar

WhenWhat
After harvestMain pruning - shape, thin, remove dead wood
Mid-seasonLight pruning - water shoots, suckers below graft
AnytimeDiseased or broken branches - remove immediately

Never prune heavily during flowering or fruit set - you lose the crop.

Suckers below the graft union

Any shoot that grows below the graft union is from the rootstock, not the variety. These must be removed immediately - they steal energy and can take over the tree.

  • Check monthly.
  • Cut or snap them off at the base.
  • Never leave them for more than a few weeks.

Orchard floor management

MethodProsCons
Mown grass between rowsErosion control, easy accessCompetes for water
Bare soil with mulchNo competition, moisture retentionNeeds weed control
Legume cover cropNitrogen fixation, erosion controlMust be mown
Volunteer weedsFree ground coverMay harbour pests

Best: mown grass or legume cover between rows, mulch ring around each tree.

Windbreaks

Avocado flowers are brittle. Wind causes flower drop and fruit rub. On exposed sites, plant windbreaks 4-6 months before the avocado.

Windbreak speciesHeightNotes
Casuarina10-15 mFast, evergreen, dense
Grevillea8-12 mFast, wind-tolerant
Moringa6-10 mFast, also fodder and food
Bamboo (clumping)5-8 mFast, dense, but invasive if not contained
The pruning rule: Keep trees under 5 m tall. Any fruit you cannot reach by hand or short ladder is fruit you will lose to birds.
Practical exercise 4: Prune 5 avocado trees this month. Remove water shoots, dead wood, and any branches below 60 cm. Photograph before and after.
