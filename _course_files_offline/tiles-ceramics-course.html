â  SAFETY WARNING â FOOD SAFETY, PESTICIDES AND CHEMICAL HANDLING

This module covers food production and processing. The following are non-negotiable:

  • Aflatoxin and mycotoxin risk: Poorly dried or stored maize, groundnuts and other staples can develop aflatoxin, which is carcinogenic and can cause liver damage. Dry to safe moisture (typically 12â13% for grains), store off the floor on pallets in a dry, ventilated space, and never blend visibly mouldy grain into food or feed. Test before sale where a laboratory is accessible.
  • Pesticide handling: Always follow the label. Wear gloves, long sleeves, boots and eye protection. Never eat, drink or smoke while handling agrochemicals. Never reuse pesticide containers for water or food. Observe the pre-harvest interval strictly â residues above maximum limits will fail buyer tests and harm consumers.
  • Storage chemicals: Fumigants and grain protectants are hazardous. Only trained, authorised personnel should apply them. Ventilate thoroughly before re-entry. Keep all chemicals locked away from children, food and animal feed.
  • Processing hygiene: Clean and sanitise equipment between batches. Control pests without contaminating product. Use potable water. Maintain a documented cleaning schedule â buyers and regulators will ask for it.
  • Worker health: Provide clean water, handwashing points and sanitary facilities. Report and treat injuries immediately. Keep a first-aid kit stocked and accessible.
  • Traceability: Record every input batch, supplier and output lot. If a contamination event occurs, you must be able to recall product quickly. This protects consumers and your business reputation.

When in doubt, stop the batch. A delayed shipment costs money; a food safety incident can end the business and harm your community.

Quick Reference Checklist

  1. Validate demand before you invest. Confirm at least three real buyers (retail, institutional, export) with written interest or purchase orders before committing capital to processing equipment.
  2. Lock in sourcing. Sign supply agreements with smallholder groups or aggregators covering volume, quality grade, moisture content and delivery schedule. Build a second supplier as backup.
  3. Know your per-tonne cost. Calculate cost per tonne of finished product weekly, not monthly. Track raw material, energy, packaging, labour and logistics separately.
  4. Protect the working capital cycle. Map the days between paying for raw material and receiving customer payment. Arrange a credit line, invoice discounting or buyer deposit to cover that gap.
  5. Invest in drying and storage first. Reducing post-harvest loss by even 10 percentage points often delivers a faster return than new processing machinery.
  6. Build a food safety file. Maintain records of supplier batches, moisture tests, cleaning schedules, pest control and staff training. This file wins contracts and satisfies regulators.
  7. Price for margin, not for volume alone. Test branded retail packs and institutional contracts separately. Do not let one large buyer dictate a price below your true cost.
  8. Measure and review monthly. Track gross margin, extraction or conversion rate, receivables days and loss rate. Set a target for each and review with your team every month.
