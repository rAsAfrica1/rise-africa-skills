Module 12 — Running the Cold Chain: Records, Traceability and Customers

The paperwork, the temperatures and the deliveries that turn a cold room into a business.

Learning outcomes

  • Keep temperature records that satisfy an inspector and actually help you.
  • Build a traceability system from animal to customer, and run a recall if you must.
  • Manage the cold chain beyond the room — transport, cold boxes, delivery windows.
  • Price, label and schedule deliveries so the cold room pays for itself.
  • Know the records to keep and for how long.

1. The cold chain is a chain, not a room

A cold room keeps meat cold. A cold chain keeps meat cold from the moment of slaughter to the moment the customer opens the package. Every link is a chance to lose the whole batch.

2. Temperature records

Two kinds, both needed:

What "compliant" actually looks like

An inspector wants to see:
• A record for every day the room has operated, with no gaps.
• Air temperature between 0 and 4 °C for chilled meat.
• Product core temperature below 7 °C at dispatch, ideally below 4 °C.
• An entry explaining any excursion above 4 °C — with time, duration, cause and action.
• Records kept for at least 12 months, ideally 24.

An excursion is not a failure. An unexplained excursion is.

3. Traceability — animal to customer

Traceability means: given a package, you can name the animal, the day it was killed, the room it chilled in, and who bought it. And given a complaint, you can find every other package from the same batch.

The batch code

Use a single code that encodes everything. Example:

Batch code format

YYMMDD-ANIMAL-CUT

Example: 250512-B07-SIRLOIN
= killed 12 May 2025, animal number B07, sirloin cut.

For mixed-cut boxes, use 250512-B07-MIX and list the contents on the box label.

4. The recall procedure

If a complaint suggests a contaminated batch, you must be able to pull every package from that batch off the market within hours.

  1. Identify the batch code from the customer's package or your dispatch record.
  2. Pull the dispatch record. List every customer who received that batch code.
  3. Contact every customer by phone within 24 hours. Ask them to stop selling and quarantine.
  4. Arrange collection or disposal and record what was recovered.
  5. Investigate the cause — the chilling record, the cutting record, the transport record.
  6. Write it up. Date, batch, cause, action, prevention. Keep for 24 months.

Test your recall once a year

Pick a random batch code from last month. Without looking at the customer file first, see how long it takes you to list every customer who received it. If it takes more than an hour, your records are not good enough.

Packing a cold box

  • Pre-chill the box for 30 minutes before packing.
  • Line the bottom with ice packs, then product, then ice packs on top.
  • Fill air gaps with crumpled paper or a second layer of ice packs.
  • Keep meat in a sealed bag — melt water must never touch the product.
  • Put a small thermometer in the box and read it at delivery.
  • Never mix raw meat with cooked or ready-to-eat products in the same box.

6. Customers, labels and pricing

Label contents

  • Product name and cut.
  • Net weight.
  • Batch code.
  • Slaughter date and packing date.
  • Storage instruction: "Keep at 0–4 °C, use within X days" or "Freeze on day of purchase".
  • Your business name, address and contact.
  • Any allergen declaration (rare for plain meat, but required for marinades and sausages).

Pricing structure

Cost-plus pricing — worked example

Live animal purchase: 450 kg × 2.20 = 990
Slaughter and inspection: 60
Chilling and room running cost (7 days share): 45
Cutting, packing, label: 35
Delivery share: 25
Total cost: 1 155

Carcass weight (55 % dressing): 450 × 0.55 = 247 kg
Saleable meat after trim and bone (about 72 % of carcass): 178 kg
Cost per kg saleable: 1 155 / 178 = 6.49 per kg

Sell at a 35 % margin: 6.49 × 1.35 = 8.76 per kg average across all cuts.
Price prime cuts higher, mince and stew lower, and check the weighted average stays above 8.76.

8. Staff, training and hygiene

  • Every person who enters the cold room is trained on door discipline, temperature checks and hand hygiene.
  • Written procedure on the wall: what to do when the temperature rises, who to call, where the log sheet is.
  • One named person responsible for records each week. Rotate so more than one person can do it.
  • No outdoor clothing or boots inside the room. Dedicated footwear or overshoes.
  • Cleaning schedule posted and signed. Weekly deep clean, daily wipe of door seals and handles.
  • Annual refresher on the recall procedure and on the log sheet.

9. Course complete — where to go next

You have designed, built, and now learned to run a cold room for ten beasts. The next steps in your operation are usually one of:

  • Scaling — Module 11 gave you the framework. Pick one path, do the arithmetic, and build it in stages.
  • Value adding — sausages, biltong, cured products. These need their own building and their own regulations; see the Preservation Buildings course.
  • Direct retail — your own shop or delivery route. This is a marketing and logistics business more than a meat business.
  • Teaching — running training for other farmers on cold room design and operation. Your records and your KPI history are your credibility.

The habit that matters most

Write the temperature down. Every day. Even when nothing is wrong. The day something goes wrong is the day your habit becomes the evidence that saves your business.

Assignment 12 — Final assignment for the course

  1. Produce a one-page daily temperature log sheet you will actually use. Print it and use it for a week.
  2. Design a batch code format for your operation and apply it to your last five animals.
  3. Write out your recall procedure in eight steps or fewer, with named responsibility.
  4. Specify your cold box packing method: box type, ice pack count, layering, thermometer.
  5. Cost one whole animal using the cost-plus worked example. State cost per kg saleable and your target price.
  6. Build a one-page KPI sheet with your targets for the seven KPIs above. Record actuals for one month.
  7. Write a single paragraph: the one habit that, if you keep it for a year, will most improve your cold room business. Explain why.
