â  SAFETY WARNING â Pesticides, Fertilizers and Food Safety

Citrus is a food crop. Residues on fruit can cause buyer rejection, export bans and serious harm to consumers and workers. Follow these rules without exception:

  • Wear gloves, goggles, a respirator and long sleeves when mixing or applying any agrochemical. Never spray against the wind.
  • Respect the pre-harvest interval (PHI) printed on every label. Do not harvest fruit before the PHI expires â this is non-negotiable for export and local retail buyers.
  • Store pesticides in a locked, labelled cabinet away from food, feed and children. Never decant into drink bottles.
  • Wash hands, face and clothing immediately after spraying. Shower and change before eating or entering the house.
  • Dispose of empty containers through an approved collection scheme. Never reuse them or burn them on the farm.
  • Keep a spray record: date, product, rate, block, operator, PHI. Buyers and certifiers will ask for it.
  • If a worker shows dizziness, vomiting or blurred vision, move them to fresh air and call emergency services immediately. Take the product label with you.

Quick Reference Checklist

  1. Test soil and water before planting. Citrus needs pH 5.5â6.5 and water with less than 1,000 mg/L total dissolved salts. Poor water kills more orchards than pests do.
  2. Choose certified disease-free nursery stock. Insist on a phytosanitary certificate and inspect for HLB (greening) symptoms before you pay. One infected tree can cost you the whole block.
  3. Map your irrigation before you dig holes. Budget 1,100â1,400 mm per year for mature trees. Drip with fertigation gives the best water-use efficiency and fastest payback.
  4. Prune for light, not for size. Open the canopy centre so 30 per cent of midday light reaches the lower fruiting wood. Remove water shoots and dead wood every season.
  5. Scout weekly for fruit fly, aphids and HLB vectors. Set protein-bait traps at 4 per hectare. Act at threshold, not after damage appears.
  6. Keep a written spray and fertiliser record. It is your evidence for buyers, certification bodies and lenders â and your early-warning system for resistance problems.
  7. Grade and pack to buyer specification. Size, colour and blemish standards decide your price. A simple digital scale and grading table pay for themselves in one season.
  8. Lock in a market before harvest. Contract 60â70 per cent of your expected volume to a packhouse, processor or wholesaler. Spot markets punish farmers who wait.
  9. Reinvest in the orchard every year. Citrus is a 40-year asset. Budget 15â20 per cent of gross revenue for pruning, nutrition, pest control and replanting gaps.
