Lesson 1

The Critical Weed-Free Window

In this lesson
  • Understand why weeks 1–6 decide most of the yield
  • Time the two weedings
  • Weed without damaging shallow roots

Soybean is slow to close canopy. In the first 6 weeks it is a poor competitor against weeds. Weeds reduce yield by 30–60% if left. Two timely weedings fix the problem.

Critical weeding window

  1. First weeding: 2–3 weeks after emergence. Shallow hoe between rows, hand-pull within row.
  2. Second weeding: 5–6 weeks after emergence. Final pass before canopy closure.

After 6 weeks, the canopy suppresses most weeds on its own. Do not hoe deep after this — soybean roots are shallow.

Mulch

A 5–8 cm mulch of dry grass or crop residue reduces weeding by 50%, keeps soil cool, and prevents soil splash onto lower leaves.

Lesson 2

Water Needs and Waterlogging

In this lesson
  • Understand soybean water needs by stage
  • Prevent waterlogging — soybean's biggest killer
  • Irrigate if you have water

Water need by stage

  • Emergence (0–10 days): moderate
  • Vegetative (10–35 days): rising
  • Flowering (35–55 days): critical — stress causes flower drop
  • Pod fill (55–85 days): critical — stress reduces seed size and quality
  • Maturation (85+ days): reduce water to firm up seed

Waterlogging

Soybean is more sensitive to waterlogging than any other African legume. Waterlogged soil suffocates roots, kills rhizobia, and causes root rot within 3–7 days. If your field holds water after rain, use ridges or raised beds.

Irrigation

Soybean responds well to irrigation at flowering and pod fill. If you have water, those are the two stages to prioritise. Furrow, sprinkler, or drip all work; drip is most water-efficient.

Lesson 3

Reading the Growth Stages

In this lesson
  • Identify soybean growth stages
  • Know what action each stage requires
  • Catch problems early

Vegetative stages (V1–V5)

V1 = first trifoliate leaf. V5 = fifth trifoliate. From emergence to about 5 weeks. Focus: weed control, scouting for stem fly and aphids.

Reproductive stages (R1–R8)

  • R1: first flower. Starts about 6 weeks. Rust scouting begins.
  • R2: full flowering. Pod borer pressure begins.
  • R3: pod set. Critical for pod borer control.
  • R4: full pod. Critical for water and rust.
  • R5–R6: seed fill. Water and potassium critical.
  • R7: beginning maturity. Pods yellowing.
  • R8: full maturity. Harvest.

Why stages matter

Every management action — weeding, spraying, irrigation, fertiliser — happens at a specific stage. Missing the stage means missing the action window.

Lesson 4

Fertility Top-Ups

In this lesson
  • Understand what to top-up and when
  • Avoid nitrogen top-ups
  • Use foliar if needed

Potassium top-up

At flowering (R1), apply 40–60 kg/ha of SOP or MOP. Potassium drives pod fill and seed size. Soybean responds strongly.

Foliar magnesium and micronutrients

If leaves show interveinal yellowing, foliar magnesium (Epsom salt 1%) or a micronutrient mix can help. Foliar is a supplement, not a substitute for soil fertility.

Do not top-dress with nitrogen

Nitrogen at any stage suppresses nodulation. The plant produces its own. Adding nitrogen is a waste of money and reduces the crop's nitrogen-fixing capacity.

Lesson 5

The Daily and Weekly Routine

In this lesson
  • Adopt the daily walk
  • Set the weekly tasks
  • Keep records that make next season better

Daily (10–15 min per 1,000 m²)

  • Walk the plot
  • Check for wilting, disease, insect damage
  • Remove obviously diseased plants
  • Water if dry

Weekly

  • Scout for rust, frogeye, pod borer
  • Weed small escapes
  • Fertilise if scheduled
  • Record observations

Monthly

  • Review records
  • Plan next spray or weeding
  • Check stored seed for weevil

Knowledge check — Module 4

1. What is the critical weeding window?

Correct: weeks 1–6. Soybean is a poor competitor before canopy closure.

2. What is soybean's biggest environmental risk?

Correct: waterlogging. Kills rhizobia and causes root rot in 3–7 days.

3. What stage causes flower drop if stressed?

Correct: flowering. Water stress now causes flower abortion.

4. What fertiliser do you top-dress at flowering?

Correct: potassium. Drives pod fill and seed size.

5. Why not top-dress with nitrogen at flowering?

Correct: suppresses nodulation. The plant makes its own nitrogen.
