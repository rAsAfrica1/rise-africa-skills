10. Irrigation and Water Infrastructure

Water infrastructure is the difference between 3 tonnes and 8 tonnes per hectare. Here is how to build it right.

Module 10 of 12 · Rice Farming for Profit

Why infrastructure matters

Rain-fed rice yields 3-5 t/ha. Irrigated rice yields 6-10 t/ha. The difference is control - you decide when to water, how much, and when to drain. Investing in irrigation infrastructure is the single biggest yield improvement available to rice farmers.

Canal systems

Gravity canals

  • Water flows downhill from a dam, river, or higher ground
  • No fuel cost - the cheapest long-term option
  • Requires 0.5-2% slope along the canal
  • Earth canals lose 30-50% to seepage
  • Concrete-lined canals lose only 5-10%
  • Maintenance: clear weeds, repair banks, desilt annually

Bunds and water control

Bund specifications

  • Height: 25-40 cm above the field level
  • Width at top: 30-40 cm
  • Width at base: 60-80 cm
  • Slope: sides at 45-60 degrees
  • Compacted well to prevent seepage
  • Repaired every season before flooding

Water inlet and outlet

  • Inlet: at the highest corner of the field. Simple wooden or metal gate, or a breach in the bund.
  • Outlet: at the lowest corner. Must allow complete drainage at end of season.
  • Overflow: a lower section of bund (5-10 cm below the top) allows excess water to escape during heavy rain without washing out the whole bund.

Drainage channels

  • Surround each field with a shallow drain 30-50 cm deep, 50 cm wide.
  • Carry excess water away during heavy rain.
  • Prevent waterlogging of neighbouring fields.
  • Allow quick drainage before harvest.

Water charges and regulations

  • Many countries require a permit to abstract water from rivers or boreholes.
  • Irrigation schemes may charge a fee per hectare per season.
  • Water user associations manage shared canals - members pay dues and do maintenance.
  • Check regulations before investing in a borehole or pump.

Shared infrastructure

Water infrastructure is expensive. Consider sharing:

  • Water user associations: Shared canal, pump, or borehole. Members pay proportional costs.
  • Cooperative pump: Group buys one pump and shares by rotation.
  • Community dam: Village-level investment, shared water.
  • Rental market: Rent a pump from a neighbour for the season.

Water use efficiency

  1. Laser levelling: Save 15-25% by even water distribution
  2. Bund repair: Save 5-15% by reducing leaks
  3. Puddling: Reduces deep percolation loss by 30-50%
  4. Intermittent irrigation (SRI): Save 30-50% total water
  5. Canal lining: Save 20-40% of transport loss
  6. Short-maturing varieties: Less total water need
  7. Mulch between rows (upland): Save 20-30%
  8. Water harvesting: Free water for supplementary irrigation
The infrastructure rule: Invest in water control before anything else. A well-designed irrigation system pays back in one or two seasons through higher yields, reduced losses, and higher-value crops in the dry season.
Practical exercise 10: Map your plot and water source on paper. Measure the elevation difference and distance. Get 2-3 quotes for pump or canal installation. Compare costs to the expected yield increase.
