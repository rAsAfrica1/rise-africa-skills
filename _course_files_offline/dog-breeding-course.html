Practical Reference

This reference block is built for the operator, not the theorist. Dog breeding in Africa is a margin business: your profit is decided by litter survival rate, feed conversion, and how fast you turn a puppy into a paid, healthy sale. Below are the numbers, the biology, and the checklists you need to run a kennel like a livestock unit. Keep this page printed in your kennel office.

â  SAFETY WARNING â ZOONOTIC DISEASE, PARASITES AND CHEMICALS

Dog breeding involves contact with animals, waste, and veterinary chemicals. The following risks are real and can affect you, your family, and your workers:

  • Rabies is fatal and not treatable once symptoms appear. Vaccinate all dogs. Any bite or scratch that breaks the skin must be washed with soap and running water for 15 minutes and the person taken to a clinic immediately.
  • Roundworm and hookworm larvae (Toxocara, Ancylostoma) can infect humans, especially children. Deworm dogs on schedule, remove faeces daily, and never use kennel waste as garden compost without proper treatment.
  • Brucella canis and Leptospira can pass from dogs to humans. Wear gloves for whelping, abortion cleanup, and handling urine. Disinfect with appropriate solutions.
  • Never use livestock or agricultural insecticides on dogs. Products such as some organophosphates and ivermectin formulations are toxic or lethal at wrong doses. Use only dog-labelled products at the correct weight-based dose.
  • Disinfectants and parasite chemicals: store locked away from feed and children, wear gloves and eye protection, and ventilate kennels when applying. Never mix chemicals.
  • Pregnant women and immunocompromised people should avoid handling newborn puppies, abortion material, and kennel waste.

Quick Reference Checklist

  1. Test before you mate. Confirm the bitch is in true estrus with progesterone testing or vaginal cytology. Mating on the wrong day is a wasted cycle and a lost year of income.
  2. Keep a dam condition score. Breed only bitches at ideal body condition. Thin or fat dams produce small litters, weak puppies, and poor milk. Feed a quality diet in the last third of gestation and through lactation.
  3. Prepare the whelping box by Day 55. Warm, dry, quiet, with a heat source and clean bedding. Whelping problems you did not prepare for become dead puppies and vet bills.
  4. Weigh every puppy at birth and daily for the first two weeks. A puppy that is not gaining is a puppy that is dying. Intervene early, not late.
  5. Run the vaccination and deworming schedule on time, every time. Parvovirus and distemper are the two diseases that can wipe out an entire litter and your reputation.
  6. Socialise puppies from 3 to 12 weeks. Handle them daily, expose them to sounds, surfaces, and people. Well-socialised puppies sell faster and at higher prices.
  7. Record everything. Mating dates, litter size, birth weights, vaccinations, sales, buyer contacts. Records are what let you price correctly, repeat what works, and prove your pedigree claims.
  8. Sell from 8 weeks, with a health record and a written contract. A signed contract, vaccination card, and follow-up call build the reputation that lets you raise prices next season.
