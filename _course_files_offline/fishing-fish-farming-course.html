â  SAFETY WARNING â Chemicals, Feed, and Food Handling

This course involves agricultural chemicals, medicated feeds, and food products for human consumption. Treat every step as a health and safety decision.

  • Agricultural lime and liming agents: Wear gloves, eye protection, and a dust mask. Lime burns skin and eyes. Store away from children and animals.
  • Medicated and antibiotic-treated feed: Use only as directed by a qualified vet or extension officer. Never use human antibiotics in fish feed. Observe withdrawal periods before harvest â selling fish with antibiotic residue is illegal in most markets and harms buyers.
  • Pesticides and herbicides near ponds: Never spray near a pond. Runoff kills fish and contaminates your product. Keep a buffer zone of at least 10 metres.
  • Food handling: Ice fish immediately after harvest. Wash hands, knives, and surfaces. Do not sell dead, spoiled, or off-smell fish. Your reputation is your market access.
  • Water safety: Do not use untreated sewage or industrial runoff as pond water. Test water before stocking and after any nearby spraying.
  • Personal protection: Wear boots when working in ponds. Cuts and open wounds can become infected. Keep a basic first-aid kit on site.

If you or a worker shows signs of poisoning, allergic reaction, or infection, stop work and seek medical help immediately. Report suspected chemical contamination to your local extension officer.

Quick Reference Checklist

  1. Confirm the market before you dig. Call 3 buyers (restaurants, hotels, markets, processors) and confirm price, volume, and timing before you spend on ponds or fingerlings.
  2. Call 3 suppliers for every input. Fingerlings, feed, lime, nets, and scale. Compare price, delivery, and quality. Record the quotes in your Business Seed Record.
  3. Test your water first. Measure pH, dissolved oxygen, and temperature before stocking. Fix problems before fish arrive, not after.
  4. Stock certified fingerlings. Buy from a hatchery that can show health records. Mixing sources risks disease.
  5. Keep a daily record. Feed used, fish behaviour, water readings, deaths, and sales. A record book is your evidence for lenders and buyers.
  6. Feed to a schedule, not to a feeling. Weigh a sample of fish weekly and adjust feed to 2â4% of body weight. Overfeeding wastes money and fouls water.
  7. Plan harvest and cold chain before day one. Line up ice, transport, and buyers before harvest week. Fish lose value fast without cold storage.
  8. Reinvest after every cycle. Set aside at least 30% of revenue for the next cycle's fingerlings and feed. Do not spend the whole harvest.
