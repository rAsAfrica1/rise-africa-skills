â  FOOD SAFETY WARNING â READ BEFORE YOU BAKE FOR SALE
  • Never sell bread made with mouldy, damp, or insect-damaged flour. Mycotoxins (aflatoxins) from spoiled grain survive baking heat and cause liver damage. One batch is not worth a customer's health or your licence.
  • Cool all bread fully before bagging. Bagging warm loaves traps moisture and grows mould within 24 hours â a food-poisoning and reputation risk.
  • Keep raw dough and finished bread separate. Cross-contamination from hands, cloths, and surfaces is the leading cause of bakery-related illness.
  • Wash hands with soap after toilet, before handling dough, and after handling waste. No exceptions, every shift.
  • Store yeast, sugar, and fats sealed and off the floor. Rodent and insect contamination destroys stock value and creates health liability.
  • If your local authority requires a food-handling certificate, get it. It protects you legally and opens institutional contracts (schools, hospitals, mines) that pay on time.

Quick Reference Checklist

  • Weigh every ingredient. Cups and spoons vary; a 2% flour error changes loaf volume and your yield. Use a scale from day one.
  • Calculate cost per loaf weekly, not monthly. Flour, fuel, and transport prices move fast in African markets. Stale costing kills margin silently.
  • Know your break-even volume. Divide monthly fixed costs by gross margin per loaf. If break-even exceeds your realistic daily output, fix costs before scaling.
  • Buy fuel and flour in bulk when cash allows. Bulk buying typically cuts 10â20% off unit cost â but only if you have dry, secure storage.
  • Build route density, not route length. Ten customers on one street beat thirty scattered across town. Density cuts fuel and time per loaf.
  • Collect cash or mobile money on delivery. Bakery credit is the fastest way to lose a business. No credit without a signed order and deposit.
  • Track wastage daily. Unsold bread is a production planning failure, not a marketing problem. Adjust tomorrow's bake to yesterday's sell-through.
  • Keep a simple daily record book. Loaves baked, loaves sold, cash in, cash out. If you cannot see the numbers, you cannot grow the business.
