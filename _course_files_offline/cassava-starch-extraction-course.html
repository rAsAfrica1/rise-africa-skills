â ï¸ SAFETY WARNING â Cyanogenic Compounds in Cassava

Cassava roots contain cyanogenic glucosides (linamarin and lotaustralin). When the root is grated or crushed, these compounds break down and release hydrogen cyanide (HCN). Bitter varieties can carry 200-400 mg HCN/kg fresh root. Untreated cassava effluent, peel and waste water are hazardous.

  • Never allow workers to drink or wash in untreated press water or settling pond water.
  • Ventilate grating and pressing rooms â HCN is a gas and accumulates in enclosed spaces.
  • Wear gloves and eye protection during grating and juice handling.
  • Do not feed untreated wet peel or press cake to livestock without proper drying or fermentation.
  • Dispose of effluent in a lined pit or lagoon â never directly into streams or community water sources.
  • Train all workers on HCN symptoms: headache, dizziness, nausea, breathlessness. Seek medical help immediately.

Reference: FAO/WHO Codex Alimentarius and IITA cassava safety guidance.

Quick Reference Checklist

  1. Test root starch content before buying. Use the specific gravity or simple settling test. Reject below 20% starch â the math will not work.
  2. Process within 24 hours of harvest. Roots lose starch and darken rapidly after 48 hours.
  3. Wash and peel thoroughly. Soil and peel carry microbes and bitter compounds into your product.
  4. Use clean, potable water only. Contaminated water ruins food-grade certification and export eligibility.
  5. Control settling time. 8-12 hours gives good starch separation without fermentation off-flavours.
  6. Dry to below 13% moisture. Test with a moisture meter before bagging. Damp starch moulds within days.
  7. Record every batch. Input weight, output weight, moisture, and buyer. This is your pricing power.
  8. Manage effluent daily. Lined pit, no runoff into community water. This protects your licence to operate.
