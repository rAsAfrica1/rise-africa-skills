3. Housing and Kennel Setup

A dog kennel must protect health, welfare, and hygiene. Get it right and disease drops dramatically.

Module 3 of 12 · Dog Breeding for Profit

Why kennels matter

Dogs are social animals and can be kept in family homes, outside kennels, or purpose-built kennel blocks. For a breeding operation, purpose-built facilities make the difference between a successful business and a source of disease and complaint.

Kennel design principles

  1. Raised kennel floor 10-15 cm above ground for drainage and warmth.
  2. Solid walls on three sides, wire mesh on the front - dogs see out and stay dry.
  3. Insulated roof - keeps heat out in summer, retains warmth in winter.
  4. Ventilation - high vents, airflow without drafts.
  5. Smooth, washable surfaces - concrete, tile, or sealed wood.
  6. Sleeping box - 1.2 x 1.2 m raised bed with bedding.
  7. Feed and water bowls - stainless steel, bolted or fixed.
  8. Drainage slope - 2% fall to a drain or soakaway.
  9. Isolation area - separate kennel for sick or new dogs.
  10. Whelping room - quiet, warm (25 C), away from other dogs.

Puppy area

From 3 weeks, puppies need a bigger area for play and toilet training:

  • Play pen - 3-4 m2, escape-proof, safe toys
  • Toilet area - newspaper or puppy pads, away from sleeping/feeding
  • Feeding area - stable, easy to clean
  • Socialisation space - safe exposure to new sights, sounds, surfaces

Isolation and quarantine

Any new dog or returning dog must be isolated for 3-4 weeks:

  • Separate kennel 30+ m from main block
  • Separate bowls, bedding, tools
  • Handle after all other dogs
  • Vaccinate and deworm during quarantine
  • Observe daily for signs of disease

Climate control

  • Shade - every kennel needs shade in summer.
  • Water - always available; add more drinking points in hot weather.
  • Cooling - wet the floor or provide paddling pools in extreme heat.
  • Warmth - insulation and bedding in winter. Puppies need 28-32 C for the first 2 weeks.
  • Ventilation - airflow without drafts prevents respiratory disease.

Noise and neighbours

Dog kennels can be noisy. Prevent complaints:

  • Locate kennels away from property boundaries.
  • Provide visual barriers (walls or hedges) - dogs bark at what they see.
  • Exercise dogs daily - bored dogs bark more.
  • Feed and water before night to reduce nighttime activity.
  • Consider a white noise machine or radio at low volume.
  • Talk to neighbours before you start breeding. Keep the peace.

The kennel rule: Money spent on good kennels saves ten times the money spent on treating disease. Dry, clean, well-ventilated kennels prevent most of the health problems you will otherwise face.
