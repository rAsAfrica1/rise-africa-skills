Nǐ hǎo! Chinese

Every Chinese character started as a little picture — and you can draw them all.

Ages 7–128 modules 🪨 Starts with no device

Chinese looks like a wall of tiny drawings, and that is exactly what it is. A person walking. Three mountain peaks. The sun. Once you see the picture inside the character, you cannot un-see it. You will draw them in sand, shout them at your friends, and count goats in Chinese before this course is done.

一 = 1 line 二 = 2 lines 三 = 3 lines

⚠ Safety Warning — Read Before Every Session

This course involves voice, hearing and screen use. Protect the learner.

  • Hearing: Never let a child use earphones at high volume for tone drills. Prolonged loud audio can cause permanent hearing damage. Keep volume below 60% and take a 5-minute break every 20 minutes.
  • Voice: Shouting tones loudly (as Module 2 encourages) can strain young voices. Limit loud practice to short bursts and stop immediately if the child reports a sore throat or hoarseness.
  • Screen and eyes: Shared phones held close for long periods cause eye strain. Follow the 20-20-20 rule — every 20 minutes, look 20 feet away for 20 seconds.
  • Posture: Children hunched over a phone for an hour risk neck and back pain. Table, chair, phone propped up.
  • Hygiene: If phones are shared between learners, wipe the screen between users. Do not share earphones.
  • Supervision: A child under 12 should not browse the internet unsupervised while hunting for “Chinese writing in the market” (Module 7). Go with them or send them with a trusted adult.
  • Emotional safety: Tone mistakes are funny, not shameful. A child who is laughed at for calling their mother a horse will stop speaking. Correct gently and privately.

If a learner has a hearing impairment, a speech delay, or a diagnosed anxiety condition, adapt the course — do not push through. Consult a qualified health professional where needed. General guidance adapted from WHO Deafness and Hearing Loss fact sheet and WHO Vision fact sheet.

  2. Print the tone diagram. One laminated copy per learner. It is the single most useful page in the course.
  3. Set up the WhatsApp line. Use the official rise AFRICA skills number (+263 77 688 1941) for certificates, supplier listings and consultations. Reply within 24 hours or you lose the lead.
  4. Price before you teach. Decide your per-learner fee using Table 2, then double-check against two local competitors.
  5. Run a free first session. Let parents see the child draw a character and say a tone. Conversion from free demo to paid enrolment is your real marketing metric.
  6. Track the 40-character target. By the end of Module 8 the learner should recognise about 40 characters and produce all four tones. Test this with a short oral and written check.
  7. Document every learner. Name, guardian contact, module completed, date. This record becomes your certificate evidence and your proof of impact for funders.
  8. Collect testimonials. One short WhatsApp voice note from a parent is worth more than any flyer.
