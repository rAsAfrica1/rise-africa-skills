â  SAFETY WARNING â Pesticides and Food Handling

Pesticides are the single most dangerous input on a smallholder farm. Follow these non-negotiables:

  • Only use registered products approved by your national regulator (e.g. Pest Control Products Board in Kenya, NAFDAC in Nigeria, Ghana EPA). Unregistered or repackaged products may be banned, adulterated, or lethal.
  • Observe the pre-harvest interval (PHI). Never harvest or sell produce before the label's stated waiting period. Residue violations can get your consignment rejected at export and can poison consumers.
  • Wear PPE: gloves, boots, long sleeves, and a face mask when mixing or spraying. Never spray against the wind. Never eat, drink, or smoke during application.
  • Store chemicals locked away from food, feed, seed, and children. Never decant pesticides into beverage bottles.
  • Triple-rinse empty containers and puncture them so they cannot be reused. Dispose at a designated collection point, never in a water source.
  • If poisoning is suspected, stop work, remove contaminated clothing, wash skin with soap and water, and go immediately to the nearest health facility with the product label.

Food safety is a commercial issue too: buyers increasingly test for Maximum Residue Limits (MRLs). One failed test can end a supply contract permanently.

Quick Reference Checklist

  1. Know your cost of production per kg. Before you negotiate any price, write down seed, fertiliser, labour, transport, and a share of fixed costs. If you cannot state your break-even price, you are not negotiating â you are accepting.
  2. Grade and sort before you sell. Uniform, clean, correctly sized produce typically earns 15â40% more than mixed unsorted lots. Grading costs labour hours; it returns multiples of that in price.
  3. Secure at least two buyers before planting. A verbal commitment is weak; a written purchase order, even a simple one, is financeable and reduces the risk of a distressed sale at harvest.
  4. Cut post-harvest loss first. Crates, shade, hermetic storage, and rapid processing deliver faster payback than almost any other investment on a smallholder operation.
  5. Keep a simple record book. Date, activity, input, quantity, cost, revenue. Records are the difference between a farm and a business â and they are required for any loan application.
  6. Check every input for registration and expiry. Buy seed, fertiliser, and crop protection products only from licensed agro-dealers and keep receipts.
  7. Respect pre-harvest intervals and MRLs. Your reputation with buyers is built over seasons and lost in a single rejected delivery.
  8. Reinvest a fixed percentage of every sale. Set aside at least 10â20% of gross revenue for the next season's inputs and one piece of productivity-improving equipment.
