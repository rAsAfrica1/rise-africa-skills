1. Why Cat Breeding Pays

Cats need less space than dogs and sell for premium prices. Here is where the money is.

Module 1 of 12 · Cat Breeding for Profit

The cat breeding opportunity

A well-bred kitten from a registered cattery sells for $200-2,000 depending on breed and pedigree. A healthy breeding queen produces 8-16 kittens per year. That is $1,600-32,000 gross revenue per breeding queen per year, on less than 6 m2 of space.

Why cat breeding works for smallholders

  • Very small space - one breeding queen needs 4-6 m2
  • Fast cycle - 65-day gestation, kittens sold by 12 weeks
  • Low feed cost - a cat eats $0.30-1.00 of food per day
  • Urban market - buyers come to you
  • Year-round demand - cats are the most popular pet in many countries
  • Recurring revenue - a breeding queen produces multiple litters per year
  • Low noise - less disturbance to neighbours than dogs

The three big challenges

  • Knowledge - breeding has serious welfare and genetic requirements. Done badly, it produces sick, poorly socialised cats that do not sell.
  • Initial investment - a quality breeding queen costs $500-5,000.
  • Time and vet bills - queening, kitten care, and vet checks need hands-on attention for weeks.
  • Reputation - one bad batch of sick kittens destroys your name in the local market.
  • Legalities - many countries require cattery registration and breeding licences.

What you need to start

  • Space - a secure indoor cattery room of 6-15 m2
  • A breeding queen plus access to a proven stud male
  • Reliable water source
  • Access to a vet who knows cats
  • Basic record-keeping and marketing
  • Starting capital of $800-3,000 for a first pair and basic cattery
The cat rule: Start with one good breed and one breeding queen. Learn the full cycle for two years before adding a second breed. Reputation in cat breeding is built slowly and lost instantly.
Practical exercise 1: Visit 3 local cat breeders. Ask which breeds sell best in your area, what prices they get, and what buyers ask for. Write the answers.
