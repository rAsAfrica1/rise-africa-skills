â  SAFETY WARNING â Mycotoxins and Fumigants

Aflatoxin and other mycotoxins are invisible, tasteless and heat-stable. They are NOT destroyed by cooking, boiling or milling. Grain that smells musty, shows green-yellow mould, or comes from a wet harvest MUST be tested before it enters the food or feed chain. In many African markets, aflatoxin-contaminated maize is a leading cause of liver cancer and child stunting. Never blend contaminated grain with clean grain to "dilute" it â this is illegal in most jurisdictions and dangerous.

If you use fumigants (phosphine tablets, e.g., aluminium phosphide): they release a highly toxic gas. Only trained, licensed operators should apply them. Always use a respirator, never enter a sealed store alone, and respect the full ventilation waiting period (typically 3â5 days) before re-entry. Keep fumigants locked away from children and away from living quarters. Follow the label â the label is the law.

Quick Reference Checklist

  • 1. Measure moisture before storage. Use a calibrated moisture meter. Do not store maize above 13% MC or legumes above 10% MC.
  • 2. Clean and grade first. Remove broken kernels, chaff and dust â they absorb moisture and host insects.
  • 3. Dry on a raised, clean surface. Never dry directly on bare ground; it re-introduces moisture and soil-borne moulds.
  • 4. Use hermetic storage. Hermetic bags, silos or metal drums starve insects of oxygen and prevent re-infestation.
  • 5. Stack on pallets, 10 cm off the floor and 30 cm from walls. Airflow prevents condensation and rodent access.
  • 6. Inspect weekly. Record temperature, smell and insect counts. Early detection saves the whole lot.
  • 7. Rotate stock (FIFO). First-in, first-out prevents old grain from being trapped at the back of the store.
  • 8. Keep records. Track purchase price, storage cost, shrinkage and sale price per lot to know your true margin.
