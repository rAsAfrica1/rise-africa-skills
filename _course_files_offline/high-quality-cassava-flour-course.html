â ï¸ SAFETY WARNING â CYANIDE AND FOOD SAFETY

Raw cassava contains cyanogenic glycosides (linamarin and lotaustralin) that release hydrogen cyanide (HCN) during processing. Poorly processed cassava flour can cause acute cyanide poisoning, and long-term dietary exposure is linked to konzo (irreversible paralysis) and tropical ataxic neuropathy. Never skip peeling, grating, pressing and drying. Peeling removes most of the cyanide; dewatering and drying remove the rest. Do not sell flour with HCN above 10 mg/kg. Do not allow workers to eat raw or under-pressed cassava cake. Do not use the same press water on crops without treatment. Keep a written batch log of peel time, press time and drying temperature â if a customer reports illness, that log is your only defence. If anyone shows dizziness, vomiting, confusion or weakness after eating cassava products, seek emergency medical care immediately.

Quick Reference Checklist

  1. Source roots within 48 hours of harvest. Cassava starts spoiling within 24â72 hours; stale roots give grey flour and lower recovery.
  2. Weigh everything. Record fresh root weight in, peel waste out, wet cake weight, dry flour weight. Without this you cannot calculate true yield.
  3. Peel thick, not thin. Aim to remove the whole brown outer layer and the inner rind â this is where most cyanogens sit.
  4. Press to at least 35% moisture reduction. Under-pressed cake dries slowly, browns and retains HCN.
  5. Dry to â¤10% moisture. Test with a moisture meter, not by touch. Flash dryers beat sun drying for consistency and food safety.
  6. Sieve to â¤250 Âµm and pass through a metal detector. Grit and metal fragments are the two most common rejection reasons.
  7. Label every batch with production date, batch number, moisture reading and HCN test result where available.
  8. Price against cost, not against the neighbour. Know your cost per kg before you quote a buyer.
