â  SAFETY WARNING â AFLATOXIN AND PESTICIDE HANDLING

Aflatoxin contamination in maize, groundnuts, and other staples is a serious human health hazard and a major cause of export rejection across Africa. Never sell or consume grain with visible mould, discolouration, or a musty smell. Dry to safe moisture content (maize â¤13.5%, groundnuts â¤9%) before storage, and use hermetic bags. Do not attempt to "dilute" contaminated grain by mixing it with clean grain â this is illegal in most markets and unsafe.

When handling any pesticide, herbicide, or fumigant: wear gloves, a mask, and long sleeves; never eat, drink, or smoke during application; store chemicals in original labelled containers away from food and children; observe the full pre-harvest interval. If you suspect poisoning, contact your nearest poison control centre or health facility immediately and bring the product label.

Quick Reference Checklist

  1. Test moisture before storage. Buy or borrow a handheld moisture meter. For maize, target â¤13.5%; for groundnuts, â¤9%. This single step prevents most aflatoxin and mould losses.
  2. Grade and sort at the farm gate. Remove damaged, immature, and diseased produce before bulking. Graded produce earns 10â30% more and reduces rejection at the buyer.
  3. Invest in the right packaging. Use hermetic bags for grains and crates for perishables. Sacks and open baskets are where most handling loss happens.
  4. Know your numbers. Track cost of production per hectare, yield, farm-gate price, and post-harvest loss percentage. If you cannot state these four figures, you cannot negotiate.
  5. Secure an off-taker before you plant. A signed or written contract with an aggregator, processor, or institutional buyer de-risks the season and can unlock working capital.
  6. Register your business and comply. Formal registration, tax compliance, and food-safety certification (e.g., HACCP, Global G.A.P.) open export and supermarket channels that informal operators cannot access.
  7. Use warehouse receipt finance. If you store grain, ask your bank or a licensed warehouse operator about receipt-based credit â it typically unlocks 60â80% of stored value without selling at harvest-time low prices.
  8. Insure what you cannot afford to lose. Index-based crop insurance and asset insurance are increasingly available through mobile platforms across Kenya, Nigeria, Ghana, and Zambia.
