â  SAFETY WARNING â CHEMICALS, CUTS & CONTAMINATION

  • Grafting knives are razor-sharp. Always cut away from your body, never toward your hand. Wear a cut-resistant glove on the holding hand.
  • If you use fungicide paste or sterilant (e.g., diluted bleach, alcohol) on blades, wear gloves and eye protection. Never mix bleach with ammonia-based cleaners.
  • Disinfect blades between trees with 70% alcohol or 10% bleach solution to prevent spreading bacterial canker, citrus greening (HLB), and avocado sunblotch viroid.
  • Store all chemicals in original labelled containers, locked away from children and food. Follow the label â it is the law and the safety manual.
  • Wash hands with soap after handling treated wood, sap, or chemicals. Some saps (e.g., mango, cashew family) cause severe skin irritation.

Quick Reference Checklist

  1. Confirm demand before you graft. Call 3 buyers (local market, nursery, juice processor) and confirm price and volume for grafted trees before you invest in rootstock.
  2. Select mother trees with proven yield. Take scion wood only from trees that have fruited 3+ seasons with good size and disease resistance.
  3. Match diameters. Scion and rootstock should be the same thickness (pencil-thick) so cambium layers align.
  4. Sharpen and sterilise. A clean, sharp cut heals faster. Disinfect between trees.
  5. Graft in the right season. In most African regions, graft at the start of the rainy season or when sap is flowing â avoid peak heat and drought stress.
  6. Label every graft. Record date, species, variety, and mother tree ID in your record book.
  7. Control shade and water. Keep grafts under 50% shade net and water consistently for 6â8 weeks.
  8. Remove tape at 6â8 weeks. Leaving it on girdles the tree and kills the graft.
