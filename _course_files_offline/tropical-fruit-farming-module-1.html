1. The Tropical Fruit Opportunity

Mango, papaya, pineapple, passion fruit, guava - five crops that thrive across tropical Africa.

Module 1 of 6 · Tropical Fruit Farming for Profit

Why tropical fruit

Tropical fruit is one of the fastest-growing agricultural sectors in Africa. Demand is rising locally (urban middle class) and internationally (Europe, Middle East, China). Most tropical fruit trees thrive on land that is too hot, too dry, or too steep for annual crops.

Why tropical fruit works

  • Year-round harvest for papaya, pineapple, passion fruit, guava - daily cash flow
  • Perennial - many crops produce for 20-40 years from one planting
  • Value-added - juice, dried fruit, jam, pulp, chips - higher margins than fresh
  • Export demand - hard currency earnings
  • Marginal land - grows on land too steep or dry for maize or vegetables

The three risks

  • Fruit fly - attacks most tropical fruit. Serious for mango, guava, passion fruit.
  • Glut season - prices crash when everyone harvests at the same time. Processing or staggered varieties solve this.
  • Post-harvest losses - fruit is delicate. Poor handling loses 30-50%.

What you need to start

  • Land suited to the crop (climate, altitude, drainage)
  • Certified planting material from a licensed nursery
  • Water source - drip irrigation strongly recommended
  • Windbreak on exposed sites
  • Starting capital: $300-1,500 per crop depending on scale
The specialty rule: Never plant one tropical fruit crop only. Plant 2-3 species that fruit at different times of year. It spreads risk and provides year-round income.
Practical exercise 1: Visit 3 markets. Check the price of mango, papaya, pineapple, and passion fruit. Find out which is in shortest supply.

Quiz
