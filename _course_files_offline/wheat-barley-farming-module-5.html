Lesson 1

Judging Harvest Time

In this lesson
  • Judge correct harvest stage
  • Understand the moisture-yield trade-off
  • Avoid shattering losses

Signs of maturity

  • Heads turn from green to golden-brown
  • Straw (leaves and stems) dry to yellow-brown
  • Grain is hard when bitten
  • Grain moisture is 14–18% — cutting at 14% avoids drying cost
  • Grain does not dent under a thumbnail (hard dough stage)

Harvest window

Wheat and barley shatter if left too long. Aim to harvest within 7–10 days of maturity. Late harvest loses 10–20% to shattering, lodging and bird damage.

Harvesting method

  • Combine harvester: fastest, lowest loss — but expensive to rent and requires dry fields
  • Reaper + thresher: for smallholders without combine access
  • Hand cutting + threshing: labour-intensive, slow, but works at any scale
Lesson 2

Harvesting, Threshing & Grain Damage

In this lesson
  • Harvest without cracking grain
  • Adjust threshing settings for wheat/barley
  • Clean grain before storage

Combine settings

The drum speed and concave clearance must be set for wheat or barley. Too fast and the grain cracks; too slow and unthreshed heads pass through.

  • Drum speed: 900–1,200 rpm for wheat; slightly lower for barley
  • Concave clearance: adjusted for grain size
  • Fan speed: enough to clean chaff but not blow grain out

Handling

  • Handle grain gently — cracked grain stores poorly and reduces milling yield
  • Do not let grain heat up in a pile
  • Clean immediately — chaff and dust cause mould

Cleaning

Winnow or use a cleaner to remove chaff, straw, weed seeds and dust. Clean grain stores longer and sells at higher prices.

Lesson 3

Drying to Milling Moisture

In this lesson
  • Dry grain to 12–13% moisture for storage
  • Understand drying methods
  • Test moisture

Target moisture

  • Wheat for milling: 12.5% or below
  • Barley for malting: 12% or below
  • Feed grain: 13–14% acceptable

Drying methods

  • Sun drying: 5 cm layer on a tarp or concrete floor, turned hourly. 2–4 days brings grain from 16% to 12% in hot weather.
  • Mechanical dryer: for commercial scale, precise control, higher capital.

Testing moisture

  • Moisture meter: the only precise way.
  • Bite test: grain should crack cleanly, not squash.
  • Salt test: 100 g salt + 100 g grain in a sealed jar. Shake, wait 15 minutes. If salt sticks, moisture is too high.
The rule: wheat at 13.5% moisture can be delivered to a flour mill at a small discount. Wheat at 12% or below commands the full price. The last 1.5% of moisture is worth 3–5% of revenue.

Storage — Grain Silo, Bag or Hermetic

  • Protect grain from insects and mould
  • Understand storage economics

Storage discipline

  • Moisture below 12.5% before storage
  • Clean grain — no chaff, dust or insects
  • Cool, dry, ventilated store
  • Off the ground, away from walls
  • Check monthly for insects and moisture
  • Rotate stock: first in, first out

Storage pests

  • Grain weevil, lesser grain borer, flour beetle
  • Rats and mice
  • Mould if moisture rises

Hermetic bags prevent all three without chemicals. Metal silos prevent rodents and reduce moisture ingress. Both are far better than bag storage over 3 months.

Lesson 5

Grading & the Mill Test

In this lesson
  • Understand milling specifications
  • Grade for the buyer's requirements
  • Test before delivery

What mills test

  • Moisture: below 12.5%
  • Test weight (hectolitre weight): above 76 kg/hl for milling grade
  • Protein content: 11–14% (higher = premium)
  • Foreign matter: below 1%
  • Broken grain: below 3%
  • Mycotoxins: below regulatory limits

What breweries test for malting barley

  • Moisture: below 12%
  • Protein: 10.5–12.5%
  • Germination: above 95%
  • Grain size: above 2.5 mm (85%+ of sample)
  • Variety: must be on the approved malting list

Grading at home

  • Screen out broken grain and foreign matter
  • Sort visually for discolouration
  • Test moisture before delivery
  • If any test fails, sell as feed grain — do not deliver to a mill and risk rejection

Knowledge check — Module 5

1. What is the moisture target for milling wheat?

Correct: 12.5% or below. Higher moisture causes a discount or rejection.

2. What moisture does malting barley need?

Correct: 12% or below. Germination and quality require dry grain.

3. What is the best chemical-free storage for wheat?

Correct: hermetic bags or metal silos. Both prevent insects and mould without chemicals.

4. What protein does malting barley require?

Correct: 10.5–12.5%. Too high reduces malt extract.

5. What happens if wheat fails the mill test?

Correct: rejected, sold as feed grain. Test before delivery to avoid this.
