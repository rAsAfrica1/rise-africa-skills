Broiler Farming for Profit

Broilers are the fastest-turnover livestock enterprise for a smallholder. A batch of 200 birds finishes in 6-7 weeks, uses a small structure, and pays back its capital inside one cycle.

Home · All courses · Broiler Farming for Profit

What this course covers

  • How to calculate the real cost of a 200-bird broiler batch before you start
  • How to build or adapt housing that keeps broilers healthy and gaining weight
  • How to brood day-old chicks with 95%+ survival through the first 2 weeks
  • How to feed for the best FCR at the lowest cost
  • How to vaccinate and keep disease out of a broiler house
  • How to slaughter, dress, chill and sell for premium market prices

Modules

  • Module 11. The Broiler Business
  • Module 22. Housing and Equipment
  • Module 33. Brooding Day-Old Chicks
  • Module 44. Feeding for Weight and FCR
  • Module 55. Health and Biosecurity
  • Module 66. Slaughter, Processing and Selling

Who this course is for

  • Smallholders starting a first broiler batch on 100-500 birds
  • Farmers who already keep chickens but lose money on every batch
  • Youth groups and women's groups setting up a collective broiler project
  • Anyone wanting a 7-week cash cycle instead of a seasonal harvest
  • Agripreneurs supplying restaurants, hotels or supermarkets

What you will need

  • A small structure or the ability to build one (3m x 4m holds 200 broilers)
  • A reliable day-old chick supplier and a feed supplier
  • Daily time - 20 minutes for 200 birds, 45 minutes for 500
  • A notebook for daily feed, water and mortality records
  • Starting capital of 250-600 USD for a 200-bird batch

â  SAFETY WARNING â HEALTH, FOOD AND CHEMICALS

Vaccines, disinfectants and any veterinary medicines used in this course are health and chemical hazards. Observe the following without exception:

  • Never eat, drink or smoke while handling vaccines, disinfectants or medicated feed.
  • Wear gloves and eye protection when mixing chemicals. Wash hands with soap immediately after handling.
  • Respect the withdrawal period on every drug or medicated feed. Selling meat inside the withdrawal window leaves residues and can cost you your market and your licence.
  • Store vaccines and medicines in a locked, cool, dry place out of reach of children. Keep the cold chain unbroken for live vaccines.
  • At slaughter, use clean water and sanitised equipment. Chill dressed birds to 4Â°C or below within two hours. Never sell birds that died of unknown causes.
  • Dispose of dead birds and slaughter waste by deep burial or incineration, away from water sources and housing.

If you suspect a notifiable disease such as Newcastle disease or avian influenza, report to your local veterinary authority immediately. Do not move birds or sell meat from an affected flock.

Quick Reference Checklist

  1. Cost the batch before you buy chicks. Write the full cost build-up (Table 1) in your record book and confirm your chick and feed prices in writing.
  2. Prepare the house 7 days ahead. Clean, disinfect, dry, then lay fresh litter. Brood the house to 32-34Â°C at chick level before the chicks arrive.
  3. Weigh feed, not scoops. Use a scale. Weigh a sample of birds every 7 days and compare to the growth curve in Figure 1.
  4. Track FCR weekly. Cumulative FCR should fall toward 1.6-1.8 by week six. If it rises, check feed quality, disease and water access.
  5. Keep water clean and always available. Broilers drink roughly 1.8-2 times their feed intake by weight. Dirty water is the fastest route to a disease outbreak.
  6. Record daily. Feed in, water in, deaths, and any symptoms. Twenty minutes a day for 200 birds protects a USD 500 investment.
  7. Plan your market before week five. Line up buyers, agree prices, and decide live sale versus dressed sale. Book transport and crates early.
  8. Chill and deliver fast. Slaughter, dress, chill to 4Â°C within two hours, and deliver the same day for premium prices.
