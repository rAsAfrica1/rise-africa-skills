Goats carry zoonotic diseases including brucellosis, Q-fever, and orf. Always wear gloves when assisting kidding or handling aborted foetuses â Brucella melitensis causes undulant fever in humans and is a notifiable disease in most African countries. Never drink raw goat milk from an untested herd; pasteurise or boil before consumption. When using anthelmintics (dewormers), observe the full withdrawal period before slaughter or milk sale â residues are a food-safety violation and can trigger market rejection. Store all veterinary chemicals in a locked cabinet away from feed and children. If you or a worker develop persistent fever after handling goats, seek medical attention and report the animal contact to the clinician.

Quick Reference Checklist

  • Tag every kid at birth with a unique ID and record sire + dam in a herd book (paper or app).
  • Weigh kids at birth and at 90 days â weaning weight is the single best predictor of market value.
  • Cull any doe that fails to conceive twice within a 12-month window; feed costs are the same whether she produces or not.
  • Replace the buck every 2 years and never use a sire on his own daughters â inbreeding depression cuts weaning weights by 10â20%.
  • Body condition score (BCS) your does monthly; target 3.0â3.5 on a 5-point scale at mating for optimal conception.
  • Quarantine all incoming animals for 30 days and test for brucellosis and CAE before joining the herd.
  • Keep a simple profit ledger per doe â revenue from her offspring minus her feed and health costs. Breed the profitable ones.
  • Book your best 10% of does to your best buck each season; genetic gain compounds faster when selection pressure is concentrated.
