â  SAFETY WARNING â PESTICIDE HANDLING AND STORAGE

  • Never store pesticides alongside food, feed, seed or drinking water. Keep a locked, ventilated, labelled store with an impermeable floor and a spill kit.
  • Wear a long-sleeved shirt, trousers, nitrile or neoprene gloves, boots, goggles and a respirator when mixing or spraying. A cloth cap and shorts are not PPE.
  • Do not re-use pesticide containers for water or food. Triple-rinse and puncture containers, then dispose of them through a licensed hazardous-waste channel â never burn or bury near a well.
  • Respect the pre-harvest interval (PHI) on the label. Selling produce with residues above the Codex maximum residue limit can close export markets and is a legal offence.
  • Restrict sales of WHO Class Ia, Ib and II actives. Where a less hazardous or non-chemical option meets the economic threshold, sell that first â it protects your licence and your customer.
  • If someone is exposed: remove contaminated clothing, wash the skin with soap and water, and take the product label to the nearest health facility immediately. Do not induce vomiting unless a clinician advises it.

Quick Reference Checklist

  1. Stock the top five actives only. Concentrate working capital on products you can turn over in under 90 days; slow movers tie up cash and expire on the shelf.
  2. Verify registration numbers against your national regulator (e.g. PCPB in Kenya, NAFDAC in Nigeria, or the national plant protection organisation) before any purchase order.
  3. Price per hectare, not per bottle. Quote customers the full-season cost so they can compare a cheap low-dose product against a higher-quality one honestly.
  4. Bundle advice with the sale. Correct dose, timing, water volume and PHI guidance raises efficacy, cuts complaints, and builds repeat business.
  5. Keep a batch and sales register. Lot numbers, dates and buyer names are essential for recalls, traceability and regulatory inspection.
  6. Train staff quarterly on label reading, spill response, first aid and safe transport. Document the training.
  7. Offer credit only against a repayment plan tied to harvest cash flow; a 30â60 day cycle matched to the season beats open-ended debt.
  8. Track two KPIs monthly: gross margin per product line and inventory days on hand. Both drive profitability more than top-line sales.
