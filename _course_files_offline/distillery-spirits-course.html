⚠ SAFETY WARNING — AGRO-CHEMICALS, AFLATOXIN, AND FOOD HANDLING

Pesticides, herbicides, and fumigants used in African crop and storage systems are hazardous if misapplied. Always follow the label, wear gloves, boots, eye protection, and a respirator when spraying or fumigating, and never eat, drink, or smoke during application. Store chemicals in a locked, ventilated space away from food, feed, seed, and children. Observe the pre-harvest interval before selling or consuming treated crops.

Aflatoxin in maize and groundnuts is a serious health and trade risk. Do not sell or consume grain that is mouldy, discoloured, or musty. Dry to safe moisture (typically 13% or below for maize grain) and store on pallets off the floor. Buyers in the EU and many African formal markets test for aflatoxin; a single rejected consignment can end a supply relationship.

For dairy, meat, and fish: maintain the cold chain, use potable water, and sanitise all contact surfaces. If you or a worker shows symptoms after chemical exposure — dizziness, vomiting, blurred vision, or skin burns — stop work, move to fresh air, wash thoroughly, and seek medical care immediately. Keep the product label or safety data sheet with the patient.

Quick Reference Checklist

  1. Know your unit economics. Calculate cost per kilogram or litre at every stage from input purchase to point of sale, and update it each season. If you cannot state your break-even price, you cannot negotiate.
  2. Fix post-harvest before chasing yield. Invest first in drying, grading, and pest-proof storage. A 20% loss reduction usually pays back faster than a 20% yield increase.
  3. Secure at least one written off-take agreement. Even a simple letter of intent with volume, quality spec, price mechanism, and delivery window reduces finance risk and strengthens loan applications.
  4. Comply with standards and certification. Confirm the moisture, aflatoxin, pesticide-residue, and packaging requirements of your target buyer before harvest, not after.
  5. Match finance to the production cycle. Align loan tenor and repayment schedules with cash flow, and explore warehouse receipt systems, aggregation contracts, and invoice discounting.
  6. Keep records that a lender or buyer will accept. Track input costs, labour, yields, sales, and losses in a simple ledger or mobile app. Records are collateral in practice.
  7. Train and protect your team. Document safe handling of chemicals and food, provide protective equipment, and keep a first-aid kit and emergency contacts on site.
  8. Plan for climate and market shocks. Diversify buyers and products, use drought-tolerant or early-maturing varieties where suitable, and hold a cash reserve for at least one production cycle.
