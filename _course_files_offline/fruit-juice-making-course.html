â ï¸ SAFETY WARNING: Fertilizer and Agrochemical Handling

Fertilizers and crop protection products are hazardous if mishandled. In Sub-Saharan Africa, the WHO estimates over 300,000 acute pesticide poisonings occur annually, with many unreported in rural areas. Observe the following mandatory precautions:

  • Never store fertilizer or pesticides near food, feed, or drinking water. Use a locked, ventilated, dry store with bunded floors.
  • Always wear nitrile gloves, eye protection, and a dust mask when handling granular or powdered products. Full PPE (coverall, boots, respirator) is required for any spraying operation.
  • Do not eat, drink, or smoke during handling. Wash hands and exposed skin with soap and clean water immediately after use.
  • Respect pre-harvest intervals (PHI) strictly â typically 7â21 days depending on active ingredient. Selling produce before PHI expires endangers consumers and can lead to export rejection.
  • Dispose of empty containers by triple-rinsing and puncturing, then returning to a licensed collection point. Never reuse pesticide containers for water or food storage.
  • Seek immediate medical attention if symptoms of poisoning appear: headache, dizziness, excessive salivation, blurred vision, or vomiting. Bring the product label to the clinic.

Source: WHO (2021), "Pesticide Residues in Food" and FAO (2020), "International Code of Conduct on Pesticide Management."

Quick Reference Checklist

  1. Test your soil before buying fertilizer. A $15â$25 soil test at a national lab or mobile testing unit prevents $100+ in wasted inputs per hectare.
  2. Buy certified seed from licensed agro-dealers. Ask for the lot number and germination rate. Counterfeit seed is the single largest cause of yield failure in West Africa.
  3. Combine at least three ISFM pillars â organic matter, mineral fertilizer, improved seed, and good agronomy â to achieve the 60â95% yield gains documented in Table 1.
  4. Calculate NUE on your farm. Divide total grain harvested (kg) by total NPK applied (kg). If below 13, review timing, placement, and soil pH before increasing fertilizer rates.
  5. Store inputs correctly. Fertilizer absorbs moisture and cakes; pesticides degrade in heat. Use raised pallets, keep below 30Â°C, and rotate stock by expiry date.
  6. Train every staff member on PPE use and maintain a written safety log. Insurance premiums and buyer audits increasingly require documented HSE compliance.
  7. Register for government subsidy programmes (e-voucher in Kenya, PFJ in Ghana, AIP in Malawi) to reduce input costs by 30â50% for eligible farmers.
  8. Track input-to-output price ratios seasonally. A maize:fertilizer price ratio above 2.0 generally signals profitable fertilizer use; below 1.5, consider organic alternatives or reduced rates.
