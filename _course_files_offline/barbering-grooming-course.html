â ï¸ SAFETY WARNING â Health, Hygiene and Chemicals

Barbering involves direct skin contact and sharp tools. Blood-borne infections (HIV, hepatitis B and C) can pass through nicks, razors, and clippers. This is a legal and moral obligation, not optional.

  • Never reuse a blade or razor. Dispose of blades in a sharps container immediately after use.
  • Disinfect clippers and scissors between every client with an approved disinfectant (e.g., barbicide or 70% alcohol).
  • Wash hands before and after each client; use a fresh towel and neck strip every time.
  • Handle chemicals safely. Store disinfectants away from food and children, keep them labelled, and never mix bleach with ammonia-based cleaners.
  • Cover cuts and sores on your own hands with a waterproof plaster before cutting.
  • Check local health regulations â many councils require a licence or health inspection for barbering premises.

Quick Reference Checklist

  1. Price check first: Call three suppliers for clippers, blades, and disinfectant before spending a cent. Prices swing 40â60% between suppliers.
  2. Set your price, not your neighbour's: Calculate cost per cut (tools + rent + time) and add 40â60% margin. Undercutting kills shops.
  3. Book the next cut before the client leaves: A two-week return booking is worth more than any flyer.
  4. Track every client: Use a record book â name, date, cut style, next visit. Follow up by WhatsApp the day before.
  5. Disinfect visibly: Let clients see you clean tools. It builds trust and justifies premium pricing.
  6. Keep a cash split: Separate business cash from personal cash daily. Pay yourself a fixed amount.
  7. Reinvest 20% monthly: Upgrade one tool or buy one marketing push every month.
