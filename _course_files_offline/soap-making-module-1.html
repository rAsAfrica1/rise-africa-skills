1. Introduction to Soap Making

Soap is one of the easiest manufacturing businesses to start in Africa. The chemistry is simple, the ingredients are cheap, and every household on your street uses soap every single day. This module shows you what soap making really involves and what it can earn.

Module 1 of 12 · Soap Making · practical exercises · quiz at the end

Lesson 1.1 - Why soap is a great first business

Soap has four properties no other product has:

  • Universal demand. Every household buys soap weekly. No marketing needed to explain what it is.
  • Cheap to start. $30-50 of ingredients makes your first 100 bars.
  • High margin. A bar costs $0.20-0.40 to make. It sells for $0.80-2.00.
  • Made from local inputs. Caustic soda, cooking oil, water, fragrance. All available in any town.

Compare this to other manufacturing: you need machines, permits, storage, and distribution. Soap needs a bucket, a mould, and a kitchen.

Lesson 1.2 - The three soap-making methods

MethodTimeDifficultyBest for
Cold process (CP)1 hour + 4 week cureMediumBest quality bars, small business
Hot process (HP)2-3 hours + 1 week cureMedium-hardFaster turnaround
Melt and pour (MP)30 minutes, no cureEasyBeginners, fancy shapes, gifts

This course teaches cold process. It gives the best bars, the lowest ingredient cost, and the most control over quality.

Lesson 1.4 - What you can earn

A 100 g bar of handmade soap sells for $0.80-2.00 in African cities. Your cost per bar is $0.20-0.40. Every batch of 100 bars:

LineValue
Materials$30
Labour (4 hours of your time)-
Total cost$30
Sale at $1.00/bar$100
Profit per batch$70

Two batches a week = $560/month. That is a real income from a kitchen.

The soap rule: Never sell a bar below $0.60. Below that, you are competing with industrial soap and losing. Handmade soap is a premium product - price it like one.
Practical exercise 1.1: Call 3 pharmacies and 3 markets. Ask what handmade soap sells for and who buys it. Write down the prices.
Practical exercise 1.2: Make a list of your local suppliers for caustic soda, cooking oil, and fragrance. Write their prices.
