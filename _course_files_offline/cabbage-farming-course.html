â  SAFETY WARNING â Chemical & Food Safety

This module covers post-harvest handling of food and may involve pesticides, fungicides, and cleaning chemicals. Always follow these rules:

  • Never use agricultural pesticides on stored grain or fresh produce unless the label explicitly permits post-harvest use. Off-label use is illegal and dangerous.
  • Always wear gloves, a mask, and eye protection when handling any chemical. Wash hands and clothing immediately after use.
  • Respect pre-harvest intervals (PHI) and maximum residue limits (MRLs). Buyers in the EU and UK reject consignments that exceed MRLs â this is a direct business risk.
  • Store chemicals in a locked, ventilated area away from food, feed, and children. Never decant into drink bottles.
  • If you suspect poisoning, contact your local poison control centre or health facility immediately. In Kenya, call the Poison Control Centre at +254 20 271 4000.
  • Keep a record of every chemical application: date, product, rate, and operator. This is required for many export certifications (GlobalG.A.P., KenyaGAP).

Quick Reference Checklist

  1. Harvest at the right maturity. Use a refractometer or maturity chart for your crop. Immature or over-mature produce loses value at every subsequent stage.
  2. Harvest in the cool of the day. Early morning or late evening reduces field heat and slows respiration. Field heat is the number one enemy of shelf life.
  3. Sort and grade within 2 hours of harvest. Remove damaged, diseased, and undersized fruit immediately. One rotten fruit can spoil a whole crate.
  4. Use clean, ventilated crates. Never use sacks for fresh produce. Plastic crates reduce bruising by up to 30% compared to woven bags.
  5. Cool or shade immediately. If you cannot afford a cold room, use evaporative cooling (wet sand, charcoal, or a zero-energy cooling chamber).
  6. Keep records. Track harvest date, volume, losses, and sales. This data is your evidence when applying for credit or negotiating with buyers.
  7. Train your team. Every worker who touches the produce affects its quality. A 30-minute training on gentle handling pays for itself in one harvest.
  8. Plan your market before you plant. A contract or confirmed buyer before harvest eliminates the pressure to sell at any price.
