Practical Reference

The okrika (mitumba) trade rewards operators who treat it like a supply chain business, not a lucky dip. Your margin lives in three decisions: what bale grade you buy, how accurately you sort, and how fast you turn stock. Everything below is built to help you make those three calls with real numbers instead of guesswork.

â  SAFETY & COMPLIANCE WARNING â READ BEFORE YOU BUY OR SELL

  • Wash and sanitise every item before resale. Imported bales may carry dust, mould, mites, and occasionally chemical residues from fumigation. Wash in hot water with detergent, dry in direct sunlight, and steam-press where possible.
  • Wear gloves and a mask when opening and sorting bales. Dust and mould spores from compressed bales are a real respiratory risk. Sort in a ventilated space, never in a closed bedroom.
  • Do not sell visibly contaminated stock. Items with mould, blood stains, or chemical odour must be removed from the sellable pile â reselling them exposes you to legal liability and destroys customer trust.
  • Check national import rules. Several African governments (including Rwanda and Uganda) have restricted or taxed second-hand clothing imports. Confirm current duties and any bans with your revenue authority before importing.
  • Never use unregulated chemicals to "brighten" or fumigate stock. Only use approved cleaning products in the recommended dilution. Keep all chemicals out of reach of children.

Quick Reference Checklist

  1. Confirm local bale prices before you buy. Call at least 3 wholesalers, compare grade, weight, and price per kg, and inspect a sample bale if allowed.
  2. Set a strict stock budget. Never spend more than 60% of your working capital on a single bale â keep cash for rent, transport, and restocking.
  3. Sort into at least 4 piles: premium, standard, budget, and rag. Price each pile differently and track the ratio per bale.
  4. Wash, dry, and press every sellable item before it hits the shelf. Clean stock sells faster and at higher prices.
  5. Price by piece, not by bale. Use a simple cost-plus formula (cost per piece Ã 3 to 6) and adjust for brand, condition, and season.
  6. Record every sale in a book or app. Track daily revenue, top-selling categories, and dead stock so you know what to buy next time.
  7. Run at least two sales channels â a physical stall plus WhatsApp/Instagram â so slow days in one channel are covered by the other.
  8. Reinvest 30â40% of monthly profit into the next bale and a small emergency fund before paying yourself a large salary.
