Practical Reference

This reference block is designed for use in the field and at the desk. It consolidates the operational data that African agribusiness managers, extension officers, and aggregators need when planning production, pricing, storage, and market entry. All figures are drawn from publicly available official sources and are intended to be used as benchmarks, not as fixed targets. Local conditions, input costs, and buyer specifications will always override generic figures, but having a defensible reference point is the fastest way to avoid underpricing a harvest or overinvesting in the wrong input.

â  SAFETY WARNING â CHEMICAL AND FOOD HANDLING

Agrochemicals and food products in the same value chain carry serious health and legal risks. Observe the following without exception:

  • Always wear the full PPE specified on the label â gloves, boots, long sleeves, eye protection, and a respirator when spraying. Never spray against the wind.
  • Respect the pre-harvest interval (PHI) on every pesticide label. Selling produce inside the PHI window can breach national food safety law and destroy buyer contracts.
  • Store agrochemicals in a locked, ventilated, labelled cabinet â never in the same room as food, feed, seed, or drinking water.
  • Never decant pesticides into beverage bottles or unlabelled containers. This is a leading cause of accidental poisoning in rural Africa.
  • Wash hands, face, and equipment with soap after handling. Do not eat, drink, or smoke during application.
  • For aflatoxin-prone crops (maize, groundnut, cottonseed), dry to safe moisture (13% or below for maize) and reject mouldy grain. Aflatoxin is a carcinogen and a major export barrier.
  • Keep the national poison control number and your nearest clinic contact posted at the store and in the spray team's vehicle.

Quick Reference Checklist

  1. Confirm the buyer before you plant. A signed offtake agreement or written letter of intent converts a speculative crop into a bankable enterprise.
  2. Calculate cost of production per hectare and per tonne. Include seed, fertiliser, labour, transport, levies, and a 10% contingency. Know your break-even price before you negotiate.
  3. Test your soil. A basic soil test (pH, N, P, K, organic matter) costs less than one bag of fertiliser and prevents waste on the wrong input.
  4. Source certified seed and registered inputs. Keep receipts and batch numbers. Counterfeit inputs are the fastest route to a failed season.
  5. Plan post-harvest handling before harvest. Secure drying, threshing, grading, and storage capacity. Every point of moisture above safe storage level is a loss waiting to happen.
  6. Track market prices weekly. Use at least two independent sources (e.g. a national market information system plus a local trader quote) to avoid being mispriced.
  7. Maintain records. Field diary, input log, sales ledger, and a simple cash-flow sheet. Records are the collateral that unlocks formal finance.
  8. Comply with food safety and traceability rules. If you export or supply supermarkets, know the maximum residue limits (MRLs), aflatoxin limits, and labelling requirements for your destination market.
