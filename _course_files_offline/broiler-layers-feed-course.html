â  SAFETY WARNING â Pesticides, Mycotoxins and Stored Grain

  • Never use unregistered or banned pesticides. In many African countries, highly hazardous pesticides (HHPs) such as certain organophosphates and paraquat are restricted or prohibited. Check your national pesticide registration authority (e.g. KEPHIS in Kenya, NAFDAC in Nigeria, DALRRD in South Africa) before purchase.
  • Always wear the full PPE stated on the label: gloves, boots, long sleeves, eye protection, and a respirator when spraying. Never spray against the wind or in the heat of midday.
  • Respect the pre-harvest interval (PHI) and re-entry interval. Selling produce with pesticide residues above maximum residue limits (MRLs) can cause rejection at export markets and harm consumers.
  • Aflatoxin risk: Poorly dried or damp-stored maize and groundnuts can develop aflatoxins, which are carcinogenic and can cause acute poisoning. Dry to safe moisture (maize â¤13%) and store in dry, ventilated conditions. Never blend mouldy grain into clean grain for sale.
  • Store pesticides in a locked, labelled cabinet away from food, feed, and children. Never decant into beverage bottles.
  • If poisoning is suspected, contact a poison information centre or nearest health facility immediately and bring the product label. Do not induce vomiting unless instructed by a medical professional.

Quick Reference Checklist

  1. Cost your enterprise per hectare or per unit before the season starts â include seed, fertiliser, labour, transport, and a contingency line of at least 10%.
  2. Test your soil at least once every three years and apply fertiliser based on results, not habit. Blanket application wastes money.
  3. Dry and store correctly. Measure moisture with a meter before bagging; use hermetic bags or silos for grain you intend to hold for more than one month.
  4. Keep records daily. Track inputs, labour, yields, and sales in a simple notebook or mobile app. Records are required for credit and are the basis of any credible business plan.
  5. Know your market before you plant. Confirm at least one buyer, price range, and quality specification (grade, moisture, aflatoxin limits) before committing land.
  6. Protect yourself and your workers. Provide PPE, train on safe pesticide handling, and observe PHIs. Document training.
  7. Diversify income streams. Combine a staple, a cash crop, and a value-added activity (e.g. milling, packaging, livestock) to smooth price and weather shocks.
  8. Join or form a farmer organisation. Aggregation lowers input costs, improves market bargaining power, and is often a precondition for off-taker contracts and structured finance.
