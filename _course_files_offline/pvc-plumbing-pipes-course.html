Poultry production involves vaccines, antibiotics, coccidiostats, and disinfectants. Misuse harms your birds, your customers, and your business reputation. Follow these non-negotiables:

  • Withdrawal periods are law. Never sell eggs or meat before the drug withdrawal period ends (typically 5â10 days for most antibiotics; check the label). Residues can lead to market rejection and legal penalties.
  • Antibiotics are not growth promoters. The WHO and FAO recommend phasing out routine antibiotic use in healthy flocks. Use only under veterinary prescription for diagnosed disease.
  • Vaccines need a cold chain. Store at 2â8Â°C. A broken cold chain means dead vaccine and vulnerable birds. Use a cooler box with ice packs when transporting.
  • Disinfectants are corrosive. Wear gloves, eye protection, and a mask when handling formalin, glutaraldehyde, or concentrated bleach. Never mix bleach with ammonia-based cleaners â it releases toxic gas.
  • Dispose of dead birds properly. Bury at least 1 metre deep, or incinerate. Never sell or feed dead birds to other animals. This spreads disease and can cause human illness.
  • Keep records. Log every drug, date, dose, and withdrawal period. Buyers and regulators increasingly demand traceability.

Quick Reference Checklist

Print this and pin it inside your poultry house. Run through it every morning before you do anything else.

  1. Check water and feed lines first. A blocked drinker in 35Â°C heat can kill birds in hours. Clean, cool water is the cheapest performance booster you have.
  2. Walk the flock and look for sick birds. Isolate any bird that is drooping, gasping, or has diarrhoea. Early isolation saves the whole flock.
  3. Record daily mortality, feed intake, and egg production. If feed intake drops 10% or mortality doubles, call your vet that day â not next week.
  4. Verify biosecurity. Footbath at the entrance, no visitors without boots and overalls, no mixing age groups, and keep wild birds out.
  5. Check ventilation and litter. Ammonia smell means litter is wet and ventilation is poor. Add dry wood shavings and open vents.
  6. Confirm the vaccination schedule. Newcastle, Gumboro, and fowl pox are non-negotiable in most African climates. Mark the calendar and stick to it.
