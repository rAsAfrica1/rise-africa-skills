â  SAFETY WARNING â Pesticides, Food Safety and Worker Health

Horticulture is a high-input, high-risk sector. Misuse of crop protection products causes acute poisoning, chronic illness, and export rejections. Follow these non-negotiables:

  • Only use registered products approved by your national regulator (e.g. Kenya PCPB, Ghana EPA, Nigeria NAFDAC). Never use unlabelled or banned chemicals.
  • Respect pre-harvest intervals (PHIs). Export rejections for MRL breaches are permanent commercial losses. Keep a spray log with dates, product, dose, and PHI.
  • Wear full PPE â gloves, boots, coveralls, eye protection, and a respirator when mixing. Re-entry intervals apply to workers too.
  • Never store pesticides near food, feed, or water. Lock them separately. Triple-rinse empty containers and puncture them.
  • Wash hands and produce. Use clean water for post-harvest washing; contaminated water spreads cholera, typhoid, and E. coli.
  • Train every worker before they handle chemicals. Keep a signed training register â buyers and auditors will ask for it.

Quick Reference Checklist

  1. Know your buyer before you plant. Secure a written off-take agreement or confirmed order specifying grade, volume, price basis, and delivery window.
  2. Cost every input and every hour. Use Table 2 as a template. If you cannot state your cost per kilogram, you cannot negotiate price.
  3. Grade and sort at harvest, not at market. Uniform grade lifts price 20â60% and reduces transport of unsellable produce.
  4. Cut post-harvest loss. Harvest in the cool of the day, use clean crates, shade, and a cold room within 4 hours of picking.
  5. Keep records. Spray logs, harvest records, sales invoices, and worker training registers are required for certification and finance.
  6. Manage food safety. Clean water, clean hands, clean crates, clean vehicle. One contamination event can end a supermarket contract.
  7. Access finance deliberately. Build 12 months of records, then approach a bank, SACCO, or agri-fintech with a costed plan and a buyer contract.
  8. Plan for certification early. GlobalG.A.P., KenyaGAP, or organic certification takes 6â12 months and requires documented compliance.
