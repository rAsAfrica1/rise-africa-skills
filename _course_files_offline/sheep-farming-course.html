â  SAFETY WARNING â AFLATOXIN AND AGROCHEMICAL HANDLING

Aflatoxin is a silent killer. It is invisible, odourless and heat-stable â cooking does not destroy it. Chronic exposure causes liver cancer and stunting in children; acute exposure can be fatal. Never sell, consume or blend mouldy grain into clean stock. Do not attempt to "dilute" contaminated maize with sound maize to meet a buyer's limit; this is adulteration, is illegal in most African jurisdictions, and shifts a health hazard downstream.

Always wear gloves, a dust mask and eye protection when handling pesticides, fumigants or treated seed. Never eat, drink or smoke during application. Store agrochemicals in a locked, ventilated, labelled cabinet away from food, feed and children. Observe the pre-harvest interval on every label. If you suspect poisoning, contact your nearest poison control centre or health facility immediately and take the product label with you.

Regulatory note: consignments exceeding Codex or national aflatoxin limits may be seized, destroyed and result in prosecution. Test before you trade.

Quick Reference Checklist

  1. Test moisture before storage. Use a calibrated moisture meter; do not rely on bite or feel. Target 13.5 percent or below for maize and 8â9 percent for groundnut kernels.
  2. Test aflatoxin before every sale. Use a rapid test kit or accredited laboratory. Keep the certificate with the lot number and date.
  3. Use hermetic storage. Hermetic bags or silos cut insect damage and mould risk without chemicals and typically pay back within one season.
  4. Cost your enterprise per hectare, per tonne and per bag. Know your break-even price before you negotiate. A gross margin you cannot calculate is a loss you have not yet discovered.
  5. Register your business and your produce. Obtain the relevant business registration, tax identification and, where required, produce or export certification.
  6. Write the contract, not just the handshake. Specify quantity, quality parameters, moisture, aflatoxin limit, delivery date, price mechanism and dispute resolution.
  7. Insure what you cannot afford to lose. Index-based crop insurance, warehouse receipt systems and storage insurance are increasingly available across Africa.
  8. Keep records daily. Inputs purchased, labour paid, kilos harvested, kilos sold, prices received. Records are the collateral that unlocks finance.
