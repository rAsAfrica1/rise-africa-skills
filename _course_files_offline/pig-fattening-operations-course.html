â  SAFETY WARNING â Veterinary Drugs, Chemicals and Food Safety

Pig production involves direct contact with veterinary medicines, disinfectants and potentially zoonotic organisms. Observe the following without exception:

  • Never slaughter or sell a pig within the withdrawal period stated on any antibiotic or anthelmintic label. Residues in meat are a public health hazard and can cost you your market licence.
  • Wear gloves and a mask when handling disinfectants, lime, and medicated feed. Store all chemicals in a locked cabinet away from feed and children.
  • Do not feed swill (kitchen or restaurant waste) unless it has been boiled for at least 30 minutes. Unboiled swill is a recognised route for African Swine Fever and foot-and-mouth disease introduction.
  • Wash hands with soap after handling pigs, and never eat, drink or smoke inside the piggery.
  • If you suspect African Swine Fever â sudden deaths, skin discolouration, abortion â do not move animals or sell meat. Report immediately to your local veterinary services.

Quick Reference Checklist

  • Weigh a sample monthly. Weigh at least 10% of your pigs every month and compare average daily gain against the 600 g benchmark. If you are below 500 g, review feed quality and health before anything else.
  • Calculate your FCR each cycle. Total feed used divided by total weight gained. Above 3.5 means you are losing money on every kilogram of pork produced.
  • Source piglets from a known, disease-free farm. Ask for the breeder's health record. Cheap piglets from unknown sources are the most expensive purchase you will ever make.
  • Vaccinate and deworm on a written schedule. Keep a wall chart with dates for each pen and mark it off. Unrecorded treatments are unmanaged treatments.
  • Keep feed dry and off the floor. Use pallets and sealed bins. Mouldy feed causes poor growth and, in the worst case, mycotoxin poisoning.
  • Provide constant clean water. A growerâfinisher pig drinks 6â10 litres per day. Restricted water cuts feed intake within hours.
  • Maintain a simple cash record. Record every purchase and sale the same day. You cannot price your pigs correctly without knowing your true cost per kilogram.
  • Secure a buyer before the pigs reach market weight. Line up two or three outlets â butcheries, hotels, fellow farmers â so you never sell under pressure at a discount.
