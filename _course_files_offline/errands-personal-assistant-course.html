Practical Reference

Errand and personal-assistant (PA) work is one of the few service businesses in Africa where you can launch with zero inventory, zero premises and near-zero capital. Your product is time and reliability. Your margin is built on route density, repeat clients and trust. The reference material below gives you the operating numbers, the trust architecture and the growth levers you need to turn a solo runner into a small agency.

â  SAFETY WARNING â Handling Money, Documents and Medicines for Clients

Errand runners routinely handle cash, identity documents, bank cards, PINs and, in PA work, sometimes prescription medicines and children. This creates real legal and health risk. Follow these rules without exception:

  • Never accept a client's bank card and PIN. Offer to accompany the client, or use a mobile-money transfer instead.
  • Never collect prescription medicines on someone else's behalf unless you have written authorisation and understand the dosage. A wrong medicine can kill. Where possible, the client or a family member collects medicines themselves.
  • Always issue a written receipt for every cash amount received, and photograph it for your records. Never mix client money with your own â keep a separate wallet or mobile-money float.
  • Always confirm the identity of the person handing over documents, cash or keys. Fraudsters pose as clients.
  • Never share client addresses, gate codes, PINs or children's details with anyone, including other clients or on social media.
  • If you carry cash above a small threshold, vary your routes and times, and avoid displaying money in public.

Quick Reference Checklist

  1. Run the Iron-Clad Price Check first. Call 3 suppliers or service providers and compare before you buy any equipment or set any price.
  2. Define your three core services. Pick the errands you can do fastest and most reliably. Do not offer everything on day one.
  3. Set a written price list with a low-high range, transport rules and a cancellation policy. Send it to every new client before the first job.
  4. Open a WhatsApp Business profile with your name, photo, service list and hours. This is your shopfront â keep it professional.
  5. Issue a receipt for every job. Use a numbered receipt book and record the job in your record book the same day.
  6. Convert every one-off client into a monthly retainer. Retainers are the difference between hustling and running a business.
  7. Protect client confidentiality. Never share addresses, PINs, documents or children's details. This is your reputation.
  8. Review your numbers every Sunday. Jobs completed, income in, costs out, clients gained. Adjust your prices and routes weekly.
