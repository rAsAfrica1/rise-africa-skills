Practical Reference

This reference block is designed for use in the field and at the desk. It consolidates the numbers, safety rules and decision points that African agribusiness operators need when moving from training to transactions. Treat it as a working document: print it, laminate it, and keep it with your production records. Every figure below is traceable to a named public source so that you can cite it in a loan application, an offtake contract, or a grant proposal.

â  SAFETY WARNING â AGRO-CHEMICALS, AFLATOXIN AND FOOD HANDLING

  • Never re-use pesticide containers for drinking water, cooking oil or food storage. Triple-rinse and puncture containers, then dispose of them at a designated hazardous waste point.
  • Always wear gloves, a long-sleeved shirt, trousers, boots and a face mask when mixing or spraying. Never spray against the wind, and never spray within 24 hours of harvest.
  • Observe the pre-harvest interval on the label. Eating or selling produce before the interval expires leaves illegal residues and can cause acute poisoning.
  • Aflatoxin is a cancer-causing toxin produced by moulds on poorly dried groundnut, maize and sorghum. Dry grain to below 13% moisture, store off the floor, and never blend mouldy grain into clean grain for sale. Blending is illegal in most African markets and destroys buyer trust permanently.
  • If you or a worker shows dizziness, blurred vision, sweating or vomiting after handling chemicals, stop work, remove contaminated clothing, wash with soap and water, and go to the nearest health facility immediately with the product label.
  • Keep all chemicals locked away from children and animals. Store them below food, never above it.

Quick Reference Checklist

  1. Know your numbers before you plant. Build a one-page budget per hectare using local input prices and the last three seasons' farm-gate prices. If the net margin is under 20% of revenue, renegotiate inputs or change the enterprise.
  2. Secure the buyer before the seed. A signed offtake or supply agreement covering at least 60% of expected output turns a crop into a bankable business.
  3. Test your soil once every three years. Fertiliser applied without a soil test is money spent on guesswork. Use your national soil laboratory or an accredited private lab.
  4. Dry and store to standard. Target below 13% moisture for grain and below 10% for groundnut. Use hermetic bags or metal silos; never store on bare ground.
  5. Keep a simple record book. Record every input purchase, labour day, harvest weight and sale. Records are the first thing a lender or off-taker asks for.
  6. Insure the risk you cannot manage. Index-based crop insurance is available in Kenya, Nigeria, Ghana, Ethiopia, Senegal and Zambia. Price it against your worst-case loss.
  7. Comply with standards. Check the destination market's maximum residue limits and aflatoxin thresholds before you ship. One rejected container can end a buyer relationship.
  8. Reinvest a fixed share of margin. Allocate at least 15% of net margin to the next season's inputs and 5% to equipment maintenance.
