â  SAFETY WARNING â READ BEFORE YOU MAKE ANYTHING

This course involves chemicals and plant materials that can cause serious harm if mishandled. Do not skip this.

  • Sodium hydroxide (lye) causes severe burns and blindness. Always add lye to water â never water to lye. Wear chemical splash goggles, nitrile gloves and long sleeves. Work in a ventilated space, away from children and animals.
  • Essential oils are not harmless. Many are skin sensitisers or toxic in high doses. Follow IFRA (International Fragrance Association) usage limits. Never sell undiluted essential oils for skin use without professional advice.
  • Preservatives are mandatory for any water-containing product. Lotions, creams and aloe gels grow mould and bacteria within days. An unpreserved water-based product is a health hazard, not a "natural" selling point.
  • Patch-test every new formula on a small area of skin for 48 hours before selling. Keep a written record.
  • Never claim a product cures, treats or prevents disease. In most African jurisdictions that converts your cosmetic into an unregistered medicine â a legal offence. Keep claims to cleansing, moisturising and cosmetic care.
  • Label every jar with a batch number and expiry date. If a customer reacts, you must be able to trace and recall that batch.

Quick Reference Checklist

  1. Confirm three supplier prices for every core input (shea, oils, jars, lye) before you set your selling price.
  2. Write your recipe down in grams and percentages. A formula you cannot repeat is not a business â it is a one-off.
  3. Test each new formula on yourself for 48 hours and record the result before offering it to anyone else.
  4. Add a preservative to every water-based product and follow the supplier's usage rate exactly.
  5. Keep a batch record book: date, batch number, ingredients, quantities, who made it, expiry date.
  6. Label correctly: product name, net weight, ingredient list (INCI where possible), batch number, expiry date, your contact details.
  7. Cost every batch before you sell it. Know your unit cost, wholesale price and retail price. Never price by guesswork.
  8. Check local cosmetics regulations with your national standards body or ministry of health before you sell beyond friends and family.
