Personal protective equipment (PPE) is not optional. When applying pesticides, herbicides, or fungicides:

  • Wear gloves, goggles, a respirator or nose mask, long sleeves, trousers, and boots. Never apply chemicals barefoot or in shorts.
  • Never eat, drink, or smoke while handling agrochemicals. Wash hands and face with soap immediately after application.
  • Store chemicals in original labelled containers, locked away from children, food, and animal feed. Never decant into soft drink bottles.
  • Observe the pre-harvest interval (PHI) on the label â typically 7â21 days between final spray and harvest. Selling produce before PHI expires can cause consumer poisoning and destroys buyer trust.
  • Dispose of empty containers by triple-rinsing and puncturing, then returning to a licensed collection point where available. Never reuse for water or food.

Aflatoxin alert: In maize and groundnuts, poor drying and damp storage produce aflatoxins â invisible, odourless, and carcinogenic. Grain above 13% moisture content is at high risk. Dry to 12â13% moisture, store on pallets off the floor, and test before selling to processors or exporters. Rejected consignments are a commercial loss; contaminated food is a public health emergency.

First aid: If chemical contacts skin or eyes, rinse with clean water for 15 minutes. If swallowed, do not induce vomiting â call the nearest poison control centre or health facility immediately and bring the product label.

Quick Reference Checklist

  1. Validate your cost of production with at least three local quotes for seed, fertiliser, labour, and transport before pricing your product or applying for credit.
  2. Calculate gross margin per hectare (or per animal, per litre) â not total revenue. A high-revenue enterprise with a 5% margin is less bankable than a modest one at 30%.
  3. Map your post-harvest loss points from field to buyer. Even a 10% reduction in losses often beats a 10% yield increase in profitability.
  4. Secure at least two buyers before planting. Verbal agreements are weak; a signed off-take contract or purchase order strengthens your loan file.
  5. Check moisture content before storage or sale using a calibrated moisture meter. For maize and groundnuts, target 12â13% to avoid aflatoxin and price discounts.
  6. Keep records of every input purchase, labour payment, and sale. Lenders and investors require at least 12 months of written records â a notebook or mobile app both work.
  7. Register your business (sole proprietorship, cooperative, or limited company) and obtain any required phytosanitary or food safety certification for your target market.
  8. Reinvest a fixed percentage of gross margin (15â25%) into next season's inputs, equipment maintenance, or a savings buffer before drawing personal income.
