Health hazard: Poorly dried or improperly stored maize, groundnuts, and other grains can develop Aspergillus flavus mould, producing aflatoxins â a Group 1 carcinogen. Chronic exposure causes liver cancer, stunting in children, and immune suppression. In Sub-Saharan Africa, an estimated 25,000-155,000 deaths per year are linked to aflatoxin exposure (Source: WHO, 2018).

Chemical safety: If using storage pesticides (e.g., phosphine fumigants, Actellic dust), always:

  • Wear gloves, a dust mask, and eye protection.
  • Never eat, drink, or smoke during application.
  • Store chemicals in locked, ventilated areas away from food and children.
  • Observe the full pre-harvest interval and re-entry period on the label.
  • Dispose of empty containers through licensed hazardous waste channels â never reuse for food or water.

Action: Test grain for aflatoxin before selling or consuming. Rapid test kits are available from KALRO, IITA, and local agrodealers. If levels exceed 10 ppb (Kenya) or 20 ppb (USA/EU), do not sell for human consumption.

Quick Reference Checklist: Post-Harvest Loss Reduction Actions

  1. Harvest at correct maturity: Test moisture before harvest. For maize, harvest at 20-25% moisture, then dry immediately.
  2. Dry to safe moisture: Target 13% for maize, 14% for rice, 9% for groundnuts. Use a moisture meter â do not guess.
  3. Use hermetic storage: PICS bags, cocoons, or metal silos. These cut storage losses from 20% to under 5%.
  4. Clean and sort before storage: Remove broken grains, chaff, and mouldy kernels. Broken grains attract pests and mould.
  5. Treat with approved pesticides only when needed: Follow label rates. Never mix chemicals. Rotate active ingredients to prevent resistance.
  6. Monitor monthly: Inspect stored grain for temperature rise, moisture, insects, or mould. Act within 48 hours of detection.
  7. Record everything: Track input costs, harvest weight, storage losses, and sale price. This data unlocks credit and insurance.
  8. Sell strategically: Use warehouse receipt systems or aggregation to sell in the lean season when prices are 30-50% higher.
