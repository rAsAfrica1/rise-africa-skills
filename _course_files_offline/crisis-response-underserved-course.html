â  SAFETY WARNING â Health, Water and Chemical Handling

This course covers emergency health response and water treatment, which involve chemicals, infection risk and medical procedures. Observe the following without exception:

  • Chlorine: Never mix chlorine products with ammonia, acids or fuels. Mix only in a well-ventilated area, wear gloves and eye protection, and store away from food and children.
  • ORS and cholera: Use only sachets within expiry date and mix with safe water. Do not improvise concentrations â incorrect mixes can worsen dehydration.
  • Infection control: Treat all body fluids as infectious. Use gloves, wash hands with soap, and disinfect surfaces. Community responders are not a substitute for a qualified clinician for severe cases â refer immediately.
  • Water treatment: Never distribute untreated surface water as drinking water. Verify free residual chlorine before distribution.
  • Records: Log every chemical used, batch number, quantity and location. This protects you legally and is required by most funders.

Quick Reference Checklist

  • Confirm local prices before you quote. Call at least three suppliers for water tanks, chlorine, tarpaulins, ORS and fuel, and record the date and price â seasonal variation in African markets can exceed 40%.
  • Map your district's hazards. List the three most likely crises for your area (flood, drought, cholera, fire, displacement) and write a one-page response plan for each.
  • Pre-position stock. Keep a minimum kit: water treatment chemicals, ORS, first aid, tarpaulins, rope, torches, and a battery radio. Rotate stock monthly and log expiry dates.
  • Train and drill. Run a simulation at least once per quarter. Record response time and gaps â this becomes evidence for funders.
  • Register and document. Formalise as an NGO, trust or social enterprise; keep minutes, a constitution and a bank account. Many donors will not fund informal groups.
  • Build three funding lines. Preparedness contracts (monthly retainer), activation fees (per event), and training fees (per participant). Do not rely on one donor.
  • Coordinate, don't duplicate. Register with your district civil protection unit and the relevant cluster (WASH, health, food security) so you are called, not bypassed.
  • Track cost per beneficiary. Calculate total cost divided by people reached. Funders compare this figure across applicants â keep it lean and documented.
