This module touches on storage chemicals, fumigants, and food handling. Read before you act:

  • Never store grain fumigants (e.g. phosphine tablets) in the same room as food, feed, or seed for planting. They release toxic gas â sealed, ventilated, locked storage only, with warning signage.
  • Never use agricultural pesticides on stored food crops unless the label explicitly lists that crop and that use. Off-label use is illegal in most African jurisdictions and creates export rejections.
  • Cold chain: raw milk, meat, and fish must be held at or below 4Â°C and frozen goods at â18Â°C or colder. A single break of more than two hours means do not sell it â write it off and record why.
  • Aflatoxin: if grain smells musty, shows mould, or tests above 10 ppb (the common export threshold), do not blend it into clean stock. Blending hides the problem and destroys buyer trust permanently.
  • Personal protection: gloves, dust mask, eye protection, and boots when handling any chemical, fumigant, or mouldy stock. Wash hands and change clothes before eating.
  • Record everything: batch, date, chemical, dose, operator. In a recall or a buyer audit, the operator with records survives and the one without does not.

Quick Reference Checklist

  • Count your handovers. Map every point where goods change hands or change vehicle. Each one is a loss and a cost. Target the three worst.
  • Set a reorder point, not a feeling. Reorder point = (average daily sales Ã lead time in days) + safety stock. Write it on the bin.
  • Know your stock turn. Stock turnover = cost of goods sold Ã· average inventory value. If it is under 4 turns a year in food, you are financing spoilage.
  • Weigh and grade at intake. Record weight, moisture, and grade at the moment goods arrive. Disputes are won or lost at intake, not at delivery.
  • Get three quotes for every recurring input. Compare landed cost, not sticker price â include transport, duty, and loss.
  • Insure the moving parts. Goods-in-transit and stock cover are cheap relative to one written-off truckload.
  • Keep a one-page supplier scorecard. On-time delivery %, quality rejects %, price trend. Review monthly, act quarterly.
  • Separate cash from stock. Never let one customer's credit terms fund your inventory. Match payment terms to your cash cycle.
