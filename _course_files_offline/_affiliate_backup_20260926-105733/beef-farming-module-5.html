5. Breeding and Calving

Good breeding turns 40 cows into 36 calves a year. Bad breeding wastes feed on empty cows.

Module 5 of 12 · Beef Farming for Profit

Cattle breeding basics

ParameterValue
Age at puberty (heifers)12-18 months
Age at first calving24-30 months (target)
Oestrus cycleEvery 18-24 days (average 21)
Heat duration12-18 hours
Gestation280-285 days (about 9.5 months)
Calving interval (target)12 months
Calving rate (target)85-90%

Signs of heat in cows

  • Standing to be mounted - the primary sign
  • Restless, seeking the bull
  • Mucus discharge from vulva
  • Swollen, reddened vulva
  • Bellowing more than usual
  • Reduced feed intake
  • Mounting other cows
  • Tailhead rubbed or dirty from being mounted

Heat lasts 12-18 hours. Best mating time: 12 hours after heat begins (or "am/pm rule" - if heat seen in morning, breed that evening; if seen in evening, breed next morning).

Breeding methods

MethodDescriptionBest for
Natural serviceBull runs with cows or hand-matingSmall herds, low technology
Hand matingBring cow to bull when in heatTracking individual pedigrees
Artificial insemination (AI)Controlled by technician with frozen semenElite genetics, no bull on farm
Embryo transferDonor cows produce multiple embryosElite breeders, high value

Bull management

Bull ageCows servedNotes
Young (2-3 years)20-25 cowsStill growing
Prime (3-6 years)30-40 cowsPeak performance
Older (6-8 years)20-30 cowsDeclining fertility
  • Bull must be fertile - check testicles (two, equal, firm), libido, and serving ability.
  • Keep bull separate from cows when not breeding - saves energy and prevents fighting.
  • Body condition score 3-4 during breeding season.
  • Feed 2-3 kg concentrate per day during heavy service.
  • Replace every 3-4 years to prevent inbreeding and maintain vigour.

The breeding calendar

MonthActivity

Aim for a defined breeding season (60-90 days) so calves are born together. Easier to manage and market.

Pregnancy diagnosis

MethodWhenAccuracy
Palpation (rectal)6-8 weeksHigh

Check all cows 6-8 weeks after mating. Cows that are open (not pregnant) should be re-bred or culled.

Calving

Normal calving takes 2-6 hours from first stage to delivery. Signs:

  • Restless, isolating from the herd
  • Tail raised, back arched
  • Frequent urination and defecation
  • Water bag appears
  • Feet and head of calf visible within 1-2 hours of water breaking

When to assist

  • Over 2 hours of hard straining with no progress
  • Water broke over 2 hours ago with no calf
  • Only one foot or tail presented
  • Cow exhausted

Wash hands, use gloves, lubricate, gently pull with contractions. If in doubt, call a vet - a difficult calving can kill both cow and calf.

Care of the newborn calf

  1. Clear nostrils of mucus - help breathing.
  2. Let the cow lick the calf dry - bond forms fast.
  3. Ensure the calf nurses within 2 hours - colostrum is critical for immunity.
  4. Dip navel in iodine to prevent infection.
  5. Weigh and record birth weight.
  6. Identify with ear tag - track lineage.
  7. Observe for first 24 hours - calving complications can appear late.

Weaning

Fence-line weaning6-8 monthsCalves can see and smell mother, low stress

Weaning tips:

  • Vaccinate calves 2-3 weeks before weaning.
  • Deworm at weaning.
  • Feed a high-quality ration for 2-3 weeks post-weaning.
  • Observe calves daily - stress can trigger pneumonia.

The calving interval rule: A cow should calve every 12 months. Every extra month costs you $50-100 in lost production. Feed cows well, breed on a defined schedule, and cull cows that cannot hold the 12-month cycle.
Practical exercise 5: Record heat dates of all cycling cows. Mark expected calving dates on the calendar (285 days from mating). Observe calving cows daily and record every birth.
