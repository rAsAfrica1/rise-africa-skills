3. Housing, Stabling and Facilities

Horses need different housing from cattle. Here is how to build it right.

Module 3 of 12 · Horse Breeding for Profit

Why housing matters

Horses are more sensitive to their environment than cattle. They need shelter from sun, wind, and rain; dry, clean bedding; and safe fencing. Poor housing causes respiratory disease, thrush, laminitis, and behavioural vices.

Space requirements

AnimalStable (m2)Paddock (m2)Pasture (ha)

For a small breeding operation, box stalls for mares plus large paddocks plus pasture is standard.

Stable construction

  1. Solid construction - brick, stone, or treated timber. Fire-resistant where possible.
  2. Size: 3.5 x 3.5 m minimum per horse (12-16 m2).
  3. Roof: insulated to keep heat out in summer, warm in winter.
  4. Ventilation: high-level vents, airflow without drafts.
  5. Doors: 1.2 m wide, sliding or Dutch doors (top opens for head).
  6. Floor: concrete or packed earth, 1% slope for drainage.
  7. Bedding: 15-20 cm of straw, shavings, or rubber mats.
  8. Manger: at chest height, removable for cleaning.
  9. Water: automatic drinker or bucket, always accessible.
  10. Hay net or rack: off the ground.

Never use barbed wire for horses - they panic and tear themselves up. Never use sheep wire - horses catch hooves in it.

Quarantine and isolation

Any new horse, or a horse returning from a show or sale, must be quarantined for 3-4 weeks.

  • Separate paddock, 100 m or more from other horses
  • Separate water and feed troughs
  • Separate tools and handling staff
  • Observe daily for signs of disease
  • Blood test if coming from an unknown source
  • Vaccinate and deworm before joining the main herd

Manure and waste management

  • Remove manure from stables daily.
  • Compost manure - it is excellent for gardens and crops.
  • Have a separate muck heap away from stables and water sources.
  • Bedding and manure compost well - turn every 2 weeks.
  • Sell compost to garden centres or landscape companies.

Safety features

  • Wide corridors - at least 2.5 m for leading horses.
  • Non-slip flooring - especially around water and feed.
  • Electric switches - out of reach of horses.
  • Fire extinguishers - in strategic locations.
  • First-aid kit - for both horses and humans.
  • Locked feed room - horses will break into feed and get laminitis.
  • Hot water - for washing wounds and cleaning equipment.
The fencing rule: Never use barbed wire or sheep mesh for horses. A horse caught in barbed wire will tear itself apart before it stops struggling. Invest in safe fencing from day one.
