1. Maize Economics

Real numbers, real yields, real profit - before you plant a single seed.

Module 1 of 6 · Maize Farming for Profit

Why maize

Maize is Africa's most important staple crop. In most countries demand exceeds supply. A well-managed hectare produces 6-10 tonnes of grain, worth $1,500-3,500 at market prices.

MetricValue
Days to maturity90-150 depending on variety
Yield per hectare (good management)6-10 tonnes
Yield per hectare (poor management)1.5-3 tonnes
Total input cost$500-900/ha
Gross revenue$1,200-3,500/ha
Net margin$400-2,000/ha

Why most farmers only get 2 tonnes

  • Late planting - missing the rains
  • Wrong variety for the rainfall zone
  • Low plant population (too wide spacing)
  • No fertiliser, or applied at the wrong time
  • Fall armyworm uncontrolled
  • Poor storage - weevils eat 20-30%

Yield comparison

ManagementYieldRevenue
Traditional - late, no fertiliser, wide spacing1.5-2.5 t$300-500
Improved - correct variety, fertiliser, weeding4-6 t$800-1,800
Commercial - hybrid, full inputs, timely6-10 t$1,500-3,500

What you need

  • Land with good drainage and pH 5.5-7.0
  • Certified seed - hybrid for high input, OPV for low input
  • Fertiliser - DAP at planting, urea or CAN at top dressing
  • Hand hoe or plough for land prep
  • Fall armyworm control
  • Adequate labour for weeding
The maize rule: Plant early. The single biggest yield driver is planting with the first reliable rains.
Practical exercise 1: Ask 3 neighbours what yield they got last year and why. Record the pattern.

Quiz

  2. Name three reasons most farmers only get 2 tonnes.
