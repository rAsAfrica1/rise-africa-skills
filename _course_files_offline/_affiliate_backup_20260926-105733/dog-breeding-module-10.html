10. Selling Puppies and Building Reputation

A puppy sale is the beginning of a relationship. Here is how to sell well and build a name.

Module 10 of 12 · Dog Breeding for Profit

Why selling well matters

Most breeders lose money because they cannot sell puppies at the right price. Two years of work collapses if a litter goes unsold or sells at half price. The skill of selling is as important as the skill of breeding.

Pricing strategy

  1. Know your cost per puppy - feed, vet, registration, kennel share, time.
  2. Research the market - what are similar puppies selling for?
  3. Set three prices - asking price, target price, floor price.
  4. Never sell below floor - it damages your brand forever.
  5. Offer payment plans - some serious buyers cannot pay all at once.
  6. Take a deposit - typically 20-30% to reserve a puppy.

Photographing puppies

  • Natural light - outdoors in shade, or near a window.
  • Clean background - plain blanket or grass.
  • Close-up head shots - clear, sharp, well-lit.
  • Full body shots - standing profile.
  • Play shots - puppies with a toy, showing personality.
  • Video - 1-2 minute clip of puppies playing, following a toy, exploring.
  • Consistent - same setup for each puppy in a litter.
  • Regular - new photos weekly as puppies grow.

Writing a good puppy listing

Every listing should include:

  • Breed and sex - clear and up front.
  • Date of birth and age - exact.
  • Parents'' names and health tests - hip scores, eye results, DNA clearances.
  • Vaccination status - which vaccines, when given, next due.
  • Deworming history - which product, how often.
  • Microchip number - if applicable.
  • Temperament notes - each puppy is different.
  • Ideal home - family, single, active, senior.
  • What is included - puppy pack, food sample, blanket with familiar smell, contract.
  • Price - clear, with deposit details.

Screening buyers

You have the right to refuse a sale. Ask:

  • Why this breed? - a serious buyer has reasons.
  • Where will the dog live? - house, garden, apartment?
  • Who will care for the dog daily? - avoid "the children will".
  • How many hours alone per day? - under 6 hours ideally.
  • Have you owned dogs before? - experience matters.
  • What is your vet''s name? - serious buyers have one.
  • Are you prepared for 10-15 years of commitment? - dogs live long.
  • What will you do if the dog is ill or you move? - red flag if no plan.

Red flags: no questions, wants the puppy "today", will not give a vet reference, wants to pay cash only, refuses a home visit, plans to resell.

Home visits and handovers

  • Home visits - visit the buyer''s home where possible, or ask for photos of the intended living area.
  • Handover - meet in person, hand over vaccination cards, puppy pack, and contract.
  • Do not ship a puppy unaccompanied by an adult human.
  • Do not sell multiple puppies from the same litter to the same household unless they have experience with littermate syndrome.

Littermate syndrome

Two puppies from the same litter raised together often become overly dependent on each other, harder to train, and prone to separation anxiety. If you sell two to the same home:

  • Advise separate sleeping areas and separate training times.
  • Encourage separate walks and separate human bonding.
  • Warn about the risks in writing.

The puppy pack

Every puppy leaves with:

  • Vaccination card
  • Deworming record
  • Microchip certificate (if applicable)
  • Sample of current food (3-7 days)
  • Blanket or toy with littermate scent
  • Care guide (feeding, training, health, first weeks)
  • Contract with health guarantee
  • Your contact details for follow-up

Legal and ethical obligations

  • Disclose all known health issues. Hiding problems destroys reputation and can be illegal.
  • Do not sell puppies under 8 weeks. They need littermates and mother for social skills.
  • Vaccinate and deworm before sale. It is a welfare obligation.
  • Provide a contract. It protects both buyer and breeder.
  • Follow local laws on kennel registration, breeding licences, and sales.
  • Refuse sales to buyers who clearly should not own a dog.
  • Take back any puppy you bred if the buyer cannot keep it. This is what reputable breeders do.
The reputation rule: One dishonest sale destroys 10 years of work. In the dog world, word travels fast. Sell honestly, price fairly, support your buyers, and you will never struggle to sell puppies.
