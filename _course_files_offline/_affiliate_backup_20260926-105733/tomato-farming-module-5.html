5. Pest and Disease Control

Tomato has more enemies than any other vegetable. This module gives the prevention and treatment plan that works.

Module 5 of 6 · Tomato Farming for Profit

The four diseases that kill tomato

DiseaseSignsPrevention
Late blightDark water-soaked patches, white fuzz, sudden collapseDrip irrigation, resistant varieties, spacing
Early blightBrown concentric rings on lower leavesMulch, remove lower leaves, rotation
Bacterial wiltSudden wilting, no yellowing, brown ring in cut stem4-year rotation, avoid waterlogging
Mosaic virusMottled leaves, distortion, no fruitControl aphids, resistant varieties

The five pests

PestDamageControl
Tomato fruit borerHoles in fruit with black frassBt spray, handpicking
AphidsCurled leaves, sticky honeydewSoap spray, ladybirds, neem
WhiteflyYellow stippling, virus spreadYellow sticky traps, neem
Spider mitesFine webbing, bronzed leavesWater spray, predatory mites
CutwormSeedlings cut at soil line overnightCollars, handpick at night

Preventive spray schedule

WeekProductTarget
Week 2Copper hydroxideBacterial spot, blight
Week 4MancozebFungal blights
Week 6Copper + BtBlight + caterpillars
Week 8+Only if needed, short PHIDo not spray at flowering
Never spray broad-spectrum insecticides at flowering. You kill pollinators and lose more yield than the pest.

IPM order of operations

  1. Prevent - rotation, resistant varieties, drip irrigation, mulch.
  2. Monitor - walk daily, count pests.
  3. Handpick - caterpillars, cutworms.
  4. Biological - Bt, neem, soap.
  5. Targeted chemical - correct product, rate, PHI.
Practical exercise 5: Walk your plot daily for 7 days. Record every pest. Spray only above threshold.

Quiz

  1. What is the signature symptom of bacterial wilt?
  2. Name the preventive spray for blight and the week to apply.
  3. Why must you never spray at flowering?
