Choosing a breeding queen

  • Age: 12-18 months at first breeding (never before her second heat).
  • Health: tested for breed-specific diseases (HCM in Maine Coons, PKD in Persians, etc.).
  • Conformation: correct for the breed standard - a good queen produces good kittens.
  • Temperament: calm, friendly, not aggressive. Aggression is heritable.
  • Pedigree: registered, known lineage, no hereditary defects in the line.
  • Reproductive history: has she kindled successfully? Any complications?
  • Body condition: fit, not fat, well-muscled, healthy coat.
  • Vaccination status: up to date (FVRCP, rabies).
  • FeLV/FIV negative: critical for a breeding cattery.

Choosing a stud male

  • Proven producer: has he sired healthy litters before?
  • Health clearances: HCM screening, DNA tests, FeLV/FIV negative.
  • Temperament: confident, calm, willing, not aggressive.
  • Conformation: excellent - he influences 50% of every litter.
  • Pedigree: unrelated to your queen for genetic diversity.
  • Age: 12 months to 8 years is peak fertility.

If you don't own a stud male, you can use artificial insemination from chilled or frozen semen, or pay a stud fee to a proven male. Stud fees typically run $200-1,500.

Never breed from a cat without health clearances. HCM, PKD, and other genetic diseases pass to offspring. Testing costs $50-300 per test but saves thousands in vet bills and reputation damage.

Breeds to avoid for beginners

  • Wild hybrids first generations (F1-F3 Savannah, early Bengal) - legal issues, difficult to handle
  • Scottish Fold - the fold gene causes arthritis in many cats
  • Munchkin - dwarf gene causes joint and mobility problems
  • Brachycephalic Persians - breathing and eye issues, high c-section rates
  • Rare or banned breeds in your area
The breed rule: Pick one breed and stick with it for 5 years. Become known for that breed. Reputation sells kittens - not variety.
Practical exercise 2: Contact 3 breeders of your chosen breed. Ask about health testing, pedigree, and kitten prices. Attend a cat club event if there is one nearby.
