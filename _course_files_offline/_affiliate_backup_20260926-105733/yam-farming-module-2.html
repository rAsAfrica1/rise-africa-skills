Lesson 1

Sett Types and Sizes

In this lesson
  • Understand the different sett types
  • Choose the right sett for your market
  • Understand the trade-off between cost and yield

Sett sizes

  • Whole small tubers (200–400 g): the best planting material. Highest yield, highest cost. 400–600 g per sett
  • Large setts (100–200 g cut pieces): standard commercial planting. Good yield, moderate cost
  • Small setts (30–80 g): cheaper, lower yield in year 1 but useful for seed multiplication
  • Mini-setts (10–25 g): for rapid multiplication systems — very low yield in year 1, used to build up seed stock

Setts for different markets

  • Food yam market: plant 100–200 g setts. Get large marketable tubers in one season
  • Seed yam market: plant 30–80 g setts. Get many small tubers as seed for next season
  • Rapid multiplication: plant 10–25 g mini-setts. Get 10–20x multiplication in one season but no food yam harvest
The trade-off: large setts = large food yams = food market. Small setts = many small tubers = seed market. Choose before you plant.
Lesson 2

Where Seed Yam Comes From

In this lesson
  • Know the sources of seed yam
  • Understand the quality difference between sources
  • Recognise nematode and disease risk in cheap seed

Sources, ranked by quality

  1. Certified seed yam producers — NARS stations, tissue-culture derived, tested for nematodes and viruses. Most expensive but reliable.
  2. Your own seed from the previous clean crop — free, but quality declines year on year. Replace every 2–3 years.
  3. Reputable local seed producers — selected clean tubers from clean fields.
  4. Market seed yam — cheapest, highest risk. Nematode and virus loads high. Only buy if you can inspect the source field.

The nematode problem

Yam nematodes (Scutellonema, Pratylenchus, Meloidogyne) survive in seed yam and in soil. A nematode-infected sett produces a small, galled, unmarketable tuber. Losses of 40–70% are documented.

How to check seed quality

  • Firm, no soft spots
  • Dry skin, no cracks
  • No visible nematode galls on the surface
  • Uniform size for the class
  • No sprouting before its time
Lesson 3

The Seed Yam Multiplication Business

In this lesson
  • Understand the seed yam multiplication business model
  • Calculate the margin multiplier
  • Decide if this is your primary business

The business model

Buy 100 kg of clean seed yam. Plant in 1,000 m². Harvest 500–1,500 kg of seed yam at the end of the season. Sell to other farmers at 2–3x food price. Repeat.

The margins

  • Input: 100 kg seed at $1.50/kg = $150
  • Other costs: land prep, labour, staking, fertiliser = $200–400
  • Total: $350–550
  • Output: 500–1,500 kg at $2.00/kg = $1,000–3,000
  • Net margin: $450–2,450 per 1,000 m²

Requirements

  • Strict nematode management (clean seed in, clean soil, rotation)
  • Grading by size class (small, medium, large)
  • Reputation — other farmers only buy seed yam from trusted sources
  • Some formal registration or certification if selling into institutional programmes

Lesson 4

Pre-Planting Sett Treatment

In this lesson
  • Treat setts to control nematodes and fungi
  • Use traditional and modern treatments
  • Cure setts before planting

Traditional treatments

  • Wood ash: coat cut surfaces. Provides potassium, reduces fungal entry.
  • Neem extract: soak setts in neem solution for 30 minutes. Reduces nematode load.
  • Hot water: 50–55°C for 10–20 minutes. Kills nematodes on the sett surface. Careful — too hot kills the sett.

Modern treatments

  • Fungicide dip: mancozeb or thiram solution for 5–10 minutes.
  • Nematicide treatment: some systemic nematicides can be applied at planting as a soil treatment. Follow label rates.
  • Combined dip: fungicide + insecticide + neem, applied before planting.

Curing

After treating, dry setts in the shade for 24–48 hours to allow cut surfaces to heal and seal. This dramatically reduces rot at planting.

Lesson 5

Choosing a Variety for Market

In this lesson
  • Match variety to market
  • Understand the difference between landraces and improved lines
  • Know the top African varieties

Market-matched varieties

  • Pounded yam market: Amula, Efuru, Danacha, local landraces with high starch and dry matter
  • Boiled yam market: varieties that hold shape when boiled
  • Fried yam / chips: low-sugar, high dry matter — water yam varieties excel
  • Flour market: any high-yielding variety, preferably water yam for its yield

Landraces vs improved

  • Landraces: farmer-adapted, well-suited to local conditions, preferred for taste. But lower yield and often nematode-prone.
  • Improved lines: higher yield, better disease resistance, sometimes less preferred for pounded yam taste. From IITA, national research institutes.

Best approach: grow landraces for the pounded yam market, and improved lines for the seed yam market and flour market.

Knowledge check — Module 2

1. What size sett is used for the seed yam market?

Correct: 30–80 g small setts. Produces many small tubers used as seed next season.

2. What is the highest-quality seed yam source?

Correct: certified producers. Tissue-culture derived, tested for nematodes and viruses.

3. What causes 40–70% yield loss in yam?

Correct: nematodes. Survive in seed yam and soil.

4. Why cure setts before planting?

Correct: healing and sealing. 24–48 hours in shade.

5. Which sett type gives the highest multiplication rate?

Correct: mini-setts. 10–20x multiplication in one season, but no food yam harvest.
