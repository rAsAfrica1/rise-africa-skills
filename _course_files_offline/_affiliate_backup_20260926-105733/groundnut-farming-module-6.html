Lesson 1

Reading the Groundnut Price Calendar

In this lesson
  • Map the seasonal groundnut price cycle
  • Plan storage to sell into the peak
  • Understand the aflatoxin premium

Groundnut prices follow a strong seasonal cycle. In most African markets:

  • Harvest months: lowest prices, $0.50–0.80/kg
  • 3–6 months after harvest: rising, $0.80–1.20/kg
  • Late dry season: peak, $1.20–1.80/kg
  • Before next harvest: highest, sometimes $2.00+/kg in urban areas

Using storage to capture the curve

Hermetic storage lets you hold kernels for 6–8 months without loss. If you harvest at $0.70/kg and sell at $1.40/kg, storage doubles your revenue on the stored portion. The cost of hermetic bags ($3–8 per 100 kg) is repaid many times over.

The aflatoxin premium

Processors and exporters pay a premium for aflatoxin-tested, clean kernels. A crop with a clean test result sells for 20–40% more than the same crop untested. Testing is worth every dollar.

Lesson 2

The Five Buyer Types

In this lesson
  • Name and price the five buyer types
  • Match grade to buyer
  • Build a buyer relationship

1. Local market vendors

Small lots, cash. $0.60–1.20/kg. Best for rapid turnover of the majority of the crop.

2. Urban retail and supermarkets

Weekly deliveries, 20–40% premium, 30-day terms. Require graded and packaged kernels. Best for the top 20% of the crop.

3. Peanut butter and snack processors

5–50 tonne lots, $0.80–1.40/kg. Aflatoxin tested. Absorbs volume.

4. Oil millers

Lower grade, $0.60–1.00/kg. Accepts any grade. Good for the bottom 30% of the crop.

5. Export via aggregators

20-tonne containers, $2.50–4.00/kg. Requires a group or aggregator, strict aflatoxin test, uniform grade, and documentation.

The rule: a smart groundnut farmer sells to two or three buyer types — premium to retail, volume to processor, bottom grade to oil mill. Every grade of the crop becomes cash.
Lesson 3

Peanut Butter — the Value-Added Product

In this lesson
  • Understand the peanut butter process
  • Calculate the margin multiplier
  • Decide when to invest in a grinder

Process

  1. Roast kernels at 160–180°C for 20–30 minutes, stirring constantly
  2. Cool quickly
  3. Remove skins (rub between cloth or use a skin blower)
  4. Grind — first pass coarse, second pass fine. Small electric grinders cost $200–2,000.
  5. Add salt (0.5–1%), sugar (optional), and stabiliser (optional, extends shelf life)
  6. Pack in clean, dry jars or tubs; seal
  7. Shelf life 6–12 months without stabiliser if sealed well

Yield and margin

  • Yield: 1 kg peanut butter from ~1.2 kg kernels
  • Kernel price: $1.00–1.50/kg
  • Peanut butter retail: $4.00–8.00/kg
  • Gross multiplier: 3–5x on the kernels used

Requirements

  • Food-safety compliance (clean premises, sanitary handling, sealed packaging)
  • Aflatoxin-tested kernels below the buyer threshold
  • Small grinder (the main capital item)
  • Marketing — this is a consumer product, not a commodity

Revenue at 1.5 t/ha (shelled kernels)

  • At $0.70/kg (harvest price) = $1,050
  • At $1.20/kg (off-season) = $1,800
  • At $2.00/kg (premium retail) = $3,000

Net margin

$300–2,500/ha depending on yield, market and value-addition. Peanut butter value addition can lift net margin 3–5x over raw kernel sales.

Lesson 5

The One-Page Business Plan

In this lesson
  • Complete the one-page business plan
  • Set targets for year 1 and year 2
  • Decide scale triggers

Before next season, fill in this one page:

  1. Business type: raw kernels, peanut butter, oil, or seed multiplication?
  2. Capital tier: $50–500, $500–5,000, or $5,000+?
  3. Land: how many hectares, and what water source?
  4. Variety: which groundnut type and which specific variety?
  5. Target yield: realistic kg/ha (start at 1.5 t/ha target)
  6. Buyer: name three real buyers you will approach
  7. Revenue target: price per kg × expected yield
  8. Total cost: every line
  9. Net margin: revenue minus cost
  10. Value step: what single product will you add in 12 months (butter, oil, seed)?
  11. Scaling trigger: what condition must hold before expanding?

Read it again at harvest. Then again next season.

Knowledge check — Module 6

1. When are groundnut prices highest?

Correct: late dry season. Storage captures the difference.

2. What does aflatoxin testing enable?

Correct: 20–40% higher price. Tested clean kernels sell into the premium market.

3. What is the margin multiplier for peanut butter?

Correct: 3–5x. Consumer packaged product sells at a large premium over the raw kernel.

4. How many kg of kernels make 1 kg of peanut butter?

Correct: 1.2 kg. Very little loss in processing.

5. When should you scale the groundnut business?

Correct: after two profitable cycles. Same discipline as every other crop.

Write your one-page groundnut business plan. Include all eleven points above. Keep it to one page. Read it at harvest, then again next season.
