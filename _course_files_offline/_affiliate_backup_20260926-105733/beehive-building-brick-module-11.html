Module 11 — Harvesting, Extraction and Honey Handling

Harvesting from fixed hives — the access, extraction and handling practices that protect a premium crop.

Home › Beehive Building — Brick › Module 11

Learning outcomes

  • Plan harvest access for a hive that cannot be moved.
  • Clear bees from supers on a fixed hive without disturbing the brood nest.
  • Extract, filter and store honey to the same standard as any other hive.
  • Manage fixed-hive harvesting so the brood nest is never opened unnecessarily.
  • Record yield and cost for a permanent apiary.

1. Fixed-hive harvesting

All the honey quality rules from the wood hive course apply here: harvest only capped honey, leave enough stores, never heat above 45 °C, never sell above 20 % moisture. What changes is how you access the honey.

ChallengeWood hiveBrick hive
Super accessLift the whole box offDepends on design: removable super, or a fixed upper chamber with frame access
Bee clearingBee escape between brood box and super, then carry the super awayBee escape still works if the super is a removable box; if fixed, use a brush and a smoker
Working heightStandardOften higher; plan the plinth height so you can work comfortably
Moving to extraction roomCarry the whole superCarry frames one at a time in a covered carrier
Bee disturbanceWhole hive opensOnly the honey chamber opens if the design allows

Design harvest access at build stage

A brick hive with a fixed roof and a solid chamber is almost impossible to harvest from. If you can still adjust the design, ensure:

• A removable super, or a hinged upper panel, or a slot that lets you lift frames out one at a time.
• Enough clearance above the top bars to lift a frame clear of the walls.
• A working height of 900–1,100 mm from the ground — so you are not reaching above your head.

2. When to harvest

The same rule as any hive: harvest capped honey. For a fixed hive, the check is done through the super access rather than by lifting the whole box.

SignalAction
8+ frames capped in the honey chamberHarvest
6–7 frames cappedWait 1–2 weeks
Fewer than 6 cappedDo not harvest; the flow is still running
Any uncapped frame at harvestLeave it for the bees

Moisture check is not optional

Target: below 18 % moisture.
Above 20 %: do not sell — ferment risk.
A refractometer at 20 °C is the standard tool. Two drops per batch.
Test at extraction, and again before jarring if the honey has been sitting for weeks.

3. Clearing bees from a fixed hive

A removable super can be cleared exactly as in a wooden hive — bee escape for 24 hours, then take the whole super to the extraction room. If the honey chamber is fixed, you need a different approach.

MethodSuitable for fixed hivesNotes
Bee escapeOnly if the super is removableGentle, 24 h
Frame-by-frame brushYesSlow, disturbs the bees, but works. Brush into the hive, never onto the ground
Smoke and shakeYesShake frames over the open hive to dislodge bees; brush any remaining
Fume boardYes, if permittedFastest; check local regulations
Bee blowerYes, but carefulDo not blow bees into the hive or against walls; low pressure

Work from the back of the chamber forward

In a fixed honey chamber, start at the back and work forward. Bees from cleared frames will move forward — do not chase them back into the chamber you have already cleared. Finish at the front, closest to the exit. Give the bees somewhere to go that is not into your face.

4. Extraction and handling

The extraction process is identical to any hive. Cover the essentials briefly and refer to the Wood course for the full method.

  1. Extract indoors, in a bee-tight room. Warm to 25–30 °C.
  2. Uncap with a hot knife, roller or fork. Save the cappings.
  3. Spin slowly at first, then faster. Turn frames and repeat.
  4. Strain through a coarse sieve into a settling tank.
  5. Settle 24–48 hours; skim foam and wax from the surface.
  6. Filter fine at 0.5 mm — not finer, or you strip pollen and aroma.
  7. Measure moisture with a refractometer.
  8. Jar and label. Store at 18–24 °C, sealed, away from strong smells.

5. Working a permanent apiary efficiently

TaskEfficient practice
Stagger harvestDo 2–3 hives per day, not all 10 at once. Preserves your back, keeps the honey cool, avoids a queue of sticky equipment
Pre-stage carriersHave covered frame carriers ready before you start. Loose frames attract bees and drip honey
Work in the eveningBees are calmer and foragers are in; easier to clear frames
Cover the chamber between framesA cloth or lid over the open chamber keeps bees out of the frames you have already cleared
Clean as you goA wet rag for tools, a bucket for cappings. Honey on the ground starts robbing
Close up carefullyMake sure the chamber is bee-tight before you leave; a gap invites robbing overnight

6. Extraction room and equipment

A fixed brick apiary benefits from a shared extraction room at the site — even a small one. It saves carrying equipment, protects the honey from the weather and from bees, and lets you handle larger batches without moving honey twice.

ElementSpecification
FloorSealed, sloped 1:100 to a drain, washable
WallsWashable to 1.5 m, light colour
BenchStainless or sealed, height 900 mm, length for uncapping and loading
Space for extractor1.5 × 1.5 m clear working area
Settling tankStainless, with a tap at the lowest point
Lighting500 lux at the bench, IP65 fittings
DoorSolid, bee-tight, self-closing
Water and drainHand-wash basin and floor drain at the lowest point

The permanent apiary has higher fixed cost per hive than a mobile one. It pays off when the site is good enough that yield per hive is high. Track yield per hive every year.

8. Common mistakes

MistakeConsequenceFix
Harvesting uncapped honeyFermentationRefractometer; wait for 8 capped frames
Opening the brood chamber for harvestDisturbs the colony, kills brood, invites robbingDesign the honey chamber so you do not need to open the brood nest
Heating honey to make it flowEnzyme loss; HMFWater bath at 40–43 °C; use a thermometer
Leaving honey spillsRobbing across the apiaryWipe up immediately; wash tools
Storing jars in full sunColour loss; accelerated crystallisationDark, cool, sealed store
