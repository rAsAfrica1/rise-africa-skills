Lesson 1

Certified Seed — Why It Matters Most for Soybean

In this lesson
  • Understand seed vigour and germination requirements
  • Test germination before planting
  • Know when to buy fresh

Soybean seed is delicate. The seed coat cracks easily, germination drops fast in storage, and virus diseases are seed-borne. Saved soybean seed loses 15–30% of germination per year in tropical conditions.

Rules

  • Buy certified seed every season
  • Only save seed from a clean crop if certified seed is unavailable
  • Test germination: 100 seeds, damp cloth, 5 days. Above 85% — plant. Below 75% — buy new seed
  • Handle gently — cracked seed is dead seed
  • Store in hermetic bags, cool and dry

Seed treatment

  • Fungicide: thiram or similar, applied before inoculant (do not mix)
  • Inoculant: soybean-specific Bradyrhizobium japonicum. Apply just before planting
  • Never mix fungicide and inoculant — the fungicide kills the rhizobium
Lesson 2

Inoculation — the Non-Negotiable Step

In this lesson
  • Apply soybean inoculant correctly
  • Avoid the mistakes that kill rhizobium
  • Recognise successful nodulation

Correct application

  1. Buy soybean-specific inoculant (Bradyrhizobium japonicum) — do not substitute cowpea or bean inoculant
  2. Store cool and dark until the day of planting
  3. Moisten seed with clean water or a weak sugar solution
  4. Add inoculant at packet rate — usually 5–10 g per kg of seed
  5. Mix until every seed is coated with a thin dark film
  6. Shade the seed; plant within 2–4 hours
  7. Do not mix with fertiliser or fungicide

Successful nodulation

At 4–6 weeks, dig up a plant and check the roots. You should see 10–30 nodules, pink when cut open. Pink = actively fixing. White or green = not yet active. No nodules = inoculation failed.

Lesson 3

Land Preparation and Spacing

In this lesson
  • Prepare land for even emergence
  • Space correctly for full yield
  • Calculate seed rate

Land preparation

  • Plough or hoe 15–20 cm deep
  • Harrow to a fine tilth — soybean seed needs good soil contact
  • Level the field — waterlogging kills soybean faster than any other legume

Spacing

  • Between rows: 45–60 cm
  • Within row: 5–10 cm
  • Target population: 350,000–450,000 plants/ha at harvest

Seed rate

50–80 kg/ha for most varieties, depending on seed size. Soybean seed is small — 40–70 g per 1,000 seeds.

Why spacing matters

Soybean compensates poorly for low plant population. Unlike maize, a soybean plant cannot produce twice the pods to make up for a missing neighbour. Get the stand right at planting or accept the yield loss.

Lesson 4

Phosphorus, Not Nitrogen

In this lesson
  • Apply phosphorus at the right rate
  • Avoid nitrogen which suppresses nodulation
  • Watch for potassium and sulphur

Phosphorus

Apply DAP or TSP at 150–200 kg/ha at planting. Soybean has a high phosphorus demand for nodulation and pod fill. Place 5 cm below and to the side of the seed.

Do not apply nitrogen

Nitrogen fertiliser suppresses nodulation and defeats the purpose of growing soybean. The plant gets all its nitrogen from the rhizobium partnership plus soil reserves.

Potassium and sulphur

  • Potassium: 40–80 kg/ha of SOP or MOP, split between planting and pod fill
  • Sulphur: soybean responds to sulphur in some soils. 15–20 kg/ha of sulphate.

Organic alternatives

  • 5–10 t/ha well-rotted manure or compost
  • Wood ash for potassium
  • Bone meal for phosphorus
The rule: soybean is a nitrogen-fixing crop. Applying nitrogen is paying for something the plant makes for free — and reducing the amount it makes.
Lesson 5

Planting Depth and Timing

In this lesson
  • Plant at the correct depth
  • Time planting for reliable moisture
  • Manage early season risks

Depth

3–5 cm. Deeper is fine if soil is dry, but do not exceed 6 cm. Uniform depth gives uniform emergence.

Timing

  • Plant at the start of reliable rains, not the first shower
  • West Africa: June–July (single season) or August (double crop)
  • East Africa: March–April (long rains) or October (short rains)
  • Southern Africa: November–December

Early risks

  • Dry spell after planting: soybean germinates fast and dies fast. Wait for reliable moisture.
  • Waterlogging: the biggest killer. If the field holds water, use ridges or raised beds.
  • Bean fly: seed treatment with imidacloprid protects early.

Knowledge check — Module 2

1. Why is soybean seed more fragile than cereal seed?

Correct: thin seed coat. Cracked seed is dead seed.

2. Which inoculant is used for soybean?

Correct: Bradyrhizobium japonicum. Soybean-specific — no substitute works.

3. What fertiliser should NOT be applied to soybean?

Correct: nitrogen. It suppresses nodulation.

4. What spacing for soybean?

Correct: 45–60 × 5–10 cm. Target 350,000–450,000 plants/ha.

5. How do you check successful nodulation?

Correct: count pink nodules at 6 weeks. 10–30 per plant, pink when cut open.
