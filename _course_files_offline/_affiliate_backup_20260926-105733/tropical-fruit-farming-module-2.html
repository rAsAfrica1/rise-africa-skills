2. Mango - the King of Tropical Fruit

Mango earns more than any other tropical fruit tree once mature. Here is how to grow it right.

Module 2 of 6 · Tropical Fruit Farming for Profit

Why mango

Mango is the most widely grown tropical fruit tree in Africa. A mature tree produces 500-1,500 fruits per year. Trees live 40-80 years and yield improves steadily for the first 15 years.

MetricValue
Years to first fruit3-5 (grafted)
Years to full production8-12
Fruit per mature tree500-1,500
Trees per hectare100-200 (8 x 8 m to 10 x 10 m)

Always buy grafted trees from a certified nursery. Seedling mangoes take twice as long and give unpredictable fruit.

Spacing

SpacingTrees/haNotes
10 x 10 m100Traditional, large canopy
8 x 8 m156Dwarf varieties, better yield/ha
6 x 8 m (hedgerow)208High density, needs pruning
5 x 5 m (dwarf)400Dwarf varieties only - Julie, Cogshall

For most smallholders, 8 x 8 m or 10 x 10 m with standard varieties.

Site preparation

  1. Clear the land, remove stumps and heavy brush.
  2. Dig holes 60 x 60 x 60 cm - do this 4-6 weeks before planting.
  3. Mix topsoil with compost (20-30 L per hole).
  4. Add 100-200 g DAP per hole.
  5. Let the hole settle for 4 weeks.
  6. Plant at start of rainy season.
  7. Keep graft union 15-25 cm above soil.
  8. Water deeply and mulch.
  9. Windbreak on exposed sides - plant 4-6 months before.

Fruiting season

Mango fruits over 3-5 months of the year. Different varieties ripen at different times. To spread harvest:

SeasonVarieties
EarlyTommy Atkins, Haden
MidKent, Ngowe, Apple mango
LateKeitt, Sabine
The variety rule: Plant at least 2 mango varieties with different seasons. It spreads your harvest from 2 months to 5 months.
Practical exercise 2: Visit a mango orchard. Ask the farmer which variety he grows and why. Look at the fruit size and colour at harvest.

Quiz

  1. Which mango variety is the export standard?
  2. Why must you plant grafted mango rather than seedling?
  3. How do you spread your mango harvest across more of the year?
