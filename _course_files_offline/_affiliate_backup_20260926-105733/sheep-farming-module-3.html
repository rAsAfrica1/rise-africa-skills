3. Housing and Fencing

Sheep are hardier than goats but still need dry shelter, secure fencing and clean water.

Module 3 of 12 · Sheep Farming for Profit

Why housing matters

Sheep are hardier than goats and tolerate more weather. But wet sheep get pneumonia, foot rot, and parasite problems. Good housing cuts mortality, improves weight gain, and protects against predators.

Floor space requirements

AnimalIndoor (m2)Outdoor run (m2)
Adult ewe1.0-1.53-5
Breeding ram1.5-2.05-8
Pregnant ewe (late)1.5-2.03-5
Ewe with lambs2.0-2.55-8
Lamb (weaned)0.5-0.82-3

A 50-ewe flock needs 60-75 m2 of shelter plus 200+ m2 of run space.

House design principles

  1. Dry floor. Concrete, raised slatted, or packed earth. No standing water.
  2. Open sides with mesh. Two or three sides open for airflow. 1-2 cm mesh.
  3. Roof overhang 60 cm minimum. Rain and sun protection.
  4. North-south orientation where possible.
  5. Roll-down curtains. Tarp or feed sacks for cold nights and rain.
  6. Feed rack. Hay off the ground - prevents waste and internal parasite infection.
  7. Water trough. Clean water always available.
  8. Mineral block holder. Free-choice minerals always available.
  9. Lambing pens. Separate, quiet, clean area for births.
  10. Sick pen. Isolated 20+ m from main house.
  11. Shearing area. If wool sheep - flat clean surface, sheltered.

Sheep are easier to fence than goats - they do not climb. But they push through weak fencing, so keep it tight.

Rotational grazing

Divide land into 4-6 paddocks. Move sheep every 3-7 days.

  • Worm burden drops 60-80%
  • Pasture recovers better
  • Less overgrazing
  • More animals per hectare
  • Better feed quality
  • Finer control of grazing pressure

Shelter for lambing

Ewes need a dry, quiet, clean place to lamb. A lambing pen allows:

  • Bonding between ewe and lamb without interference from other sheep
  • Observation of difficult births
  • Warmer environment for newborn lambs (critical in cold highlands)
  • Prevention of mismothering in a large flock

Lambing pens: 1.2-1.5 m2 each, one per 10 ewes for a short lambing period.

Water

AnimalWater per day
Adult sheep4-6 L
Lactating ewe8-10 L
Lamb (weaned)2-4 L
  • Water must always be available - 24 hours a day.
  • Clean troughs weekly.
  • Mount on a concrete base - no mud around the trough.
  • In hot weather, sheep drink up to 8 L per day.

The dry rule: Sheep must have dry feet and dry bodies. Wet sheep get pneumonia, foot rot, and internal parasites. Raised floor and roof overhang are non-negotiable.
