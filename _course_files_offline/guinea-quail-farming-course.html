â  SAFETY WARNING â Grain, Aflatoxin and Chemical Handling

  • Aflatoxin is a serious health hazard. Poorly dried or badly stored maize and groundnuts can develop aflatoxin, a mould toxin linked to liver cancer and stunting in children. Never sell or consume visibly mouldy, discoloured or musty grain.
  • Dry to safe moisture before storage. Target 13% moisture or below for maize. Damp grain in warm conditions is the single biggest driver of mould growth.
  • Never use banned or unregistered pesticides. Follow the label exactly, respect pre-harvest intervals, and keep all agrochemicals locked away from food, feed and children.
  • Wear protective equipment when applying pesticides or fumigants â gloves, mask, long sleeves and eye protection. Wash thoroughly afterwards.
  • Test before you trade. Buyers in formal and export markets increasingly require aflatoxin test certificates. Budget for testing as a cost of doing business, not an optional extra.
  • If in doubt, do not sell. Contaminated grain destroys buyer trust, triggers recalls and can end a supply contract permanently.

Quick Reference Checklist

  1. Know your numbers. Calculate cost per tonne for every input, including transport, energy, labour and losses â not just the purchase price.
  2. Verify moisture and quality before buying or storing any grain. Use a moisture meter and record results per batch.
  3. Segment your buyers. Separate retail, wholesale, institutional (schools, hospitals) and export channels, and price each one deliberately.
  4. Lock in supply. Use written contracts or outgrower agreements with smallholder farmers to secure volume and quality.
  5. Cut post-harvest losses. Invest in proper drying, hermetic storage and pest control â every percentage point saved is margin recovered.
  6. Build a simple record system. Track purchases, sales, stock and cash daily. Records are the first thing lenders and investors ask for.
  7. Comply and certify. Understand local food safety, weights-and-measures and export certification requirements before scaling.
  8. Plan for finance. Prepare a 12-month cash-flow forecast and know your working capital gap before approaching a bank or investor.
