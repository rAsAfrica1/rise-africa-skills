Reinvestment priorities

  1. Better ram - the single biggest genetic gain per dollar
  2. Pasture improvement - legumes, rotational paddocks
  3. Water system - reliable year-round
  4. Housing upgrade - dry floors, lambing pens
  5. Handling facility - race, crush, weighing crate
  6. Feed storage - bulk buying saves 15-20%
  7. Processing equipment - if selling meat
  8. Transport - reliable delivery to buyers

Market expansion strategy

  1. Stage 1: Local market, direct neighbours, festive season.
  2. Stage 2: Add 3-5 butcheries and 1-2 restaurants on weekly supply.
  3. Stage 3: Add processing (smoked, dried, cheese) for premium pricing.
  4. Stage 4: Add wool sale to wool mills or spinners.
  5. Stage 5: Add breeding-stock sale to other farmers.
  6. Stage 6: Add institutional contracts (schools, hospitals, defence).

Systemising the farm

At 300+ ewes, you cannot rely on your own memory. Build systems:

  • Daily routine list - posted on the wall, ticked each day
  • Feeding schedule - fixed times, quantities on a chart
  • Health protocols - vaccination and treatment calendar
  • Staff roles - who feeds, who cleans, who records
  • Records template - one notebook per class of sheep
  • Monthly review - fixed day, review all numbers, make decisions

When to stop scaling

Scale only when:

  • Current stage profitable for 6 consecutive months
  • Mortality under 5% (adults) and 10% (lambs)
  • Lambing rate above 150%
  • Buyers confirmed for 80% of expected output
  • Cash reserve covers 2 months of feed
  • Housing and staff ready before the new flock arrives

The patience rule: Sheep flocks grow exponentially. Grow slowly, retain daughters, invest in genetics, and sell consistently into premium markets. From 50 to 300 ewes takes 5 years - but the income grows with the flock and compounds.
