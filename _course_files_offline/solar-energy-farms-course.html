â  SAFETY WARNING â Electrical and Battery Hazards

Solar DC systems can deliver lethal current even at "low" voltages. A 48 V battery bank stores enough energy to weld metal, cause deep burns, and start fires. Never work on live DC cables with bare hands. Always disconnect the array first, then the battery, and use insulated tools rated for 1,000 V. Lead-acid batteries vent explosive hydrogen â charge them in ventilated spaces, away from sparks and livestock housing. Lithium batteries must use a BMS matched to the inverter; bypassing it voids warranties and risks thermal runaway. If you are not trained, hire a qualified solar technician. Faulty installation is the leading cause of off-grid system fires in Africa.

Quick Reference Checklist

  1. Audit your load first. List every appliance, its wattage, and daily hours. Buy equipment to match that list â never the other way round.
  2. Size the battery for two cloudy days. Multiply daily energy by 2 and divide by depth of discharge (0.8 for lithium, 0.5 for lead-acid).
  3. Oversize the array by 30%. Dust, heat, and shading cut real output below nameplate ratings across most of Africa.
  4. Use MPPT, not PWM, controllers. MPPT typically recovers 15â30% more energy from the same panels.
  5. Ground and fuse everything. DC breakers on array and battery, surge protection on AC output, earth rod at the array.
  6. Mount panels at 10â15Â° for most of sub-Saharan Africa, tilted north in the south and south in the north, cleared of dust weekly.
  7. Keep a maintenance log. Record battery voltage, water levels (lead-acid), and cleaning dates. This log is your evidence for warranty and loan repayment.
  8. Train one person per site. A single trained operator prevents 80% of avoidable system failures.
