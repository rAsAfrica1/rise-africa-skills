8. Training and Behaviour

Well-trained dogs sell for more. Here is the training system that turns puppies into premium dogs.

Module 8 of 12 · Dog Breeding for Profit

Why training matters for a breeder

A trained puppy is worth more. Basic training (sit, down, stay, come, walk on lead) adds 20-40% to the puppy value. Advanced training (obedience, protection, working ability) adds 100-500%. And trained dogs have fewer behaviour problems - which means fewer complaints, fewer returns, and a stronger reputation.

The five training principles

  1. Consistency. Same commands, same expectations, every time. If "sit" means sit today, it means sit tomorrow.
  2. Timing. Reward within 1-2 seconds of the behaviour. Later rewards teach nothing.
  3. Patience. A dog that is rushed learns fear. Slow is fast.
  4. Positive reinforcement. Reward what you want. Ignore or redirect what you do not want.
  5. Short sessions. 5-15 minutes, 2-3 times per day. Long sessions bore the dog and the trainer.

Never use physical punishment. Hitting, kicking, or hanging a dog creates fear, aggression, and destroys trust. Modern training is based on positive reinforcement. Physical punishment produces dangerous dogs - and dangerous dogs do not sell.

Teaching sit - step by step

  1. Hold a treat just above the puppy''s nose.
  2. Move it slowly back over the head. The puppy will naturally sit to follow.
  3. As the rear touches the ground, say "sit" and give the treat.
  4. Repeat 5-10 times per session.
  5. After 3-5 sessions, add the word "sit" before the hand signal.
  6. Fade the treat - reward every other time, then every third time, then randomly.
  7. Practice in different places - kitchen, garden, street, park.

Teaching recall ("come")

Recall is the most important command for safety. Build it carefully:

  1. Start indoors or in a small enclosed space.
  2. Say the puppy''s name + "come" in a happy voice.
  3. When the puppy comes, reward with treats, praise, and play.
  4. Never call the puppy to punish - if you punish after recall, the puppy learns that coming is dangerous.
  5. Increase distance and distractions gradually.
  6. Use a long lead (5-10 m) in open spaces before going off-lead.
  7. Reward with high-value treats (cheese, chicken) for difficult recalls.
  8. Practice every day. Recall is never fully learned.

Crate training

Crates are valuable for housetraining, travel, and safety. Make the crate a positive place:

  1. Place the crate in a family area, not isolated.
  2. Feed meals inside the crate with the door open.
  3. Throw treats in for the puppy to find.
  4. Gradually close the door for short periods.
  5. Increase duration - 1 minute, 5, 15, 30.
  6. Never use the crate for punishment.
  7. Reward calm behaviour in the crate.
  8. Keep sessions short at first. Build up to 2-4 hours as the puppy grows.

Working with a professional trainer

If you are breeding working or sport dogs, professional training is essential. A good trainer:

  • Uses force-free, positive-reinforcement methods.
  • Has proven results - titles, competition wins, or working placements.
  • Understands your breed and its working traits.
  • Is patient, methodical, and communicates clearly.
  • Provides ongoing support and follow-up.
  • Does not promise results faster than realistically possible.

Expect to pay $300-3,000 per month for advanced training. The value added to the dog is usually 2-5x the cost.

The training rule: A well-trained dog sells for 3-10x an untrained one. Every hour you spend on training pays back tenfold in price and reputation.
