SAFETY WARNING â ELECTRICAL AND MACHINE HAZARDS

Robotics work combines live electricity, stored energy and moving parts. Treat every build as hazardous until proven otherwise.

  • Isolate before touching. Switch off and unplug the supply before wiring. Never work on a live circuit â 12V can still burn, and mains-powered chargers and pumps can kill.
  • Fuse everything. Fit an inline fuse on the positive line close to the battery or supply. An unfused short can start a fire in seconds.
  • Guard the moving parts. Any belt, roller, blade or linkage must have a physical guard. Loose clothing, sleeves and jewellery must be removed before testing.
  • Never bypass a limit switch or emergency stop to "make it run". These are the last line of defence for fingers and hands.
  • Batteries: use the correct charger, never charge lithium packs unattended, and never short the terminals. Swollen or punctured cells must be disposed of at a proper collection point, not in household waste.
  • Water and power together: irrigation controllers must use low-voltage DC solenoids and a residual-current device (RCD) on any mains supply. Keep all electronics in an IP-rated enclosure.
  • If in doubt, stop and get a qualified electrician. A burnt-out controller is a cost; a burnt-out workshop is a business.

Quick Reference Checklist

  • Define the one job. Write the single task the machine must do in one sentence. If you cannot, the scope is too wide â cut it.
  • Price three suppliers. Get written quotes for every component before committing. Compare landed cost, not shelf price.
  • Sketch the sense-plan-act loop. Name the sensor, the controller and the actuator before you buy anything.
  • Build a bench prototype first. Test the logic on a table with no load, no water and no product at risk.
  • Fuse and guard. No build goes into service without an inline fuse, a physical guard and an accessible off switch.
  • Run a 7-day trial. Log every failure, false trigger and jam. Fix the top three before you scale.
  • Document the build. Photograph the wiring, label every cable and keep a parts list. This is what makes the machine serviceable and sellable.
  • Calculate payback. Divide total build cost by monthly savings in labour, waste or water. If payback exceeds two seasons, redesign before you build.
