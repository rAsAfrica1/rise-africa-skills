2. The Chemistry of Saponification

You cannot make good soap without understanding saponification. It is not a mysterious process - it is one chemical reaction with three inputs and one output. Once you understand it, you control the whole craft.

Module 2 of 12 · Soap Making · practical exercises · quiz at the end

Lesson 2.1 - What saponification actually is

Saponification is the reaction between a strong base (lye) and a fat (oil or butter). It produces two things:

  1. Soap (the salt of the fatty acid)
  2. Glycerin (a natural moisturizer, kept in handmade soap and removed from industrial soap)

Equation in plain English:

Oil + Lye → Soap + Glycerin

That is it. There is no magic. The reaction is exothermic - it produces heat - and it takes 24-48 hours to fully complete, then 4 weeks to fully cure.

Lesson 2.2 - Sodium hydroxide (NaOH) vs potassium hydroxide (KOH)

PropertyNaOHKOH
Also calledCaustic soda, lyeCaustic potash
MakesHard bar soapLiquid soap, soft soap
Common formWhite flakes or pelletsFlakes or solution
Price$3-6/kg$5-9/kg
StorageAirtight, dry - it absorbs waterAirtight - very hygroscopic

This course uses NaOH. Liquid soap (KOH) is a separate craft with different safety and formulation rules.

Lesson 2.3 - SAP values

Every oil has a SAP value - the amount of lye it takes to fully saponify 1 gram of that oil. SAP values come from a lab, but a reliable lye calculator will do the math for you.

OilNaOH SAP (mg/g)Notes
Olive oil0.135Mild, slow to trace, high glycerin
Coconut oil0.190Big lather, drying in high amounts
Palm oil0.141Hard bar, stable lather, cheap
Sunflower oil0.135Cheap, soft bar, goes rancid if old
Shea butter0.128Creamy, moisturizing, expensive
Castor oil0.129Boost lather, use under 10%

Lesson 2.4 - Superfatting

If you use exactly enough lye to saponify all the oil, the result is a harsh soap with free lye left. Nobody wants that. So we deliberately add extra oil - typically 5% more than the lye will react with.

Example: a recipe with 1,000 g of oil. At 5% superfat, you calculate the lye for 950 g of oil. The remaining 50 g stays as free oil in the finished soap - making it milder and creamier.

SuperfatResult
0-2%Harsh, drying, lye burn risk
5%Standard. Mild and hard bar.
8%Very mild, softer bar, shorter shelf life
Over 10%Soap may go rancid, lather weak
The SAP rule: Never guess lye amounts. Always use a lye calculator. Never trust a recipe you found online without checking its math.
Water-to-lye ratio: Standard is 2 parts water to 1 part lye by weight. Too little water - soap seizes. Too much - soap takes months to harden.
Practical exercise 2.1: Go to soapcalc.net. Enter a recipe of 500 g olive oil + 500 g coconut oil + 500 g palm oil, 5% superfat. Write down the lye and water amounts it calculates.
Practical exercise 2.2: Verify the SAP value for your cooking oil. If it is a blend, use the highest SAP value in the mix.
