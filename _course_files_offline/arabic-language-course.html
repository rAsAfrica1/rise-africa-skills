⚠ SAFETY WARNING — Food, Feed & Chemical Compliance

Agricultural exports to Arabic-speaking markets are subject to strict food-safety and phytosanitary rules. Before shipping any food, feed, or treated produce:

  • Never apply pesticides, fumigants, or veterinary drugs above the label rate, and always observe the stated pre-harvest interval. Residues above Codex Alimentarius maximum residue limits (MRLs) will cause rejection at Gulf ports.
  • Gulf Cooperation Council (GCC) standards require halal certification for meat and animal products; falsified or expired certificates trigger bans on the exporter, not just the consignment.
  • Aflatoxin contamination in groundnuts, maize, and sesame is a leading cause of rejected African cargo. Test every lot before loading.
  • Store agricultural chemicals in a locked, ventilated area away from food, feed, and children. Wear gloves and a mask when handling.
  • Keep safety data sheets (SDS) and phytosanitary certificates with the shipping documents. Missing paperwork means demurrage charges at your cost.

Quick Reference Checklist

  • ✓ Confirm your buyer's preferred Arabic dialect — Gulf (Khaliji) for UAE/Saudi, Egyptian for Cairo, Maghrebi for Algeria/Morocco — before your first call.
  • ✓ Prepare a one-page Arabic product sheet with price, quantity, packaging, and Incoterms. Send it before the meeting.
  • ✓ Verify halal, phytosanitary, and certificate-of-origin requirements for the specific destination country — they differ between GCC states.
  • ✓ Get a lab test for aflatoxin and pesticide residues on every export lot. Budget for it as a fixed cost, not an optional one.
  • ✓ Confirm payment method in writing — letter of credit, documentary collection, or advance — and check the buyer's bank before shipping.
  • ✓ Learn 20 high-frequency trade phrases by heart; fluency in negotiation matters more than grammar perfection.
  • ✓ Price freight and insurance for at least two routes (e.g. Djibouti vs. Port Sudan) before quoting CIF.
  • ✓ Keep a bilingual record of every quotation, order, and payment. Disputes in cross-border trade are won with documentation.
