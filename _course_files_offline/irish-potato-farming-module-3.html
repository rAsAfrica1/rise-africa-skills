Lesson 1

Earthing Up (Hilling) — the Critical Practice

In this lesson
  • Understand why hilling matters
  • Time the two hillings correctly
  • Hill without damaging the roots

Earthing up means drawing soil up around the base of the plants to form a ridge. It is the single most important cultural practice in potato growing after planting.

Why it matters

  • Prevents new tubers from being exposed to light, which causes greening (green tubers are bitter and toxic)
  • Provides loose soil for tubers to expand into
  • Improves drainage around the tubers
  • Reduces weed competition
  • Protects tubers from potato tuber moth

When to hill

  • First hilling: 20–25 days after emergence, when plants are 15–20 cm tall. Bring soil to about half the plant height.
  • Second hilling: 10–15 days later, at 40–50 cm plant height. Bring more soil to make a full ridge 30–40 cm high.

How to hill

Use a hoe to pull soil from between ridges up around the plant. Do not bury the leaves. Do not cut the roots — work carefully, especially in dry soil.

Lesson 2

Feeding at the Right Stages

In this lesson
  • Apply the base NPK correctly
  • Top-dress at the right stages
  • Watch for magnesium and calcium deficiency

Base fertiliser (at planting)

400–600 kg/ha of compound NPK (17-17-17 or similar) placed in a band at planting, 5 cm below and to the side of seed.

Top-dress 1 (at first hilling, ~25 days)

100–150 kg/ha of urea or CAN, broadcast or banded, then covered with soil by the hilling.

Top-dress 2 (at second hilling, ~40 days)

100 kg/ha of urea or CAN. Some growers use a potassium-rich fertiliser (SOP) at this stage for tuber quality.

Magnesium and calcium

Potato responds to magnesium in acidic soils. Foliar spray of magnesium sulphate (Epsom salt) at 1% at tuber initiation. Calcium deficiency causes internal brown spot — a foliar calcium spray may help.

Over-fertilising

Too much nitrogen produces big leafy plants with small tubers. Stick to the schedule above. Potassium is more important than nitrogen after tuber initiation.

Lesson 3

Water Needs and Irrigation

In this lesson
  • Match water to the three critical stages
  • Choose irrigation method
  • Avoid over-watering

Water need by stage

  • Establishment (0–20 days): moderate — keep soil moist but not wet
  • Vegetative (20–45 days): rising — soil moisture should be steady
  • Tuber initiation (45–60 days): critical — never stress now
  • Tuber bulking (60–90 days): critical — steady moisture, no fluctuations
  • Maturation (90+ days): reduce water to firm up skins before harvest

Irrigation methods

  • Furrow: cheap, common in highland Africa
  • Sprinkler: efficient, but wets foliage which increases blight risk — irrigate early in the day so leaves dry
  • Drip: the best option — delivers water to the ridge, keeps foliage dry, saves 40–60% water

Waterlogging

Potato is highly sensitive to waterlogging — tubers rot within days. Ensure ridges drain; do not irrigate heavily after rain.

Lesson 4

Weed Control Without Damaging Tubers

In this lesson
  • Time the two critical weedings
  • Weed without disturbing the ridge
  • Use mulch where possible

Weeds compete heavily with potato in the first 6 weeks. Once the canopy closes, potato suppresses most weeds on its own.

Two weedings are enough

  1. First weeding: 2–3 weeks after emergence, combined with first hilling. Shallow hoe between ridges, hand-pull within ridge.
  2. Second weeding: at second hilling (5–6 weeks). Final pass before canopy closure.

Do not hoe deep or after tuber initiation — you will damage tubers that are forming.

Mulch

A mulch of 5–8 cm of straw or dry grass over the ridge reduces weeds, keeps soil cool, and helps retain moisture. Excellent in hot dry areas.

Lesson 5

The Daily and Weekly Routine

In this lesson
  • Adopt the daily walk
  • Set the weekly tasks
  • Keep records that make next season better

Daily (10–15 min per 1,000 m²)

  • Walk the plot at the same time each morning
  • Check for wilting, blight lesions, insect damage
  • Water if dry (finger test)
  • Remove any obviously diseased leaves or plants

Weekly

  • Scout for late blight carefully — every plant, look at lower leaves
  • Hand-weed escapes
  • Check for aphids and Colorado potato beetle
  • Record observations

Monthly

  • Review records
  • Plan next hilling or spray
  • Check for tuber moth signs in the soil near plants

The walk catches blight in the first plant on day one. That single habit prevents a 10-day crop wipe-out.

Knowledge check — Module 3

1. What is earthing up?

Correct: drawing soil up. Prevents greening, gives tubers room, improves drainage.

2. When is the first hilling done?

Correct: 20–25 days. Then again 10–15 days later.

3. What water stage is most critical for potato?

Correct: tuber initiation and bulking. Stress here reduces yield sharply.

4. Why avoid deep hoeing after tuber initiation?

Correct: damages tubers. Also increases rot risk.

5. When should irrigation be reduced?

Correct: at maturation. Prevents skin damage during lifting.

Module 3 capstone

Write your hilling and feeding plan: (1) first and second hilling dates; (2) base and top-dress fertiliser rates and dates; (3) water source and irrigation schedule; (4) weeding dates; (5) daily walk time and routine.
