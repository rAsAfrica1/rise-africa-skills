✅ Iron-Clad Price Check

Before you buy a boar, a sow, feed, or build housing, call 3 suppliers and compare. Prices and breed availability vary widely between regions and seasons. This course teaches you the method and the real biology — you confirm the local prices and market demand.

The numbers this course is built on, sourced and dated:
  • Gestation is fixed by pig biology at 114 days — three months, three weeks, three days — regardless of breed or country.
  • Pre-weaning piglet mortality typically runs 10–20%, and can reach 25% in poorly managed systems. Crushing by the sow is the single biggest cause, responsible for roughly 55% of liveborn deaths.
  • A sow returns to fertile heat 4–7 days after weaning if she is in good body condition — this "wean-to-service interval" is the number that decides how many litters she gives you per year.
  • Smallholder litters in East and Southern Africa commonly run 10–14 piglets born; well-managed commercial herds elsewhere reach considerably higher.
  • African Swine Fever has no vaccine and no cure. It can wipe out a herd in days. Biosecurity is not optional reading — it is survival.
Sources: peer-reviewed swine research (Muns et al. 2016; PMC studies on farrowing duration and crushing mortality), Merck Veterinary Manual, Uganda/Nigeria smallholder pig surveys. Figures are ranges — your herd, breed and management will move them. Verify against your own vet and local extension officer.

Practical Reference

⚠️ SAFETY WARNING — African Swine Fever and Zoonoses

African Swine Fever (ASF) has no vaccine and no cure. It is a notifiable disease in every African Union member state. If you suspect ASF — sudden high mortality, blue-purple skin blotches on ears and belly, bloody diarrhoea, abortion in sows — do not move pigs, do not sell pigs, do not slaughter for home consumption. Report immediately to your District Veterinary Officer or national veterinary authority. Moving infected pigs off your farm is how ASF travels between farms and destroys village economies.

Biosecurity rules that are non-negotiable: quarantine every new pig for at least 21 days in a pen you can disinfect; never feed uncooked swill or kitchen waste (ASF survives in cured and frozen meat for months); disinfect boots and vehicles at the gate; keep a closed herd where possible. Brucellosis, leptospirosis and pork tapeworm (Taenia solium) are also transmissible to humans — wear gloves when handling aborted material, and cook pork to an internal temperature of 71°C. Always consult a registered veterinarian before administering any drug, and observe withdrawal periods strictly before slaughter.

Quick Reference Checklist

  • Record every service date. Mark the 114-day farrowing date on your calendar the same day you serve the sow. No date, no planning.
  • Body condition score your sows monthly. A sow that is too thin or too fat will not return to heat in 4–7 days after weaning — and you lose a whole litter per year.
  • Weigh or estimate feed, do not eyeball it. Recalculate your FCR at least quarterly against actual kilos of feed and actual kilos of pig sold.
  • Count piglets born alive and count deaths daily. Identify the cause of each pre-weaning death — crushing, starvation, disease — and act on the biggest category.
  • Maintain a closed herd and a 21-day quarantine pen. Never bring a new pig directly into your main pens.
  • Keep a written biosecurity log. Visitors, vehicles, feed deliveries, new stock — date and time. This is your defence if ASF hits your area.
  • Price your pigs before you finish them. Know your target market weight, your buyer, and your break-even price per kg live weight at least 8 weeks before slaughter.
  • Build a 3-month feed reserve. Feed price spikes and supply interruptions kill more small pig businesses than disease does.
