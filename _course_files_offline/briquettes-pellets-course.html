Read before handling any pesticide, fertiliser, veterinary drug, or stored food product.

  • Pesticides: Always wear the full label-specified PPE (gloves, boots, coveralls, face shield or goggles, respirator where required). Never eat, drink, or smoke during application. Never re-use pesticide containers for water or food storage â this causes fatal poisonings every year across Africa.
  • Re-entry intervals: Do not enter treated fields or handle treated produce before the label-specified re-entry interval has elapsed. This is a legal requirement in most African regulatory frameworks and a life-safety rule in all of them.
  • Pre-harvest intervals: Respect the stated interval between last application and harvest. Selling produce with illegal residues can destroy an export contract, trigger a market ban, and harm consumers.
  • Storage: Store agro-chemicals in a locked, ventilated, clearly labelled cabinet away from food, feed, seed, and children. Never store pesticides in beverage bottles or unlabelled containers.
  • Fumigation: Only licensed, trained operators should fumigate stored grain or warehouses. Fumigants such as phosphine are acutely toxic and require gas-tight seals, monitoring, and clearance before re-entry.
  • Aflatoxin: Damp or mouldy maize, groundnuts, and other staples can carry aflatoxins, which are carcinogenic and can cause acute poisoning. Dry to safe moisture content (typically 13% or below for grain), store off the floor, and reject visibly mouldy or discoloured lots. Never blend mouldy grain into clean grain for sale.
  • Emergency action: If someone shows symptoms of poisoning â vomiting, blurred vision, breathing difficulty, or convulsions â call the nearest poison control centre or emergency services immediately and bring the product label. Do not induce vomiting unless instructed by a medical professional.
  • Record keeping: Log every chemical application: product name, active ingredient, rate, date, operator, and crop. This protects your business in a traceability audit and helps you manage resistance and residue risk.

Regulatory reference: FAO/WHO International Code of Conduct on Pesticide Management and national pesticide registration authorities. When in doubt, stop and consult your national extension service or a certified agronomist.

Quick Reference Checklist

Run this checklist at the start of every planning cycle, before every planting season, and before every investor or lender conversation.

  1. Validate your numbers locally. Confirm at least three current supplier quotes and two buyer price indications for your main input and output. Do not rely on published benchmarks alone.
  2. Map your value chain position. Identify exactly which of the five stages you operate in, and which stage captures the most margin in your market. Decide whether to move up, down, or deepen where you are.
  3. Quantify your post-harvest loss. Measure actual loss at harvest, transport, storage, and processing. A 10-point reduction in loss is often worth more than a 10% price increase.
  4. Stress-test your working capital. Model a 30% input price rise and a 30-day delay in buyer payment. If the business fails either test, fix financing before scaling production.
  5. Confirm regulatory and certification status. Check product registration, export certification, phytosanitary requirements, and tax registration before signing supply contracts.
  6. Complete a chemical and food safety audit. Verify PPE availability, storage security, label compliance, re-entry and pre-harvest intervals, and aflatoxin risk controls for all stored staples.
  7. Secure at least two market channels. A single buyer is a single point of failure. Contract, negotiate, or pilot a second route to market before the season begins.
  8. Document everything. Keep records of costs, yields, losses, sales, and compliance. Clean records are the single strongest asset when applying for finance or a grant.
