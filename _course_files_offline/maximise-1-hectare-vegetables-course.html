Agricultural chemicals can kill or cause serious long-term illness. Before handling any pesticide, herbicide, fungicide, or concentrated fertilizer:

  • Wear the full PPE: gloves, long sleeves, trousers, boots, eye protection, and a nose/mouth mask. Never spray in shorts or bare feet.
  • Read the label in a language you understand. If there is no label or it is illegible, do not use the product.
  • Never eat, drink, smoke, or touch your face while handling chemicals. Wash hands and face with soap immediately after.
  • Store chemicals locked away from children, food, and animal feed. Never decant into drink bottles.
  • Respect the pre-harvest interval (PHI) on the label â harvesting too early leaves illegal residues that can make buyers and consumers sick and get you banned from markets.
  • If someone swallows a pesticide, call emergency services and take the container to the clinic. Do not induce vomiting unless told to by a medical professional.
  • Dispose of empty containers by triple-rinsing and puncturing them. Never reuse for water or food.

Reference: WHO/FAO International Code of Conduct on Pesticide Management (2023); FAO Pesticide Registration Toolkit.

Quick Reference Checklist

  1. Plan the whole hectare before planting. Map every bed, path, and water line. Decide which crop goes where and when it will be harvested and sold. A written plan prevents cash-flow gaps.
  2. Choose crops for your market, not for fashion. Confirm a buyer or market before you plant. Grow at least three crops so one failure does not wipe out your season.
  3. Raise seedlings in trays, not in the open seedbed. Tray-raised seedlings give 90â95% survival, use less seed, and transplant faster. Harden off for 5â7 days before transplanting.
  4. Install drip irrigation and mulch together. This combination saves 50â60% of water and suppresses weeds. Check emitters weekly and flush lines monthly.
  5. Build soil fertility for free. Make compost continuously, rotate crop families, and plant cover crops in every fallow period. Test soil at least once a year.
  6. Scout your fields at least twice a week. Walk a W-pattern, check undersides of leaves, and record what you see. Act on thresholds, not on fear.
  7. Harvest at the correct maturity and grade immediately. Sort into uniform sizes and grades. Uniform produce sells for 20â50% more than a mixed pile.
  8. Keep records of every cost and sale. A simple notebook beats memory. Know your cost per crate and your break-even price before you negotiate.
