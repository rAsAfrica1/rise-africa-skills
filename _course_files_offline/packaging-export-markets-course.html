Packaging materials that touch food must be food-grade. Do not use recycled or unknown-origin plastics, printed sacks, or repurposed chemical drums for food. Non-compliant materials can leach mineral oils, phthalates and heavy metals into your product â a direct health risk and an automatic rejection at EU and UK borders.

If you handle or store pesticides, fumigants or cleaning chemicals, keep them in a locked, separately ventilated store, never in the same room as packaged food. Follow the label's PPE instructions (gloves, mask, eye protection). Observe the pre-harvest interval strictly â maximum residue limits (MRLs) are enforced by testing, and a single exceedance can blacklist your farm for the season.

For groundnuts, maize and tree nuts, moisture above 10% at packing invites Aspergillus growth and aflatoxin. Aflatoxin is a carcinogen; it is not destroyed by cooking or roasting. Test every lot before export and retain a sample for 12 months. If in doubt, do not ship.

Quick Reference Checklist

  1. Confirm the destination standard first. Get the buyer's written specification and the importing country's labelling regulation before you print a single label.
  2. Lock the food-contact certificate. Obtain a written declaration from your packaging supplier that the material meets EU Reg. 1935/2004, FDA 21 CFR, or your target market's equivalent.
  3. Pre-shipment sample test. Send one packed sample to the buyer or an accredited lab for moisture, aflatoxin, MRL and drop-test before the full lot is packed.
  4. Design labels for the strictest market. If you sell to both the EU and the Gulf, build one label that satisfies both â it reduces SKU complexity and cost.
  5. Control the cold chain. Use data loggers on every reefer consignment; a 2-hour temperature break above 8Â°C can void a fresh-produce claim.
