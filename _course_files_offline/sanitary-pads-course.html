Practical Reference

This reference block is designed for use in the field and at the desk. It gives you the numbers, the visual logic, and the checklists that turn a training idea into a bankable operation. Everything below is built for African conditions: smallholder aggregation, variable rainfall, informal markets that still move real money, and buyers who pay on delivery or not at all. Use the tables to benchmark your own costs, use the diagram to explain the system to a lender or off-taker, and use the checklist before you commit capital.

â  SAFETY WARNING: Agro-Chemicals, Aflatoxins and Food Handling

This material involves health, food and chemical risk. Read before any field activity.

  • Pesticides and herbicides: Always wear gloves, boots, long sleeves and a face mask. Never eat, drink or smoke while handling. Store locked away from food, feed and children. Follow the label â the label is the law. Dispose of empty containers through approved collection points, never in water sources or fields.
  • Aflatoxin and mycotoxin risk: Maize, groundnuts and other staples can carry aflatoxin if dried or stored poorly. Damp grain is dangerous grain. Dry to safe moisture (maize: 13% or below) and store in hermetic or well-ventilated conditions. Never sell or feed visibly mouldy grain. Aflatoxin is a cumulative liver carcinogen and cannot be removed by cooking.
  • Food handling: Keep raw and processed products separate. Wash hands and equipment. Use potable water. For dairy, meat and fresh produce, maintain the cold chain or sell within safe time limits.
  • Personal protective equipment (PPE): Budget for PPE as a real cost line, not an afterthought. It is cheaper than a hospital visit and it protects your workforce, your licence and your buyer relationship.
  • Emergency: Keep the local poison control or nearest clinic number posted at the store. If exposure occurs, remove contaminated clothing, wash with clean water, and seek medical help immediately with the product label in hand.

Quick Reference Checklist: Before You Commit Capital

  1. Confirm demand before you produce. Get a written or at least documented verbal commitment from a buyer â volume, quality spec, price formula and payment terms. No buyer, no business.
  2. Cost the full chain, not just the farm. Include aggregation, transport, storage, processing, finance cost, levies and losses. A 30% farm margin can become a 5% business margin after the chain takes its share.
  3. Stress-test your cash cycle. If your buyer pays in 60 days and your farmers need cash in 7, you need working capital. Arrange it before planting, not after harvest.
  4. Lock your quality standard. Agree moisture, grade, packaging and traceability requirements in writing. Quality rejection at the gate is the most common cause of smallholder enterprise failure.
  5. Build the records habit. Track every input, every kilogram, every payment. Records are the collateral that unlocks finance and the evidence that wins contracts.
  6. Plan for post-harvest loss. Budget for drying, hermetic storage and proper handling. Cutting loss by 10 percentage points is often more profitable than raising yield by 10 percent.
  7. Comply and document. Licences, food safety, environmental and labour compliance are market access requirements, not paperwork. Non-compliance closes doors permanently.
  8. Protect people and product. Enforce PPE, safe chemical handling and aflatoxin control. One contamination incident can destroy a brand and a community's market access.
