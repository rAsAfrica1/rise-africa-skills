Practical Reference

Forestry and tree planting is a capital business with a long compounding curve. The operators who win are the ones who plan the harvest calendar before they plant the first seedling, secure a market or off-take agreement, and treat every hectare as a balance sheet line. Use the reference material below to pressure-test your plan, benchmark your costs, and confirm the numbers with three local suppliers before you commit money.

â  SAFETY WARNING â Chemicals, Chainsaws and Fire

  • Agrochemicals: Never mix, store, or apply pesticides, herbicides, or fertilisers without gloves, boots, eye protection, and a respirator. Follow the label exactly. Keep children and livestock out of treated blocks for the full re-entry interval.
  • Chainsaws: Wear cut-resistant trousers, helmet with visor, ear defenders, and steel-toe boots. Never cut above shoulder height. Kickback kills â keep the chain brake engaged when not cutting.
  • Fire: Maintain a 3â6 m firebreak around every timber block before the dry season. Never burn slash on a windy day. Have a knapsack pump and water drums on site.
  • Tree climbing and felling: Felling is the single most dangerous farm task. Use trained labour, never work alone, and clear a two-tree-length escape path before every cut.
  • Carbon credit contracts: Never sign a carbon or off-take agreement without independent legal review. Some contracts lock land use for 30+ years.

Quick Reference Checklist

  1. Confirm the market first. Call three buyers (sawmill, fruit exporter, pole merchant) and get written indicative prices before you plant a single seedling.
  2. Match species to your agro-ecological zone. Check rainfall, altitude, and soil pH against the species fact sheet. Wrong species = 10 lost years.
  3. Cost the full rotation, not just Year 1. Budget weeding, firebreaks, beat-up, and maintenance for every year up to harvest.
  4. Secure your land tenure in writing. A tree crop is a long-term asset â a verbal lease is a business risk. Get it documented.
  5. Plant a nitrogen-fixing intercrop. Leucaena, Gliricidia, or pigeon pea cut fertiliser costs and feed livestock.
  6. Register for carbon credits only with a verified standard. Use Verra VCS or Gold Standard; avoid informal brokers promising quick cash.
  7. Keep a record book from day one. Track every input, labour hour, and sale. This is what lenders and buyers trust.
  8. Plan the harvest calendar and transport. A mature tree with no road access or buyer is a liability, not an asset.
