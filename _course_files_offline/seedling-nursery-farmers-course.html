â  SAFETY WARNING â Chemicals, Seed Treatments and Human Health

  • Never mix, store or apply pesticides, fungicides or seed treatments near seedlings you will sell for food crops within the pre-harvest interval on the label.
  • Wear gloves, boots, long sleeves and a face mask when handling treated seed or any agrochemical. Wash hands and clothes immediately after.
  • Keep all chemicals locked away from children, animals and food. Use a dedicated, labelled container â never a drink bottle.
  • Follow the label rate exactly. Over-dosing kills beneficial soil life, contaminates water and leaves residues that can fail buyer tests.
  • If you or a worker shows dizziness, vomiting, blurred vision or skin burns after chemical contact, stop work, wash with clean water and seek medical help immediately. Take the product label to the clinic.
  • Dispose of empty containers through a licensed collection point â never burn them or dump them in a stream or field.

Quick Reference Checklist

  1. Secure orders before sowing. Collect 30â50% deposits from at least 10 farmers so seed and media are funded by customers, not savings.
  2. Test every seed lot. Germinate 100 seeds on damp cloth before committing a full tray. If below 70%, buy fresh seed.
  3. Sanitise trays, tools and benches between batches. Damping-off and root rot are the fastest way to lose a season's margin.
  4. Keep a daily record book: sowing date, variety, germination %, water, treatments, losses. This is your pricing and planning data.
  5. Grade before you sell. Grade A (uniform, strong), Grade B (slightly smaller), reject (weak, diseased). Price by grade; never mix.
  6. Harden seedlings 7â10 days before delivery by reducing shade and water gradually. Hardened seedlings survive transplant and earn repeat orders.
  7. Deliver early morning or late afternoon in shaded crates. Seedlings that arrive wilted cost you the next order.
  8. Review margins weekly. If gross margin per 1,000 seedlings falls below USD 15, cut input costs or raise price before the next sowing.
