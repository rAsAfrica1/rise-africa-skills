â  SAFETY WARNING â AGROCHEMICALS AND AFLATOXIN

Agrochemicals used in crop and livestock production can cause acute poisoning and long-term illness. Follow these non-negotiable rules:

  • Never re-use pesticide containers for water, food or animal feed. Triple-rinse and puncture them before disposal.
  • Wear gloves, boots, long sleeves and a face mask when mixing or spraying. Never spray against the wind.
  • Respect the pre-harvest interval (PHI) on the label. Selling produce inside the PHI is illegal in most African jurisdictions and harms consumers.
  • Store pesticides in a locked, ventilated cabinet away from food, feed and children. Keep the label and safety data sheet.
  • Aflatoxin from poorly dried and stored maize and groundnuts is a carcinogen. Dry grain to safe moisture (maize below 13.5%) and test before selling into food markets.
  • If poisoning is suspected, remove the person from exposure, keep the label, and call the nearest poison control centre or health facility immediately. Do not induce vomiting unless instructed by a medical professional.

Quick Reference Checklist

  1. Know your unit economics before you plant or place stock. Write down cost per hectare or per bird, expected yield, and the break-even price. If you cannot state your break-even, you are not ready to scale.
  2. Lock the buyer before you lock the seed. Verbal interest is not a contract. Get volume, grade, delivery window and price mechanism in writing, even if it is a simple purchase order.
  3. Budget the whole working-capital cycle. Poultry runs 42â49 days; maize runs 120â150 days. Match your loan tenor to the cycle, not to the calendar year.
  4. Invest in post-harvest handling first. Crates, tarpaulins, hermetic bags and shade nets often return more per dollar than extra fertiliser.
  5. Keep a daily record book. Inputs, labour, rainfall, mortality, prices. Twelve months of clean records is the single strongest asset in any loan application.
  6. Comply before you are forced to. Register your business, obtain the relevant county or national permits, and keep agrochemical and food-safety documentation current. Buyers and lenders screen for this.
  7. Build one reliable market channel at a time. Depth beats breadth. A single hotel or cooperative contract that pays on time is worth more than five speculative buyers.
  8. Reinvest a fixed share of every harvest. Allocate at least 20% of net margin to the next cycle's inputs before personal spending. This is how smallholder operations become businesses.
