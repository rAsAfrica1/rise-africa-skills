Practical Reference

This reference section is designed for agribusiness operators, farm managers, and extension officers working across African value chains. It consolidates the operational benchmarks, safety protocols, and decision tools you need to move from theory to profitable execution. All figures are drawn from publicly available official sources and reflect conditions relevant to smallholder and commercial operations in sub-Saharan Africa. Use these tables as planning baselines, then adjust for your specific agro-ecological zone, market access, and input costs.

This warning applies to all operations involving pesticides, herbicides, fungicides, and post-harvest chemicals.

  • Never eat, drink, or smoke while handling agrochemicals. Wash hands and face thoroughly with soap after any contact.
  • Always wear personal protective equipment (PPE): gloves, boots, long-sleeved clothing, and a respirator mask when spraying. In many African markets, subsidised PPE is available through extension offices.
  • Respect pre-harvest intervals (PHI). Do not harvest or sell produce before the labelled waiting period after pesticide application. Selling contaminated produce can cause acute poisoning, market bans, and legal liability.
  • Store chemicals separately from food, feed, and seed. Locked, ventilated stores away from living quarters are mandatory.
  • Dispose of empty containers safely. Never reuse pesticide containers for water or food. Triple-rinse and puncture containers before disposal at designated collection points.
  • Aflatoxin risk: Poorly dried or stored maize and groundnuts can develop aflatoxins, which are carcinogenic and cause stunting in children. Dry to below 13% moisture and use hermetic storage. Test before selling to premium markets.
  • First aid: If poisoning is suspected, call the nearest poison control centre immediately. In Kenya: 0800 720 021. In Nigeria: 112. In South Africa: 0861 555 777.

Quick Reference Checklist

  1. Calculate your cost of production per hectare before planting. Include seed, fertilizer, labour, transport, and a 10% contingency. Know your break-even price.
  2. Test your soil at least once every three years. Soil testing kits are available through national agricultural research institutes and some agro-dealers for under USD 30.
  3. Use certified seed from registered suppliers. Save receipts and batch numbers for traceability and insurance claims.
  4. Plan storage before harvest. Hermetic bags (e.g., PICS, GrainPro) cost USD 2-3 each and can save 15-20% of your crop value.
  5. Aggregate with neighbours or a cooperative to access better prices, transport discounts, and formal buyers. A 10-tonne lot typically commands 15-25% more than a 1-tonne lot.
  6. Keep records. Track every input, labour hour, and sale. Digital tools like FarmDrive, Apollo Agriculture, or simple spreadsheets improve access to credit.
  7. Comply with food safety standards. If selling to processors or exporters, ensure aflatoxin levels are below 10 ppb (EU standard) or 20 ppb (US standard).
  8. Invest in post-harvest processing where feasible. Milling, packaging, or drying can increase margins by 25-40% compared to selling raw commodities.
