Practical Reference

Sugar preservation is a margin business, not a hobby. The operators who make money in jams, jellies, syrups and candied fruit are the ones who standardise their recipe, lock their sugar-to-fruit ratio, and control the setting point every single batch. Below is the working reference you will return to after each module. Print it, laminate it, and stick it above your production table.

⚠️ SAFETY WARNING — READ BEFORE YOU BOIL

Sugar syrups at setting point exceed 104°C and cause severe burns that are worse than boiling water because the syrup clings to skin. Always wear long sleeves, closed shoes and heat-resistant gloves. Never fill jars over your hands. Never seal a jar with a bulging or swollen lid — that signals Clostridium botulinum or yeast spoilage, and the toxin is odourless. Discard any jar with a broken vacuum, mould, off-smell or fizzing. Do not taste suspect product. If you use citric acid, pectin or preservative-grade inputs, store them away from food, label every container, and keep the supplier's safety data sheet on file. In many African markets food handlers must hold a valid medical certificate — check your local public health authority before selling to retailers.

Quick Reference Checklist

  • Weigh, don't guess. Record fruit weight, sugar weight and final yield for every batch. A recipe that is not written down cannot be repeated or costed.
  • Test the setting point twice. Use both the temperature method (104-105°C at your altitude) and the saucer/freeze test before you stop boiling.
  • Check pH. Fruit jellies need pH ≤ 3.5 for safe gelling and microbial control. A cheap pH strip costs less than one spoiled batch.
  • Sterilise everything. Jars, lids, spoons, funnels and cloths. Hot-fill at above 85°C and leave 6 mm headspace.
  • Water-bath process sealed jars for 10-15 minutes after filling to kill surface contaminants and form a vacuum.
  • Label with batch number and date. This is your traceability system and your defence if a customer complains.
  • Cost every batch. Know your cost per jar before you set a price. Target a gross margin of at least 50%.
  • Store cool, dark and dry. Heat and light degrade colour and pectin gel. Rotate stock first-in, first-out.
