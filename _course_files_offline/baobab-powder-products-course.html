Business takeaway: The yield gap is the single biggest commercial opportunity on the continent. African maize yields are roughly one-third of the global average, and cocoa yields are roughly half. Closing even 30% of that gap through better inputs, agronomy, and post-harvest handling represents billions in additional revenue. For an operator, this means your competitive advantage is often operational excellence, not market timing.

â  SAFETY WARNING â AGROCHEMICALS AND FOOD SAFETY

Agrochemicals used in crop production can cause acute poisoning, chronic illness, and death if mishandled. In Sub-Saharan Africa, the World Health Organization estimates that pesticide poisoning affects hundreds of thousands of agricultural workers annually, largely through unsafe handling and reuse of containers. Follow these non-negotiable rules:

  • Always wear the correct personal protective equipment (PPE): gloves, mask, goggles, long sleeves, and boots. Never spray in shorts or bare feet.
  • Never eat, drink, or smoke while handling agrochemicals.
  • Respect pre-harvest intervals (PHI) strictly. Crops sprayed with pesticides must not be harvested or sold until the labelled PHI has passed â this protects consumers and protects your market access.
  • Never reuse pesticide containers for water, food, or cooking oil. Triple-rinse and puncture containers before disposal.
  • Store all agrochemicals in a locked, ventilated, dedicated store away from food, feed, and children.
  • Seek immediate medical attention if you experience dizziness, nausea, blurred vision, or excessive sweating after handling chemicals. Bring the product label to the clinic.
  • Use only registered products approved by your national regulator. Counterfeit and banned pesticides are common in informal markets and are a serious food safety and export risk.

Quick Reference Checklist

Run through these eight action points before committing capital to any agribusiness venture:

  1. Validate demand with buyers, not assumptions. Secure at least one written expression of interest or off-take agreement before you plant, process, or invest in equipment.
  2. Build a full cost-of-production model using your own farm data plus the benchmarks in Table 2. Include labour, transport, finance costs, and a 10% contingency.
  3. Stress-test the numbers. Model a 30% yield drop and a 30% price drop simultaneously. If the business fails in that scenario, your capital structure is too fragile.
  4. Confirm input quality and legality. Buy seed and agrochemicals only from registered dealers. Keep receipts and batch numbers for traceability.
  5. Plan post-harvest handling before harvest. Drying, storage, and transport losses in Africa routinely reach 20â30% for grains and perishables. Budget for them.
  6. Identify your value chain position and your upgrade path. Use Figure 1 to decide whether you are a producer, aggregator, or processor â and what it would take to move one stage right.
  7. Arrange finance early. Working capital is the most common killer of African agribusinesses. Explore warehouse receipt systems, value chain finance, and blended finance facilities before you need them.
  8. Comply with food safety and export standards. If you intend to sell to formal processors, supermarkets, or export markets, confirm your compliance requirements (e.g., GlobalG.A.P., HACCP, national bureau of standards certification) at the planning stage, not after production.
