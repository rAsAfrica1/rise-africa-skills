Lesson 1

Soybean Rust

In this lesson
  • Identify soybean rust early
  • Act before it spreads
  • Use resistant varieties and timely sprays

Soybean rust (Phakopsora pachyrhizi) is the most destructive disease of soybean worldwide. In Africa it can reduce yield 30–80% in a susceptible variety. The disease starts on lower leaves and moves upward.

Symptoms

  • Small brown or reddish-brown spots on the underside of lower leaves
  • Spots produce powdery spore masses (tan or brown)
  • Leaves yellow, dry, and drop
  • Disease moves upward through the canopy
  • Pods fill poorly

Control

  1. Resistant varieties. Some varieties have partial resistance. Ask your research institute.
  2. Early planting. Rust severity increases later in the season.
  3. Fungicide programme. Triazole or strobilurin fungicides at first symptoms, repeat every 14 days. Usually 2–3 sprays per season.
  4. Wider spacing. Improves airflow, slows spread.
  5. Rotation and residue removal. Break the disease cycle.
The rule: rust is prevented by early action. Once it hits the upper canopy, spraying rarely recovers the yield. Scout weekly from flowering.
Lesson 2

Frogeye Leaf Spot and Other Foliar Diseases

In this lesson
  • Identify frogeye and other leaf diseases
  • Choose resistant varieties
  • Time sprays

Frogeye leaf spot (Cercospora sojina)

Circular spots with grey centres and dark red-brown borders — look like frog eyes. Mostly on upper leaves. Can cause 20–40% yield loss in susceptible varieties.

Control: resistant varieties, crop rotation, residue removal, fungicide (chlorothalonil or a triazole) at first symptoms.

Downy mildew (Peronospora manshurica)

Pale yellow patches on upper leaf surface with grey fungal growth on the underside. Worse in cool, humid weather. Manage with resistant varieties and fungicide if severe.

Anthracnose (Colletotrichum truncatum)

Dark lesions on stems, pods, and leaves. Worse in wet weather. Seed-borne. Control with certified seed, rotation, and fungicide.

Lesson 3

Bacterial Pustule, Mosaic and Other Viruses

In this lesson
  • Recognise bacterial and viral diseases
  • Understand why there is no cure
  • Prevent with certified seed and vector control

Bacterial pustule (Xanthomonas axonopodis)

Small raised pustules with a yellow halo on leaves. Rarely devastating but reduces yield 10–30% in wet weather.

Soybean mosaic virus (SMV)

Mottled, puckered, distorted leaves. Transmitted by aphids and by infected seed. Yield losses 20–50%. No cure.

Bean pod mottle virus and other viruses

Various viruses transmitted by beetles or aphids. Manage the same way as SMV.

Control for all viruses

  • Certified virus-free seed
  • Resistant varieties
  • Rogueing of infected plants
  • Aphid control with neem, soap, or imidacloprid
Lesson 4

Insects — Pod Borer, Stem Fly, Aphids

In this lesson
  • Identify the main insect pests of soybean
  • Scout weekly from flowering
  • Time sprays at pod set

Pod borer (Helicoverpa and Maruca)

The main yield-limiting insect pest. The larva bores into pods and eats the seed. Damage 20–50% if unmanaged.

Control: Bt spray weekly from flowering; chemical spray (chlorantraniliprole, spinosad, lambda-cyhalothrin) if heavy pressure; early harvest if pod damage becomes severe.

Stem fly (Melanagromyza sojae)

The larva mines the stem, causing wilting and plant death. Most damaging in the first 4 weeks. Control with seed treatment (imidacloprid or thiamethoxam).

Aphids

Suck sap and transmit viruses. Control with neem, soap, or targeted imidacloprid.

Whitefly and thrips

Secondary pests. Transmit some viruses. Manage as part of the general IPM programme.

Lesson 5

The Scouting and Spray Programme

In this lesson
  • Set a weekly scouting routine
  • Decide when spraying is justified
  • Respect pre-harvest intervals

Weekly scouting (15 min per hectare)

  1. Walk a W pattern
  2. At 10 points, check 5 plants: upper leaves (rust, frogeye), undersides (rust spores, aphids), stems (stem fly), pods (pod borer, when forming)
  3. Record observations with dates
  4. Photograph anything uncertain

Spray decisions

  • Rust: act at first symptom. Spray triazole or strobilurin.
  • Frogeye: spray if lesions are on >10% of leaf area.
  • Pod borer: spray at flowering onset if larvae present, or Bt weekly.
  • Aphids: spray if heavy pressure (virus risk).

Spray log

Record every spray: date, product, active, rate, area, PHI. Keep for next season and for buyers who test residues.

Knowledge check — Module 3

1. What is the most destructive disease of soybean?

Correct: soybean rust. Can cut yield 30–80%.

2. When do you first spray for rust?

Correct: at first symptom. Rust spreads fast once established.

3. What is the main insect pest of soybean?

Correct: pod borer. Bores into pods and eats seed.

4. What protects against stem fly?

Correct: seed treatment. Applied before planting.

5. What is the critical spray window?

Correct: flowering and pod fill. 2–3 sprays at this stage.
