6. Harvest, Storage and Selling

Bad storage eats 30% of your harvest. Bad timing costs 40% of the price.

Module 6 of 6 · Maize Farming for Profit

When to harvest

  • Husks dry and turn brown
  • Kernels hard, dented
  • Black layer at the base of the kernel (physiological maturity)
  • Grain moisture 20-25% at harvest
  • 90-150 days after planting depending on variety

Harvest as soon as mature. Leaving it in the field invites weevils, birds, and rot.

Drying

  • Dry on a clean concrete slab or tarpaulin - never on bare ground.
  • Spread 5-10 cm thick. Turn 3 times a day.
  • Target moisture: 13% for safe storage.
  • Test: bite a kernel - if it shatters, it is dry enough. If it dents, keep drying.
  • 5-7 days of sun drying usually gets there.

Shelling

  • Hand shelling for small plots.
  • Mechanical sheller for larger volumes - 500-1,500 kg/hour.
  • Do not shell too hard - cracked grain attracts weevils.

Storage

MethodCapacityNotes
Hermetic bags (PICS, GrainPro)50-100 kgNo chemical needed. Kills insects by oxygen deprivation.
Metal silos500-5,000 kgBest long-term. Rodent-proof.
Traditional crib, raised, with ash500-2,000 kgCheap but needs chemical dusting.
Actellic dust + airtight containerAnyCommon. Follow label rate.

Rules for storage:

  • Dry to 13% before storage.
  • Store off the ground on pallets.
  • Never mix old and new grain.
  • Check monthly for weevils and moisture.
  • Store away from walls, in the dark.

Price timing

Maize prices are lowest at harvest (gluts) and highest 4-6 months later (lean season). The price difference can be 40-100%.

MonthPrice index
Harvest (April-June)100
+2 months120-140
+4 months150-180
+6 months (lean season)180-250

Store properly and sell into the lean season for the best price.

The storage rule: 30% of African maize is lost to weevils and rot. Dry to 13% and store in airtight bags, and you keep that 30%.
Practical exercise 6: Store 100 kg of maize in a hermetic bag and 100 kg in a traditional sack. Weigh both after 3 months. Compare losses.
