â  SAFETY WARNING â AGROCHEMICALS AND FOOD SAFETY

Agro-chemicals used in crop production and post-harvest storage can cause acute poisoning, chronic illness, and death if mishandled. They can also leave residues that make food unsafe for local consumption and export markets, leading to rejected consignments and loss of contracts.

  • Always wear the full protective kit: gloves, boots, long sleeves, eye protection, and a respirator when spraying or handling concentrates.
  • Never eat, drink, smoke, or touch your face while handling pesticides. Wash thoroughly with soap after any contact.
  • Store chemicals in a locked, ventilated, dedicated store â never in the home, near food, feed, or water sources.
  • Observe the pre-harvest interval (PHI) on the label strictly. Do not harvest or sell produce before the PHI has elapsed.
  • Use only products registered by your national regulator and follow the label rate. Over-dosing does not increase yield and increases residue risk.
  • Dispose of empty containers through approved collection schemes. Never reuse pesticide containers for drinking water or food.
  • If poisoning is suspected, contact your nearest poison control centre or health facility immediately and bring the product label.

Quick Reference Checklist

Use this checklist at the start of each season or business planning cycle. Tick each item only when you have documented evidence â not just an intention.

  1. Confirm market before planting or purchasing stock. Have at least one written or verbal purchase commitment, or a verified spot price from two independent buyers, before committing working capital.
  2. Calculate your break-even price and volume. Know the exact price per kilogram or per unit at which you cover all variable and fixed costs. Write it on the wall of your store.
  3. Audit your post-harvest handling. Check moisture content, storage bags, pallet stacking, ventilation, and pest control. Reduce losses by even 5% and margins improve immediately.
  4. Verify input quality and registration. Buy seed, fertiliser, and agro-chemicals only from licensed dealers. Keep receipts and batch numbers for traceability.
  5. Maintain a simple record system. Record every input purchase, labour payment, sale, and loss. Without records you cannot price, borrow, or scale.
  6. Check your safety and compliance status. Protective equipment available and used? Pre-harvest intervals observed? Storage locked and ventilated? Waste disposal arranged?
  7. Review working capital and credit terms. Match your repayment schedule to your cash cycle. Do not take short-term credit for a long-cycle enterprise.
  8. Plan for one shock. Identify your single biggest risk â drought, price crash, pest outbreak, or buyer default â and write down one concrete mitigation action.
