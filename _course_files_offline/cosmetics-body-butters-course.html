Health risk: Aflatoxins are carcinogenic mycotoxins produced by Aspergillus flavus and A. parasiticus in poorly dried or stored maize, groundnut, and sorghum. Chronic exposure causes liver cancer, immune suppression, and stunting in children. In Africa, aflatoxin contamination contributes to an estimated 25,000â155,000 liver cancer cases annually (WHO, 2018).

Chemical safety: Never apply storage pesticides (e.g., phosphine, permethrin) without PPE â gloves, mask, goggles, and coveralls. Phosphine fumigation must only be performed by certified operators. Re-entry intervals must be observed. Store all agrochemicals in locked, ventilated, labelled cabinets away from food and children.

Action: Test every batch destined for human or animal consumption. Reject any grain with visible mould, discolouration, or musty odour. Report suspected contamination to national plant protection authorities immediately.

Quick Reference Checklist

  1. Harvest at physiological maturity: Delay increases field losses and aflatoxin risk. Target moisture content below 20% at harvest.
  2. Dry to safe moisture: Maize and sorghum to 13%; groundnut to 8â9%; rice to 14%. Use a calibrated moisture meter â do not rely on bite or feel tests.
  3. Thresh on clean tarpaulins: Never thresh on bare ground. This alone reduces contamination by 30â50%.
  4. Clean and grade before storage: Remove broken grains, chaff, and foreign matter. Broken kernels are entry points for mould and insects.
  5. Use hermetic storage: PICS bags or metal silos for grain intended for sale or seed. Inspect seals weekly for the first month.
  6. Monitor temperature and moisture monthly: Any temperature rise above 5Â°C within a storage lot signals insect or mould activity. Act immediately.
  7. Maintain traceability records: Record farm origin, harvest date, moisture at storage, and treatment history. Buyers and regulators increasingly require this.
  8. Train every worker on chemical and aflatoxin safety: Annual refresher training is a legal requirement in most African jurisdictions and a market access prerequisite.
