â  SAFETY WARNING â PESTICIDES, FERTILISERS AND FOOD CONTAMINATION

Pesticides and some fertilisers are toxic to humans and animals. In many African countries, misuse is the leading cause of farm-level poisoning and of export rejections due to residue limits.

  • Always read the label and follow the dilution rate exactly. Never guess.
  • Wear gloves, boots, long sleeves, and a face mask when mixing or spraying.
  • Never eat, drink, or smoke while handling chemicals.
  • Respect the pre-harvest interval (PHI) on the label â the number of days between the last spray and harvest. Selling before PHI can fail buyer tests and harm consumers.
  • Store chemicals in a locked, labelled cabinet away from food, feed, and children.
  • If someone is exposed, remove contaminated clothing, wash with soap and water, and call a poison centre or clinic immediately. In Zimbabwe, contact the Medicines Control Authority of Zimbabwe poison information service via your nearest clinic.
  • For aflatoxin risk (groundnut, maize), dry to safe moisture (below 13% for grain) and never sell mouldy grain for human consumption.

Quick Reference Checklist

  1. Test your soil before you plant. A basic soil test costs less than a bag of fertiliser and tells you exactly what to add. Contact your nearest government agricultural extension office.
  2. Call three suppliers before you buy any input. Compare price, delivery, and quality. Record the quotes in your record book.
  3. Calculate your break-even yield before planting. Total costs divided by expected price equals the tonnes you must sell to avoid a loss.
  4. Scout your crop twice a week. Walk a W-pattern across the field. Early pest detection saves more money than any spray.
  5. Plan your harvest date and buyer before the crop is ready. A confirmed buyer beats a full storehouse.
  6. Dry and store correctly. Target below 13% moisture for grain. Use hermetic bags or clean, raised, ventilated storage.
  7. Keep a simple record book. Every input cost, every sale, every date. This is the single habit that separates profitable farms from busy ones.
  8. Reinvest a fixed percentage of every sale. Set aside at least 20% of gross revenue for next season's inputs and equipment.
