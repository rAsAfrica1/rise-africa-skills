11. Record Keeping and Business Management

Horses are high-value animals. Every dollar needs tracking - this is how to run a real business.

Module 11 of 12 · Horse Breeding for Profit

Why records matter more with horses

A single horse can be worth $10,000-100,000. Mistakes cost far more than with cattle, sheep, or goats. Written records are the only way to know:

  • Which mare produces the best foals
  • Which stallion crosses work best
  • Which horses eat more than they earn
  • What each horse costs per year
  • Which buyers pay best and on time

Insure horses over $5,000 in value. Below that, self-insurance may be more economic.

Tax and compliance

  • Register the business once you sell horses regularly.
  • VAT only above your country's threshold.
  • Animal movement permits for cross-border sales.
  • Equine passports required in many countries for competition and export.
  • Vaccination records required for entry to shows and for export.
  • Keep all receipts for input purchases - they are deductible.
  • Depreciate facilities over 10-20 years for tax purposes.

  • Notebook - one paper book for daily events still works

Compliance records for export

If you export horses, keep meticulous records of:

  • Vaccination history (especially influenza, EHV, AHS)
  • Blood test results (EIA/Coggins, EVA, dourine, glanders)
  • Veterinary health certificates
  • Pedigree and registration papers
  • Microchip / brand identification
  • Movement records
The records rule: A horse business without records is a hobby. Detailed records tell you which mare earns her keep, which stallion crosses work, which buyers pay on time, and where you can cut costs.
Practical exercise 11: Start a per-horse ledger this month. Record every cost for each horse. At month-end, total each horse's cost. This is the number that tells you which horses are assets and which are liabilities.
