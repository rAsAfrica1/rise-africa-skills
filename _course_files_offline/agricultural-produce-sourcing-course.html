â  SAFETY WARNING â CHEMICAL RESIDUES AND FOOD SAFETY

Fresh produce is a food product. If you handle, store, grade or transport it, you carry legal and reputational liability. Never accept produce that smells of pesticide, has visible powder residue, or was harvested within the pre-harvest interval stated on the label.

  • Pesticide residues: Buyers and exporters test. A single rejected container can end a contract. Require farmers to declare the last spray date and product used.
  • Aflatoxin: Groundnuts, maize and other staples can carry aflatoxin â a carcinogen. Test before you sell to processors or feed buyers. Reject mouldy, discoloured or damp grain outright.
  • Personal protective equipment: If you or your team handle treated grain or apply storage chemicals, wear gloves, mask and eye protection. Never eat, drink or smoke during handling.
  • Wash and sanitise: Crates, scales and vehicles must be cleaned between loads. Cross-contamination between a manure load and a food load is a serious risk.
  • Do not use unregistered chemicals: Only use products registered in your country. Unregistered storage pesticides can render a whole consignment unsaleable and are illegal.

Reference: FAO/WHO Codex Alimentarius Maximum Residue Limits (fao.org/fao-who-codexalimentarius) and WHO Food Safety guidance (who.int).

Quick Reference Checklist

  1. Confirm three buyer prices before you buy. Never purchase a consignment without a confirmed or near-confirmed buyer price. A verbal "maybe" is not a price.
  2. Grade at the farm gate, not at the market. Sorting where the produce is grown cuts transport cost on rejected fruit and protects your reputation with buyers.
  3. Weigh everything twice. Use a calibrated digital scale and record farm-gate weight and delivered weight. The gap is your loss â track it weekly.
  4. Calculate landed cost before you quote. Purchase + labour + packaging + transport + fees + expected loss. If your margin is under 15%, walk away or renegotiate.
  5. Secure the cold chain or shade. Even a simple shade net and early-morning transport can cut losses by half on leafy greens and tomatoes.
  6. Get a signed delivery note at every handover. Date, weight, grade, condition, and the name of the person receiving. This is your evidence if a buyer disputes quality.
  7. Keep a daily cash book. Record every purchase, expense and sale on the day it happens. Cash businesses die from untracked leakage, not from bad prices.
  8. Build one repeat buyer before chasing ten new ones. A restaurant or processor that orders weekly is worth more than five one-off market sales.
