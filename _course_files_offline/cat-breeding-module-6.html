6. Health, Vaccines and Parasite Control

Cats are cheaper to keep healthy than to cure. Here is the full prevention program.

Module 6 of 12 · Cat Breeding for Profit

The seven diseases that kill cats and kittens

DiseaseCauseSignsPrevention
Panleukopenia (feline parvo)Virus (faecal-oral)Vomiting, diarrhoea, sudden death in kittensVaccinate at 6, 10, 14 weeks
Feline herpesvirus (FHV-1)VirusSneezing, eye discharge, feverVaccinate (part of FVRCP)
Feline calicivirus (FCV)VirusMouth ulcers, sneezing, lamenessVaccinate (part of FVRCP)
Feline leukaemia (FeLV)VirusWeight loss, anaemia, tumoursVaccinate, test before introduction
Feline immunodeficiency (FIV)VirusChronic infections, weight lossTest, no vaccine widely used. Manage carefully.
RabiesVirus (bite)Behaviour change, aggression, deathVaccinate - legally required in most countries
Feline infectious peritonitis (FIP)Coronavirus mutationWeight loss, fever, fluid in abdomenReduce stress, good hygiene, no cure historically

Vaccination schedule

AgeVaccine
6-8 weeksFirst FVRCP (herpes, calici, panleukopenia)
10-12 weeksSecond FVRCP + FeLV (if outdoor/at risk)
14-16 weeksThird FVRCP + FeLV booster + rabies (legal requirement)
12 monthsBooster of all
AnnuallyBoosters of core vaccines + rabies (every 1-3 years depending on country)

Breeding queens should be vaccinated before mating, not during pregnancy. Kittens get antibodies from colostrum, which protect them for 6-12 weeks.

FIV and FeLV testing

  • Every breeding cat must be tested. FIV and FeLV are lifelong infections.
  • Test at 6 months+ and again 3 months later for a definitive FeLV result.
  • Positive cats must not be bred. They can transmit to kittens and other cats.
  • Positive cats can live long lives but should be kept separate from negative cats.

Biosecurity in a cattery

  1. Quarantine new cats for 3-4 weeks in a separate room.
  2. Test for FIV/FeLV before introduction.
  3. Vaccinate before mixing.
  4. Disinfect between cats. Use feline-safe disinfectant.
  5. Footbaths at cattery entrance.
  6. Limit visitors. No visits from other cat owners without disinfection.
  7. Separate tools for quarantined animals.
  8. Do not share water or food bowls between litters.
  9. Isolate sick cats immediately - even a suspected case.
  10. Vaccinate all adults annually to maintain herd immunity.
  11. Keep vaccination records for every cat.

Dental care

  • Brush teeth 2-3 times per week (cat toothpaste only).
  • Provide dental chews and dental diet food.
  • Check teeth monthly for tartar and gum disease.
  • Professional scaling by vet every 1-3 years.
  • Never use human toothpaste - fluoride and xylitol are toxic.

Nail and ear care

TaskFrequency
Nail trim (if indoor only)Every 2-4 weeks
Ear check and cleanWeekly
Brush coatDaily for long coats, weekly for short
Bath (only if needed)Rarely - cats self-clean

When to call the vet - routine

  • Annual check-up for all adult cats.
  • Pre-breeding check for the queen.
  • HCM and PKD screening before first breeding.
  • Pregnancy diagnosis at 20-25 days.
  • Kitten health check before sale.
  • Any change in appetite, water intake, or behaviour.
  • Any new lump, wound, or lameness.
  • Sudden weight loss or gain.
  • Change in litter box habits.

First aid kit for a cattery

  • Digital thermometer
  • Antiseptic solution (chlorhexidine, iodine)
  • Eye drops (vet-approved)
  • Bandages, gauze, self-adhesive wrap
  • Saline solution for eye flushing
  • Bulb syringe (for newborn kittens)
  • Kitten milk replacer and bottles
  • Dewormer (kitten and adult doses)
  • Flea and tick treatment (cat-specific)
  • Glucose syrup (for hypoglycaemia in small kittens)
  • Notebook for records
  • Emergency vet contact number
The vaccination rule: Never skip a vaccine. Panleukopenia and URI are endemic in Africa and can kill whole litters. The $10-20 vaccine is the cheapest insurance you will ever buy.
Practical exercise 6: Set up a health calendar for your cattery. Mark vaccination dates, deworming dates, and flea/tick treatment dates. Post it on the cattery wall. Track every cat on the calendar.
