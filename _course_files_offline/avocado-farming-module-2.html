For most smallholders: plant 70% Hass + 30% Fuerte. Hass for export, Fuerte for local and diversification.

Rootstocks

Grafted trees combine a rootstock (soil-borne half) and a scion (fruiting half). The rootstock determines disease resistance and site adaptation.

RootstockAdvantage
Seedling rootstock (Mexican race)Hardy, cold-tolerant, common
Clonal rootstock (Dusa, Latas)Resistant to Phytophthora root rot
Dwarf rootstockSmaller tree, higher density planting

For sites with any risk of root rot, use clonal rootstock (Dusa, Latas).

Site selection

FactorIdealAvoid
Altitude800-2,100 mBelow 500 m (heat stress)
Rainfall800-1,600 mm/yearBelow 600 mm without irrigation
Soil depthOver 1 m, well drainedLess than 60 cm, waterlogged
Soil pH5.5-6.5Below 5.0 or above 7.5
SlopeGentle 5-15% for airflowSteep (erosion) or flat (frost pockets)
WindShelteredExposed to strong wind
Water qualityLow salinity (EC under 1.0)Salty, high chloride
Avocados are very sensitive to salty water. Test your irrigation water EC before planting. Above 1.0 EC, avocados struggle.

Spacing

SpacingTrees/haNotes
6 x 6 m278Traditional, easy management
5 x 5 m400High density, good for dwarf rootstocks
7 x 7 m204Large canopy varieties
4 x 2 m (hedgerow)1,250Very high density - needs heavy pruning

For smallholders, 5 x 5 m or 6 x 6 m is the practical choice.

The site rule: Avocados hate waterlogged soil. If your site holds water after rain, do not plant. Choose another plot or install drainage first.
Practical exercise 2: Do a simple drainage test on your plot. Dig a 30 x 30 x 30 cm hole, fill with water, and time how long it takes to drain. Should drain fully within 24 hours.
