Practical Reference

This reference block turns the six modules into working numbers you can act on today. Smoking is a preservation business first and a flavour business second: your margin lives in shelf-life, yield, and repeat customers. Use the tables below as costing and process checkpoints, then adapt them to your own smokehouse, your own species mix, and your own local market prices. Always confirm current prices with at least three suppliers before you commit capital.

⚠ SAFETY WARNING - Food, Smoke, and Chemicals
  • Never use treated, painted, resinous, or chemically-preserved wood. Burning these releases carcinogens and toxic residues onto your product. Use only clean hardwood, coconut shell, or approved food-grade sawdust.
  • Smoking does not sterilise. It reduces moisture and adds antimicrobial compounds. Core temperature and salt/water-activity control are what make the product safe. Undercooked or under-salted product can carry Clostridium botulinum, Listeria, and Salmonella.
  • Do not use industrial salt, fertiliser-grade salt, or non-food-grade nitrites. Only food-grade curing salt (e.g. Prague Powder #1) at the manufacturer's stated dose. Overdosing nitrite is acutely toxic.
  • Keep raw and finished product physically separated at all times. Cross-contamination from raw drip onto smoked product is the most common cause of customer illness.
  • Maintain cold chain below 4°C for any product not fully dried and shelf-stable. If you cannot hold the cold chain, sell within 24-48 hours and label clearly.
  • Protect your workers. Smoke exposure causes respiratory disease. Site the smokehouse downwind of dwellings, provide ventilation, and rotate operators. See WHO guidance on household air pollution.

Quick Reference Checklist

  1. Verify raw material temperature at intake - reject anything above 4°C or with off-odours. Log batch, supplier, weight, and time.
  2. Weigh salt, not scoop it. Use a digital scale and a salinity meter. Record brine strength and cure start/end times for every batch.
  3. Form the pellicle by air-drying until the surface is tacky - this is what makes smoke adhere and colour develop evenly.
  4. Monitor core temperature with a calibrated probe, not by eye or timer. Hot smoking needs 60-85°C core depending on species and thickness.
  5. Cool fast - under 2 hours to below 4°C if you are not selling immediately. Slow cooling is a bacterial incubator.
  6. Label every pack with batch code, production date, best-before date, storage instruction, and your business contact. This is your traceability and your brand.
  7. Keep a batch cost sheet. Update raw material, fuel, packaging, and labour costs weekly. Know your true margin per kilogram, not per batch.
  8. Test shelf-life quarterly with a simple sensory and microbial check (or partner with a local lab). Never extend a best-before date without evidence.
