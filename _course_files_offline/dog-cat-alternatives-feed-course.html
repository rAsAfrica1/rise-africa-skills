This is a health and food-safety topic. Read before you mix a single batch.

  • Never feed mouldy or fermented material. Aflatoxins from poorly dried maize and groundnuts cause liver damage in pets and can transfer to humans handling contaminated feed. If it smells musty or shows discolouration, discard the batch â do not "dilute" it with clean material.
  • Rendered by-products must reach 133Â°C for 20 minutes at 3 bar (EU rendering standard) to kill pathogens including Salmonella and prions. Small-scale open-pot rendering does not achieve this. Source rendered meal from licensed renderers only.
  • Wear an N95 mask and gloves when milling dried insect meal or by-product meal. Fine protein dust is a respiratory sensitiser and can trigger asthma and allergic reactions in workers.
  • Keep pets away from raw insect colonies and from the mixing floor. Cross-contamination between raw input and finished feed is the single most common cause of batch rejection by buyers.
  • Do not use veterinary drugs or growth promoters in pet feed without a licensed veterinarian's written formulation. Residues are illegal in most African markets and will end your contract with any serious buyer.
  • Label every bag with batch number, production date, protein content, and a "not for human consumption" notice. This protects you legally and builds buyer trust.

Quick Reference Checklist

  1. Lock your protein source first. Before buying any equipment, secure a reliable weekly supply of BSF larvae, cricket meal, or licensed by-product meal. No input, no business.
  2. Test moisture on every batch. Target below 10% for dried meal. A $30 moisture meter saves a $300 ruined batch.
  3. Cost per kilogram before you price. Add input, transport, milling, packaging, and 15% wastage. If your cost is above 60% of your selling price, fix the input chain before scaling.
  4. Run a 7-day palatability trial with at least 5 dogs or cats before any commercial sale. Refusal means reformulate, not discount.
  5. Register your brand and get a feed licence where required (e.g. Zimbabwe's Department of Livestock and Veterinary Services, Kenya's KEPHIS/DAFF equivalents). Unlicensed feed cannot be sold to kennels, vets, or retail chains.
  6. Keep batch records. Input source, date, moisture reading, weight in, weight out, and buyer. Traceability is your insurance policy.
  7. Package for shelf life. Use food-grade, moisture-barrier bags with a heat seal. Open sacks lose quality in 2-3 weeks in humid African conditions.
  8. Reinvest 30% of profit into drying capacity first, then milling, then packaging. Drying is the bottleneck that kills most small feed operations.
