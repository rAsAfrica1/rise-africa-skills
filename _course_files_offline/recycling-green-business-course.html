â  SAFETY WARNING â E-Waste, Chemicals and Used Cooking Oil

E-waste contains lead, mercury, cadmium and brominated flame retardants. Never burn cables or circuit boards to recover copper â burning releases toxic dioxins that cause cancer and permanent neurological damage. Do not break cathode-ray tubes (old TVs, monitors) or handle lithium batteries without proper training.

Used cooking oil collection carries a fire and slip hazard. Store in sealed, labelled drums away from heat. Never mix UCO with other waste streams.

Minimum protection for all sorting and dismantling work: cut-resistant gloves, N95 or better respirator, safety glasses, closed shoes, and tetanus vaccination. Wash hands and change clothes before eating. Follow national environmental and occupational health regulations in your country.

Quick Reference Checklist

  1. Run the three-supplier price check. Call three buyers and three equipment suppliers before spending a cent. Record prices, dates and names in your record book.
  2. Secure your supply before your market. Lock in two or three steady waste sources (a market, a hotel group, a residential estate) before buying processing equipment.
  3. Sort at source where possible. Clean, single-stream material sells for two to three times mixed waste. Train your collectors on this.
  4. Weigh everything. Use a calibrated digital scale for every in-bound and out-bound load. Margin lives in the kilogram.
  5. Get your licences. Register the business, obtain any required waste-handling or environmental permit, and keep copies on site.
  6. Protect your workers. Budget for gloves, respirators, eye protection and first-aid kit as a fixed monthly cost, not an afterthought.
  7. Track three numbers weekly: cost per tonne collected, price per tonne sold, and kilograms processed. If cost per tonne is rising faster than price, fix logistics first.
  8. Document for funders. Keep a simple monthly report â tonnes handled, revenue, jobs created, COâe avoided. Grant and corporate funders ask for exactly this.
