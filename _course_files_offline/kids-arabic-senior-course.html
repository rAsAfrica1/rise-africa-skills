Arabic for Teens

Three consonants make a root, and a root makes a whole family of words.

Ages 12–168 modules 🪨 Starts with no device

Arabic is built on three-letter roots. Take k-t-b and you get 'he wrote', 'book', 'office', 'library' and 'writer' — five words from one root, each with the same three letters sitting inside it. Learn to see the root and Arabic stops being a memory test and becomes a system. This course takes you from dots and joins to reading real signs and writing a real message.

This reference block is built for the parent, tutor, or teen who wants to move from "I can recognise a letter" to "I can read a street sign and write a message that a shopkeeper in Cairo, Khartoum, or Nouakchott will understand." Everything below is a tool. Use the diagram to see how the root system works, use the tables to drill the highest-frequency patterns, and use the checklist as your weekly teaching script. Arabic is one of the working languages of the African Union and an official language across North Africa, the Sahel, and the Horn, so the skill you build here has real commercial and cultural reach across the continent.
