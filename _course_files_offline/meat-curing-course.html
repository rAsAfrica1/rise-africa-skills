â ï¸ SAFETY WARNING â Agrochemicals, Food Handling and Human Health

Read before any field, storage or processing activity.

  • Only use pesticides registered for your crop and country. Unregistered or banned products (including many obsolete organochlorines) are illegal and expose you to prosecution, market rejection and export bans.
  • Never re-use pesticide containers for water, food, cooking oil or animal feed. This is a leading cause of acute poisoning in African farming communities.
  • Wear personal protective equipment â gloves, boots, long sleeves, eye protection and a respirator when spraying. Do not spray against the wind. Observe the full pre-harvest interval before selling or eating the crop.
  • Store agrochemicals locked, labelled and away from food, feed and children. Keep a record of every application: date, product, rate, operator.
  • Mycotoxins (especially aflatoxin) are a food safety and trade risk, not just a quality issue. Damp grain, poor drying and humid storage drive contamination. Dry to safe moisture (typically 13% or below for maize), use pallets, and control humidity.
  • If poisoning is suspected, stop work, remove contaminated clothing, wash the affected area, keep the product label, and seek medical attention immediately. Contact your national poison information centre or nearest health facility.
  • Food businesses: maintain traceability from farm to buyer. One contamination incident can end a supply contract and trigger regulatory action under national food safety law and CODEX-aligned standards.

Quick Reference Checklist

  1. Validate demand before you produce. Secure at least one signed offtake or letter of intent from a buyer, processor or institutional purchaser before committing land or capital.
  2. Map your value chain position. Identify which stage you occupy and which adjacent stage you can realistically enter within 24 months â aggregation or primary processing are usually the lowest-barrier moves.
  3. Cost your unit economics per hectare, per tonne and per bag. If you cannot state your break-even price, you cannot negotiate. Include transport, levies, wastage and financing costs.
  4. Lock in input supply and quality. Buy certified seed and registered inputs from licensed dealers; keep receipts and batch numbers for traceability and dispute resolution.
  5. Invest in post-harvest handling first. Drying, pallets, hermetic storage and cold chain typically deliver faster payback than expanding cultivated area.
  6. Build a compliance file. Keep product registrations, phytosanitary certificates, food safety records, and staff training logs. Buyers and lenders now ask for these as standard.
  7. Arrange working capital before the season. Agree trade finance, invoice discounting or warehouse receipt facilities in advance; do not fund a harvest with emergency borrowing.
  8. Track and report. Maintain simple records of yield, cost, loss and margin per cycle. Data is what converts a farm into a financeable business.
