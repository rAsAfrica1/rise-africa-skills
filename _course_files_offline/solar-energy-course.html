Practical Reference

This reference section is built for the working solar technician and the solar business owner. Every figure below is a benchmark you can quote to a client, use to price a job, or hand to a supplier when negotiating. Solar is a cash business in most of Africa: the customer pays a deposit, you buy components, you install, you get paid the balance, and you service the system for years afterwards. The numbers below are what make that cycle profitable instead of a race to the bottom.

⚠ SAFETY WARNING — ELECTRICAL AND BATTERY HAZARDS

Solar DC circuits cannot be switched off at the panel. A string of panels in daylight is always live and can deliver lethal voltage. Never work on a DC string without covering the array or isolating at the combiner box, and never assume a "dead" inverter means a dead DC side. Lithium (LiFePO4) batteries can deliver thousands of amps into a short circuit and can enter thermal runaway if punctured, overcharged, or charged below 0°C. Use insulated tools, correct DC-rated breakers and fuses, and a torque wrench on all terminals. Lead-acid batteries vent explosive hydrogen: charge in a ventilated space and keep sparks away. Always install a DC isolator, an AC isolator, and an RCD/earth-leakage device. If you are not a licensed electrician, work under one. In most African jurisdictions, grid-tied connection without a certified installer is both illegal and uninsurable.

Quick Reference Checklist

  • Load audit first. List every appliance, its wattage, and hours per day before quoting. Never size a system from a client's guess.
  • Size for autonomy, not just daily yield. Decide 1, 2, or 3 days of battery autonomy based on the client's tolerance for outages and budget.
  • Match voltage and chemistry. Confirm battery bank voltage matches the inverter and that the charge controller supports the battery's required charge profile.
  • Check the roof and structure. Verify rafter condition, wind loading, and mounting angle before ordering rails; a failed mount destroys the panels and your reputation.
  • Cable and protection sizing. Use DC-rated cable sized for voltage drop under 3%, with correct fuses, breakers, and earthing on both DC and AC sides.
  • Commissioning record. Photograph and log open-circuit voltage, short-circuit current, earth resistance, and inverter settings; give the client a signed handover sheet.
  • Price with a margin, not a hope. Quote components + labour + transport + 15–25% margin + a service retainer. Underquoting kills solar businesses faster than competition.
  • Service contract. Offer a paid annual service: clean panels, check torque, test batteries, review monitoring data. Recurring revenue is what makes the business durable.
