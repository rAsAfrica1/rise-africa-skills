Practical Reference

This reference block is built for operators who are already handling carcasses, cutting to order, and watching the cold chain. Use it as a counter-side tool: print it, laminate it, and keep it where the knives and scales live. Every figure below is drawn from published, verifiable sources so you can defend your pricing, your yields, and your hygiene claims to customers, inspectors, and lenders.

â  SAFETY WARNING â Biological and Chemical Hazards in Meat Handling

Raw meat carries Salmonella, E. coli O157:H7, Listeria, Campylobacter, and parasites such as Taenia and Trichinella. Brucellosis and tuberculosis can pass from infected carcasses to butchers through cuts and aerosol. Never handle an animal that died of unknown causes or shows fever, abscesses, or swollen lymph nodes â condemn the carcass and report it to the veterinary authority.

  • Wear cut-resistant gloves, an apron, and eye protection. Cover any open wound with a waterproof dressing before you start.
  • Never eat, drink, smoke, or touch your face while dressing or cutting. Wash hands with soap for at least 20 seconds after handling raw meat.
  • Keep knives razor-sharp â dull blades slip and cause the worst lacerations. Store them in a rack, never loose in a sink.
  • Sanitise knives, blocks, and tables between species and between raw and cooked product. Chlorine at 100â200 ppm for food-contact surfaces, rinsed after.
  • If you use disinfectants or pesticides, store them in a locked cabinet far from meat, and never decant into drink bottles. Read the label; mix only at the stated strength.
  • Stop work and seek medical care for deep cuts, fever, or persistent diarrhoea. Report suspected zoonoses to your local veterinary officer.

Quick Reference Checklist

  • Weigh and record every carcass at intake and after cutting. Your yield variance is your first signal of theft, shrinkage, or a dull boning technique.
  • Check the cold chain twice daily with a calibrated probe â cold room, display counter, and delivery box. Log the readings; a written log protects you in a dispute.
  • Rotate stock first-in-first-out. Date every tray. Minced meat and offal leave the counter the same day or become pet food, not customer food.
  • Sharpen before every session. A steel for realignment, a stone weekly. Sharp knives raise yield and cut accident rates.
  • Price by cut, not by carcass. Build a simple margin sheet: cost per kg of carcass, saleable kg per cut, and target gross margin of 25â35% on prime cuts.
  • Separate clean and dirty zones. Live animal handling, dressing, cutting, and retail display must not share the same floor space or tools.
  • Train every staff member on hand hygiene and knife safety. One untrained hand can contaminate a whole day's production.
  • Keep a customer complaint and recall log. If a batch is suspect, you must be able to trace it back to a carcass and a date within minutes.
