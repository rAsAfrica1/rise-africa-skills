3. Papaya - Fast Fruit, Fast Cash

Papaya fruits within 12 months. It's the fastest-returning tropical fruit crop.

Module 3 of 6 · Tropical Fruit Farming for Profit

Why papaya

Papaya fruits within 9-12 months of planting. It is the fastest-cash tropical fruit crop. The tree keeps producing continuously for 3-5 years before declining. A hectare produces 40-80 tonnes per year.

The critical papaya problem - sex

Papaya has three sex types: male, female, and hermaphrodite (bisexual). Only female and hermaphrodite trees bear fruit. Males produce no fruit.

TypeProduces fruit?Notes
FemaleYes, but needs pollinationRound fruit, needs male nearby
MaleNo20-30% of trees grown from seed
HermaphroditeYes, self-pollinatingElongated fruit, preferred for market

Sex is only visible at flowering (5-8 months). You must plant 3-4 seeds per hole and remove males and excess females as they flower.

The sex rule: Plant 3-4 seeds per hole. At flowering, keep only 1 hermaphrodite or female per hole. Remove all others. Space to final population.

Papaya dies in waterlogged soil. If your land holds water after rain, plant papaya on raised beds or mounds.

Planting

  1. Prepare raised beds or mounds 30-40 cm high if drainage is uncertain.
  2. Dig holes 40 x 40 x 40 cm.
  3. Mix topsoil with 10-20 L compost per hole.
  4. Add 100 g DAP per hole.
  5. Plant 3-4 seeds per hole, 1 cm deep.
  6. Thin to 1 plant per hole at flowering (5-8 months).
  7. Water daily for first 2 weeks, then every 2-3 days.

Papaya ring spot virus - the killer

PRSV is the most destructive papaya disease. It is spread by aphids and has no cure. Trees show:

  • Yellow mottling on leaves
  • Distorted, ringspot-marked fruit
  • Stunted growth, tree dies within 6-12 months

Prevention:

  • Plant resistant or tolerant varieties (Tainung 2, some hybrids)
  • Control aphids with weekly sprays of mineral oil or neem
  • Remove and destroy infected trees immediately
  • Do not plant papaya next to older papaya plantings
  • Do not plant more than 3 years in the same plot
The PRSV rule: Once you see ringspot on the fruit, remove the tree. There is no cure, and it spreads to neighbouring trees fast.
Practical exercise 3: Visit a papaya plot. Count the ratio of male to female/hermaphrodite trees. Note the varieties being grown and their fruit size.
