Improper canning of low-acid vegetables (beans, carrots, pumpkin, tomato with added low-acid ingredients) can create Clostridium botulinum toxin â odourless, tasteless and potentially fatal. A boiling water bath does not reach the 116â121Â°C needed to destroy botulinum spores in low-acid foods. Never sell or taste a swollen, leaking or foul-smelling jar. Do not "open-kettle" process low-acid vegetables. Acidify to pH 4.6 or below (test with calibrated pH strips) or use a validated pressure canner. Quick Reference Checklist

  1. Source at glut prices. Buy when farm-gate prices bottom out (harvest peak); your margin is made at procurement, not at the market.
  2. Grade before you process. Reject bruised, mouldy or over-ripe fruit â one spoiled unit can inoculate a whole batch.
  3. Wash and sanitise. Potable water plus a food-grade sanitiser; clean hands, tables, knives and jars every single run.
  4. Measure, don't guess. Record moisture, pH, aw, brine strength and processing time for every batch in a simple logbook.
  5. Label legally. Product name, net weight, ingredients, batch/lot number, production and best-before dates, and your contact â required by most national standards authorities.
  6. Cost every batch. Raw material + fuel + packaging + labour + spoilage allowance; then set price at 2.5â3Ã total cost for retail.
  7. Store cool, dark and dry. Off the floor, away from walls, with stock rotation (first in, first out).
  8. Test the market small. Sell 20â50 units before scaling; collect feedback on taste, pack size and price point.
