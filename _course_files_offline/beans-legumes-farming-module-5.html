Lesson 1

Harvest Indicators by Crop

~10 min
In this lesson
  • Judge correct harvest timing for each legume
  • Avoid shatter losses
  • Recognise groundnut's unique harvest indicators

Common bean

Harvest when pods are yellow to brown and the seed is hard, but before pods split (shatter). For a 2-week window, harvest at 80–90% pod maturity. Beans harvested too late lose 15–30% to shatter on the ground.

Cowpea

Harvest when pods are dry and dark, before they open. Cowpea pods shatter easily — some improved varieties are pod-shatter resistant, but most are not. Pick in 2–3 passes over the field.

Groundnut

Judged by the shell test: dig a few plants, open the pods, and examine the inner shell. Dark brown to black markings on the inner shell ("blackening") mean the pods are mature. Also look at the leaves — they yellow and drop at maturity.

Soybean

Harvest when pods are fully yellow-brown and the seed rattles inside. Moisture should be 13–15% at harvest. Combining too dry leads to seed cracking; too wet causes mould.

Pigeon pea

Harvest when the pods are dry and brown. Pigeon pea matures over several weeks — 2–3 pickings from the same plants.

Lesson 2

Harvesting, Threshing and Cleaning

~10 min
In this lesson
  • Harvest and thresh without breaking seed or contaminating with soil
  • Clean grain to market standard
  • Handle groundnut carefully to avoid aflatoxin

Harvest

  • Harvest in the cool of the morning or evening
  • Cut plants at ground level and lay on tarps or in the field for 2–5 days to dry
  • Never harvest wet — the pods stick and the seed moulds

Threshing

  • Hand threshing by beating with sticks is common but damages seed. Reduce force.
  • Mechanical threshers must be adjusted for the crop — too fast cracks seed.
  • Thresh on a clean tarp or hard surface — never bare soil.

Cleaning

  • Winnow to remove chaff and light material
  • Remove stones, dirt, and damaged seed by hand
  • Grain should be uniform and free of foreign matter before sale

Groundnut-specific handling

Groundnut is high-risk for aflatoxin. After lifting:

  • Dry pods in the sun in a thin layer (5 cm) for 5–7 days
  • Turn daily
  • Do not let pods re-wet at night
  • Store only after shells are fully dry and rattle when shaken
  • Never pile wet or damp pods — Aspergillus grows in hours
Lesson 3

Drying to Safe Moisture

~10 min
In this lesson
  • Dry legume seed to safe storage moisture
  • Test moisture without a meter
  • Avoid mould and aflatoxin

Target moisture

CropSafe storage moisture
Common bean12–13%
Cowpea12–13%
Groundnut (kernels)8–9%
Soybean12%
Pigeon pea12%

How to dry

  • Sun drying on a clean tarp or concrete floor
  • Thin layer (5 cm) and turn every hour
  • Cover at night to prevent re-wetting from dew
  • Do not dry on bare soil or on hot concrete — the seed cracks and picks up dirt

How to test without a meter

  • Bite test: seed should crack cleanly, not squash
  • Salt test: in a sealed jar, 100 g dry salt and 100 g of seed. Shake and leave 15 minutes. If salt sticks to the seed, moisture is too high.
  • Rattle test (groundnut): shake a pod next to your ear. If the seed rattles freely, dry enough.

Lesson 4

Hermetic Storage

~10 min
In this lesson
  • Store legume seed without chemicals
  • Use hermetic bags correctly
  • Compare storage options on cost and effectiveness

Hermetic bags

Airtight plastic liners inside a woven bag. When sealed, the insects inside consume oxygen and die. No chemicals, no residue, seed remains edible and saleable.

  • Cost: $3–8 per 100 kg bag
  • Lifespan: 2–3 years for reuse, if not punctured
  • Storage life of seed: 12+ months without weevil damage
  • Best practice: fill the bag, expel air, seal at the correct fold line, keep in a cool dry place, check monthly for punctures

Other storage methods

  • Metal silos: $300–800 for 500 kg. Effective, rodent-resistant, long life. Best for larger volumes.
  • Drum storage: old oil drums cleaned and sealed. Effective for small lots, cheap if drums are available.
  • Traditional granary: 20–40% loss. Only if modern options are unavailable.
  • Chemical protectants: effective but with residue issues. Respect label rates and PHIs.

Storage discipline

  • Dry to correct moisture before storage
  • Clean seed of dirt and chaff
  • Store off the ground, away from walls
  • Never mix old and new seed
  • Check monthly for weevil activity and mould
Lesson 5

Seed Selection for Next Season

~10 min
In this lesson
  • Select seed from your own crop
  • Keep seed quality high across seasons
  • Know when to buy fresh certified seed

Selecting your own seed

You can save seed from your crop for the next 2–3 seasons. Choose seed from the best plants:

  • Healthy plants with no disease symptoms
  • Well-filled pods with uniform seed
  • Plants that match the variety characteristics
  • Plants that matured on time

Mark these plants in the field with a ribbon. Harvest them separately. Store separately in a hermetic bag labelled with variety and date.

When to buy fresh certified seed

  • Every 3 years, to refresh genetics
  • After a disease year, to break seed-borne cycles
  • After a poor germination test (below 85%)
  • When you want to try a new variety

Seed storage discipline

  • Dry seed to 12% moisture or lower
  • Store in hermetic bags or sealed containers
  • Keep cool, dark, and off the ground
  • Label with variety, harvest date, and germination test result
  • Test germination a month before planting

Knowledge check — Module 5

1. When is bean ready to harvest?

Correct: 80–90% pod maturity. Any later and you lose 15–30% to shatter.

2. What moisture should groundnut kernels be for storage?

Correct: 8–9%. Lower than other legumes; critical for aflatoxin prevention.

3. How long does hermetic bag storage keep legume seed?

Correct: 12+ months. Chemical-free, under 2% loss.

4. What does the blackening test tell you about groundnut?

Correct: pods are mature. Dark markings on the inner shell indicate harvest time.

5. How often should you refresh your seed with certified stock?

Correct: every 3 years. Refreshes genetics and breaks seed-borne disease cycles.

Write your legume post-harvest plan. Include: (1) harvest date by crop; (2) drying method and target moisture; (3) storage choice and cost; (4) seed selection criteria and where you will save it; (5) the one buyer you will approach before harvest.
