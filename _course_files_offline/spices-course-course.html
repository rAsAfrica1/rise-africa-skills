Aflatoxin contamination in maize and groundnuts is a serious health hazard and a trade barrier. Chronic exposure causes liver cancer and stunting in children. The EU rejects 30â40% of African groundnut and maize shipments due to aflatoxin exceeding 4â10 ppb limits.

  • Never sell or consume grain with visible mould, discolouration, or a musty smell. Aflatoxin is invisible, tasteless, and heat-stable â cooking does not destroy it.
  • Dry grain to below 13% moisture before storage. Use a moisture meter, not guesswork.
  • Store in hermetic bags or metal silos. Never store on bare ground or in damp rooms.
  • When handling pesticides or fertilisers, wear gloves, mask, and long sleeves. Wash hands and clothes immediately after use. Never eat or smoke during application.
  • Observe pre-harvest intervals â the waiting period between last spray and harvest. Violating this leaves illegal residues that block exports.
  • Keep all agro-chemicals in original labelled containers, locked away from children and food. Never transfer to drink bottles.

Quick Reference Checklist

  1. Know your unit economics. Calculate cost per kilogram or per litre for every product line. If you cannot state your gross margin, you are not ready to scale.
  2. Grade and standardise. Buyers pay premiums for consistent quality. Grade before you sell â unsorted produce attracts discounts of 15â30%.
  3. Cut post-harvest loss first. Reducing loss from 30% to 10% is equivalent to a 20% yield increase at a fraction of the cost.
  4. Secure offtake before planting. A signed contract or letter of intent de-risks production and unlocks value chain finance.
  5. Track moisture and aflatoxin. Buy a moisture meter and test kits. Document results per lot for buyers and regulators.
  6. Formalise records. Maintain a simple cash book, inventory log, and customer list. Banks and investors require 6â12 months of records.
  7. Diversify buyers. Do not depend on a single buyer. Aim for at least three channels: local, institutional, and regional/export.
  8. Invest in storage and cold chain. Storage lets you sell in the lean season when prices rise 30â60%. It is often the highest-return asset on the farm.
