â  SAFETY AND COMPLIANCE WARNING â AGRICULTURAL CHEMICALS AND FOOD SAFETY

Pesticides, herbicides, and fumigants used in storage are toxic to humans. The following are non-negotiable:

  • Never use a pesticide that is not registered in your country. Check the national registrar (e.g. Kenya's PCPB, Nigeria's NAFDAC/NAQS, Ghana's EPA, South Africa's DALRRD) before purchase.
  • Observe the pre-harvest interval (PHI) on the label. Selling grain with residues above the maximum residue limit (MRL) can result in rejection at export and, in some markets, criminal liability.
  • Wear gloves, a long-sleeved shirt, boots, and a face mask when handling or applying. Never eat, drink, or smoke during application. Wash immediately after.
  • Store chemicals in a locked, ventilated, labelled cabinet â never in the same room as food, seed for planting, or animal feed.
  • Grain treated with storage insecticides must be clearly labelled and must not be consumed before the stated waiting period. Fumigated stores must be ventilated and declared safe before re-entry.
  • Aflatoxin is a separate, serious food-safety risk. Damp or mouldy grain must never be blended with clean grain for sale. Test before you sell into any formal market.
  • In case of poisoning, contact your local poison information centre or nearest clinic immediately and bring the product label with you.

Quick Reference Checklist

  1. Know your numbers before you plant. Build a one-page enterprise budget using local input prices, your realistic yield, and the price your buyer is actually paying â not the price on a government bulletin.
  2. Test your soil. A USD 20â40 soil test prevents a USD 200 fertiliser mistake. Apply according to the result, not habit.
  3. Buy inputs as a group. Co-operative or farmer-group procurement of seed and fertiliser typically saves 10â20% on landed cost.
  4. Invest in storage first. Hermetic bags or a metal silo pay back within one to two seasons and let you sell into the post-harvest price rise rather than the harvest glut.
  5. Grade and sort. Clean, graded grain commands a premium and reduces aflatoxin rejection risk. Remove broken and discoloured kernels before sale.
  6. Contract before harvest. A written offtake agreement with a defined grade, moisture content, delivery point, and payment term converts a crop into a bankable asset.
  7. Keep records. Input receipts, labour days, yields, and sale prices. Records are what turn a farm into a business that can access credit.
  8. Check chemical registration and PHI every season. Regulations and product registrations change; a product legal last year may not be legal this year.
