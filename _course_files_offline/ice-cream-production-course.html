Practical Reference

This reference section is built for operators, farm managers, and agribusiness owners who need numbers they can act on today. Every figure below is drawn from published FAO, IFAD, or World Bank data. Use the diagrams and tables as planning tools, not as decoration. Where a number is a range, plan your budget on the conservative end and your revenue on the midpoint.

Pesticides, fumigants, and mycotoxin-contaminated grain are direct health hazards. Follow these non-negotiables:

  • Never store pesticides, veterinary drugs, or fuel in the same room as food, feed, or seed. Cross-contamination causes acute poisoning and long-term illness.
  • Wear gloves, boots, long sleeves, and a face mask when mixing or applying any agro-chemical. Wash contaminated clothing separately from family laundry.
  • Respect pre-harvest intervals and re-entry periods on every label. Selling produce before the interval expires can breach export and supermarket residue limits.
  • Reject mouldy grain. Aflatoxin is invisible, heat-stable, and carcinogenic. Drying to safe moisture (maize 13%, groundnut 8â9%) is your primary defence.
  • Keep a written record of every chemical applied: product, rate, date, plot, and operator. Buyers and regulators increasingly demand this traceability.
  • In case of suspected poisoning, contact your nearest health facility or poison centre immediately. Do not induce vomiting unless instructed.

Quick Reference Checklist

  1. Know your unit economics. Calculate cost per kilogram produced and gross margin per kilogram sold before scaling. If you cannot state these two numbers from memory, stop and compute them.
  2. Measure post-harvest loss. Weigh a sample lot at harvest and again after storage. A 5% reduction in loss is often a larger profit gain than a 10% yield increase.
  3. Match finance to purpose. Use short-tenor working capital for inputs and trade, and long-tenor capital only for assets that generate returns over years. Never fund a warehouse with a 90-day loan.
  4. Secure an off-taker before you plant. A signed or written supply agreement converts production risk into a manageable operational task.
  5. Budget for compliance. Residue limits, traceability, and food safety certification are entry tickets to formal and export markets, not optional extras.
  6. Build a three-month cash buffer. Agriculture is seasonal and weather-exposed. A buffer covering three months of fixed costs prevents forced selling at low prices.
  7. Digitise records. Use a simple mobile ledger for purchases, sales, and chemical applications. Records unlock credit, contracts, and better decisions.
  8. Review monthly, not annually. Track yield, loss, price, and cost monthly against plan. Correct course while the season still allows it.
