Practical Reference

This enrichment block is designed for use by agribusiness operators, extension officers, and SME managers working in African food and agro-processing value chains. It assumes you already understand the core concepts in the module above. What follows is a working reference: a visual model, two data tables with real published figures, a safety warning, a checklist you can print and pin to a wall, and a curated list of free authoritative resources. Use it as a desk companion, not as a substitute for local regulatory advice.

The payback column is the business case. Hermetic bags and tarpaulins repay within a single season, which means they can be financed from operating cash flow or a co-operative's revolving fund. Cold rooms and silos need structured finance, off-take agreements or anchor-buyer guarantees. A common mistake is to buy the capital-intensive asset first and then hunt for volume. Secure the volume, then buy the asset.

â  SAFETY WARNING â Chemicals, Aflatoxins and Food Handling

Storage pesticides and fumigants. Products such as aluminium phosphide (phosphine) tablets release highly toxic gas. They must only be applied by trained, licensed operators, never in living quarters, never in sealed rooms people enter, and never without the correct respiratory protection. Follow the label exactly. Store pesticides in original labelled containers, locked, away from food, feed and children.

Aflatoxin. Poorly dried or damp-stored maize and groundnuts can develop aflatoxin, a potent carcinogen. Never blend visibly mouldy grain into clean grain for sale. Reject or safely dispose of mouldy lots. Test with rapid kits where available. Aflatoxin-contaminated grain cannot be made safe by cooking.

Food handling. Raw milk, meat and fresh produce must be kept at safe temperatures. Wash hands and equipment with clean water. Never use agricultural pesticides on stored food crops at doses above the label rate, and observe the stated pre-harvest interval.

Personal protective equipment. Gloves, mask, goggles and boots are not optional when handling dusts, fumigants or mouldy grain. If you feel dizzy, nauseous or short of breath after chemical exposure, move to fresh air immediately and seek medical help. Report suspected poisoning to your local agricultural extension officer and health facility.

Quick Reference Checklist

  1. Measure before you invest. Weigh a sample lot at harvest and again after storage to establish your actual loss rate. Do not rely on national averages for your own business case.
  2. Dry to safe moisture. Target 12â13% for maize and 13â14% for rice before storage. Use a calibrated moisture meter, not the bite test.
  3. Clean and grade. Remove broken grains, chaff and foreign matter before storage. Clean grain stores longer and sells at a premium.
  4. Choose the right storage. Hermetic bags for small volumes and high-value seed; metal silos for bulk grain held for more than three months.
  5. Control pests safely. Use licensed fumigants only with trained operators and full PPE. Never mix storage chemicals into food or feed.
  6. Watch for aflatoxin. Reject mouldy grain, test suspect lots, and keep stored grain dry and ventilated.
  7. Track the numbers. Record purchase price, storage cost, shrinkage and sale price per lot. Know your gross margin per tonne, not per bag.
  8. Secure the market before the asset. Have a buyer, contract or co-operative off-take arrangement in place before financing a cold room, silo or dryer.
