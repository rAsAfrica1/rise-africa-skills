Lesson 1

Rosette Virus — the Field Killer

In this lesson
  • Identify groundnut rosette symptoms
  • Understand how it spreads
  • Prevent with resistant varieties and aphid control

Groundnut rosette virus (GRV) is the single most destructive disease of groundnut in Africa. Yield losses of 60–100% are common in epidemic years. It is transmitted by aphids, especially Aphis craccivora.

Symptoms

  • Severe stunting — affected plants do not grow
  • Leaves mottled with yellow/green patches
  • Leaves rolled or bunched tightly (rosette pattern)
  • Small or absent pods

Prevention

  1. Resistant varieties. The single most important defense. Ask your national research institute for current rosette-resistant varieties — many have been released.
  2. Early planting. The aphid population peaks later in the season. Early-planted crops escape heavy pressure.
  3. Early aphid control. Seed treatment with imidacloprid or thiamethoxam. Foliar spray if aphid pressure is heavy in the first 4 weeks.
  4. Rogueing. Remove and destroy any plant showing symptoms within the first 6 weeks.
  5. Border barriers. Plant a border of a non-host crop (cereal) to slow aphid entry.
The rule: rosette is prevented, not cured. Resistant variety + early planting + early aphid control. Get those three right and rosette stays a footnote, not a catastrophe.
Lesson 2

Early and Late Leaf Spot

In this lesson
  • Distinguish early and late leaf spot
  • Time fungicide sprays
  • Use resistant varieties and rotation

Early leaf spot (Cercospora arachidicola)

Brown spots with a yellow halo, mostly on older leaves. Starts early in the season. Can cause 10–30% yield loss in susceptible varieties.

Late leaf spot (Phaeoisariopsis personata)

Dark brown or black spots, no halo, on older leaves. Appears later in the season. More destructive — 30–60% yield loss possible. Causes heavy defoliation.

Control (both)

  1. Resistant varieties. Several improved African varieties carry tolerance.
  2. Rotation. Break the disease cycle with a cereal.
  3. Sanitation. Remove crop residue after harvest.
  4. Fungicide spray. Chlorothalonil, mancozeb or a triazole. Begin at first symptoms, repeat every 10–14 days. Usually 3–4 sprays per season. Stop 2 weeks before harvest.
  5. Avoid overhead irrigation — it spreads spores.
Lesson 3

Rust, Stem Rot and Collar Rot

In this lesson
  • Identify the main soil and foliar diseases of groundnut
  • Manage stem rot with calcium and rotation
  • Recognise the conditions that trigger each disease

Rust (Puccinia arachidis)

Small orange-brown pustules on leaf undersides. Spreads fast in humid weather. Control with resistant varieties and fungicide sprays if heavy.

Stem rot (Sclerotium rolfsii)

White fungal mat at the base of the stem, often with small round sclerotia. Causes sudden wilting. Worse in hot, humid weather and in soil with low calcium. Control:

  • Gypsum application (calcium suppresses the fungus)
  • Rotation with cereals
  • Deep ploughing of residue
  • Avoid excessive nitrogen

Collar rot (Aspergillus niger)

Black mould at the stem base, often following seed treatment failure. Control with seed treatment and clean seed.

Wilt and crown rot

Various fungi and bacteria. Rotation, drainage, and clean seed are the standard controls.

Lesson 4

Field-Level Aflatoxin Prevention

In this lesson
  • Prevent aflatoxin at the field stage
  • Prevent at harvest and drying
  • Test and sort

Field-level prevention

  • Irrigate during pod fill if possible. Drought stress is the main trigger. Even one watering at 90 days can cut aflatoxin by half.
  • Control soil insects. Termites and white grubs create pod damage that allows the fungus in. Seed treatment helps.
  • Calcium. Well-filled pods with intact shells resist infection.
  • Timely harvest. Late harvesting lets pods crack and fungus enter.

Harvest and drying

  • Lift pods as soon as mature (shell test in Module 5)
  • Dry immediately — do not pile wet
  • Dry in a thin layer (5 cm) on tarpaulin or concrete, sun for 5–7 days
  • Turn daily; cover at night
  • Target kernel moisture 8–9% before storage

Storage

  • Store in a dry place with airflow
  • Hermetic bags for long storage (see Module 5)
  • Never store in humid conditions or on the ground
  • Check monthly for mould or insect activity

Testing and sorting

  • Some countries have aflatoxin testing services (national bureau of standards, private labs). Test before selling to a processor.
  • Sort out discoloured, shrivelled, or mouldy kernels before sale. These carry the highest aflatoxin load.
  • If you find heavily contaminated lots, do not sell them for food use.
Lesson 5

Scouting and the Spray Decisions

In this lesson
  • Set up a weekly scouting routine
  • Decide when spraying is justified
  • Respect pre-harvest intervals

Weekly scouting (15 min per hectare)

  1. Walk a W pattern
  2. At 10 points, check 5 plants: leaves (both sides), stems at base, and newly formed pegs/pods
  3. Record: leaf spot severity, aphids, other insects, wilting, rosette symptoms
  4. Take a photo if unsure; ask an extension officer or WhatsApp group

Spray decisions

  • Leaf spot: spray if lesions appear on >10% of leaves. Usually 3–4 sprays per season, 10–14 days apart.
  • Rust: spray if pustules are spreading.
  • Aphids (rosette vector): spray only if heavy pressure in the first 4 weeks.
  • Pod borer: spray at flowering onset if larvae present.

Pre-harvest intervals

Every pesticide has a PHI — the days between the last spray and saleable harvest. Respecting PHI is a legal and commercial obligation. Keep a spray log with dates, products, rates and PHIs.

Knowledge check — Module 4

1. What is the most destructive disease of African groundnut?

Correct: rosette virus. Can cause 60–100% yield loss in epidemic years.

2. What is the most important defence against rosette?

Correct: resistant variety + early planting + aphid control. Rosette is prevented, not cured.

3. When does aflatoxin form most often?

Correct: all four situations. Prevention is a season-long discipline.

4. What is the target kernel moisture for safe storage?

Correct: 8–9%. Lower than other legumes.

5. What single cultural practice most reduces leaf spot?

Correct: rotation + residue removal. Breaks the disease cycle.
