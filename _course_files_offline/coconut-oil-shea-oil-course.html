â  SAFETY WARNING â PESTICIDES AND POST-HARVEST CHEMICALS

If your enterprise uses pesticides, fumigants, or post-harvest treatments, the following are non-negotiable:

  • Only use products registered by your national regulator (for example, the Pest Control Products Board in Kenya, NAFDAC in Nigeria, or the Registrar of Pesticides in Tanzania). Unregistered products may be counterfeit or banned.
  • Respect the pre-harvest interval (PHI) and maximum residue limits (MRLs). Export buyers reject entire consignments for MRL breaches â one rejection can end a contract.
  • Wear the full protective kit: gloves, respirator, goggles, long sleeves, and boots. Never spray against the wind or re-enter a treated field before the label says it is safe.
  • Store chemicals in a locked, ventilated, child-proof store away from food, feed, and water sources. Never decant into beverage bottles.
  • Triple-rinse empty containers, puncture them, and dispose of them through approved channels. Do not reuse them for drinking water or food storage.
  • Keep a spray log: date, product, rate, operator, field, and PHI. This log is a commercial asset â it proves due diligence to buyers and auditors.

Quick Reference Checklist

  1. Know your unit economics. Calculate cost per kilogram, per litre, or per hectare before you scale. If you cannot state your break-even price, you cannot negotiate.
  2. Secure market before production. Get a written or verifiable buyer commitment (volume, grade, price mechanism, payment terms) before planting or expanding.
  3. Grade and standardise. Consistent size, colour, and packaging typically raise farm-gate prices by 10â30% and reduce buyer rejection.
  4. Cut post-harvest loss first. Crates, shade, correct harvest maturity, and rapid cooling are usually cheaper than expanding production.
  5. Keep records that lenders trust. Maintain a simple ledger of inputs, labour, yields, sales, and losses. Digitise with a mobile app if possible.
  6. Aggregate to negotiate. Cooperatives and contract farming groups access better prices, transport rates, and input credit than isolated farmers.
  7. Manage chemical compliance. Keep spray logs, respect PHIs and MRLs, and train every operator on safe handling and disposal.
  8. Plan for finance. Prepare a one-page business case with your margin table, buyer evidence, and loss-reduction plan before approaching banks, SACCOs, or impact investors.
