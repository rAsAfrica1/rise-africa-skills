Catering is a health-regulated activity. A single food-poisoning incident at a funeral or church event can end your business and expose you to legal liability.

  • Keep hot food at 60Â°C or above and cold food at 5Â°C or below. The danger zone is 5Â°Câ60Â°C â never hold cooked food there for more than 2 hours.
  • Cook poultry and minced meat to a core temperature of 74Â°C; whole cuts of beef and pork to 63Â°C followed by a 3-minute rest.
  • Cool large batches fast: divide into shallow trays no deeper than 5 cm and refrigerate within 90 minutes.
  • Never store raw meat above ready-to-eat food. Use separate boards and knives, colour-coded if possible.
  • Wash hands with soap for at least 20 seconds after handling raw meat, using the toilet, or handling waste.
  • Store all cleaning chemicals, pesticides and fuel away from food areas, in labelled, sealed containers. Never decant chemicals into drink bottles.
  • Do not cater if you or a helper has vomiting, diarrhoea, or an infected cut on the hands. This is non-negotiable.
  • Keep a written temperature log for every job. It protects your client and it protects you.

Thresholds drawn from WHO Five Keys to Safer Food and the FDA Food Code cold-holding and cooking-temperature requirements.

Quick Reference Checklist

  1. Confirm the job in writing. Date, venue, headcount, menu, serving time, and total price â send it on WhatsApp and ask the client to reply "confirmed".
  2. Collect a 50% deposit before buying stock. No deposit, no purchase. The deposit must cover 100% of your food and transport cost.
  3. Build a portion sheet before you shop. Multiply Table 1 by your headcount, then add 10% buffer for funerals and church events where numbers swell.
  4. Call three suppliers and compare. Meat, staples, and packaging prices swing by region and season. Record what you paid so you can price the next job accurately.
  5. Check your permits. Confirm with your local council or health authority what a home food business needs â food handler medical certificates, premises inspection, or a trading licence.
  6. Prep the day before, cook the day of. Marinate, chop, and portion in advance. Cook proteins and starches in batches, not all at once.
  7. Log temperatures. Hot above 60Â°C, cold below 5Â°C, poultry to 74Â°C. Write it down.
  8. Invoice the balance on delivery, then ask for a referral and a testimonial. Repeat bookings are cheaper than new customers â this is the whole growth engine.
