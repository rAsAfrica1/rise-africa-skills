If you are planning, use them to build a credible budget.

Veterinary drugs, vaccines, and feed additives are chemicals. Misuse harms animals, people, and your business. Follow these rules without exception:

  • Withdrawal periods are law and market requirement. Never sell meat, milk, or eggs before the labelled withdrawal period after treatment. Residues cause rejections and health risks.
  • Use only registered products from licensed agro-vets. Counterfeit drugs are common and dangerous.
  • Store correctly. Vaccines need a cold chain (2â8Â°C). Keep drugs locked away from children, feed, and food.
  • Wear gloves and masks when handling concentrates, antibiotics, and disinfectants. Wash hands after handling.
  • Never use human antibiotics in animals. This drives antimicrobial resistance, a major public health threat.
  • Record every treatment: date, animal ID, product, dose, withdrawal end date. If you cannot show records, buyers and regulators will not trust your product.

Quick Reference Checklist

  1. Calculate your feed cost per kg gain or per litre of milk every week. If you do not know this number, you do not know if you are making money.
  2. Keep a herd/flock register. Record births, deaths, treatments, weights, and sales. This is your bank statement for livestock.
  3. Set a mortality threshold and investigate immediately if deaths exceed 1% per week in poultry or 0.5% per month in cattle.
  4. Buy feed inputs in bulk and store in dry, raised, pest-proof conditions. Feed that spoils is money lost.
  5. Vaccinate on schedule and deworm strategically. Prevention is 5â10 times cheaper than treatment.
  6. Weigh or condition-score animals monthly. What gets measured gets managed.
  7. Negotiate at least two market outlets before you need to sell. Do not be a price-taker at the last minute.
  8. Separate business and household money. Pay yourself a fixed salary and reinvest the rest.
