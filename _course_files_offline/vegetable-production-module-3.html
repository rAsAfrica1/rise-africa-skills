Lesson 1

Reading the Crop — When to Water

~10 min
In this lesson
  • Judge water need by look, feel and stage
  • Water deeply, not often
  • Understand critical water stages for each crop

The most common irrigation mistake is watering shallowly every day. The crop's roots stay in the top 5 cm of soil, which heats up and dries out between waterings. Water deep, less often — soak the root zone to 20–30 cm and let the top few centimetres dry slightly between waterings.

How to judge water need

  • Look: leaves wilt slightly in mid-afternoon heat — normal. Leaves wilted first thing in the morning — the crop is genuinely dry. Leaves curled, pale, or with brown edges — drought stress, urgent.
  • Feel: stick a finger 5 cm into the soil. If it feels dry, water. If it feels cool and moist, wait.
  • Stage: flowering and fruit-fill are the two most critical stages. Water stress at flowering causes flower drop; at fruit-fill it causes small fruit and blossom-end rot.

Lesson 2

Drip, Sprinkler, Furrow, Can — Choosing and Running

~11 min
In this lesson
  • Match the irrigation method to your water source and scale
  • Run drip and sprinkler systems correctly
  • Maintain the system to avoid blockages and breakdowns

Drip irrigation

The best system where water is scarce or expensive. Delivers 2–4 L per hour directly to the plant's root zone. Water use drops 40–60% compared to furrow. Requires filtration — a simple mesh or sand filter — to prevent emitter blockage.

Basic kit: 16 mm dripline, 30 cm emitter spacing, one line per crop row, connected to a header pipe and a tank or pump. Cost: $400–2,000 per 1,000 m². Lifespan 3–6 years with proper maintenance.

Sprinkler

Best for close-planted leafy greens and for larger plots. Delivers 5–15 mm per hour over the whole area. Loses 20–30% to evaporation in hot dry weather if run at midday. Run at dawn or evening.

Furrow

Water is run down furrows between crop rows. Cheap and simple, but loses 30–50% to seepage and evaporation. Only suitable where water is abundant and cheap.

Watering can

Best for plots under 300 m² and for very young seedlings. The control is excellent. The labour is heavy. A 15 L can weighs 15 kg when full; a 500 m² plot needs 30–50 cans per day in the dry season.

Maintenance

  • Flush driplines monthly
  • Clean filters weekly
  • Repair leaks immediately — a single small leak wastes more water than it saves
  • Store driplines in the shade during the off-season — UV destroys them
Lesson 3

Feeding the Crop — Compost, NPK, and Foliar

~12 min
In this lesson
  • Build a base fertility plan with compost and manure
  • Use NPK correctly at the right stages
  • Understand when foliar feeding helps

Base fertility

Before planting, mix into the bed: 5–10 kg compost per m² or 3–5 kg well-rotted manure per m². This is the foundation. Compost feeds soil biology, improves water holding, and releases nutrients slowly.

NPK for tomato

StageProductRate
Base (before planting)DAP or NPK 17-17-1750–100 g per plant in the hole
Vegetative (2–4 weeks)Urea or CAN20–30 g per plant, ring around
FloweringNPK 12-24-12 or similar30 g per plant
Fruit fillPotassium-rich (SOP or NPK 6-6-12)30 g per plant, every 2 weeks

Foliar feeding

Foliar sprays — calcium, magnesium, micronutrients — can correct a specific deficiency quickly. Calcium foliar every 2 weeks during fruit fill reduces blossom-end rot. Foliar is a supplement, not a substitute for soil fertility.

Over-fertilising

Too much nitrogen produces big leafy plants with few fruit. Too much potassium at the wrong time locks out calcium and causes blossom-end rot. Follow the stages above and do not overdo it.

Lesson 4

Daily and Weekly Routine

~9 min
In this lesson
  • Adopt the daily walk that catches problems early
  • Set the weekly tasks that keep the plot productive
  • Keep the records that make the season a business

Daily (15–30 minutes for 1,000 m²)

  • Walk the whole plot at the same time each morning. Look at every plant — an experienced grower spots disease in one plant before it spreads to ten.
  • Check water — finger test, then water if needed.
  • Pull any weeds you see while walking — 5 minutes of hand-weeding per day beats a full day every two weeks.
  • Remove any diseased leaves or fruit — bag and burn, do not compost.
  • Check the irrigation system for leaks and blockages.

Weekly

  • Thinning, pruning, or training of tomato.
  • Fertiliser application if scheduled.
  • Foliar spray if needed (calcium, magnesium, micronutrients).
  • Harvest and post-harvest handling of ripe fruit.
  • Record sales and expenses in the notebook — quantity, price, buyer, date.

Monthly

  • Review records: which crop, which buyer, which price. Which bed made the most money?
  • Plan the next planting. Seed order, bed preparation, timing.

The single discipline: walk the plot every day. Ninety percent of the losses in a small vegetable plot come from something that was visible for a week and nobody looked.

Lesson 5

Mulching and Weeding

~9 min
In this lesson
  • Use mulch to cut water use and suppress weeds
  • Time the two critical weedings for vegetable crops
  • Weed without disturbing the crop

Mulch

A 5–8 cm layer of dry grass, straw, or crop residue on the soil surface reduces evaporation by 30–50%, suppresses weeds, keeps the soil cool, and prevents soil splash onto fruit. It is the single cheapest water-saving technique available.

Do not use fresh green material (it attracts termites and can introduce disease) or material with seed heads (it plants weeds). Well-dried grass or straw is best.

Weeding windows

  • First weeding: 2–3 weeks after planting — the critical one.
  • Second weeding: 5–6 weeks — final pass before the crop canopy closes.
  • After that, hand-pull as needed. A dense crop or a mulched plot makes the post-canopy weeding mostly unnecessary.

Weeding technique

Shallow hoeing between rows — deep hoeing damages crop roots. Hand-pulling within the row. Always remove weeds from the bed, do not leave them to re-root or set seed.

Knowledge check — Module 3

1. What is the correct approach to watering vegetables?

Correct: deep, less frequent. Shallow watering produces shallow roots that stress easily.

2. Which irrigation method uses the least water?

Correct: drip. 90–95% efficient; reduces water use by 40–60% vs furrow.

3. What causes blossom-end rot in tomato?

Correct: calcium deficiency, triggered by irregular watering. Steady moisture plus calcium foliar prevents it.

4. What is the single most important daily task?

Correct: walking the plot. Problems caught in one plant on day one do not become problems in ten plants on day ten.

5. What does mulch do?

Correct: reduces evaporation, suppresses weeds, cools soil, prevents splash. The cheapest water-saving technique available.

Module 3 capstone

Write your daily and weekly routine for the coming month. Include: (1) the time you will walk the plot each day; (2) your watering schedule by crop and stage; (3) your fertiliser plan with dates and products; (4) your weeding dates; (5) the notebook or spreadsheet you will use to record sales and expenses. Do not write a plan you cannot keep — write one you will actually run.
