Benchmark Data: Input Costs and Yield

The table below uses indicative international reference prices converted to a planning range. Your local landed cost will differ - in many African markets, imported fragrance and specialty oils carry a heavy premium while locally produced palm, coconut, and shea fractions are far cheaper. The point is the structure of the cost, so you can slot your own numbers in and see your true margin before you buy a single drum.

⚠ SAFETY WARNING - CAUSTIC SODA AND RAW SOAP

Sodium hydroxide causes immediate, deep chemical burns on skin and permanent damage to eyes. Raw, uncured soap still contains active lye and will burn you. Follow these rules without exception:

  • Always add soda to water, never water to soda - the reverse can cause a violent boil and splash.
  • Wear chemical splash goggles and nitrile gloves for every step until the bar is fully cured.
  • Work with open ventilation or outdoors; lye fumes irritate the lungs.
  • If lye touches skin, flush with cool running water for at least 15 minutes. If it reaches an eye, flush immediately and seek medical care.
  • Keep caustic soda locked away from children, food, and animals. Never store it in aluminium or other reactive metal containers.
  • Never cure soap where food is prepared or stored - cross-contamination is a real risk.

Quick Reference Checklist

  • Weigh everything, never scoop. A kitchen scale accurate to 1 g is the cheapest insurance in this business.
  • PPE on before the soda bag opens. Gloves, goggles, and ventilation are not optional and not negotiable.
  • Add soda to water, slowly. Stir and let it cool to 40-45 Â°C before blending with oils.
  • Match temperatures. Oils and lye solution within 5 Â°C of each other prevents seizing and false trace.
  • Set superfat at 3-8%. Use a lye calculator every time; never trust a copied recipe blindly.
  • Log every batch. Recipe, date, temperatures, scent, and result - consistency is what lets you sell.
  • Cure 4-6 weeks, no shortcuts. Sell batch zero while batch one cures; keep the pipeline moving.
  • Test pH before selling. Anything above pH 10 stays on the shelf, not on a customer's skin.
