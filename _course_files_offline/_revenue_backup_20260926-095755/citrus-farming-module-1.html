1. Why Citrus Is a Long-Term Asset

One planting, forty years of harvest. Citrus is the most patient investment in farming.

Module 1 of 6 · Citrus Farming for Profit

The citrus opportunity

Citrus - oranges, lemons, mandarins, grapefruit, limes - is one of the highest-value fruit crops in Africa. A mature orange tree produces 200-400 fruits per year. A lemon tree produces 500-1,000 fruits. And the tree keeps producing for 40-50 years from a single planting.

Why citrus works

  • Long-term asset - plant once, harvest for 40 years
  • Storable - oranges keep 4-8 weeks without refrigeration
  • Processing market - juice, dried peel, essential oils
  • Local demand always strong - everyone buys oranges and lemons
  • Intercrop for first 3-4 years - grows vegetables between young trees
  • Reliable in dry areas - once established, citrus tolerates drought

The three big challenges

  • Long wait - 3-4 years before meaningful income
  • Citrus greening (HLB) - the #1 killer worldwide. No cure once infected.
  • Fruit fly - ruins fruit before harvest if not controlled

What you need to start

  • Land with well-drained soil, pH 5.5-6.5
  • Grafted trees from certified nursery (not seedlings)
  • Reliable water (irrigation strongly recommended in dry areas)
  • Windbreak on exposed sites
  • Starting capital of $800-2,000 for 100 trees
The grafting rule: Buy only grafted trees from a certified nursery. Seed-grown citrus takes 7-10 years and gives variable fruit quality.
Practical exercise 1: Visit 3 markets. Ask the current price of oranges, lemons, and mandarins. Check if a juice factory buys locally.
