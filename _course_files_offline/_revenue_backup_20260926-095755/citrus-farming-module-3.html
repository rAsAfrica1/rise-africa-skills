3. Planting and Orchard Establishment

The first 2 years decide whether the tree survives and produces for 40 years.

Module 3 of 6 · Citrus Farming for Profit

Land preparation

  1. Clear the site of trees, stumps, and heavy brush.
  2. Deep rip to 60-80 cm if there is any hardpan.
  3. Test soil pH - lime if below 5.5, add gypsum for clay soils.
  4. Mark positions at your chosen spacing (6 x 6 m or 5 x 5 m).
  5. Dig holes 60 x 60 x 60 cm - do this 4-6 weeks before planting.
  6. Mix topsoil with compost and let it settle.
  7. Plant windbreaks 4-6 months before citrus on exposed sides.

Planting

ItemDetail
Best timeStart of rainy season
Hole size60 x 60 x 60 cm
Compost per hole20-30 L well-rotted
DAP per hole100-150 g mixed with soil
Graft union height15-25 cm above soil level
  1. Remove the tree from the bag carefully - keep the root ball intact.
  2. Place the tree in the hole with the graft union facing away from the sun.
  3. Set the root ball level with the surrounding soil - do not bury deeper.
  4. Fill with soil-compost mix, firm gently.
  5. Water deeply (20-30 L per tree).
  6. Mulch 5-10 cm around the base, keeping mulch 10 cm away from the trunk.
  7. Stake the tree if tall or in a windy site.
Never bury the graft union. If it goes below soil, the scion will root and lose disease resistance.

Deep, infrequent watering makes stronger roots than shallow daily watering.

Weeding and mulching

  • Keep a 1 m circle around each young tree free of weeds.
  • Mulch with 5-10 cm of dry grass, straw, or compost.
  • Keep mulch 10 cm away from the trunk - prevents collar rot.
  • Never hoe deep - citrus roots are shallow and easily damaged.

Intercropping for the first 3-4 years

Young citrus orchards have empty space for the first 3-4 years. Plant short crops that do not compete:

Good intercropsAvoid
Beans, peas, groundnutsMaize, sorghum (tall, compete)
Cabbage, kale, spinachTomato (shared disease)
Onion, garlicOther citrus
Herbs (coriander, mint)Banana (heavy feeder, tall)
Pineapple (ground cover)Squash (heavy feeder)

Intercropping pays the costs of establishing the orchard. Stop by year 4 when the canopy closes.

Windbreaks

Citrus is sensitive to wind - fruit rubs and drops. On exposed sites, plant a windbreak 4-6 months before the citrus:

SpeciesHeight
Casuarina10-15 m
Grevillea8-12 m
Moringa6-10 m
Gliricidia6-10 m
The graft union rule: Keep the graft union 15-25 cm above soil. Burying it ruins the tree's disease resistance and dwarfing.
Practical exercise 3: Plant 10 grafted citrus trees this season. Record survival at 1 month, 6 months, and 12 months. Target 90%+ survival.
