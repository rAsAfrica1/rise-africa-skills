Lesson 1

Certified Seed — Why It Matters More for Groundnut

In this lesson
  • Understand why saved groundnut seed degrades faster than cereal seed
  • Know when to buy certified seed
  • Do a germination test

Groundnut seed is expensive (2–4x cereal seed per kg) and fragile. The seed coat cracks easily, and virus diseases are seed-borne. Saving your own seed for 3+ seasons is why so many African groundnut fields underperform — the crop is not the same as it was in year 1.

The rule: buy certified seed every 3 years, or whenever you see >10% plants with virus symptoms in the field. In between, save seed from the healthiest plants.

Germination test

Count 100 kernels, wrap in a damp cloth, keep warm and moist for 7 days. Count how many sprout. Above 85% — plant. Below 70% — buy new seed.

Breaking dormancy

Freshly harvested groundnut seed has dormancy that can last 4–6 weeks. If you must plant fresh seed, break dormancy by drying seed at 35–40°C for 7–10 days or storing for 2 months before planting.

Lesson 2

Seed Treatment — Safe and Effective

In this lesson
  • Treat seed for disease without killing rhizobia
  • Understand the correct products and rates
  • Handle seed carefully to avoid damage

Fungicide treatment

Groundnut seed should be treated with a seed-dressing fungicide to control Aspergillus and soil-borne fungi. Common products: thiram, carbendazim, mancozeb. Follow label rates exactly.

Rhizobium inoculation

Groundnut benefits from inoculation, especially in soils that have not grown it. But rhizobium is sensitive — never mix with fungicide. Treat first, let dry, then inoculate. Plant within 2–4 hours.

Timing

  • Treat with fungicide and dry
  • Inoculate just before planting
  • Do not store treated seed — plant within days
Lesson 3

Land Preparation — Loose Soil Wins

In this lesson
  • Prepare soil that lets pods form properly
  • Decide between ridges and flat planting
  • Avoid compaction that traps pods

Groundnut pods form underground. They need loose, well-drained soil to fill properly. On compacted or heavy soil, pods are small, misshapen and empty.

Ridges vs flat

  • Ridges (60–75 cm wide, 20–25 cm high): best on medium to heavy soil, ensures good drainage and easy lifting
  • Flat planting: acceptable on light, well-drained soils
  • Mounds: used in very heavy soils or where waterlogging is a risk

Organic matter

5–8 t/ha of well-rotted manure incorporated before planting improves soil structure and pod shape. It also supplies some calcium.

pH

Groundnut prefers pH 6.0–6.8. Below 5.5, apply agricultural lime 6 months ahead at 1–2 t/ha.

Lesson 4

Spacing and Plant Population

In this lesson
  • Space for the correct plant population
  • Match spacing to the variety type
  • Calculate seed rate

Spacing by type

TypeRow × PlantPlants/ha
Bunch / Spanish45 × 15 cm~150,000
Spreading / Virginia60 × 20 cm~85,000
Runner75 × 20 cm~65,000

Seed rate

  • Spanish: 80–100 kg/ha kernels
  • Virginia: 100–120 kg/ha kernels
  • Kernel vs pod: 70% kernel recovery from pods means 1 kg of seed kernels requires ~1.4 kg of pods

Do not crowd. Groundnut produces more per plant when spaced properly, and dense stands are the main cause of small-pod syndrome in African fields.

Lesson 5

Planting Depth and Timing

In this lesson
  • Plant at the correct depth for good germination
  • Time planting for reliable moisture
  • Handle early season risks

Depth

4–6 cm in moist soil; 5–7 cm in drier soil where moisture is deeper. Uniform depth matters — uneven emergence costs yield.

Timing

  • Plant with the first reliable rains (not the very first shower)
  • Sahel: June–July
  • West Africa (forest zone): March–April or August–September
  • East Africa: March–April (long rains) or October–November (short rains)
  • Southern Africa: November–December

Early risks

  • Dry spell after planting: groundnut seed rots in dry soil. Wait for reliable moisture.
  • Termites: attack young seedlings. Seed treatment can help.
  • Birds: pick seed from the surface. Plant 4+ cm deep.

Knowledge check — Module 2

1. How often should you buy fresh certified groundnut seed?

Correct: every 3 years. Groundnut seed degrades faster than cereal seed.

2. What spacing is used for bunch type (Spanish)?

Correct: 45 × 15 cm. ~150,000 plants/ha.

3. What is the correct planting depth for groundnut?

Correct: 4–6 cm. Uniform depth matters more than any other factor for even emergence.

4. What should you never mix with rhizobium inoculant?

Correct: fungicide. It kills the rhizobium bacteria.

5. How do you break seed dormancy in freshly harvested groundnut?

Correct: dry at 35–40°C. Or store for 2 months.
