5. Pests, Diseases and Harvest

Avocado has few pests but one killer disease. Harvest timing decides your price.

The killer disease - Phytophthora root rot

Phytophthora cinnamomi is the number one avocado killer. It attacks the roots in waterlogged soil, causing slow decline and death over 1-3 years.

SignWhat it means
Small pale leaves, thin canopyEarly root damage
Wilting in midday even with waterRoots cannot take up water
Dieback of twigs from the topAdvanced stage
Dark brown to black roots (cut and look)Active infection
Tree dies slowly over 1-3 yearsUntreated

Prevention (much better than cure):

  • Plant only on well-drained soil.
  • Use clonal rootstocks resistant to Phytophthora (Dusa, Latas).
  • Never overwater - avocado dies faster from overwatering than underwatering.
  • Mulch 10 cm away from the trunk.
  • Apply phosphonate trunk injections or foliar sprays as preventives on susceptible sites.
  • Add gypsum to soil - it improves drainage in clay.

How to tell when to harvest

Avocado does NOT ripen on the tree - it ripens after picking. So maturity is measured differently:

  • Oil content: minimum 8-10% for most markets (lab test)
  • Fruit size: Hass reaches 200-300 g
  • Skin colour: Hass stays green when mature - the black colour develops after harvest
  • Stem test: the fruit should snap off easily when mature

Harvesting technique

  1. Harvest in the cool of the day.
  2. Snap or clip fruit with 5-10 mm of stalk attached - do not tear.
  3. Never drop fruit - bruises show 3-5 days later.
  4. Place gently in padded crates.
  5. Move to shade immediately.
  6. Do not wash before sale - the natural bloom helps shelf life.
The Phytophthora rule: Avocado hates wet feet. Well-drained soil is non-negotiable. If you doubt drainage, do not plant avocado.
Practical exercise 5: Cut a mature avocado fruit at harvest. Check oil content by taste - if it tastes watery, it is not ready. Oil develops on the tree, sweetness after picking.
