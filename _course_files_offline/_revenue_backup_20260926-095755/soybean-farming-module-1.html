Lesson 1

Why Soybean Is Africa's Fastest-Growing Legume

In this lesson
  • Explain why soybean demand has exploded in Africa
  • Understand the poultry-feed driver
  • Recognise the current supply gap

Soybean is the world's most traded agricultural commodity after maize and wheat. In Africa, demand has grown 15–25% per year over the last decade — driven almost entirely by the poultry industry. Chicken feed requires 20–30% soybean meal. As Africa's poultry sector grows, soybean demand grows with it.

Africa produces a fraction of the soybean it consumes. Most countries import soybean meal at 30–60% above local production cost. That gap is the business opportunity.

Soybean also fixes more nitrogen than any other African legume — 60–120 kg/ha. A soybean rotation rebuilds the soil for the following cereal more effectively than cowpea, groundnut or common bean.

Lesson 2

The Three Markets — Feed, Food, Oil

In this lesson
  • Name and price the three soybean markets
  • Match grade to buyer
  • Plan for the highest-margin channel

Market 1 — Feed mills (soybean meal)

The largest buyer. Poultry feed manufacturers buy soybean meal by the tonne. Prices track the global soybean meal market — $0.40–0.70/kg in most African markets. Requires low moisture (below 12%), clean grain, and often a protein specification (36–40%).

Market 2 — Food processors (soya chunks, soya flour, soya milk)

The growing food market for human consumption. Soya chunks (textured soy protein) sell at $2.00–4.00/kg. Soya flour at $1.50–3.00/kg. Soya milk at $1.50–3.00/L. Requires food-grade quality — clean, no contamination, tested.

Market 3 — Oil millers

Buy soybean for oil extraction. Soybean oil sells at $1.20–2.50/kg; the meal by-product (soybean cake) is sold to feed mills. Requires specific oil content (18–20%).

Market 4 — Export and seed

Regional export (soybean to India, China) trades at global commodity prices. Seed multiplication for certified soybean seed pays 2–3x food price.

Lesson 3

Yield Reality and the African Gap

In this lesson
  • Know the African average vs achievable yield
  • Identify the four factors that close the gap
  • Set a realistic first-season target

African average soybean yield is 0.7–1.2 t/ha. Achievable with certified seed, inoculation, phosphorus and correct spacing: 2.0–3.0 t/ha. Under irrigation and good management, 4+ t/ha is recorded.

Four factors that close the gap

  1. Inoculation. Soybean needs a specific rhizobium strain (Bradyrhizobium japonicum). African soils usually lack it. Inoculation is essential — not optional.
  2. Certified seed. Saved soybean seed loses vigour and carries virus.
  3. Phosphorus, not nitrogen. Like all legumes, nitrogen suppresses nodulation. Apply DAP or TSP at planting.
  4. Correct spacing and stand. Soybean needs 40–60 plants/m² at harvest for full yield.
Lesson 4

Nitrogen Fixation — the Highest of Any African Legume

In this lesson
  • Understand why soybean fixes the most nitrogen
  • Value the nitrogen credit in money terms
  • Use soybean in a 4-year rotation

Soybean fixes 60–120 kg of nitrogen per hectare — 2x cowpea, 3x common bean. In a rotation, that nitrogen is available to the following cereal crop. It is the single largest non-purchased nutrient input available to African smallholders.

Value in money

  • At $0.60–1.20 per kg of nitrogen (typical urea equivalent), 100 kg N is worth $60–120
  • Over a 4-year rotation, this compounds
  • Plus the pest and disease break that soybean gives the cereal

The 4-year rotation

  • Year 1: Cereal (maize)
  • Year 2: Soybean
  • Year 3: Cereal (sorghum or maize)
  • Year 4: Soybean or other legume

Rotate two cereals and two legumes. Soybean is the best legume for nitrogen; cowpea and groundnut are alternatives where soybean does not fit.

Lesson 5

Choosing Your Business

In this lesson
  • Match business model to capital and market
  • Understand the three entry points
  • Set first-season targets

Three entry points

  • Smallholder grain producer ($300–1,500): 0.5–1 ha, sell raw soybean to a feed mill or aggregator. Learn the crop.
  • Commercial grain + feed contracts ($2,000–10,000): 2–4 ha, contract with a feed mill. Add seed production for other farmers.
  • Value-added soy enterprise ($10,000+): 5+ ha plus a small processing unit for soya chunks, soya flour or soya milk. Direct consumer sales.

First-season targets

  • Yield: 1.5–2 t/ha in year 1 (aim for 2.5 in year 3)
  • Buyer: 1 named feed mill or aggregator
  • Cost: budget $350–700/ha
  • Revenue target: $700–1,500/ha at $0.45–0.70/kg
