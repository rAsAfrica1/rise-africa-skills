5. Your First Cold Process Recipe

This is the moment you actually make soap. Follow this module exactly for your first batch. Do not improvise. Once you have made one successful batch, you can start experimenting.

Module 5 of 12 · Soap Making · practical exercises · quiz at the end

Lesson 5.1 - Reading a recipe

A soap recipe has three parts:

  1. Oils and butters - by weight
  2. Lye - calculated from the oils, not guessed
  3. Water - usually 2x the weight of the lye

Optional additions: fragrance, colour, exfoliants, milk, honey, clay.

Lesson 5.3 - Step-by-step method

  1. Weigh your oils into a large stainless steel bowl. Warm gently until any solid oils (coconut, palm, shea) are melted. Target 40-45°C.
  2. Weigh water into a heat-proof jug. Add ice cubes if it is a hot day.
  3. Put on PPE. Goggles, gloves, mask, long sleeves.
  4. Weigh lye in a small dry container.
  5. Slowly add lye to the water, stirring gently. The mixture will heat up and turn cloudy. Keep stirring until it is clear. Set aside to cool to 40-45°C.
  6. Wait for both oil and lye to be 40-45°C. Within 5°C of each other.
  7. Pour lye into oils (not the other way around). Stir for 30 seconds.
  8. Stick-blend in short bursts - 5 seconds on, 5 seconds off - to avoid bubbles. Continue until the mixture reaches trace.
  9. Trace is when you drizzle batter from the blender and it leaves a visible trail on the surface for 2-3 seconds.
  10. Add fragrance and colour at light trace. Stir gently - over-mixing accelerates trace.
  11. Pour into your mould in a slow, steady stream to avoid air pockets.
  12. Tap the mould on the counter 5-10 times to release bubbles. Smooth the top with a spatula.
  13. Cover with cardboard or a towel to insulate and slow cooling. This prevents "soda ash" (a white powdery layer on top).
  14. Do not move it for 24 hours. It will be warm and semi-solid.
  15. Unmould after 24-48 hours when it is firm enough to handle without dents.

Lesson 5.4 - Cutting and curing

  • Cut into bars with a sharp knife or soap cutter. Standard bar: 100 g, roughly 8 × 6 × 2.5 cm.
  • Place bars on a rack - a wooden slatted shelf, baking tray, or cardboard with air gaps.
  • Cure 4-6 weeks in a cool, dry, dark place with airflow. Not in the fridge, not in a humid room.
  • Turn bars over at week 2 so both sides dry evenly.
  • Ready when the bar is hard, the surface is not tacky, and the pH has dropped below 10.
The cure rule: Do not rush the cure. Soap that has cured 6 weeks lasts 2 years. Soap sold at 2 weeks lasts 3 months and disappoints the customer.
Practical exercise 5.1: Make your first 1 kg batch this week. Record every step in your notebook, including temperatures and trace time.
Practical exercise 5.2: Cut 10 bars and cure them for 4 weeks. Test one bar at week 2 and one at week 4. Feel the difference in hardness and lather.
