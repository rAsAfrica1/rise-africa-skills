1. Why Sheep Farming Pays

Meat, wool, milk and skins - four income streams from one animal that thrives on marginal land.

Module 1 of 12 · Sheep Farming for Profit

The sheep opportunity

Sheep are one of the most versatile livestock in Africa. They produce meat (mutton and lamb), wool, milk, and skins. They thrive on marginal land that crops cannot use. They have lower feed costs than cattle and are easier to handle than goats for some farmers.

Why sheep work

  • Thrive on poor land - can graze steep slopes, dry areas, and poor soils
  • Low capital - a breeding ewe costs $60-150 vs $400-800 for a cow
  • Fast turnover - lambs reach market weight in 6-9 months
  • Multiple products - meat + wool + milk + skins
  • Small size - easier handling than cattle for women and youth
  • Cultural demand - sheep are the preferred meat at many African festive seasons (Eid, Christmas)
  • Grazing efficiency - sheep can graze closer to the ground than cattle, using land more effectively

What you need to start

  • Land - 0.2-0.4 ha per ewe for grazing, plus fodder
  • Simple shelter - 1-1.5 m2 per sheep
  • Reliable water source
  • Initial stock - start with 20-50 ewes
  • Breeding ram (or share one with neighbours)
  • Basic veterinary supplies
  • Starting capital of $1,500-4,000 for 20-ewe flock
The sheep rule: Start with 20-50 ewes, not 200. Learn the system, breed your own replacement stock, and expand from within. Sheep flock growth is exponential if you retain ewe lambs.
Practical exercise 1: Visit 3 sheep markets. Ask prices for ewes, rams, and lambs. Find the seasonal peak (usually Eid and Christmas). Write the numbers down.
