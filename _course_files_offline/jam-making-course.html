â  SAFETY WARNING â AFLATOXIN AND CHEMICAL HANDLING

Aflatoxin is a silent killer. It is produced by Aspergillus flavus and A. parasiticus moulds that thrive when grain is stored above 13% moisture or in humid conditions. Chronic exposure causes liver cancer and stunting in children. Acute exposure can be fatal.

  • Never eat or sell visibly mouldy grain, even if only part of the bag is affected.
  • Dry maize to below 13% moisture before storage. Use a moisture meter â do not guess.
  • Wear gloves and a nose mask when applying storage pesticides (e.g., Actellic dust). Follow label rates exactly.
  • Store pesticides away from food, feed, and children. Never repurpose food containers for chemicals.
  • If you suspect aflatoxin contamination, contact your local extension officer or the nearest laboratory for testing. Do not feed contaminated grain to livestock either.

Quick Reference Checklist

  1. Test moisture before storage. Buy or borrow a moisture meter. Target <13% for maize, <12% for rice. This single step prevents most aflatoxin and mould losses.
  2. Invest in hermetic storage. PICS bags or metal silos pay for themselves in one season by cutting storage losses from 15â25% to under 2%.
  3. Clean and sort before storing. Broken grains and debris attract insects and mould. A simple winnowing and hand-sorting routine reduces risk significantly.
  4. Treat storage structures. Apply approved residual pesticides to empty stores at least two weeks before filling. Never mix chemicals or exceed label rates.
  5. Monitor monthly. Open and inspect stored grain every 30 days. Look for heating, off-smells, webbing, or live insects. Act immediately if detected.
  6. Record everything. Keep a simple log of harvest date, moisture reading, storage method, and any treatments. This data is gold for buyers and lenders.
  7. Aggregate for market power. Small volumes attract low prices. Work with a cooperative or aggregator to sell in bulk and negotiate better terms.
  8. Know your buyer's standards. EU aflatoxin limit for maize is 10 ppb; Kenyan and Nigerian standards are 10â20 ppb. Test before you ship, not after rejection.
