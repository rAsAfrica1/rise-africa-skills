â  SAFETY WARNING â Chemicals, Drugs and Food Safety
  • Withdrawal periods are not optional. Never sell meat or eggs during the withdrawal period of any antibiotic, coccidiostat or dewormer. Residues can make consumers sick and can get your product banned from formal markets.
  • Disinfectants are poisons. Wear gloves, a mask and eye protection when handling formalin, glutaraldehyde, bleach or caustic soda. Mix in ventilated spaces, never indoors, and never mix bleach with ammonia-based products â the gas is lethal.
  • Vaccines need a cold chain. Store at 2â8Â°C, use within the stated window after reconstitution, and never re-use a reconstituted vial the next day.
  • Dead birds are a biosecurity event. Burn or deep-bury carcasses immediately. Do not sell, feed to other animals, or leave them where dogs and rodents can reach them.
  • Wash hands and change footwear between houses and before eating. Keep a dedicated pair of gumboots per house.
  • If in doubt, throw it out. A spoiled batch of feed or a suspect carcass costs far less than a food-poisoning incident or a market ban.

Quick Reference Checklist

  • Cost the feed before you buy the chicks. Feed is 65â75% of total cost â get three written quotes and confirm the price holds for the full cycle.
  • Size the house correctly. Allow roughly 10 birds/mÂ² for broilers and 5â6 birds/mÂ² for layers, with at least 0.6 m of feeder space per 25 birds.
  • Lock in your buyer first. Confirm price per kg or per tray with a hotel, butcher, retailer or trader before the batch matures.
  • Keep a simple daily record. Record mortality, feed used, water consumed and eggs collected every single day â these four numbers tell you if the flock is in trouble.
  • Run strict biosecurity. Footbath at every entrance, no visitors into houses, all-in/all-out batches where possible, and a 2-week rest between broiler cycles.
  • Vaccinate on schedule. Newcastle (ND) and Infectious Bronchitis are non-negotiable in most African climates â follow a vet-approved programme.
  • Weigh a sample weekly. Weigh 10 birds each week and compare against breed targets; if they are behind, fix feed or ventilation immediately, not next month.
  • Reinvest the first two cycles. Use early margin to build feed storage, better housing and a reserve fund for one full feed cycle.
