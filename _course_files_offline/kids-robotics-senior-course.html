Robotics combines electricity, moving parts, and sometimes chemicals. Treat every build as if it can hurt you, because it can.

  • Never power a salvaged motor or servo directly from a phone or laptop USB port. Use a separate battery pack (4ÃAA or 6 V NiMH) with a common ground. USB current limits are 0.5â2 A; a stalled servo can pull 2.5 A and damage the port or start a fire.

  • Servo arms and gears can pinch fingers. Keep hands clear of the sorting arm's travel path when powered. Use a physical stop or software limit.
  • If you use lead-acid or lithium batteries, charge them on a non-flammable surface, never overnight unattended, and never in a bedroom. Lithium cells that swell, hiss, or smell sweet must be taken to a proper disposal point â do not throw them in household waste.

Quick Reference Checklist

  1. Define the loop rate before you write code. Write it in a comment: # 50 Hz = 20 ms per cycle. If you cannot state the rate, you cannot debug the machine.
  2. Test every sensor in isolation first. Print raw values to the console for 60 seconds. Confirm the range (e.g. moisture probe 0â1023) before trusting it.
  3. Use a separate power rail for actuators. Logic (phone/ESP32) on one supply, motors on another, grounds joined. This prevents brown-outs and resets.
  4. Add a physical emergency stop. A simple inline switch on the battery positive line costs under US$1 and can save a finger or a motor.
  5. Log your Kp value and the result. A notebook entry like "Kp=1.2, overshoot 5%, settle 0.8 s" turns tuning from guesswork into engineering.
  6. Run the simulator for at least 100 cycles before hardware. Free crashes in software are cheaper than burnt servos.
  7. Photograph and number every build. Buyers, lenders, and employers respond to evidence. A labelled photo with a date is your proof of capability.
  8. Price your build before you sell it. Add up: parts, battery, phone share, labour hours Ã your hourly rate, plus 20% for faults and returns. If the margin is under 30%, redesign or raise the price.
