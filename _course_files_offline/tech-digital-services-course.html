â  SAFETY WARNING â Electrical, Battery and Chemical Hazards

Phone repair and printing involve real hazards. Lithium-ion batteries can catch fire or explode if punctured, bent or overheated. Never pry a swollen battery, never charge a damaged battery, and keep a dry-powder or COâ extinguisher within reach â water makes lithium fires worse. Soldering produces lead and flux fumes: work in a ventilated area, use a fume extractor or fan, wash hands after handling solder, and never eat or drink at the bench.

Toner and ink are fine respiratory irritants. Do not blow toner dust with compressed air; use a damp cloth and a vacuum rated for fine particles. Wear nitrile gloves and eye protection when handling solvents, screen adhesives and cleaning fluids. Keep all chemicals in labelled containers, out of reach of children, and store away from food and drinking water.

Electrical work must be earthed and protected by a residual-current device (RCD/earth leakage breaker). Do not overload sockets or daisy-chain extension leads. If you are not qualified to work on mains wiring, hire a licensed electrician. Customer data on repaired devices is confidential â never copy, share or sell it.

Quick Reference Checklist

  • Price-check before you buy. Call or visit three suppliers for every major item (printer, toner, router, tools) and record quotes in your record book before spending.
  • Build a one-page cost sheet. List startup items, monthly recurring costs and your target selling prices so you know your break-even number of jobs per week.
  • Test demand before stock. Run a one-week trial (printing, hotspot or repair intake) to confirm real customer volume before buying expensive equipment.
  • Set a price floor, not a guess. Never price below direct cost plus 30%; review prices monthly against local competitors and input costs.
  • Keep daily records. Log every sale, every expense and every job in a record book or simple spreadsheet â this is what lenders and buyers ask for.
  • Protect yourself and customers. Use an RCD, ventilate soldering areas, store chemicals safely, and keep customer device data confidential.
  • Deliver reliably. Agree turnaround times in writing, communicate delays early, and offer a short written warranty on repairs.
  • Upsell deliberately. After every repair or print job, offer one higher-value service (training, social media, website) to raise average revenue per customer.
