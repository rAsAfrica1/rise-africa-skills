Practical Reference

This reference block is designed for agribusiness operators, cooperative managers, extension officers, and agro-dealers who need to move from theory to transactions. Whether you are aggregating maize in Nakuru, running a poultry unit in Ogun State, or packaging dried mango for export from Ouagadougou, the figures, checklists, and tools below are intended to be used at the desk, in the field, and in the negotiation room. Treat them as a working appendix: annotate them, adapt the local costs, and revisit them at each planning cycle.

  • Never use a pesticide that is banned or unregistered in your country. Check the national registrar (e.g., KEPHIS in Kenya, NAFDAC in Nigeria, DALRRD in South Africa) before purchase.
  • Always wear the full PPE specified on the label â gloves, boots, face shield, and long clothing. Re-entry intervals and pre-harvest intervals are legal requirements, not suggestions.
  • Store agrochemicals in a locked, ventilated, labelled cabinet away from food, feed, and children. Never decant into beverage bottles.
  • Observe withdrawal periods for veterinary drugs in meat, milk, and eggs. Residue violations can close your market access permanently.
  • For food processing: maintain potable water, sanitise surfaces, control pests, and keep temperature logs. A single contamination incident can end a supply contract and trigger regulatory action.
  • In case of poisoning or suspected residue exposure, contact your nearest poison control centre or health facility immediately and retain the product label for medical staff.

Quick Reference Checklist

  1. Unit economics first: Calculate cost per kilogram, litre, or bird before you scale. If the unit does not make money, scaling multiplies the loss.
  2. Secure the buyer before the harvest: A signed or written off-take agreement reduces price risk and strengthens your loan application.
  3. Track the five costs: Inputs, labour, energy, transport, and finance. These account for the majority of cash outflow in most African agri-enterprises.
  4. Invest in storage and grading: Reducing post-harvest loss by 10 percentage points often beats a 10% price increase in net margin terms.
  5. Keep records daily: Production, sales, expenses, and stock. Without records you cannot price, borrow, or prove creditworthiness.
  6. Comply with registration and standards: Product registration, weights and measures, and food safety certification are market access requirements, not optional extras.
  7. Diversify buyers, not products, first: Two or three reliable buyers for your core product is usually safer than five untested product lines.
  8. Review monthly: Compare actuals against budget, adjust prices and volumes, and recheck your break-even point each month.
