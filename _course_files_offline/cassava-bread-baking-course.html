Raw cassava roots contain cyanogenic glycosides (linamarin and lotaustralin). If processing is incomplete, these compounds release hydrogen cyanide (HCN) and can cause acute poisoning. This is not a theoretical risk â it is a documented cause of illness and death in cassava-consuming regions.

  • Always peel and grate thoroughly. Peeling removes most of the cyanogens; grating and pressing removes more.
  • Ferment for the full recommended period (typically 24â72 hours depending on variety and method). Do not shorten it to save time.
  • Dry to â¤12% moisture before milling to HQCF. Incomplete drying leaves residual HCN risk.
  • Never sell or bake with bitter (high-cyanide) varieties without full processing and, where available, laboratory verification.
  • Do not use raw cassava wastewater for irrigation or livestock without treatment â it carries concentrated cyanogens.
  • Train every worker on these steps and record batch fermentation times. If a batch smells strongly of bitter almond, discard it.

If anyone shows symptoms after handling or eating under-processed cassava â dizziness, headache, vomiting, confusion â seek emergency medical care immediately. Keep the batch for testing and stop production until the cause is confirmed.
