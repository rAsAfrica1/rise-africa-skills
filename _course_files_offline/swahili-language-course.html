Practical Reference

Swahili (Kiswahili) is the working language of trade across East Africa â from the port of Mombasa to the markets of Kampala, Kigali, Bujumbura and the DRC's eastern provinces. For an African agribusiness operator, it is not a cultural nicety; it is a commercial instrument. The East African Community (EAC) recognises Kiswahili as an official language, and the African Union adopted it as a working language in 2022. If you buy maize, beans, coffee or livestock across borders, the ability to negotiate in Swahili directly affects your margin. This Practical Reference gives you the numbers, phrases and market realities you need to close deals â not pass an exam.

Two corridors carry most formal East African trade. The Northern Corridor runs from Mombasa through Nairobi to Kampala and on to Kigali and Bujumbura. The Central Corridor runs from Dar es Salaam inland to Rwanda, Burundi and eastern DRC. Knowing which corridor your goods travel on tells you which Swahili dialect variant and which currency you will meet at the border. Swahili in Mombasa is fast and coastal; in Kigali it is slower and more formal; in Dar es Salaam it is the everyday lingua franca of the market.

Swahili negotiation is relational before it is transactional. You greet, you ask after family, you ask after the season â then you talk price. â  SAFETY WARNING â Pesticides, Veterinary Drugs and Food Handling

When you negotiate the purchase of agricultural chemicals, veterinary medicines or any food product in Swahili markets, never rely on a verbal description alone. A trader may call a product dawa ya wadudu (insecticide) without stating the active ingredient, concentration or expiry date. Always demand the written label and, where sold in Kenya, the PCPB registration number; in Tanzania the TPRI number; in Uganda the MAAIF permit. Food products must carry a country standards mark â KEBS (Kenya), TBS (Tanzania), UNBS (Uganda), RSB (Rwanda). Buying unregistered chemicals or expired veterinary drugs to save money can kill your livestock, contaminate your produce and disqualify you from export contracts. In case of suspected poisoning, contact your national poison centre immediately â Kenya: 0800 720 021; Tanzania: 0800 750 010; Uganda: 0800 101 999. Store all agrochemicals away from food, out of reach of children, and never in a beverage bottle.
