â  SAFETY WARNING â READ BEFORE EVERY TRIP

Transport is a safety-critical business. A single serious accident can end your enterprise and destroy a family. You are legally and morally responsible for every passenger, every parcel and every driver working under your name.

  • Never overload. Exceeding the rated payload or seating capacity causes tyre blowouts, brake failure and rollovers. Overloading also voids insurance claims in most African jurisdictions.
  • Never allow a driver under the influence. Alcohol or drugs behind the wheel is an instant dismissal offence and a criminal matter. Test randomly and record it.
  • Enforce rest periods. Long-haul drivers must rest after 4 hours of continuous driving. Fatigue kills more drivers than bad roads do.
  • Inspect before every departure. Tyres, brakes, lights, mirrors, fluid levels, load security. Log the inspection â unrecorded inspections do not exist in a court or insurance dispute.
  • Carry a fire extinguisher, first-aid kit, reflective triangles and a spare wheel on every vehicle, every trip. No exceptions.
  • Never transport undeclared or sealed cargo you have not inspected. You can be prosecuted for what is in your vehicle, even if you did not pack it.
  • Insist on seatbelts for all passengers and refuse to depart until they are worn. Your licence is worth more than one impatient client.

Quick Reference Checklist

Run this checklist weekly. It takes twenty minutes and saves thousands in downtime, fines and lost contracts.

  1. Vehicle inspection log completed for every vehicle, every day â tyres, brakes, lights, fluids, load security. Signed by the driver and countersigned by the supervisor.
  2. All documents current and filed: registration, insurance, fitness certificate, operating licence, driver permits. Set a 30-day reminder before each expiry date.
  3. Fuel and mileage recorded per trip. Calculate cost per kilometre weekly. If it rises more than 8% without a fuel price change, investigate mechanical faults or fuel theft.
  4. Pricing reviewed against current fuel price. If you have no fuel-adjustment clause in your client contracts, add one at the next renewal.
  5. Driver hours and rest days monitored. No driver exceeds legal driving hours; rest days are scheduled, not optional.
  6. Cash and trip reconciliation done daily. Every trip has a signed trip sheet, a fare or invoice record, and a cash deposit receipt. No trip goes unrecorded.
  7. Client follow-up within 24 hours of delivery or completed trip. Ask for the next job and a referral. Repeat clients are cheaper than new ones.
  8. Maintenance reserve funded. Move 10% of every payment into a separate maintenance account. Tyres, brakes and clutches are not emergencies â they are scheduled costs you already know are coming.
