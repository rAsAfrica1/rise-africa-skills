â  SAFETY WARNING â Clostridium botulinum

Fresh garlic, chillies, herbs, and vegetables submerged in oil create an oxygen-free environment â exactly what Clostridium botulinum needs. If the water phase of your product is above pH 4.6, spores can germinate and produce a toxin that is lethal in tiny doses. Never sell a garlic-in-oil or fresh-vegetable-in-oil product that has not been acidified to pH 4.6 or below, or dried to below 10% moisture before infusion. Do not rely on refrigeration alone. Do not taste suspicious jars. Use a calibrated pH meter or accurate pH strips on every batch and record the reading. If in doubt, discard the batch â a lost batch costs US$50; a fatality costs a business, a family, and a life.

Quick Reference Checklist

  1. Calibrate your pH meter daily against fresh buffer solutions 4.0 and 7.0 before any production run.
  2. Acidify the water phase, not the oil. Add lemon juice, vinegar (5% acetic acid), or citric acid to the vegetable or herb before it goes into the oil, and verify pH â¤ 4.6.
  3. Dry fresh herbs and chillies to below 10% moisture if you want a shelf-stable product without acidification.
  4. Sterilise every jar and lid in boiling water for 10 minutes; bottle the oil hot (60â80Â°C) and invert the sealed jar for 5 minutes.
  5. Record batch number, date, pH, and aw for every production run â this is your legal defence and your quality control.
  6. Keep a retained sample of every batch for 30 days at room temperature and inspect for gas, cloudiness, or off-odour.
  7. Label every jar with ingredients, net weight, batch code, best-before date, and the instruction "Refrigerate after opening".
  8. Price for margin, not for volume. Target 45â55% gross margin; below 35% you cannot absorb a recall or a bad batch.
