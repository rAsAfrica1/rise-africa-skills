Citrus greening (HLB)

Huanglongbing (HLB), or citrus greening, is the world's most destructive citrus disease. It is caused by a bacterium spread by the Asian citrus psyllid.

SymptomWhat it means
Blotchy mottling on leaves (asymmetric)Classic HLB sign
Yellow shoots growing from one branchEarly infection
Small, lopsided fruit with bitter juiceAdvanced infection
Tree decline over 2-5 yearsUntreated
There is NO cure for citrus greening. The only defence is prevention.

Preventing HLB

  1. Plant certified disease-free trees from a licensed nursery.
  2. Control the psyllid vector - the tiny insect that spreads the disease. Spray with imidacloprid or thiamethoxam (respect pre-harvest intervals).
  3. Monitor with yellow sticky traps - check weekly for psyllids.
  4. Remove infected trees immediately - bag and burn.
  5. Never bring budwood or trees from an infected area.
  6. Quarantine new trees for 3 months in a separate area before planting.

Fruit fly

Fruit fly is the biggest cause of premature fruit drop in citrus. Female flies lay eggs in fruit. Larvae tunnel through the flesh. Fruit drops and rots.

ControlMethodFrequency
Orchard sanitationCollect and destroy all fallen fruitDaily in season
Protein bait sprayAttract-and-kill. Spray on foliage (not fruit)Weekly in season

The HLB rule: Plant only certified disease-free trees from a licensed nursery. Never bring budwood from an unknown source. It is the difference between a 40-year orchard and a 5-year one.
Practical exercise 5: Install 5 yellow sticky traps per hectare in your citrus plot. Check weekly. Record any psyllids or fruit flies caught.
