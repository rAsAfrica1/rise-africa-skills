Choosing a breeding cow

  • Body condition: well-fleshed, not fat, ready to breed
  • Udder: well-formed, four good teats, no lumps, no mastitis history
  • Age: 3-8 years (peaked productivity)
  • Teeth: full set, no broken or missing (indicates age and health)
  • Feet: sound, no limping, no overgrown hooves
  • Eyes: bright, no discharge
  • History: has she calved successfully? How many calves weaned?
  • Frame: good length and depth, feminine head
  • Temperament: calm - aggressive cows are dangerous and culled

Choosing a bull

  • Frame: long, deep, well-muscled, masculine
  • Testicles: two, equal, firm, large
  • Age: 2-5 years (peaked fertility)
  • Sound feet and legs - crucial for serving cows
  • Not related to your cows
  • Lineage: from a registered breeder with performance data
  • Temperament: calm, easy to handle. Aggressive bulls are dangerous.
  • Fertility tested: semen check if buying an expensive bull

One bull serves 25-40 cows naturally. For 100+ cows, use 3-4 bulls or artificial insemination.

Two-breed rotation: keep F1 heifers as replacements, use a different breed bull on them. Three-breed rotation: best hybrid vigour but requires 3 breeds.

Never buy from a market without quarantine. Auction cattle often carry disease. Quarantine for 21-30 days before adding to your herd. Test for brucellosis and TB if possible.

Selection for future productivity

When you keep heifer calves as replacements, choose:

  • From cows that calve every year
  • Born without assistance (easy calving)
  • Higher-than-average weaning weight
  • Sound structure - straight legs, good feet
  • Good body condition without extra feeding
  • Calm temperament
  • From a line with good udders (heritable)
The breed rule: For most African smallholders, an indigenous cow crossed with a Bonsmara or similar composite bull is the best combination. You get the hardiness of local stock plus the growth of improved genetics.
Practical exercise 2: Visit 3 cattle farms. Compare their breeds and crosses. Ask about calving rates, weaning weights, and disease problems. Note which breed fits your conditions best.
