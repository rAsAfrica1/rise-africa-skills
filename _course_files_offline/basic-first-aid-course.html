â  SAFETY WARNING â Health, Blood and Chemical Exposure

This is a health and body-fluids topic. Treat every casualty as potentially infectious. HIV, hepatitis B and C, and tuberculosis are all transmitted through blood, saliva, and airborne droplets. You must:

  • Wear disposable gloves before touching any wound, blood, or body fluid. If no gloves, use a clean plastic bag as a barrier â never bare hands.
  • Use a face shield or barrier device for rescue breathing. Direct mouth-to-mouth without a barrier is a last resort only.
  • Wash hands with soap and running water for at least 20 seconds immediately after any contact, even with gloves.
  • Dispose of sharps, needles, and blood-soaked materials in a sealed, puncture-proof container. Never in open bins or pits.
  • Get vaccinated against hepatitis B and tetanus before working as a trainer or workplace first aider.
  • Never apply kerosene, petrol, ash, toothpaste, or traditional caustic substances to burns or open wounds. These cause infection and delay healing.
  • If you are exposed to blood through a needle-stick or splash, seek medical care within 2 hours â post-exposure prophylaxis (PEP) is time-critical.
  • Do not give food, drink, or oral medicine to an unconscious casualty. Aspiration can kill.

Legal note: In most African jurisdictions, a trained first aider acting in good faith within their competence is protected by Good Samaritan provisions. Working outside your training, or charging for services you are not certified to deliver, removes that protection and exposes you to liability.

Quick Reference Checklist

  1. Check the scene before you touch anyone. Traffic, fire, live wires, and violent crowds kill rescuers. Your safety comes first, or there will be two casualties.
  2. Shout for help and call emergency services. Save the number now: know your national ambulance line and the nearest clinic. Delegate someone specific â "You in the blue shirt, call now."
  3. Run ABCDE in order, every time. Airway, Breathing, Circulation, Disability, Exposure. Do not jump to a bleeding leg while the airway is blocked.
  4. Control severe bleeding with direct pressure. Press hard on the wound with a clean cloth or gloved hand. Add layers, do not remove them. Use a tourniquet only for catastrophic limb bleeding that pressure cannot stop, and note the time applied.
  5. Start CPR if the casualty is unresponsive and not breathing normally. 30 compressions to 2 breaths, centre of chest, 5â6 cm deep, 100â120 per minute. Push hard, push fast, do not stop until help arrives or the person revives.
  6. Keep the casualty warm and treat for shock. Lay them flat, raise the legs if no spinal injury, cover with a blanket or clothing. Shock kills more slowly than bleeding but just as surely.
  7. Document everything. Time of incident, what you found, what you did, who you called. This record protects you legally and is the first thing an employer or insurer will ask for.
  8. Restock your kit and review the incident. Every used item must be replaced the same day. Debrief what went well and what to improve â this is how you become the trainer others pay.
