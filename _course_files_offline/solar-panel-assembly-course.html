Practical Reference

This reference section is built for operators, agripreneurs, extension agents and trainers who need numbers they can act on. Every figure below is drawn from a named public source so you can cite it in a business plan, loan application, grant proposal or buyer contract. Use the tables as working tools: plug your own farm gate price, yield and cost lines into the same structure and you have a defensible gross margin model within an hour.

The diagram makes one commercial point clearly: in grain systems, the largest single margin pool sits downstream of the farm gate, and the largest avoidable cost sits in post-harvest handling. If you are a producer, that tells you where to invest next. If you are a processor or aggregator, it tells you where your buying price can be justified to farmers.

Read the table as a decision tool, not a benchmark to admire. Note that common bean and groundnut generate gross value per hectare comparable to maize despite far lower tonnage, because the unit price is two to three times higher. For a smallholder with limited land, that is a strong argument for including a legume in rotation, particularly where nitrogen fixation reduces the fertiliser bill on the following cereal crop.

Two commercial observations matter here. First, the value at risk per ten tonnes is often larger than the cost of the storage solution that would prevent it, which makes post-harvest investment one of the highest-return decisions available to a smallholder. Second, losses are not evenly distributed: they concentrate at specific handling steps, so a targeted fix at the right step beats a general improvement across the whole chain.

SAFETY WARNING: Grain Storage Chemicals and Aflatoxin Risk

Stored grain treatments and mould contamination are a health risk, not just a quality issue. Follow these rules without exception:

  • Never apply storage pesticides directly to grain intended for immediate consumption. Respect the stated withholding period on the label.
  • Wear gloves, a dust mask and eye protection when handling any storage insecticide or fumigant. Never mix or apply in an enclosed room without ventilation.
  • Do not eat, drink or smoke while handling treated grain or chemicals. Wash hands and face thoroughly afterwards.
  • Store chemicals in their original labelled container, locked, away from food, feed, children and livestock. Never decant into beverage bottles.
  • Grain with visible mould, discolouration or a musty smell may contain aflatoxin. Do not feed it to dairy animals or sell it for human consumption. Aflatoxin is heat-stable and is not destroyed by cooking or milling.
  • Dry grain to safe moisture (typically 13% or below for maize) before storage. Mould risk rises sharply above that threshold.
  • If you or a worker shows symptoms after exposure, seek medical attention immediately and bring the product label.

Turning These Numbers Into a Business Case

Lenders and off-takers respond to structure, not enthusiasm. Build your case in four lines. First, state yield per hectare from your own records or a conservative local average. Second, state the verified farm gate or buyer price, with the source and date. Third, list direct costs per hectare: seed, fertiliser, labour, transport, storage. Fourth, subtract to get gross margin, then subtract fixed costs to get net margin. The tables above give you defensible starting values for lines one and two, but your own records always beat a published average in a credit committee.

Where the numbers point to a clear opportunity, act on it. If your post-harvest loss is above 15%, the storage investment usually pays for itself within one or two seasons at current grain prices. If your gross value per hectare on a cereal is under USD 500 and you have reliable market access for legumes, a rotation change may lift whole-farm returns without buying a single extra hectare. Both moves are capital-light and both are supported by the data above.

For aggregators and processors, the same tables support a different argument. If you can demonstrate to farmers that a hermetic storage bag protects USD 250 to USD 500 of value per ten tonnes, you have a commercially credible reason to bundle storage inputs with your buying programme. That is a supply chain strategy, not a charitable gesture, and it should be costed and priced as such.

Quick Reference Checklist

  • Verify your price before you plan. Confirm today's farm gate or buyer price from a named source (national market information system, co-operative, or written buyer quote) and date-stamp it in your records.
  • Measure your actual yield. Weigh at least one representative plot per season rather than estimating from bags. Yield assumptions drive every downstream number.
  • Test grain moisture before storage. Use a moisture meter or the salt-and-bottle method. Target 13% or below for maize and 12-13% for beans.
  • Quantify your post-harvest loss. Track grain in and grain out of storage. A loss figure you can defend is the foundation of any storage investment case.
  • Cost the fix before buying it. Compare the price of hermetic bags, pallets or a moisture meter against the value at risk from the loss table above.
  • Keep a chemical register. Record product name, date applied, rate, withholding period and operator. This protects your workers and your market access.
  • Build a one-page margin model. Yield, price, direct costs, gross margin, fixed costs, net margin. Update it every season with real figures.
  • Review your crop mix annually. Compare gross value per hectare across cereals and legumes before committing land for the next season.
