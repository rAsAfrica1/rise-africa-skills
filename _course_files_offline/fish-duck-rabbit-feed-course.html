Practical Reference

This reference block is written for the operator, not the academic. It assumes you are already mixing feed for fish, ducks, or rabbits and need defensible numbers you can put in front of a lender, a co-op buyer, or your own bank statement. Everything below is drawn from published FAO, IFIF, and African Union data. Where figures are ranges, treat the low end as your conservative planning case and the high end as your stretch case. Never plan a business on the stretch case.

How the Three Enterprises Interlock

The commercial logic of a fish-duck-rabbit feed operation is not three separate businesses. It is one feed mill serving three protein markets with staggered cash cycles. Rabbits convert feed to meat in roughly 8-11 weeks. Ducks reach market weight in 8-10 weeks. Tilapia and catfish reach table size in 5-8 months depending on species and system. That means rabbit and duck revenue funds the feed inventory that carries the fish through their long cycle. If you run only fish, you carry feed cost for months before a single dollar returns. If you run all three, you have cash arriving every 8-10 weeks.

Data Table 1: Feed Conversion Ratios and Target Protein Levels

Feed conversion ratio (FCR) is the single most important number in your business. It tells you how many kilograms of feed produce one kilogram of meat or fish. A 0.2 improvement in FCR across a 500-bird or 500-fish operation is worth more than most marketing campaigns. Know your FCR monthly, not annually.

Aflatoxin and mycotoxin contamination in maize, groundnut cake, and poorly dried fishmeal is the single largest killer of fish, ducks, and rabbits in African smallholder systems â and a serious human health risk when contaminated meat or eggs enter the food chain. Never use mouldy, discoloured, or musty-smelling grain, even if the price is attractive.

  • Store all ingredients below 12% moisture in a dry, raised, ventilated store. Test with a moisture meter before purchase.
  • Never feed rabbits or ducks any material treated with pesticides, herbicides, or veterinary drugs not cleared for that species.
  • Wear a dust mask and gloves when grinding and mixing. Feed dust causes chronic respiratory disease.
  • Keep all premixes, medicines, and treated seed locked away from children and separate from food preparation areas.
  • If livestock show sudden mortality, refusal to eat, or liver damage signs, stop feeding immediately, isolate the batch, and contact your nearest veterinary officer.

Quick Reference Checklist

  1. Test every incoming batch for moisture and smell. Reject anything above 12% moisture or showing mould. One bad maize delivery can wipe out a month of profit.
  2. Weigh, do not estimate. Use a scale for every ingredient. A 5% formulation error on protein can drop FCR by 0.3 and erase your margin.
  3. Record FCR monthly per species. If FCR rises for two consecutive months, stop and investigate feed quality, water quality, or disease before buying more stock.
  4. Keep 3 months of core ingredient inventory. Maize and protein meals are seasonal. Buying at harvest is the cheapest capital you will ever deploy.
  5. Grow at least one protein input on-farm. Moringa, cassava leaf, or azolla cut your purchased protein bill by 10-20% within one season.
  6. Stagger your species cycles. Time rabbit and duck batches so revenue lands in the months when fish feed costs peak.
  7. Lock in two buyers before you scale. A signed supply agreement with a hotel, restaurant, or co-op is worth more than a bigger pond.
  8. Re-check ingredient prices quarterly. Reformulate rather than raise your selling price where the market will not bear it.
