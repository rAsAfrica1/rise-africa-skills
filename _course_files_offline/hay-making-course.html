Practical Reference

Hay making is a cash business, not a hobby. In Southern and East Africa, the dry-season feed gap runs roughly from June to October, and that is when a well-made bale earns its highest price. The references below give you the numbers, targets, and checks you need to move from "cutting grass" to selling a consistent, repeatable product into the livestock feed market.

  • Never bale above 20% moisture. Damp hay heats internally above 50Â°C. Stacks have been known to self-ignite within 3â6 weeks. Check stack temperature daily for the first 6 weeks with a probe or iron rod â if too hot to hold, call the fire service and break the stack.
  • Mouldy hay is toxic. Aspergillus and Fusarium moulds produce mycotoxins that cause liver damage, abortions, and death in cattle. Do not feed mouldy hay, and do not sell it. Destroy it.
  • Wear a P2/N95 dust mask when handling dry hay. Hay dust causes "farmer's lung" (hypersensitivity pneumonitis), a permanent lung disease. This is a real, documented occupational hazard in Africa.
  • Keep bales away from housing and fuel stores. Minimum 10 m separation. Store in a well-ventilated shed, never against a wall.
  • Do not use fire to clear fields near hay stacks. One spark in dry season = total loss.

Quick Reference Checklist

  • Cut at the right stage. For most tropical grasses (Rhodes, Napier, Star grass), cut at early flowering â roughly 45â60 days after the last cut or start of rains. Protein drops sharply after this point.
  • Check the weather forecast for 4 dry days. Do not cut if rain is forecast within 72 hours. A single rain event on a curing windrow can cut feed value by 20â40%.
  • Ted within 24 hours. Spread the swath to speed drying and prevent the underside going yellow and mouldy.
  • Test moisture before baling. Use a moisture meter (target 15â18%) or the twist test â a properly cured stem should snap, not bend. Do not guess.
  • Bale tight and stack off the ground. Use pallets or a raised platform. Ground contact wicks moisture and rots the bottom layer.
  • Monitor stack temperature for 6 weeks. Probe daily. If the stack is hot to the touch, break it open and spread the bales.
  • Weigh and label every bale. Consistent 20 kg bales sell faster and command a premium. Buyers trust a seller who weighs.
  • Lock in buyers before the dry season. Contract with dairy cooperatives, feedlots, and commercial livestock farms in AprilâMay. Dry-season prices peak JuneâOctober.
