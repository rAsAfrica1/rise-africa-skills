â  SAFETY WARNING â Aflatoxin and Chemical Handling

Maize, groundnuts and other staples in humid African storage conditions are highly susceptible to aflatoxin contamination, a known carcinogen. Never sell or mill grain with visible mould, discolouration or a musty smell. Test suspect lots with rapid aflatoxin test kits before purchase.

Store all pesticides, fumigants and grain protectants in locked, ventilated, clearly labelled cabinets away from food, feed and children. Wear gloves, eye protection and a respirator when applying. Observe the full pre-harvest interval and never exceed label dosage. Report suspected poisoning immediately to a health facility and retain the product label.

Quick Reference Checklist

  • Know your true cost per tonne. Recalculate after every harvest â input, fuel and FX movements in Africa routinely swing margins by 20% or more within a single season.
  • Secure raw material contracts before you buy equipment. A mill with no reliable grain supply is a depreciating asset, not a business.
  • Test for aflatoxin at intake, not at dispatch. Rejected loads at the buyer's gate destroy both margin and reputation.
  • Target the processing step, not just production. Aggregation, milling and packaging capture more value than farmgate sales (see Figure 1).
  • Price in post-harvest loss. Budget 10â20% loss on cereals and roots unless you have verified hermetic storage (e.g. PICS bags, metal silos).
  • Formalise compliance early. Certification (KEBS, ZABS, NAFDAC, equivalent national bodies) is a precondition for supermarket and export contracts.
  • Use mobile money for input credit and farmer payments. It reduces leakage, builds transaction records, and unlocks working capital from lenders.
  • Map your buyer before your first harvest. Off-taker agreements, even non-binding, anchor your pricing and de-risk loan applications.
