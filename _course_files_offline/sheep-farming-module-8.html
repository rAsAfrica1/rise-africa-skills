Festive market timing

Sheep prices spike at major festive seasons. Plan lambing so lambs reach market weight in time:

FestiveTimingPremium
Eid al-AdhaRamadan + 10 weeks (varies)+40-100%
Eid al-FitrEnd of Ramadan+30-60%
Christmas / New YearDecember+20-50%
EasterMarch-April+20-40%
Independence / harvest daysVaries by country+10-30%

To hit Eid al-Adha with a 12-month lamb: breed 12 months before Eid. For a 6-month old lamb: breed 6 months before. Work backwards.

Direct selling

Selling directly to consumers (farm gate or delivery) earns the best prices:

  • Whole live sheep: customer picks a sheep for slaughter at home or a butcher.
  • Half carcass or quarters: customer pre-orders for the freezer.
  • Cut-and-packed boxes: 5 kg or 10 kg boxes of cuts for urban families.
  • Butcher's order: regular customers order cuts weekly or monthly.

Marketing: WhatsApp groups, community boards, social media, word of mouth.

Pricing strategy

  1. Know your break-even - cost per kg to produce.
  2. Never sell below break-even - even in a glut.
  3. Price the grade, not the sheep - premium sheep earn premium prices.
  4. Consistency beats one-off premiums - regular buyers are worth more than a one-time high price.
  5. Invoiced vs cash: cash buyers pay less, invoiced buyers pay more but later. Factor the delay into your pricing.
The market rule: One confirmed buyer before lambing is worth more than 10 buyers you hope to find at market time. Sign simple written agreements - quantity, quality, price range, delivery day.
Practical exercise 8: Visit 5 potential buyers - 2 butcheries, 2 restaurants, 1 direct customer. Ask their current prices, weekly volumes, and delivery preferences. Write everything down.
