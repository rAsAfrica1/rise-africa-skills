This module involves handling agricultural inputs, food products and possibly veterinary or crop-protection chemicals. Non-compliance can cause poisoning, market rejection and legal liability.

  • Personal protective equipment (PPE): Gloves, boots, eye protection and a respirator are mandatory when mixing or applying pesticides. Never eat, drink or smoke during application.
  • Re-entry intervals: Respect the label re-entry period (commonly 24â72 hours) before workers or livestock re-enter treated fields.
  • Pre-harvest intervals (PHI): Never harvest before the stated PHI. Residue violations cause border rejections and destroy buyer trust.
  • Storage: Keep chemicals locked, labelled, off the ground and away from food, feed and water sources. Use a dedicated, ventilated store.
  • Food hygiene: Apply HACCP principles; separate raw and cooked; control cold chain temperature (0â4Â°C chilled, â¤ -18Â°C frozen).
  • Aflatoxin and mycotoxin risk: Dry grains to safe moisture (maize â¤13.5%) and store below 65% relative humidity to prevent contamination.
  • Emergency response: Keep the product label, a first-aid kit and the nearest poison-control contact posted at the store. Report suspected poisoning immediately.

Quick Reference Checklist

  1. Validate demand before you invest. Confirm at least two buyers, volumes, prices and payment terms in writing before committing capital to production or processing.
  2. Cost your unit economics. Calculate cost per kilogram, per bird or per litre â including transport, losses and finance â before setting a price.
  3. Secure working capital. Match loan tenor to the crop or production cycle; short-cycle enterprises should not carry long-term debt.
  4. Control post-harvest losses. Invest in drying, hermetic storage or cold chain where losses exceed 10% â this is often the fastest margin gain.
  5. Comply with standards. Obtain required food-safety, phytosanitary and export certifications before approaching formal or export buyers.
  6. Digitise records and payments. Use mobile money and simple digital records to build a credit history and reduce cash leakage.
  7. Manage chemical and food safety risk. Train all staff on PPE, PHI, re-entry intervals, hygiene and emergency response; keep a signed training log.
  8. Diversify market channels. Combine at least one formal buyer with local or regional outlets to reduce dependence on a single customer.
