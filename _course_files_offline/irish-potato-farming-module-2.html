Lesson 1

Seed Size and Cutting

In this lesson
  • Choose the right seed size for planting
  • Decide whether to cut seed tubers
  • Avoid the common mistakes that ruin seed

Whole seed vs cut seed

Whole seed (30–60 g tubers) is preferred because cut seed is vulnerable to rot before emergence. In Africa, whole seed is standard for smallholders. Large-scale growers sometimes cut seed but with careful curing (24–48 hours at 15–20°C).

Seed size

Ideal seed tuber: 30–60 g (about the size of a hen's egg). Smaller seed has fewer eyes and lower vigour. Larger seed wastes money.

Seed rate

At 30–60 g/tuber and 40,000 plants/ha, you need 1,200–2,400 kg of seed per hectare. At $0.40–0.80/kg, that is $500–2,000 per hectare just for seed — the biggest cost line.

The rule: seed is 40–50% of potato cost. Buy certified seed from a licensed producer. Saving $200 on seed can cost you $2,000 in lost yield.
Lesson 2

Chitting — Sprouting Before Planting

In this lesson
  • Understand why chitting speeds up emergence
  • Chit correctly
  • Handle chitted seed carefully

What chitting does

Chitting is sprouting seed tubers before planting in a cool, light, airy place. It gives the crop a 7–14 day head start and improves uniformity. Especially valuable in short-season conditions.

How to chit

  1. 3–4 weeks before planting, place seed tubers in a single layer in shallow trays or boxes
  2. Keep in a cool (10–15°C), well-lit, ventilated space
  3. Light exposure prevents long white sprouts — you want short, dark green sprouts
  4. By planting time, each tuber should have 1–3 short (1–2 cm) green sprouts

Handling

Chitted seed is fragile. Plant gently, sprout upwards, without knocking off the sprouts. If sprouts break off, a new sprout will form but 7–10 days are lost.

Lesson 3

Land Preparation and Ridging

In this lesson
  • Prepare a fine, loose seedbed
  • Build ridges at the correct dimensions
  • Incorporate organic matter and fertiliser correctly

Primary preparation

Plough or hoe to 25–30 cm. Harrow to a fine tilth. Remove stones and previous crop residue. Loose soil is essential — tubers cannot expand in compacted soil.

Fertiliser

Incorporate at planting:

  • Compound NPK (e.g. 17-17-17): 400–600 kg/ha
  • DAP: 200 kg/ha if phosphorus is deficient
  • Organic: 10–20 t/ha of well-rotted manure if available

Ridging

Ridges should be 30–40 cm high when complete, spaced 75–90 cm apart (measured centre to centre). Plant seed on the ridge or in furrows that will become ridges as hilling progresses.

Lesson 4

Spacing and Plant Population

In this lesson
  • Space for the correct plant population
  • Adjust spacing for seed size and market
  • Calculate seed rate

Standard spacing

  • Between ridges: 75–90 cm
  • Within ridge: 25–30 cm
  • Plant population: ~40,000 plants/ha

Tighter spacing

Some growers use 60 × 20 cm for higher plant population (~65,000 plants/ha) and higher yield, at the cost of more seed per hectare. Only worthwhile if seed is cheap or you are growing for the seed market.

Looser spacing

For large table potatoes (baker's market), space 90 × 35 cm (~30,000 plants/ha). Individual tubers get bigger. Total yield drops but price per kg rises.

Seed rate calculation

At 40,000 plants/ha and 50 g average seed weight, you need 2,000 kg/ha. Adjust up or down based on seed size.

Lesson 5

Planting Depth and Timing

In this lesson
  • Plant at the correct depth
  • Time planting for cool weather and reliable water
  • Avoid heat stress at tuber initiation

Depth

Plant seed tubers 8–12 cm deep, on the ridge or in a furrow that will be ridged. Too shallow and tubers green; too deep and emergence is delayed.

Timing

Plant when soil is cool and moisture is reliable. In highland Africa:

  • Long rains planting: March–April
  • Short rains planting: August–September
  • Irrigated off-season: any cool period

Avoid planting into the hottest months. Tuber initiation happens 4–6 weeks after emergence; if that coincides with heat, yield is reduced sharply.

The cool-season rule

If you can only plant in the hot season, plant at high altitude (over 1,800 m) or use irrigation to keep soil temperature down. Otherwise wait for the cool season.

Knowledge check — Module 2

1. What is the ideal seed tuber size?

Correct: 30–60 g. Smaller has few eyes; larger wastes money.

2. Why chit seed potatoes?

Correct: 7–14 day head start. Especially valuable in short-season conditions.

3. What is the correct planting depth for potato?

Correct: 8–12 cm. Shallower greens tubers; deeper delays emergence.

4. What proportion of potato production cost is seed?

Correct: 40–50%. The single biggest cost line.

5. Why should chitted seed be handled gently?

Correct: sprouts break easily. Handle carefully, sprout upwards.
