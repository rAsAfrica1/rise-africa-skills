10. Feeding Systems and Cost Control

Feed is 60-70% of cost. Every point of FCR you improve is profit.

Module 10 of 12 · Guinea Fowl and Quail Farming

Feed cost is everything

Feed is 60-70% of the cost of raising guinea fowl and quail. A farmer who masters feed cost makes twice the profit of one who buys feed blindly.

Home-mixing requires a $20-30 scale. "A bucket of this and a bucket of that" gives an unbalanced ration. Weigh every ingredient. If you cannot weigh, buy commercial.

Reducing feed cost

  • Buy in bulk - 500 kg bags save 15-20% vs 50 kg
  • Store properly - sealed, off ground, dry, rodent-proof
  • Free-range guinea fowl - cuts feed cost 30-50%
  • Feed at fixed times - less waste than free-choice
  • Fill feeders 2/3 - less spillage
  • Use nipple drinkers - dry feed, less waste
  • Grow your own maize and soya if you have land
  • Plant fodder trees (moringa, leucaena) for guinea fowl greens
  • Cull poor layers - a hen not laying eats the same as one that is

Water quality

  • Fresh, clean water always available.
  • Change drinkers twice daily for quail, once daily for guinea fowl.
  • Water consumption: guinea 200-300 ml/day, quail 60-100 ml/day.
  • Add electrolytes during heat stress (above 30 C).
  • Never let drinkers grow algae - quail disease thrives in dirty water.

The cost-per-bird rule: Know your cost per bird to market, to the cent. If your guinea fowl costs more than $16 to raise, you are losing money on every bird.
