6. Harvest, Storage and Selling

Two weeks of drying decides whether your potatoes store for 6 months or rot in 6 weeks.

Module 6 of 6 · Potato Farming for Profit

When to harvest

  • Vines (leaves and stems) have died back and turned brown
  • Skins are set - you cannot rub them off with your thumb
  • 90-120 days after planting, depending on variety
  • For fresh market early harvest: 75-90 days (skins not set, sell quickly)

For storage, wait for full maturity. For early market price, harvest at 75-90 days and sell immediately.

Harvesting technique

  1. Stop watering 2 weeks before harvest - this sets the skins.
  2. Cut vines (optional) 7-10 days before harvest.
  3. Dig carefully with a fork - damaged tubers do not store.
  4. Lift tubers and leave on the soil surface for 1-2 hours to dry.
  5. Move to a shaded area for 10-14 days of curing.
  6. Do not wash potatoes destined for storage.
  7. Sort out damaged, green, or diseased tubers immediately.

Never expose harvested potatoes to direct sun. Sun turns them green - green potatoes are toxic (solanine) and unsaleable.

MethodDurationConditions

Storage rules:

  • Never store in sealed bags - potatoes need airflow.
  • Keep in darkness - light makes them green.
  • Remove any rotting tubers weekly.
  • Do not store near onions or apples - they release ethylene that makes potatoes sprout.

Price timing

Potato prices drop at main harvest (everyone plants for the same window). Store properly and sell 2-4 months later for 1.5-2x the price.

The storage rule: Cured potatoes store 4-6 months. Sell into the lean season and double your price. Never sell straight off the field.
