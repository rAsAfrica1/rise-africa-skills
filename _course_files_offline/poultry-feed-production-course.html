Practical Reference

Feed manufacturing is a margin business. Your profit is not made by mixing ingredients â it is made by controlling three variables: the cost of your raw materials per kilogram of finished feed, the consistency of your formula, and the reliability of your supply to farmers who pay on time. The reference material below is built for operators who are already running a mixing line, or who intend to within the next 90 days. Use it as a bench tool, not as theory.

Two things separate a feed business that survives from one that closes within 18 months. First, batch discipline â every batch is weighed, recorded and sampled. Second, cash discipline â no bag leaves the yard without a deposit. The tables below give you real benchmark figures to plan against.

â  Safety Warning â Feed, Moulds and Chemicals

This is a health-critical operation. Read before you buy another bag of maize.

  • Aflatoxin: Maize and groundnut cake stored above 14% moisture can develop aflatoxin, a known human carcinogen that passes into eggs and meat. Never mix visibly mouldy or musty-smelling grain into feed to "save money". Reject the load.
  • Premix handling: Vitamin-mineral premixes are concentrates. Wear a dust mask and gloves when weighing. Never handle premix in the same area where you eat or where children play.
  • Medicated feed: If you add coccidiostats or antibiotics, follow the withdrawal period exactly. Selling medicated feed to layers producing eggs for human consumption is illegal in most African markets and dangerous.
  • Milling dust: Grain dust is explosive and a respiratory irritant. Mill in a ventilated space, never near an open flame, and fit a dust collection bag.
  • Machinery: Isolate and lock the hammer mill before clearing a blockage. Never put a hand into a running mill or mixer.
  • Record keeping: Keep a batch log of every ingredient, weight, supplier and date. If a problem appears, you need to trace it within 24 hours.

Quick Reference Checklist

  1. Test moisture before purchase. A cheap moisture meter pays for itself in one rejected load. Target 12-14% for maize.
  2. Weigh every ingredient, every batch. Volume scoops drift; a 2% error in premix is a 2% error in bird performance.
  3. Keep a written batch record. Date, formula, ingredient weights, supplier, mixer operator, batch number. This is your defence when a farmer complains.
  4. Grind to spec. Check your screen monthly. Replace worn screens â coarse feed separates in transit.
  5. Add oil and premix last, after a dry blend. Ten to fifteen minutes of dry mixing first prevents hot spots and overdosing.
  6. Store feed off the floor on pallets, in a dry, ventilated room. Use within 4-6 weeks. Never stack bags against a damp wall.
  7. Price with a margin, not a guess. Know your cost per 50 kg bag before you quote a farmer. Recalculate every time ingredient prices move.
  8. Collect a deposit on every order. Even 30% protects your working capital. Feed sold entirely on credit is a grant, not a business.
