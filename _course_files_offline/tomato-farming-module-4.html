4. Staking, Pruning and Feeding

An unstaked tomato plant produces half the fruit and rots the other half.

Module 4 of 6 · Tomato Farming for Profit

Staking - why it matters

Unstaked tomato plants lie on the ground. Fruit touches soil and rots. Airflow is poor, disease spreads fast, and yields drop 40-60%.

MethodCost/ha
Individual wooden stakes (2 m)$400-800
Stakes + horizontal strings$500-1,000
Florida weave (posts + twine)$200-400

Pruning

  • Remove suckers between the main stem and leaf branch.
  • Remove leaves below the first fruit cluster once fruit is set.
  • Keep 1-2 main stems per plant.
  • Prune in the morning, dry weather.

Never let flowering plants dry out. One dry day at flowering drops 20-40% of potential fruit.
