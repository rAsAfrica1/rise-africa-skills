12. Business Planning, Records and Scaling

Every goat farm needs numbers. This module is how to run a real business, not a hobby.

Module 12 of 12 · Goat Farming for Profit

Why plan and record

Without numbers, you guess. With numbers, you know which does make money, which buyers pay best, and how much you can grow. Most small livestock farms fail not from disease, but from lack of financial control.

The six records to keep

RecordWhat to writeHow often
Flock registerDate, source, tag number, breed, sex, costEvery addition
Breeding registerDoe ID, buck ID, breeding date, kidding date, kids born/aliveEvery breeding

Sales registerDate, buyer, kg or number, price, paidEvery sale
Expense registerLabour, feed, vet, housing, transportEvery purchase

Goats are seasonal. Plan for 2-3 months of negative cash flow. Keep a reserve equal to 2 months of feed.

Never borrow to buy goats you have not already sold. Debt on unsold stock destroys cash flow. Debt with a confirmed buyer is safe.

Scaling rules

Scale up only when:

  • Current flock profitable for 6 consecutive months
  • Mortality under 5% (adults) and 10% (kids)
  • Kidding rate above 150%
  • Buyer for the extra goats confirmed
  • Housing and feed storage ready before the new flock arrives
  • Cash reserve covers 2 months of feed

Records for decision-making

After one year, your records will tell you:

  • Which does to keep and which to cull
  • Which months have best market prices
  • Which buyers pay best and which to drop
  • Whether your feed cost per kg gain is competitive
  • Whether you should add dairy, meat processing, or breeding stock sales

Long-term strategy

StageFocusYear
1Small flock, learn management, breed from within1
2Grow to 30-50 does, add a good buck, build buyers2-3
3Add value chain (dairy, meat processing, breeding stock)4-5
4Commercial scale with staff, formal contracts6+

Reinvestment priorities

  1. Better buck - the single biggest genetic gain
  2. Fodder planting - moringa, leucaena for year-round feed
  3. Water system - reliable year-round
  4. Housing upgrade - raised floors, slatted
  5. Processing equipment - if selling meat
  6. Transport - reliable delivery to buyers
The profitable goat farm rule: Focus on numbers, not animal count. 50 well-managed does earn more than 200 poorly-managed ones. Track every metric. Cull ruthlessly. Feed correctly. Sell into premium markets.
Practical exercise 12: Write a 3-year plan for your goat flock. Include: flock size per year, kidding rate target, revenue estimate, buyers, capital needed, and reinvestment plan. Review every 6 months.
