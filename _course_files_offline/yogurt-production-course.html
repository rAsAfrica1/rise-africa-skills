This is a health, food and chemical safety notice. Read it before every spraying, storage or processing activity.

  • Agrochemicals: Always read and follow the product label. Wear gloves, boots, long sleeves, eye protection and a mask. Never eat, drink or smoke while handling pesticides. Observe the pre-harvest interval (PHI) â the waiting period before harvest â without exception. Store chemicals in a locked, labelled cabinet away from food, feed and children.
  • Food safety: Wash hands and equipment with clean water and soap. Keep raw and cooked products separate. Use food-grade containers only â never pesticide drums or fuel containers for water, milk or grain.
  • Aflatoxin and mould: Do not eat or sell mouldy grain or groundnuts. Dry properly and discard contaminated lots. Aflatoxin is a serious liver carcinogen and cannot be removed by cooking.
  • Milk and meat: Use clean, cool chains. Do not sell milk from sick animals or meat from animals that died of unknown causes.
  • Emergency: If pesticide touches skin or eyes, rinse with clean water for at least 15 minutes and seek medical help immediately. Keep the product label or container for the health worker.
  • Business risk: A single food safety incident can destroy a brand, close a market and end a contract. Safety is not a cost â it is market access.

Quick Reference Checklist

  1. Know your buyer before you plant. Confirm volume, quality grade, price mechanism and delivery date in writing.
  2. Cost it properly. Include seed, fertiliser, labour, transport, packaging, finance cost and your own time. If the margin is thin on paper, it is thinner in reality.
  3. Record everything. Keep a simple record book of inputs, labour, yields, sales and prices. Records are what lenders and off-takers trust.
  4. Cut post-harvest loss first. Proper drying, hermetic storage and clean handling are the highest-return investments on most farms.
  5. Aggregate to gain power. Sell through a co-operative or group to meet volume contracts and negotiate better prices.
  6. Protect your health and your brand. Follow all label instructions, pre-harvest intervals and food hygiene rules. Train every worker.
  7. Manage cash flow, not just profit. Match loan repayments and input purchases to your harvest and sales calendar.
  8. Diversify your market. Do not depend on one buyer, one crop or one season. Add a second product line or a second outlet.
