Practical Reference

This reference block is for the grown-up who holds the phone and the young entrepreneur who holds the questions. Claude is a text tool. It has no eyes on your street, no knowledge of today's tomato price at Mbare Musika, and no way to verify what it just told you. That is not a weakness you fight â it is a business fact you plan around. The child who learns to ask well, check hard, and sell small will out-earn the child who only learns to type fast. Everything below is built for that.

Table 1: What a "Fat Question" Looks Like in a Real African Micro-Business

The difference between a thin question and a fat question is the difference between a hobby and a till. Thin questions are free to ask and worthless to answer. Fat questions cost the child a minute of thinking and return a plan they can act on before sunset.

Business idea Thin question (thin answer) Fat question (usable answer) What it unlocks
Tomato resale "Tell me about tomatoes." "I buy 20kg of tomatoes at Mbare Musika on Saturday morning and resell in my street. What spoils first, and how do I sort them so I lose less than 3kg?" A sorting rule and a loss budget
Air-time top-up "How do I make money?" "I have US$5 float and 15 neighbours who buy airtime weekly. What price per unit keeps me profitable if my supplier charges 4% commission?" A price card and a margin check
Handmade soap "Is soap a good business?" "List the 5 cheapest ingredients for 20 bars of bar soap in Harare, what each costs, and which one I can substitute if it is out of stock." A shopping list with backups
Phone charging "Charging business tips." "I charge 8 phones a day at US.50. My electricity is US$15/month. What is my monthly profit, and how many phones must I charge to break even?" Break-even number on the wall
Maize meal repacking "Maize prices." "If a 50kg bag costs US$18 and I repack into 5kg bags, what must I charge per bag to make US$6 profit per 50kg after bags cost US.15 each?" A price that survives the maths

Source: Question framing principles from the rise AFRICA skills "Talking to Claude" module (Modules 1 and 4); pricing examples use 2024 Zimbabwe informal retail price bands reported by the Zimbabwe National Statistics Agency (ZIMSTAT) Consumer Price Index bulletins.

Quick Reference Checklist

Print this. Stick it where the phone is charged. Run it before every session.

  1. Grown-up unlocks, child types. The account belongs to an adult; the questions belong to the learner. Confirm the free tier's current usage limit before you start â limits change.
  2. Write the fat question on paper first. Who, what, how many, how much, by when. If it fits in four words, it is probably too thin.
  3. Ask for simpler language, then for a local language. "Say it again, simpler please." "Now in Shona please." Understanding is the deliverable, not the answer.
  4. Circle anything Claude stated as a fact about money, health, law or chemicals. Those four categories get verified every single time.
  5. Verify with two independent real-world sources. Two shops, or one shop plus one market trader. Write both prices down.
  6. Do the maths by hand. Cost + your time + a fair margin = price. If the hand calculation and Claude disagree, the hand calculation wins.
  7. Keep one paper record book. Date, item, money in, money out. No customer personal data ever goes into the AI chat.
  8. Sell one thing to one real customer before adding anything new. One small thing done well beats five things done badly.
