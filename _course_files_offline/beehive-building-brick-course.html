Practical Reference

Fixed masonry hives are a capital asset, not a consumable. Every rand, kwacha, shilling or cedi you sink into fired brick or stabilised block is recovered over a 15â25 year working life, provided the cavity geometry, thermal performance and ant defence are right from day one. This reference block gives you the numbers, the diagram and the decision rules you need before you commit labour and material. Treat it as the commercial annex to the twelve modules above.

Before you pour a single foundation, confirm three numbers with your national beekeeping authority: cavity width, top-bar spacing and entrance dimensions. A hive built to the wrong species spec is scrap. In East Africa, Apis mellifera scutellata colonies run smaller than European races, and a cavity sized for a European hive will leave the colony struggling to defend and thermoregulate. In West Africa, the same principle applies to A. m. adansonii. Get the spec in writing, then build.

Two levers move payback fastest. First, colonisation rate: a well-sited, well-baited hive placed at the right height and orientation colonises in one season, not three. Second, price: selling into a branded retail or export channel rather than a roadside bucket can double your per-kilo price. Neither lever requires more bricks. Both require market work.

â  SAFETY WARNING â Cement, Lime and Smoke

Wet cement is strongly alkaline and causes chemical burns and irreversible eye damage. Always wear sealed goggles, nitrile or rubber gloves, and a dust mask when mixing mortar, cutting block or rendering. Never mix cement barefoot or in open sandals. Wash any skin contact with clean water immediately and seek medical attention for eye contact.

When smoking hives for harvest, use only clean, dry, untreated plant material. Burning plastic, painted timber, rubber or chemically treated wood releases toxic fumes into the honey and the colony. Never harvest honey from a hive treated with unauthorised pesticides. Honey sold with chemical residue can be rejected at market and is a food safety offence.

Quick Reference Checklist

  1. Confirm the cavity spec in writing with your national beekeeping authority before buying a single brick. Species, cavity width, top-bar spacing and entrance size must all match.
  2. Site the hive on a level, well-drained pad facing away from prevailing wind, with morning sun and afternoon shade. Never place it in a flood line or on bare rock that radiates heat.
  3. Pour a reinforced base plate and slope it 1:20 to the rear for drainage. A hive that holds water will rot its comb and lose the colony.
  4. Install an oil-filled ant barrier at the base and inspect it monthly in the dry season. Ants are the single biggest cause of failed fixed-hive apiaries in Africa.
  5. Fit a 150 mm roof overhang on all sides with a galvanised sheet. Rain entering the cavity is a slow, silent colony killer.
  6. Verify ventilation with a screened top vent and a low entrance. Thermal mass keeps the hive cool, but only if hot air can escape.
  7. Record every hive in a ledger: date built, date colonised, yield, treatment dates. Data is how you price and scale.
  8. Register with your national beekeeping authority and keep a food-safety record of every harvest. Buyers and lenders now ask for it.
