11. Record Keeping and Business Management

Every profitable sheep farm runs on numbers. Here is how to run a real business, not a hobby.

Module 11 of 12 · Sheep Farming for Profit

Why records matter

Without records, you guess. With records, you know which ewes make money, which buyers pay best, and how much you can grow. Most small sheep farms fail not from disease, but from lack of financial control.

The six records to keep

RecordWhat to writeHow often
Flock registerDate, source, tag number, breed, sex, costEvery addition
Breeding registerEwe ID, ram ID, breeding date, lambing date, lambs born/aliveEvery breeding
Feed registerDate, type, kg, cost per kgDaily
Health registerVaccination, deworming, treatments, deaths, symptomsEvery event
Sales registerDate, buyer, kg or number, price, paidEvery sale
Expense registerLabour, feed, vet, housing, transportEvery purchase

Sheep farming is seasonal in cash flow. Plan for 2-3 months of negative cash flow. Keep a reserve equal to 2 months of feed.

Records for decision-making

After one year, your records will tell you:

  • Which ewes to keep and which to cull
  • Which months have best market prices
  • Which buyers pay best and which to drop
  • Whether your feed cost per kg gain is competitive
  • Whether you should add wool, dairy, or meat processing
  • Your cost per kg of lamb produced

Simple bookkeeping

Use one notebook. Two columns:

DateCash inCash outBalanceNotes
1 Jan-$200-$200Feed
15 Jan$300-$100Cull ewe sold
1 Feb-$150-$50Vaccines

At the end of every month, total the columns. Cash in - cash out = net profit (or loss). Review at month-end.

Flock valuation

Value your flock at least once a year:

Track this yearly. Flock value should grow 15-30% per year if managed well.

Compliance and tax

  • Register the business once you exceed household needs.
  • VAT only above the threshold in your country.
  • Food safety certificates needed for selling meat to restaurants and shops.
  • Animal movement permits for cross-district sales.
  • Keep receipts for all input purchases - they are deductible.
The records rule: A farm without records is a hobby. Ten minutes a day of bookkeeping tells you which sheep make money, which buyers pay best, and which costs you can cut.
Practical exercise 11: Start a monthly P&L notebook. Record all revenue and expenses for 30 days. Calculate net profit at month-end. Do this for 3 months to see trends.
