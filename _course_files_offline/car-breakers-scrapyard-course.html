â  SAFETY WARNING â Aflatoxin & Chemical Handling

Health risk: Poorly stored maize, groundnuts, and other grains can develop aflatoxins â toxic compounds produced by moulds. Aflatoxin exposure is linked to liver cancer, stunting in children, and immune suppression. In sub-Saharan Africa, up to 40% of staple crops may exceed safe limits in bad seasons (Source: WHO, 2018, Aflatoxin Control in Africa).

Never sell or consume visibly mouldy grain, or grain with a musty smell. Do not attempt to "dilute" contaminated grain by mixing it with clean grain â this spreads the toxin.

Chemical safety: If using pesticides (e.g., for storage pests), always wear gloves, a nose mask, and long sleeves. Store chemicals in original labelled containers, away from food, children, and livestock. Follow the pre-harvest interval and never use banned substances such as dichlorvos on food crops.

Action: Test suspicious batches with rapid aflatoxin test kits (available through local agro-dealers) before sale or consumption. Report suspected contamination to your local public health or agriculture extension officer.

Quick Reference Checklist

  • Assess your loss baseline: Weigh or estimate losses at each stage (harvest, handling, storage, transport, market) for one full season before investing in any intervention.
  • Prioritise the biggest leak: If storage is your largest loss point, invest in hermetic bags or improved silos first â not in a cold room you don't need.
  • Train staff on handling: Simple grading, gentle offloading, and shade during transport can cut losses by 5â15% at almost no cost.
  • Use crates, not sacks, for perishables: Plastic crates reduce bruising and allow stacking; they pay for themselves within one or two market cycles.
  • Monitor moisture content: For grains, target <13% moisture before storage. Buy a moisture meter â it is one of the highest-ROI tools you can own.
  • Test for aflatoxin: Before selling or consuming stored maize or groundnuts, use a rapid test kit. Never sell contaminated grain.
  • Keep records: Track purchase price, loss quantity, and sale price per batch. This data is what lenders and investors want to see.
  • Join or form a collective: Aggregating volumes with neighbouring farmers or SMEs lowers per-unit cost of storage, transport, and testing.
