⚠ SAFETY WARNING â BATTERIES, PROPELLERS AND SPRAY CHEMICALS

Lithium-polymer (LiPo) drone batteries can catch fire if punctured, over-charged, or charged while swollen. Never charge a LiPo unattended, never charge it inside a house or vehicle, and never charge it above 4.2 V per cell. Store at 3.8 V per cell (storage charge) if unused for more than a week. In Zimbabwe, Zambia and most of SADC, LiPo fires are treated as Class B/C fires â a standard water extinguisher will not stop the reaction. Keep a bucket of dry sand nearby.

Propellers spin at 5,000â10,000 RPM and can cause deep lacerations. Remove propellers before working on motors or firmware. Never fly within 30 m of people who are not part of your flight crew.

If your drone is used for agricultural spraying, the chemical being sprayed is a pesticide. Wear gloves, goggles and a respirator when handling concentrate. Observe the pre-harvest interval (PHI) on the label â for example, 14 days for many pyrethroids on leafy vegetables. Spraying without a valid pesticide applicator licence is illegal in Zimbabwe (Fertilizer, Farm Feeds and Remedies Act, Chapter 18:12) and in South Africa (Act 36 of 1947).

Quick Reference Checklist

  1. Register the drone. In Zimbabwe, all drones above 250 g must be registered with the Civil Aviation Authority of Zimbabwe (CAAZ) before first flight. Keep the certificate on your phone.
  2. Check the NOTAM and airspace. Before every flight, check for Notices to Airmen and confirm you are not inside a 5 km radius of an aerodrome. Use the free CAAZ or SACAA airspace map.
  3. Confirm line of sight. You must be able to see the drone with your own eyes at all times â no flying behind trees or buildings. This is a legal requirement, not a suggestion.
  4. Pre-flight the battery. Check for swelling, dents or a sweet chemical smell. If any are present, retire the battery. Log the cycle count.
  5. Set the return-to-home altitude. Set it at least 20 m above the tallest obstacle in your flight area â trees, silos, power lines.
  6. Fly the mission, not the drone. Plan the grid pattern on the ground first. A 70% front overlap and 60% side overlap is the minimum for a usable orthomosaic.
  7. Back up the SD card the same day. Data lost is money lost. Copy to two devices before you sleep.
  8. Log every flight. Date, time, location, duration, battery used, purpose, and any incident. This log is your evidence if a regulator or insurer asks questions.
