Practical Reference

This reference section is built for one purpose: to help you turn your business plan from a document into a financing and operating tool. In African agribusiness, most plans fail not because the idea is weak, but because the numbers are unbankable, the market evidence is thin, and the risk section is copy-pasted. Use the diagram, tables, and checklist below as working instruments. Print them, fill them in, and attach them to your plan as annexes. Lenders and grant panels in Southern and East Africa consistently reward plans that show unit economics, seasonality, and a realistic path to first revenue.

These are planning benchmarks, not guarantees. Use them to sanity-check your own assumptions, then replace with your quoted local input prices and confirmed buyer prices. Always build in a 10-15% contingency on inputs.

If your business plan involves pesticides, herbicides, veterinary drugs, fumigants, or food processing, you are handling substances that can cause acute poisoning, chronic illness, and contamination of food and water. Before you write a single line of your operations plan:

  • Never store pesticides, veterinary medicines, or fuel in the same room as food, feed, or seed. Cross-contamination is a legal and health hazard.
  • Always follow the label and the Safety Data Sheet (SDS). Wear gloves, boots, eye protection, and a respirator when mixing or spraying. Never mix with bare hands.
  • Respect pre-harvest intervals and withdrawal periods. Selling produce or meat that still contains residues can close your market access permanently and harm consumers.
  • Dispose of empty containers and expired chemicals through authorised channels - never burn them, never reuse them for water or food.
  • Train every worker on safe handling, emergency washing, and reporting. Keep a first-aid kit and a written emergency contact list on site.
  • Check national registration. Only use products registered by your national regulator (e.g. the Pest Control Products Board in Kenya, the Registrar of Pesticides in Zimbabwe, or your country's equivalent). Unregistered products are illegal and uninsurable.

Quick Reference Checklist

  1. Name your buyer before you name your product. Write down at least three confirmed buyers, their volumes, and the price they last paid. Verbal "maybe" is not evidence.
  2. Build a unit economics sheet. Calculate cost per kg, per bird, or per litre, and your gross margin. If you cannot state your break-even volume, your plan is not yet financeable.
  3. Separate capex from working capital. Fund at least one full production cycle of working capital before you spend on equipment.
  4. Produce a 12-month cash flow, month by month. Show the lowest cash point and how you will cover it. Lenders look here first.
  5. List your top five risks with a mitigation for each. Drought, pest outbreak, price collapse, theft, and currency or input price shocks are standard for African agribusiness.
  6. Confirm all permits, registrations, and food safety requirements before your first sale - including any chemical handling or processing licence.
  7. Set 90-day milestones with named owners. Break the plan into what happens in weeks 1-4, 5-8, and 9-12, with a measurable KPI for each.
  8. Review actuals against plan monthly. Update assumptions, not just the story. A plan that never changes is a plan nobody is using.
