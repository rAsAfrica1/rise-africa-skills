â  SAFETY WARNING â Agro-Chemicals and Food Handling

Pesticides, fumigants, and veterinary drugs are hazardous. Misuse causes acute poisoning, chronic illness, and market rejection of your produce. Follow these non-negotiables:

  • Always wear the label-specified personal protective equipment (PPE): gloves, mask/respirator, goggles, long sleeves, boots. Never spray in shorts or bare feet.
  • Respect pre-harvest intervals (PHI). Do not harvest or sell treated crops before the labelled waiting period expires. Buyers test for residues; rejection is total loss.
  • Never store pesticides in food containers, near food, or in living areas. Store locked, labelled, and separate.
  • Do not re-use pesticide containers for water or food. Triple-rinse and puncture containers, then dispose per local regulations.
  • Wash hands, face, and contaminated clothing immediately after handling. Never eat, drink, or smoke during application.
  • If exposure occurs: move to fresh air, remove contaminated clothing, rinse skin/eyes with clean water for 15 minutes, and take the product label to the nearest health facility immediately.
  • Use only registered products approved by your national regulator, and only at label rates. Over-dosing does not improve results â it increases residue and cost.

Quick Reference Checklist

  1. Measure your actual loss. Weigh or count produce at harvest, at aggregation, and at sale. You cannot fix what you do not measure.
  2. Set a moisture target for grains (typically <13% for safe storage) and buy or borrow a moisture meter. This single tool prevents mould and aflatoxin risk.
  3. Lock in at least one forward contract per season with a buyer or off-taker, even if partial. It stabilises cash flow and justifies input investment.
  4. Track your top three cost lines monthly (raw material, energy, labour) against the benchmarks in Table 2. Investigate any line that drifts more than 5 percentage points.
  5. Invest in aggregation quality â grading tables, crates, and shade â before investing in processing machinery. Quality at intake determines price at exit.
  6. Maintain a preventive maintenance log for every machine. Downtime during harvest is the most expensive downtime you will ever have.
  7. Keep records that a lender will accept: sales receipts, input invoices, production logs, and a simple cash-flow sheet. Records are collateral in practice.
  8. Review safety compliance (PPE, PHI, storage, disposal) at least once per month and after every new chemical is introduced to the farm or facility.
