â  SAFETY WARNING â FOOD CONTACT AND CHEMICALS

Only use waxes and sanitisers explicitly labelled food-grade and approved for the crop you are coating. Never substitute industrial paraffin, furniture polish, car wax, or unlabelled "shine" products â they are not safe for human consumption and can carry carcinogenic residues. Keep all wax concentrates, sanitisers, and cleaning chemicals in locked, labelled storage away from produce, food, and children. Wear gloves and eye protection when mixing concentrates, and never mix chemicals in unmarked drink bottles. Use only potable water for washing and wax emulsions; contaminated water is the fastest route to a rejected export consignment. If a worker shows skin, eye, or breathing reactions, stop work, move them to fresh air, rinse affected areas, and seek medical help. Record every batch number, dilution, and application date â traceability is a legal and commercial requirement, not paperwork.

Quick Reference Checklist

  • Confirm the buyer's spec first. Before you buy wax, get the target crop, wax type, gloss level, and residue limits in writing from your buyer or export agent.
  • Check the label, every time. Confirm food-grade status, approved crop list, dilution range, and expiry date on the container you are actually opening.
  • Pre-cool before you wax. Waxing warm fruit traps field heat and accelerates decay â remove field heat first.
  • Wash with potable water and a registered sanitiser. Test water quality and change it on a fixed schedule, not when it "looks dirty".
  • Dry to no free moisture. A wet surface dilutes the emulsion and gives an uneven, streaky coat.
  • Calibrate your dilution and application rate. Weigh or measure the concentrate; do not eyeball it. Log the batch.
  • Run a 24-hour spot test. Coat a small sample, hold it under your normal storage conditions, and check for off-flavour, cracking, or uneven gloss before a full run.
  • Keep the cold chain unbroken after waxing. Wax buys you days, not weeks â storage temperature still decides final quality.
  • Record everything. Batch number, date, operator, dilution, crop lot, and destination. This is your defence in any quality dispute.
