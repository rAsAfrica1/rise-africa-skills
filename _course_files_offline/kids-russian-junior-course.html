Privet! Russian

Cyrillic is a secret code, and there are only three kinds of letter to crack.

Ages 7–128 modules 🪨 Starts with no device

Russian writing looks like someone wrote English in a mirror during an earthquake. It is not. It is a code with exactly three sorts of letter: friends you already know, tricksters that pretend to be English letters and are not, and brand new shapes nobody can fake. Crack those three groups and you can read Russian words out loud in about a week.

â  Safety Warning: Reading Medicine Labels and Chemicals

If you use Cyrillic reading skills on medicine boxes, pesticide containers, or industrial chemical labels, a wrong reading can cause serious harm. Cyrillic letters that look like English are the most dangerous â a trickster letter can turn a safe dose into an overdose.

  • Never rely on a single reading. Confirm with a pharmacist, agronomist, or bilingual speaker before use.
  • Store agricultural chemicals away from food, water, and children, as required by Zimbabwe's Environmental Management Agency (EMA) and Kenya's Pest Control Products Board (PCPB).
  • Wear gloves and eye protection when handling concentrates, even if the label is only partly understood.
  • If poisoning is suspected, contact your local poison control centre immediately. In Zimbabwe, call the Medicines Control Authority hotline; in Kenya, call the Kenya Poisons Information Centre.
  • Do not translate medical dosage instructions alone. Dosage errors are the leading cause of preventable medicine harm in low-resource settings (WHO, 2023).
