â ï¸ SAFETY WARNING â Chemical and Veterinary Inputs

Agro-chemicals and veterinary drugs are business inputs, not casual purchases. Misuse destroys your margin, your livestock, and your health.

  • Never use crop pesticides on livestock or in poultry houses. Different chemistry, different toxicity, different rules.
  • Always wear gloves, mask, and boots when handling or mixing chemicals. Wash thoroughly after use.
  • Respect withdrawal periods for all veterinary drugs. Selling meat or eggs with residues gets you banned from markets and can poison consumers.
  • Store chemicals in a locked, ventilated place away from feed, water, and children. Never decant into food or drink containers.
  • Dispose of empty containers by triple-rinsing and puncturing. Never reuse them for water or food.
  • If a worker shows signs of poisoning â dizziness, vomiting, blurred vision â stop work, move to fresh air, and seek medical help immediately. Bring the product label.
  • Buy only from licensed agro-dealers. Counterfeit products are a direct financial and health risk.

Quick Reference Checklist

  1. Know your numbers before you spend. Calculate cost per bird, per tonne, or per litre before committing capital. If you cannot state your break-even, you are gambling, not farming.
  2. Lock in one input price. Negotiate a bulk feed or fertiliser price with a supplier and put it in writing. A 5â8% input saving is often the difference between profit and loss.
  3. Record daily, not monthly. Feed use, mortality, milk yield, and sales must be logged the same day. Retrospective records hide theft and waste.
  4. Vaccinate on schedule. A missed Newcastle or Gumboro vaccination can wipe out an entire flock in days. Set phone reminders and stock vaccines before the cycle starts.
  5. Secure one off-take agreement. A signed contract with a hotel, processor, or co-operative reduces price risk and strengthens your loan application.
  6. Cut post-harvest loss first. Hermetic storage, crates, and shade are cheaper than producing more. Recovering 10% of a harvest is often more profitable than a 10% yield increase.
  7. Separate business and household money. Open a dedicated account. Pay yourself a fixed salary. Reinvest the rest. This single discipline builds credit history.
  8. Insure or self-insure. If formal livestock or crop insurance is unavailable, build a 10% reserve fund from every sale cycle to absorb shocks.
