5. Breeding and Whelping

From mating to weaning in 10 weeks. This module covers the reproductive cycle, whelping, and puppy care.

Module 5 of 12 · Dog Breeding for Profit

Reproductive cycle of the female

StageDurationSigns
Proestrus (heat start)7-10 daysVulva swelling, bloody discharge, not receptive
Oestrus (mating time)5-9 daysDischarge lightens, bitch receptive to male
Dioestrus (after ovulation)60-90 daysReceptive ends, pregnancy begins
Anoestrus (rest)4-5 monthsSexually inactive

Full cycle: every 5-8 months (average 6 months). Most bitches cycle twice per year.

Breeding cycle - a 12-month calendar

MonthEvent
Month 1Health checks, hip/eye tests, titres, deworming
Month 2Wait for heat
Month 3Heat cycle - monitor progesterone if possible
Month 3.5Mating days (best 2-5 days after ovulation)
Month 4-5Pregnancy - whelping box ready, feeding adjusted
Month 5.5Whelping
Month 6-7.5Puppy care, weaning, vaccination
Month 7.5-8Puppies sold or placed
Month 8+Rest - no breeding

Determining the right day to mate

MethodAccuracyCost
Day-counting from first red dischargeModerateFree
Vaginal smear cytologyHighLow
Progesterone blood testVery highModerate - 2-3 tests
LH surge testVery highHigher

Best practice: mate 2-3 times, 48 hours apart, on days 3-5 after ovulation. If using progesterone: mating days are when progesterone is 5-15 ng/ml (typically 2-4 days after ovulation).

Mating rules

  1. Never force the mating. Both dogs must be willing.
  2. Same location, quiet, familiar. Not in a crowded kennel.
  3. Neutral ground is best - especially if the male is visiting.
  4. Hold the bitch for support - head and body.
  5. The tie (locking) lasts 5-30 minutes - do not separate them.
  6. Two matings 48 hours apart improves conception rate.
  7. Record the date, time, and male.

Pregnancy diagnosis

MethodDays post-matingAccuracy
Palpation (skilled vet)25-30 daysGood if trained
Ultrasound25-30 daysVery high
Relaxin blood test25-30 daysVery high
X-ray (count puppies)45-55 daysVery high
External signs (size, milk)50+ daysModerate

X-rays at day 55 give an exact puppy count - critical for whelping planning.

Pregnancy care by week

WeekCare
Week 1-3Normal routine, no stress, no drugs, no vaccines
Week 4-6Gradual increase in feed, moderate exercise
Week 6-7Prepare whelping box, deworm (safely) at week 6
Week 7-8Reduce exercise, increase feed, watch for nesting
Week 8-9Temperature monitoring (drops below 37.5 C 24 hours before whelping)

Whelping - three stages

Stage 1 (6-24 hours)

  • Restless, panting, nesting
  • Refuses food
  • Vomiting possible
  • Temperature drops to 36.5-37.2 C

Stage 2 (delivery of puppies)

  • Strong straining begins
  • First puppy usually within 1 hour
  • Subsequent puppies every 15-60 minutes
  • Total whelping usually 3-12 hours

Stage 3 (after each puppy)

  • Placenta passes after each puppy
  • Save the placentas (count them - should equal puppy count)

When to call the vet during whelping

  • Strong straining for over 30 minutes with no puppy
  • More than 2 hours between puppies with continuing contractions
  • Green or black discharge before first puppy
  • Bitch in obvious pain, exhausted
  • Puppy stuck in the birth canal
  • More than 4 hours total without producing a puppy
  • Bitch''s temperature rises above 39.5 C

Assisting with a whelping

  1. Wash hands, use clean gloves.
  2. Break the sac if the puppy is born still inside.
  3. Clear the airway - wipe the mouth and nose, then hold head down to drain fluid.
  4. Rub the puppy vigorously with a warm towel to stimulate breathing.
  5. If no breathing within 30 seconds - hold the puppy head-down and swing gently, or use a bulb syringe.
  6. Clamp and cut the cord 2 cm from the body, dip in iodine.
  7. Place the puppy on the bitch''s teat - colostrum is critical in the first hour.
  8. Weigh and record the puppy (typical 200-600 g depending on breed).
  9. Keep the whelping box warm (28-32 C).

Care of the bitch after whelping

  • Fresh water and food available.
  • Warm, quiet, dark whelping area.
  • Check for retained placentas - count them. Retained placenta = vet.
  • Check discharge - should be red-brown, becoming clear within 7 days.
  • Check for metritis - smelly discharge, fever, off-feed = vet.
  • Monitor milk production - swollen, hot udders = mastitis.
  • Continue feeding free-choice for 4-6 weeks.
  • Do not bathe for 7 days.
  • Minimal visitors for 2 weeks.

Puppy care by week

WeekCare
Week 0-1Warmth (28-32 C), feeding every 2 hours, daily weight check, umbilical care
Week 2Eyes open, temperature 26-28 C, continue feeding, first deworm at 2 weeks
Week 3Ears open, starting to walk, temperature 22-26 C, introduce play area
Week 4Introduce solid food (gruel), deworm at 4 weeks, first vaccinations if vet advises
Week 5-6Full weaning, deworm at 6 weeks, first vaccines (usually 6-8 weeks)
Week 7-8Second vaccines (8-10 weeks), microchip, health check, ready for sale
Week 9-12Third vaccines (12 weeks), rabies (12-16 weeks depending on country)

Weighing and monitoring

AgeExpected daily gain
Day 0-710-20% of birth weight per day
Week 2Doubled birth weight
Week 44x birth weight
Week 88-10x birth weight

Weigh puppies daily for the first 2 weeks and weekly after. A puppy not gaining weight is sick - check for:

  • Insufficient milk (bitch problem)
  • Rejection by mother
  • Infection (pneumonia, parvo)
  • Too cold (puppies cannot digest when cold)
  • Congenital defect

Common whelping emergencies

ProblemAction
Dystocia (difficult birth)Vet immediately - may need C-section
Retained placentaVet within 24 hours
Stillborn puppiesContinue whelping, call vet
Bitch with no milkBottle-feed or find surrogate, vet for hormones
Eclampsia (milk fever)Vet immediately - calcium deficiency, can kill
MastitisVet for antibiotics, keep pups feeding or bottle-feed
MetritisVet for antibiotics - infection of the uterus
HaemorrhageVet immediately - life-threatening
The vet rule: During whelping, if anything seems wrong, call the vet. Waiting 30 minutes can be the difference between a live litter and a dead one.
