Choosing a breeding ewe

  • Body condition: well-fleshed, not fat, not thin
  • Udder: two well-formed teats, no lumps, no mastitis history
  • Age: 2-5 years (peaked production)
  • Teeth: full set, no broken or missing
  • Feet: no limping, no overgrown hooves, no foot rot
  • Eyes: bright, no discharge
  • History: has she lambed successfully before?
  • Mouth: no scabs or sores (orf)

Choosing a ram

  • Body: long, deep, well-muscled
  • Testicles: two, equal, firm, not too small
  • Age: 1.5-4 years
  • Not related to your ewes
  • Sound feet and legs
  • Good lineage - from a registered breeder if possible
  • No history of disease

One ram serves 25-40 ewes. Change the ram every 2 years to avoid inbreeding.

The most profitable system for most smallholders: keep ewes at 50-75% Dorper (or another good meat breed), use a purebred ram.

Never buy from a market without quarantine. Auction sheep often carry disease. Quarantine for 3 weeks before adding to your flock.

Sexing lambs

Lambs are easy to sex at birth:

  • Males: visible scrotum below the tail, rudimentary penis sheath on the belly
  • Females: no scrotum, vulva below the tail, two teats on the belly

Selection for future productivity

When you keep ewe lambs as replacements, choose:

  • Twins or triplets (mothering and fertility are heritable)
  • From ewes that lambed easily and milked well
  • Higher-than-average weaning weight
  • Sound structure - straight legs, good mouth
  • Good body condition without extra feeding
  • Strong mothering behaviour shown at first lambing
The breed rule: Buy the best ram you can afford. He influences 50% of every lamb. A cheap ram costs you money every year for the 2 years he serves.
Practical exercise 2: Visit 3 sheep farms. Look at their ewes and rams. Ask about lamb survival and growth rates. Compare breeds available in your area.
