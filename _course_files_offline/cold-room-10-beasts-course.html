Who this is for

Rural butchers, abattoir operators, farm enterprises slaughtering for their own retail, and meat traders who currently lose stock to ambient temperatures. The course assumes no prior refrigeration or construction training. It assumes you are willing to make decisions by evidence and write them down.

This course teaches general principles of cold room design for a 10-carcass beef operation in tropical Africa. Refrigeration sizing, electrical installation, structural design and health licensing require a qualified local professional. Every figure is indicative and must be verified with local suppliers and the relevant authority before money is spent.

Quick Reference Checklist

  1. Confirm the kill cycle first. Ten beasts per week means one 10-beast kill day. Size the room for that peak, not the weekly average.
  2. Verify power before buying the compressor. Measure actual voltage and outage frequency for 30 days. If outages exceed 4 h/week, budget for a generator or solar hybrid.
  3. Check the envelope. 100 mm PIR panels minimum for tropical Africa. Inspect every joint for vapour barrier continuity before commissioning.
  4. Confirm the floor drain slopes to a trapped gully. Standing water is the fastest route to a failed health inspection.
  5. Install an air curtain and a strip curtain on the main door. This alone can cut infiltration load by 40%.
  6. Calibrate two thermometers â one at carcass height, one at the evaporator return. Log temperatures twice daily.
  7. Write the HACCP plan before the first carcass. Identify critical control points (receival, chilling, storage, dispatch) and set limits.
  8. Test the backup power under full load before commissioning. A generator that cannot start the compressor is worthless at 2 a.m.
  9. Negotiate a service contract with a local refrigeration engineer before you need one. Downtime costs more than the contract.
