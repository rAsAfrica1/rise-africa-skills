Practical Reference

This enrichment block gives you the operational detail that turns trade theory into shipped containers and settled invoices. Incoterms 2020: Who Pays, Who Bears Risk

Choosing the wrong Incoterm is one of the fastest ways to erase your margin. The table below shows the four terms most used by African agri-exporters and where the cost and risk transfer happens. Figures are indicative for a 20-foot container (FCL) moving from Mombasa or Durban to Rotterdam, based on 2024 market rates.

Table 1: Incoterms 2020 cost and risk transfer for a 20-ft FCL, East/Southern Africa to Rotterdam
Incoterm Exporter pays up to Risk transfers at Indicative exporter cost (USD) Best used when
EXW (Ex Works) Packing at your premises Your gate 0 - 150 Buyer has own African agent; you lack export licence
FOB (Free On Board) Loaded on vessel at port Ship's rail 1,200 - 1,800 Default for most first-time African exporters
CIF (Cost, Insurance, Freight) Delivered to Rotterdam port Rotterdam port 3,400 - 4,600 Buyer is new and wants one landed price
DAP (Delivered At Place) Buyer's warehouse door Buyer's door 4,200 - 5,800 High-trust relationship; you control inland haulage

Source: International Chamber of Commerce, Incoterms 2020 (iccwbo.org); indicative freight costs from UNCTAD Review of Maritime Transport 2023 and Freightos Baltic Index averages, 2024.

Payment Instruments: Risk vs. Cost

Cash-in-advance is safest for you and worst for the buyer's cash flow. Open account is the reverse. Most African exporters settle in the middle band. Use the table to price the risk you are accepting.

Table 2: Export payment instruments ranked by exporter risk and cost
Instrument Exporter risk Typical bank cost Settlement time African market note
Cash in advance (T/T) Very low 0.5 - 1.5% + transfer fee 1 - 3 days Common for first trial orders under $10,000
Letter of Credit (confirmed) Low 0.75 - 2% of value 10 - 21 days Afreximbank and PAPSS reduce confirmation cost
Documentary Collection (D/P) Medium 0.25 - 0.75% 7 - 14 days Widely used for Kenya-EU horticulture
Open Account (30-90 days) High Nil direct cost; credit risk 30 - 90 days Requires credit insurance or factoring

Source: International Trade Centre, Trade Finance Guide (intracen.org); African Export-Import Bank, African Trade Report 2024 (afreximbank.com).

â  SAFETY WARNING - PHYTOSANITARY AND CHEMICAL COMPLIANCE

Agricultural exports are food products. Incorrect pesticide use, banned active ingredients, or aflatoxin contamination will cause your consignment to be rejected, destroyed, or re-exported at your cost - and can trigger a country-wide ban on your product. Before shipping:

  • Never apply a pesticide that is not registered for your crop in the destination market. The EU, UK and Gulf states ban dozens of actives still legal in parts of Africa.
  • Respect the pre-harvest interval (PHI) to the day. Residue test failures are the single largest cause of African produce rejections at EU border posts.
  • Store and transport treated grain below 14% moisture to prevent aflatoxin. Above 14%, Aspergillus growth accelerates within days.
  • Wear the full PPE on the label - gloves, respirator, coveralls. Chronic organophosphate exposure causes irreversible neurological damage.
  • Keep a batch-level spray record. Buyers and regulators now demand traceability back to the field.

Quick Reference Checklist

  • Register your export identity. Obtain a tax number, exporter code and, where required, a phytosanitary registration before you quote a single buyer.
  • Confirm the Incoterm in writing. State it on the proforma invoice - "FOB Mombasa, Incoterms 2020" - never leave it verbal.
  • Match payment instrument to buyer risk. New buyer = LC or advance. Repeat buyer with history = documentary collection. Open account only with credit insurance.
  • Budget the landed cost, not the farm-gate price. Freight, insurance, port charges, documentation and demurrage risk can add 25-40% to your cost base.
  • Prepare the document pack early. Commercial invoice, packing list, bill of lading, certificate of origin, phytosanitary certificate and, for EU/UK, a compliant due-diligence statement.
  • Test residues before shipment. One accredited lab test costs far less than a rejected container and a banned exporter code.
  • Insure the cargo. Marine cargo cover at 110% of CIF value is standard; uninsured loss is a business-ending event.
  • Reconcile forex exposure. Quote in a hard currency or hedge; a 15% local currency slide can wipe out your entire margin between contract and settlement.
