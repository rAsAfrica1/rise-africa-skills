This module covers agronomic practices involving chemical crop protection products. Improper handling can cause acute poisoning, chronic illness, environmental contamination and rejection of your export consignment.

  • Always wear personal protective equipment (PPE): gloves, mask, goggles, boots and long-sleeved clothing when handling, mixing or applying pesticides.
  • Never eat, drink or smoke while handling pesticides. Wash hands and face thoroughly after application.
  • Respect pre-harvest intervals (PHI) strictly. Export markets (especially EU) enforce maximum residue limits (MRLs). A single violation can lead to a border rejection and loss of your buyer.
  • Store chemicals in a locked, ventilated, dedicated store away from food, feed and children. Keep original labelled containers.
  • Dispose of empty containers according to national regulations. Never reuse pesticide containers for water or food.
  • In case of poisoning, seek immediate medical attention and bring the product label. Contact your local poison control centre or agricultural extension officer.
  • Organic and export-oriented farms should use integrated pest management (IPM) and only approved inputs. Keep records of all applications for traceability.

Reference: WHO/FAO International Code of Conduct on Pesticide Management; Kenya Pest Control Products Board (PCPB) regulations; EU Regulation (EC) No 396/2005 on MRLs.

Quick Reference Checklist

  1. Know your market before you plant. Secure a buyer or off-take agreement (local, regional or export) before investing in inputs. Confirm variety, volume, quality grade and delivery schedule.
  2. Calculate your cost of production per kilogram. Use the enterprise budget above as a template. Know your break-even price and your target margin before you negotiate.
  3. Invest in post-harvest handling. Harvest at the correct maturity, use clean crates (never sacks for fresh produce), sort and grade, and pre-cool within 2 hours of harvest where possible.
  4. Plan your cold chain. Even a simple shade structure, evaporative cooler or cold room can cut losses by 10â20%. For export, confirm reefer availability and temperature settings with your logistics provider.
  5. Comply with food safety and MRL standards. Keep spray records, respect PHIs, use approved pesticides only, and test residues before export. Consider GlobalG.A.P. or organic certification for premium markets.
  6. Manage your cash flow. Map income and expenses monthly. Export payment terms can be 30â60 days; ensure you have working capital or invoice financing.
  7. Track key metrics. Monitor yield per hectare, loss rate, cost per kg, revenue per kg and gross margin. Review monthly and adjust.
  8. Build relationships. Join a farmer group or cooperative for input buying, aggregation, transport and market access. Collective bargaining improves prices and reduces transaction costs.
