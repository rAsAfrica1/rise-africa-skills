â  RED SAFETY WARNING â Chemical and Biological Input Handling

DANGER: Agrochemicals can cause acute poisoning, chronic illness, and death. In Sub-Saharan Africa, an estimated 1.1 million cases of acute pesticide poisoning occur annually, with a significant proportion affecting smallholder farmers and their families (WHO, 2022). Follow these non-negotiable rules:

  • NEVER store pesticides in food or drink containers. Every year, children die from drinking paraquat or other agrochemicals stored in soda bottles.
  • NEVER eat, drink, or smoke while handling agrochemicals. Wash hands with soap before any break.
  • NEVER re-use empty pesticide containers for water, food, or household storage. Triple-rinse and puncture them before disposal.
  • ALWAYS wear the PPE specified on the label. A cloth over the face is NOT a respirator.
  • ALWAYS observe the pre-harvest interval (PHI). Harvesting too early leaves toxic residues that can sicken consumers and destroy export contracts.
  • ALWAYS keep the nearest poison control centre number and the product label accessible in case of emergency.
  • IF EXPOSED: Remove contaminated clothing, wash skin with soap and water, flush eyes for 15 minutes, and seek medical attention immediately. Bring the label.

Sources: WHO (2022) Pesticide Poisoning Estimates; FAO/WHO International Code of Conduct on Pesticide Management; Africa Union Framework on Pesticide Regulation.

Quick Reference Checklist â Daily Agrochemical & Input Safety Actions

  1. Verify product registration: Confirm the product is registered by your national regulator (e.g., PCPB in Kenya, NAFDAC in Nigeria, DALRRD in South Africa) and check the expiry date before purchase.
  2. Inspect PPE before use: Check gloves, respirator cartridges, goggles, and boots for cracks, tears, or saturation. Replace immediately if compromised.
  3. Check weather conditions: Do not spray if wind speed exceeds 10 km/h, if rain is forecast within 6 hours, or if temperature exceeds 30Â°C (heat increases volatilisation and drift).
  4. Calibrate spray equipment: Test nozzle output before every spray session. A 10% calibration error can mean a 10% under-dose (pest resistance) or over-dose (crop damage and residue violation).
  5. Maintain a spray log: Record date, product name, active ingredient, batch number, dose, plot ID, applicator name, and PHI. This is your legal and commercial protection.
  6. Secure storage: Keep all agrochemicals in a locked, ventilated, dry store, away from food, feed, seed, and water sources. Post warning signs.
  7. Manage empty containers: Triple-rinse, puncture, and deliver to an approved disposal point. Never burn, bury, or leave containers in the field.
  8. Train and re-train workers: Every person handling agrochemicals must receive annual training on safe handling, first aid, and emergency response. Document attendance.
