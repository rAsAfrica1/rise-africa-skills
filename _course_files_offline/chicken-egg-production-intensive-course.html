â ï¸ Safety Warning â Veterinary Drugs, Chemicals and Food Safety

Eggs and poultry meat enter the human food chain. Misuse of veterinary drugs creates drug residues that can harm consumers and destroy your market reputation.

  • Observe withdrawal periods strictly. Never sell eggs or meat from birds still inside the labelled withdrawal period after any antibiotic, coccidiostat or antiparasitic treatment.
  • Use only registered products approved for laying hens in your country. Some antibiotics are banned in food-producing animals entirely.
  • Never use human antibiotics on poultry, and never use chloramphenicol, nitrofurans or other banned substances.
  • Store and dispose of chemicals safely â disinfectants, insecticides and rodenticides must be kept away from feed, water lines and eggs.
  • Record every treatment with date, product, dose and withdrawal end date. This record protects you commercially and legally.
  • Wash hands and use clean collection trays. Dirty or cracked eggs are a food-poisoning risk and a price discount.

Quick Reference Checklist

  1. Lock your feed supplier and price before buying chicks. Feed is 65â75% of layer production cost â a 10% feed price swing can erase your entire margin.
  2. Budget the full 20-week rearing gap. Confirm you have cash or credit to cover feed, vaccines and labour until the first egg is sold.
  3. Set a target body weight of 1.3â1.5 kg at 18 weeks. Underweight pullets never reach peak lay and cost you the whole cycle.
  4. Follow a written vaccination programme for Newcastle disease, Gumboro, fowl pox and Marek's, using a qualified vet or extension officer.
  5. Provide 16 hours of light at point of lay and 110â125 g of balanced layer feed per bird per day.
  6. Collect eggs at least three times daily and cool them quickly; heat is the biggest cause of egg spoilage in African markets.
  7. Keep daily records of eggs collected, feed used, mortality and treatments â this is your pricing and culling decision tool.
  8. Plan the exit before you start. Decide at what lay percentage (usually 60â65%) you will cull and sell spent hens, and line up a buyer.
