â  SAFETY WARNING â FOOD SAFETY AND CONTAMINATION

Mushrooms are a high-risk food product. Never sell or eat mushrooms from a bag that shows green, black, or pink mould â that is Trichoderma, Aspergillus, or Neurospora, and some produce mycotoxins. Discard the entire bag, do not just cut off the mould. Wash hands and sterilise tools after handling contaminated bags. Never use agricultural fungicides or unregistered chemicals on growing substrate â they are not approved for food production and residues will make your crop unsellable and unsafe. If you use bleach or alcohol for sterilisation, keep them away from fruiting bodies and rinse surfaces. If you are uncertain whether a wild-collected mushroom is safe, do not eat it. Build your business on cultivated spawn only.

Market Positioning for African Growers

Do not compete on price with imported button mushrooms. Compete on freshness and proximity. Hotels in Nairobi, Accra, Lagos, and Harare pay a premium for mushrooms delivered within 24 hours of harvest because imports arrive 5â10 days old. Your advantage is the cold chain you do not have to cross. Target, in order of margin: (1) restaurants and hotels, (2) health food shops and supermarkets, (3) direct-to-consumer via WhatsApp and farmers' markets, (4) dried and powdered mushrooms for off-season income.

Value-addition multiplies margin. Fresh oyster mushrooms at $4/kg become dried mushrooms at $25â40/kg, and mushroom powder for soups and supplements at $40â60/kg. Solar drying is the cheapest entry point and works across most of Africa. Build the fresh business first for cash flow, then layer drying for shelf-stable inventory.
