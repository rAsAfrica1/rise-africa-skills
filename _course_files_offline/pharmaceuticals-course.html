Practical Reference

Agribusiness operators in Africa rarely fail because of a lack of agronomic knowledge. They fail because of cash-flow timing, post-harvest losses, market access, and weak record-keeping. This reference block gives you the numbers, decision rules, and official sources you need to move from production thinking to profit thinking. Use it as a working tool: print the tables, pin the checklist, and verify every figure against the cited source before you commit capital.

â  SAFETY WARNING: PESTICIDES, AFLATOXINS AND MILK HANDLING

Agrochemicals and contaminated produce can kill or cause chronic illness. Observe the following without exception:

  • Never apply a pesticide without reading the label and wearing gloves, boots, and a face mask. Re-entry intervals and pre-harvest intervals are legal limits, not suggestions.
  • Never store pesticides in food containers, near food, or in the home. Lock them separately.
  • Aflatoxin: mouldy maize and groundnuts can carry aflatoxin, a liver carcinogen. Do not blend mouldy grain into good grain. Dry to safe moisture (maize: 13.5% or below) and use hermetic storage.
  • Milk: unpasteurised milk from sick animals transmits brucellosis and tuberculosis. Cool milk within two hours and boil or pasteurise before sale or consumption.
  • If poisoning is suspected, stop work, move to fresh air, keep the label, and call your nearest poison control centre or health facility immediately. Do not induce vomiting unless instructed.

Quick Reference Checklist

  1. Know your cost per unit before you plant. Build a full budget (seed, fertiliser, labour, transport, packaging, levies) and calculate break-even price. If you cannot state your break-even, you are gambling, not farming.
  2. Secure a buyer or a contract before harvest. Verbal agreements fail. Get volume, grade, price basis, and payment terms in writing, with a named contact and a delivery date.
  3. Plan cash flow monthly, not seasonally. Map every inflow and outflow across 12 months. Identify the month you run short and arrange input credit or a bridge facility in advance.
  4. Cut post-harvest loss first. It is the highest-return, lowest-risk investment available. Prioritise drying, crating, hermetic storage, and cold chain before expanding acreage.
  5. Keep records daily. Track yields, input use, labour hours, sales, and losses. Records are the evidence lenders and off-takers require, and they reveal your true margin.
  6. Aggregate to negotiate. Join or form a cooperative or farmer group to pool volume, access better prices, share transport, and qualify for structured finance.
  7. Comply and certify. Meet food safety, phytosanitary, and traceability requirements for your target market. Certification opens higher-value and export channels.
  8. Reinvest a fixed share of profit. Allocate at least 20â30% of net profit to inputs, equipment, or working capital before drawing personal income.
