Break-Even Math You Can Do in Your Head

If your average mobile service sells for USD 45 and your monthly operating cost is USD 180, you need 4 clients per month just to cover the business. Everything after that is gross profit. Add your own living cost to that number and you have your true minimum. A solo operator doing 4 to 6 clients per week in a mid-tier African city can realistically clear USD 600 to USD 1,100 per month before tax once the calendar is full. That is the business case. The variable is not the technique. It is the booking discipline.

â ï¸ Safety Warning â Health, Skin, and Chemical Hazards

This business involves physical contact with clients and the use of oils, lotions, and cleaning chemicals. The following rules are not optional.

  • Never massage a client with an undiagnosed lump, open wound, fever, recent fracture, or suspected blood clot. Refer them to a qualified health professional. Massage can worsen some conditions.
  • Get written informed consent before every first session. Record allergies, medications, pregnancy, recent surgery, and skin conditions. Keep the form for at least 12 months.
  • Patch-test essential oils and new products on every new client at least 24 hours before full application. Citrus and cinnamon oils cause photosensitivity and burns.
  • Never use industrial or undiluted essential oils on skin. Dilute to 1â2% for adults, 0.5% for sensitive skin, and avoid entirely on infants unless trained.
  • Wash hands and forearms before and after every client. Use hand sanitizer between clients and change linen after every single session â no exceptions.
  • Store oils and sanitizers away from food, children, and open flames. Many massage oils are flammable. Keep a fire blanket or extinguisher in your vehicle.
  • Do not work if you have a contagious skin condition, cold, flu, or fever. You will lose more money from a damaged reputation than from one cancelled booking.
  • Carry a basic first-aid kit with gloves, antiseptic, plasters, and a burns gel. Know the emergency number for every area you serve.

Quick Reference Checklist

Print this. Keep it in your kit bag. Run through it before every booking.

  1. Confirm the booking in writing â time, address, service, price, travel fee, and deposit received. A WhatsApp message counts.
  2. Send the intake form 24 hours ahead â health history, allergies, consent, and any contraindications flagged before you travel.
  3. Pack the kit the night before â table, linen, oils, sanitizer, wipes, first-aid kit, phone charger, and business cards.
  4. Arrive 10 minutes early, not 20 â early is professional, too early is intrusive. Text when you are 5 minutes away.
