This section relates to health, food, and chemicals. Read before handling any pesticide, herbicide, or veterinary drug.

  • Never use banned or unregistered pesticides. Check your national regulator's register (e.g., Kenya's PCPB, Nigeria's NAFDAC, South Africa's DALRRD) before purchase. Counterfeit products are common and lethal.
  • Always wear personal protective equipment (PPE): gloves, boots, long sleeves, and a face mask. Never spray against the wind. Never eat, drink, or smoke during application.
  • Respect pre-harvest intervals (PHI). Applying a chemical too close to harvest leaves residues that can fail export tests and harm consumers. Record every application date and product.
  • Store chemicals in a locked, ventilated, labelled cabinet away from food, feed, and children. Never decant into beverage bottles.
  • For veterinary drugs, observe withdrawal periods for meat, milk, and eggs. Residues can cause antibiotic resistance and market rejection.
  • If poisoning occurs, call your local poison centre immediately, keep the product label, and do not induce vomiting unless instructed. In Kenya, call the Kenya Poisons Information Centre; in Nigeria, contact NAFDAC; in South Africa, the Poisons Information Helpline.
  • Food safety: wash produce with clean water, maintain cold chain for perishables, and train all staff on hygiene. A single food safety incident can destroy years of buyer trust.

Quick Reference Checklist: Weekly Operating Rhythm for African Agribusiness Managers

  1. Check cash flow vs. enterprise budget. Compare actual income and expenses against the gross margin benchmarks in Table 1. Flag any line item more than 10% off plan.
  2. Walk the farm or facility. Inspect crops, livestock, storage, and equipment for pests, disease, moisture, or damage. Record findings in a simple logbook or mobile app.
  3. Verify input and chemical compliance. Confirm all agro-chemicals are registered, labelled, and stored safely. Check PPE availability and staff training records.
  4. Review post-harvest handling. Check drying, grading, and storage conditions against Table 2. Measure moisture content for grains; check cold chain temperatures for perishables.
  5. Update market intelligence. Record current prices from at least three buyers or market sources. Compare with last week and last season. Adjust sales timing accordingly.
  6. Confirm buyer and contract obligations. Review delivery schedules, quality standards, and payment terms. Follow up on outstanding receivables older than 30 days.
  7. Train and brief your team. Hold a 15-minute safety and quality huddle. Cover one topic per week: pesticide safety, hygiene, animal welfare, or record-keeping.
  8. Back up records. Ensure farm records, financials, and traceability data are backed up (cloud or duplicate notebook). Records are your access to finance and premium markets.
