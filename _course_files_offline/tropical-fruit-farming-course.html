Practical Reference

This reference block is built for operators who already understand the theory and now need numbers, benchmarks, and checklists they can use on the ground. Every figure below is drawn from published sources you can verify, and every action point is designed to protect margin while reducing risk. Treat this as your field manual for the post-harvest and processing stage of the value chain.

Figure 1 makes the commercial case immediately: storage is where you lose the most, and therefore where a shilling spent on hermetic bags, metal silos, or improved drying returns the most. If you are running a small or medium agribusiness, your first capital allocation should be toward reducing storage loss before you spend on anything else downstream.

â  SAFETY WARNING â Aflatoxin and Chemical Handling

Aflatoxin is a serious health hazard. It is produced by moulds (Aspergillus flavus and A. parasiticus) that grow on poorly dried and poorly stored maize, groundnuts, and other crops. Aflatoxin is a known human carcinogen and is linked to liver cancer and stunting in children. It is invisible, tasteless, and heat-stable â cooking does not destroy it.

  • Never sell, consume, or blend visibly mouldy grain. Drying to safe moisture and storing in hermetic containers is the primary control.
  • Wear gloves, a dust mask, and eye protection when applying any storage pesticide or fumigant. Never handle these chemicals with bare hands.
  • Follow the label exactly. Over-dosing does not improve control and creates illegal residue that can fail export tests and destroy your buyer relationship.
  • Store all agrochemicals in a locked, ventilated place away from food, feed, and children. Never decant into beverage bottles.
  • If you suspect aflatoxin contamination in a lot, do not attempt to "dilute" it by mixing with clean grain. Get it tested. Reject the lot.

Source of hazard guidance: World Health Organization, Aflatoxins fact sheet (who.int), and FAO/WHO Codex Alimentarius guidance on mycotoxin control.

Quick Reference Checklist

  1. Test moisture before storage. Use a calibrated moisture meter; do not store maize above 13.5%, rice above 14%, or beans above 14%. Moisture is the number one driver of loss and mycotoxin.
  2. Buy hermetic storage first. If you can only afford one intervention this season, buy hermetic bags or a metal silo. Storage is your biggest loss point and your fastest payback.
  3. Clean and dry the store before loading. Sweep out old grain, repair roof leaks, and raise pallets off the floor. Old grain residue carries insects into your new lot.
  4. Never mix old and new stock. Segregate lots by date and quality. One infested bag can contaminate an entire store.
  5. Record every lot. Keep a simple log of weight in, weight out, date, moisture, and treatments. You cannot manage loss you do not measure, and buyers pay more for traceable supply.
  6. Price your loss into your margin. If you expect 9% storage loss, that is 9% of your working capital evaporating. Fix it or price it in.
  7. Train your staff on chemical safety. Gloves, masks, locked storage, correct dosage. A safety incident is a business-ending event.
  8. Know your buyer's standard. Export and formal processors test for aflatoxin and pesticide residue. Confirm limits before you commit a lot.
