Practical Reference

Grasscutter (cane rat, Thryonomys swinderianus) farming is one of the most bankable small-livestock plays in West and Central Africa. The animal converts cheap, fibrous plant waste into premium-priced bushmeat, reaches slaughter weight in 5â7 months, and a single breeding female can produce 8â12 offspring per year across 2â3 litters. Below is the operational data you need to price a pen, plan feed, and forecast cash flow. Confirm every figure locally before you commit capital â prices move with season, fuel cost, and regional demand.

Grasscutter meat sells at a premium over chicken and goat in most West African cities because it is culturally valued as bushmeat and is legally farmed. The commercial case rests on three ratios: low feed cost per kilogram of gain, short generation interval, and a market that absorbs volume without price collapse at small scale. Your job is to hold those ratios steady while you scale from 8 to 50 breeding females.

â  SAFETY WARNING â Health, Food & Chemical Handling

  • Zoonoses: Grasscutters can carry Lassa virus, Yersinia, Salmonella, and ectoparasites. Never handle wild-caught animals bare-handed. Wear gloves, wash hands with soap after every contact, and quarantine new stock for 21 days.
  • Lassa fever: In West Africa, rodent contact is a documented transmission route. Keep pens away from the home, control household rodents, and store feed in sealed containers.
  • Meat safety: Slaughter on a clean surface, use potable water, cook meat to an internal temperature of 74Â°C. Never sell meat from an animal that died of unknown causes.
  • Chemicals: If you use acaricides, anthelmintics, or disinfectants, follow the label exactly. Wear gloves and a mask, never mix products, and observe withdrawal periods before slaughter. Store chemicals locked away from feed and children.
  • Bites and scratches: Grasscutters have strong incisors. Handle by the tail base with a firm grip; clean any bite wound immediately and seek medical advice.

Quick Reference Checklist

  1. Confirm local demand and price with 3 buyers (restaurants, hotels, market traders) before buying stock â get a price range in writing.
  2. Call 3 suppliers for breeding animals and record the price, sex ratio, and health status quoted.
  3. Build the pen first â roofed, well-drained, escape-proof, with separate breeding and grow-out sections.
  4. Source clean stock from a known breeder; quarantine 21 days; deworm and inspect for wounds or parasites.
  5. Establish a cut-and-carry forage plot (Panicum maximum, elephant grass, cassava leaves) before animals arrive.
  6. Start a daily record book â births, deaths, weights, feed used, and sales. This is your Business Seed Record evidence.
  7. Re-mate females within 10 days of kindling to hit 2.5 litters per year.
  8. Weigh a sample monthly and cull slow growers; target 3.5 kg live weight at 6 months.
  9. Set your selling price against chicken and goat, not against wild bushmeat, and negotiate a standing order with at least one buyer.
