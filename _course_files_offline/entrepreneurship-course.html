Practical Reference

This reference section is built for one purpose: to give you numbers you can act on today. Every figure below is drawn from official, publicly available sources so you can verify it, cite it to a bank or investor, and defend it in a business plan. Use these tables as a working baseline, then replace the figures with your own quoted prices from local suppliers before you commit money.

Read the cycle as a loop, not a line. The business only survives if cash completes the full circle faster than your costs arrive. Most micro-enterprises in Africa fail not because they are unprofitable on paper, but because they run out of cash while waiting for the loop to close. Your job as the owner is to shorten the loop: buy smaller quantities more often, collect receivables within seven days, and never let owner drawings exceed net profit for the month.

â  SAFETY WARNING â Credit Sales and Record Keeping

Selling on credit without a written record is the single most common cause of cash collapse in small African businesses. If you extend credit:

  • Record every credit sale in a book or phone app on the same day. A memory is not a ledger.
  • Never advance credit beyond 10% of your monthly cash sales. One default must not stop your restocking.
  • Agree a written repayment date with the customer and their phone number before goods leave your hands.
  • Do not mix business cash with household cash. Use a separate wallet, tin, or mobile money account.
  • If a customer defaults twice, stop credit sales to them. Protect the business, not the relationship.

Quick Reference Checklist

  • Get three quotes for every input, tool or stock item before you buy. Prices differ by town, season and supplier â never accept the first price.
  • Calculate your break-even units before launch: fixed monthly costs Ã· (selling price â variable cost per unit). If it exceeds what you can realistically sell, change the plan.
  • Open a separate mobile money wallet for the business. Keep household money out of it completely.
  • Record every sale and every expense daily, even the small ones. A cash book or a free app is enough.
  • Collect receivables within seven days. Follow up on day three, not day thirty.
  • Pay yourself a fixed monthly amount and stick to it. Random drawings are the hidden killer of micro-enterprises.
  • Check your licences and tax obligations with your local authority before trading, and budget for them monthly.
  • Review your numbers every month: revenue, total costs, net profit, and cash in hand. If profit is positive but cash is falling, your collection cycle is too slow.
