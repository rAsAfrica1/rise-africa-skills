Aflatoxins are among the most dangerous contaminants in African poultry feed, particularly in groundnut cake, maize, and cottonseed meal. Chronic exposure at levels as low as 20 ppb in poultry diets reduces growth, suppresses immunity, lowers egg production, and can transfer residues into eggs and meat â creating a food safety risk for consumers.

  • Test every incoming batch of maize and oilseed cake for aflatoxin B1. Reject any batch exceeding 20 ppb for poultry feed.
  • Store ingredients below 13% moisture and below 25Â°C. Damp, warm stores are aflatoxin factories.
  • Never use visibly mouldy, discoloured, or musty-smelling grain â dilution does not make it safe.
  • Use a registered aflatoxin binder (e.g. hydrated sodium calcium aluminosilicate) at 1â2 kg per tonne when risk is elevated.
  • Wear a dust mask and gloves when handling premixes, mould inhibitors, and medicated feed. Wash hands after mixing.
  • Keep all feed additives and veterinary drugs locked away from children and untrained workers.
  • Comply with your national feed safety authority (e.g. KEBS in Kenya, NAFDAC in Nigeria, GSA in Ghana, SABS in South Africa).

Quick Reference Checklist

  1. Source ingredients with verified quality. Buy maize at â¤13% moisture and oilseed cakes with a recent aflatoxin test certificate. Build relationships with two or three reliable suppliers so a single failure does not stop production.
  2. Analyse before you formulate. Never assume book values for local ingredients. A single wet-season maize batch at 16% moisture can wreck your energy calculation and your FCR.
  3. Run least-cost formulation weekly. Ingredient prices in African markets move fast. Re-run your formulation whenever maize, soya, or fish meal prices shift by more than 5%.
  4. Calibrate your mixer and grinder monthly. Target a coefficient of variation (CV) below 10% for mixed feed. Poor mixing means some birds get overdosed and others underdosed.
  5. Monitor FCR and mortality every batch. If FCR rises above 1.85 for broilers or mortality exceeds 8%, stop and investigate before the next batch.
  6. Keep batch records. Record ingredient lot numbers, formulation date, mixer operator, and test results. Traceability protects your business if a customer reports a problem.
  7. Control feed storage. Use pallets, keep bags off the floor, maintain ventilation, and rotate stock on a first-in-first-out basis. Target less than 1% storage loss.
  8. Price for profit, not for volume. Know your break-even cost per kg of feed and per kg of live weight. Review pricing monthly against input costs.
