2. Varieties and Day-Length

The single most important decision in onion farming: match variety to your latitude.

Module 2 of 6 · Onion Farming for Profit

The day-length rule

Onions form bulbs in response to day length. If you plant the wrong type, they grow leaves but no bulbs. This is the number one cause of onion failure in Africa.

TypeDay length neededLatitudeExamples
Short-day10-12 hours0-15 degrees (Kenya, Uganda, Nigeria)Red Creole, Bombay Red, Texas Grano
Intermediate12-13 hours15-25 degrees (Zimbabwe, Zambia, Malawi)Red Pinoy, Granex
Long-day14-16 hours25+ degrees (South Africa, North Africa)Yellow Globe, Rossa di Milano

Seed vs sets

OptionTime to harvestCostBest for
Seed (transplanted)120-150 daysLowCommercial scale
Sets (small bulbs)90-110 daysHighQuick turnaround, small plots
Direct seeding130-160 daysVery lowLarge scale, low labour

For commercial smallholder production, seed transplanted to the field gives the best balance of cost and control.

The variety rule: Match day-length to your latitude. Get this wrong and the plant grows leaves only - no bulb.
Practical exercise 2: Find your latitude on a map. Confirm which day-length type suits your area. Buy seed accordingly.
