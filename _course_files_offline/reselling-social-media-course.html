Practical Reference

Social reselling works because it collapses three costs that kill traditional retail in Africa: rent, staff, and shelf space. You replace them with data bundles, a phone camera, and consistency. The operators who scale are not the ones with the biggest budgets â they are the ones with the tightest unit economics. This section gives you the numbers, the process map, and the checklists you need to run this as a business rather than a hobby.

â  SAFETY WARNING â Payments, Fraud and Restricted Goods

  • Never release goods before cleared payment. A mobile money SMS screenshot is not proof of payment. Confirm the balance in your own wallet or app before handing over stock.
  • Beware overpayment scams. If a buyer "accidentally" sends too much and asks for a refund of the difference, it is fraud. Refund only after your bank or mobile money provider confirms the original funds are real and settled.
  • Do not sell restricted or regulated goods without a licence: medicines, unregistered supplements, cosmetics with unapproved ingredients, agricultural chemicals, seeds of unregistered varieties, weapons, and counterfeit branded goods. Selling these can lead to arrest, fines, and permanent platform bans.
  • Chemicals and cosmetics: if you resell any product that touches skin, food, or crops, store it away from food, keep it in original labelled containers, and never repackage without the manufacturer's permission. Keep the safety data sheet or label on file.
  • Meet-up safety: for cash-on-delivery handovers, meet in a public place in daylight. Tell someone where you are going. Do not invite unknown buyers to your home.
  • Protect your accounts: enable two-factor authentication on every platform. Do not share login codes. A hijacked business page can destroy a year of customer trust in one day.

Quick Reference Checklist

  1. Confirm your numbers before you buy stock. Call three suppliers, get written prices, and calculate your break-even units using the Table 2 structure. If you cannot hit at least a 20% net margin, change product or supplier.
  2. Set up a proper business profile. Business name, clear profile photo, one-line description of what you sell and where you deliver, working phone number, and a pinned post with your catalogue and payment methods.
  3. Post daily, sell weekly. Minimum one piece of content per day per active platform. Aim for 5â7 posts per week that show the product in use, not just the product on a table.
  4. Reply within 15 minutes during trading hours. Speed of response is the single biggest lever on enquiry-to-sale conversion in social selling. Set an auto-reply for after hours.
  5. Keep a written record of every order. Buyer name, phone, item, price, payment method, delivery date, and status. A $2 record book prevents $200 disputes.
  6. Confirm payment before dispatch, every time. Check your own wallet balance. No exceptions, no matter how trusted the buyer seems.
  7. Follow up 7 days after delivery. One message asking if the product worked well generates repeat orders and referrals at near-zero cost.
  8. Review your unit economics monthly. Recalculate margin, ad spend return, and delivery cost. Cut any product line below 15% net margin or raise its price.
  9. Protect your accounts and your reputation. Two-factor authentication on, passwords unique, and a written policy for returns and refunds posted publicly.
