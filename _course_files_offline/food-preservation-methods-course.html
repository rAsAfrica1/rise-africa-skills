Quick Reference Checklist

  1. Match method to product acidity. High-acid foods (pH < 4.6 â most fruit, tomato, pickles) can be water-bath processed. Low-acid foods (meat, fish, vegetables, milk) require pressure canning, curing, or fermentation.
  2. Target water activity below 0.85 for shelf-stable dried products. Buy a cheap aw meter or use the FAO moisture-content tables for your specific commodity.
  3. Weigh curing salts and brine on a 0.1g scale. Never estimate. Record every batch in a logbook with date, weight, and salt concentration.
  4. Sanitise all jars, lids, and equipment with boiling water or food-grade sanitiser before every batch. A dirty jar ruins six months of work.
  5. Label every batch with product name, production date, batch number, and best-before date. This protects you legally and builds retailer trust.
  6. Test pH and aw on new recipes before scaling. Send one sample to a lab (e.g. a national bureau of standards or university food science department) â it costs little and de-risks your whole product line.
  7. Price three suppliers for every input (salt, vinegar, jars, fuel) before committing. Prices vary 30â100% between towns and seasons.
  8. Keep a cold chain for any product labelled "chilled" or "fresh". If you cannot guarantee it, reformulate to a shelf-stable version rather than risk a recall.
