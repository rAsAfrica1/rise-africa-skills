8. Drying, Milling and Storage

Rice quality is decided after harvest, not in the field. Drying and milling separate premium rice from feed-grade rice.

Module 8 of 12 · Rice Farming for Profit

Why drying matters

Rice harvested at 20-25% moisture must be dried to 14% for storage. Poor drying causes:

  • Mould and aflatoxin
  • High broken rice percentage during milling (30-50% instead of 15%)
  • Yellow discolouration (poor quality)
  • Insect attack in storage
  • Lost income - broken rice sells for 30-50% less

Sun drying rules

  1. Dry on tarpaulin or concrete - never on bare soil (contaminates with dirt and stones).
  2. Spread 3-5 cm thick - thicker takes longer, thinner dries too fast (cracks).
  3. Turn every 30-60 minutes - even drying, prevents mould.
  4. Do not dry on hot ground. If you cannot walk barefoot on the ground, it is too hot for the rice - the grains will crack.
  5. Cover at night - dew re-wets the rice.
  6. Target 14% moisture - test with a moisture meter, or bite test (should shatter not squash).
  7. Dry slowly at first - 2-3 days of shaded drying, then full sun.
Never dry rice on hot concrete or tarmac. Above 45 C, the grain cracks. Cracked rice breaks during milling, losing 30-50% value.

Milling

Paddy rice has a husk. Milling removes the husk and bran to give white rice. Milling quality determines your price.

Milling stages

  1. Cleaning: Remove stones, dust, straw, weed seeds.
  2. De-husking (hulling): Removes the outer husk. Result: brown rice.
  3. Polishing: Removes bran layers. Result: white rice.
  4. Grading: Separates head rice, broken rice, and bran.
  5. Bagging: Packs for sale.

Good mills achieve 65% head rice. Poor mills only get 45-55%. Milling quality depends on variety, drying, and mill equipment.

Parboiling

Parboiling is a partial cooking process before milling. It:

  • Increases head rice recovery by 5-10%
  • Retains more nutrients (moves vitamins from bran into grain)
  • Makes rice firmer and less sticky
  • Popular in West Africa and South Asia

Traditional parboiling steps:

  1. Soak paddy in water 24-48 hours.
  2. Steam for 20-30 minutes until husk splits.
  3. Dry to 14% moisture.
  4. Mill normally.

Storage

Paddy or milled rice must be stored at 14% moisture in a cool, dry, insect-proof store.

Storage rules

  1. Dry to 14% moisture before storage - always test.
  2. Clean grain - no chaff, no dust, no stones.
  3. Store off the ground (pallets, shelves).
  4. Keep away from walls (rodents and moisture).
  5. Never mix old and new rice.
  6. Use airtight containers if possible - kills insects without chemicals.
  7. Check monthly for insects, moisture, rodents.
  8. Never store near chemicals, fuel, or strong smells - rice absorbs odours.

The drying rule: Dry rice slowly and evenly to 14%. Sun-dry on tarpaulin, turn every hour, never on hot concrete. Good drying gives 65% head rice; bad drying gives 50% or less. The difference is your profit.
