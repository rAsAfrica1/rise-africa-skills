â  SAFETY WARNING â Aflatoxin and Feed Contamination

Never feed mouldy maize, groundnut cake or cottonseed cake to pigs, sheep or goats. Damp-stored grain produces aflatoxins, which cause liver damage, reduced growth, abortion and death in livestock â and pass into meat and milk consumed by humans. Aflatoxin is a Group 1 human carcinogen.

  • Store feed below 12% moisture. Test with a moisture meter before bagging.
  • Reject any ingredient with visible mould, musty smell or caking. Do not "dilute" it into a mixed ration.
  • Cottonseed cake contains gossypol â never exceed 10% in pig rations and never feed to young stock.
  • Wear a dust mask and gloves when grinding or mixing. Feed dust causes chronic respiratory disease.
  • Keep all mineral premixes and medicated feeds locked away from children and out of the food kitchen.
  • If animals show sudden feed refusal, bloody diarrhoea or unexplained abortion, stop the batch immediately and call a veterinarian.

Source: FAO/WHO Codex Alimentarius, Code of Practice for the Prevention and Reduction of Mycotoxin Contamination in Cereals (CXC 51-2003); WHO, Aflatoxins fact sheet.

Quick Reference Checklist

  1. Weigh every ingredient. Eyeballing ratios is the single biggest cause of poor FCR in smallholder feed businesses.
  2. Test moisture before storage. Target below 12% for grain, below 10% for finished meal.
  3. Calculate FCR weekly. Feed used Ã· weight gained. If it rises two weeks in a row, reformulate.
  4. Keep a batch log. Date, ingredients, weights, supplier, and animal performance. This is your proof of quality when selling to commercial buyers.
  5. Match formulation to species and stage. A grower pig ration will underperform a finisher, and a lactating doe needs more than a dry doe.
  6. Include a mineral/vitamin premix at 2â3%. Skipping it causes silent losses you will not see until the abattoir.
  7. Price per kg of gain, not per bag. Sell your feed on animal performance â that is what commercial farmers buy.
  8. Register your business and label your bags. Include batch number, date, protein %, and a phone number. Buyers pay more for traceability.
