Practical Reference

This reference section is built for the operator, not the academic. If you are running a farm, a processing unit, an agri-dealership, or a team of casual labourers anywhere from Kenya's Rift Valley to Ghana's Ashanti Region, the numbers below are your working baseline. Mental health is not a "soft" cost line â it is a productivity, retention, and safety cost line. Untreated depression and anxiety cost the global economy an estimated US$1 trillion per year in lost productivity, and sub-Saharan Africa absorbs a disproportionate share of that loss because treatment coverage sits below 10% in most countries. Treat the tables below as you would a fertiliser rate chart: a starting point you adjust to your own soil.

â  SAFETY WARNING â Read before acting on anything in this course

This module teaches awareness and support skills. It does not qualify you to diagnose, prescribe, or treat any mental health condition. Never advise a worker to stop, start, or change psychiatric medication â that is a clinician's decision alone. Never use coercion, threats of dismissal, or "tough love" as a wellbeing tool; it causes harm and may breach labour law. If a person discloses thoughts of self-harm or suicide, do not leave them alone, do not promise secrecy you cannot keep, and connect them immediately to a trained professional or emergency service. In Zimbabwe, the national helpline is operated through the Ministry of Health and Child Care and Friendship Bench-trained community grandmothers; in Kenya, contact the Kenya Red Cross or Befrienders Kenya; in Nigeria, the SURPIN network. If you are unsure, call your nearest district hospital and ask for the mental health focal person. When in doubt, refer up â it is always the safer business decision.

Quick Reference Checklist

  1. Appoint one trained peer champion per 25â50 staff. This person is not a counsellor â they are a signpost. Give them a written one-page referral list with names, phone numbers, and hours.
  2. Print and post the referral pathway in the staff room, the packhouse, and the vehicle depot. A helpline nobody can find is a helpline that does not exist.
  3. Run a 90-minute awareness session at induction for every new hire, including seasonal and casual labour. Repeat annually. Keep an attendance register for your HR file.
  4. Build a "notice and ask" script for supervisors: one private opening question, no diagnosis, no interrogation. Role-play it twice a year.
  5. Audit your rosters for fatigue risk. Long shifts, night work, and unpaid overtime are the three biggest drivers of burnout in agri-processing. Fix the roster before you fix the person.
  6. Track four numbers monthly: absence days, voluntary exits, recorded incidents, and referrals made. If referrals are zero, your culture is the problem, not your staff.
  7. Keep every disclosure confidential and store records under lock or password. Breach of confidentiality destroys the programme faster than any budget cut.
  8. Review the programme quarterly against the cost table above. Adjust, do not abandon. A programme that runs for one year and stops is a cost with no return.
