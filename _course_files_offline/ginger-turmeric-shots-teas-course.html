If your post-harvest strategy includes grain storage pesticides (e.g., phosphine fumigants, Actellic dust), you are handling hazardous chemicals. The following are non-negotiable:

  • Never enter a fumigated silo or sealed storage without a fitted respirator and at least 24 hours of ventilation after opening. Phosphine gas is lethal and odourless at dangerous concentrations.
  • Never store pesticides in the same room as food, feed, or seed. Cross-contamination is a documented cause of acute poisoning in African farming communities.
  • Always wear gloves, goggles, and a mask when applying storage chemicals. If you smell a sweet or garlic-like odour near a sealed store, evacuate immediately and ventilate.
  • Always follow the label rate. Over-application does not improve control â it increases residue risk and buyer rejection.
  • Report any suspected poisoning to your nearest health facility immediately. Symptoms include headache, dizziness, vomiting, and difficulty breathing.
  • Comply with your country's pesticide registration authority (e.g., KEPHIS in Kenya, NAFDAC in Nigeria, EPA Ghana) and the FAO/WHO Code of Conduct on Pesticide Management.

Quick Reference Checklist

  1. Measure your baseline loss. Weigh or count a representative sample at harvest, after drying, after storage, and at point of sale. You cannot manage what you have not measured.
  2. Identify the single biggest loss point. Use the chart above as a template. For maize it is usually storage; for tomato it is transport; for milk it is cooling. Fix the biggest leak first.
  3. Calculate the revenue value of loss, not just the percentage. Multiply your loss rate by your annual volume and your average selling price. That number is your business case for investment.
  4. Choose interventions with a payback under two seasons. Hermetic bags, crates, and metal silos typically qualify. Cold rooms require a longer horizon and a confirmed buyer.
  5. Train every person who touches the product. Loss reduction is 30% equipment and 70% handling discipline. A crate system fails if workers still throw bags.
  6. Secure a buyer before you invest in storage. Storage without a market is just delayed loss. Contract farming or off-take agreements de-risk the investment.
  7. Document everything. Keep records of loss rates, intervention costs, and recovered value. This data is your strongest asset in loan applications and grant reporting.
  8. Review and adjust each season. Post-harvest loss is seasonal and variable. What worked in a dry year may fail in a wet one. Re-measure annually.
