Marhaba! Arabic

Arabic letters hold hands, and some of them are the same shape wearing different dots.

Ages 7–128 modules 🪨 Starts with no device

Arabic runs right to left, and its letters join up like people holding hands in a line. Even better: many letters are the SAME shape, and only the dots tell them apart. Learn one shape and you get three letters free. You will be writing your own name in Arabic much sooner than you think.

â  SAFETY WARNING â FOOD, MEDICINE AND CHEMICAL PACKAGING

Module 7 asks learners to read Arabic on real packaging in their own kitchen. This is the highest-value and highest-risk module in the course. Apply the following without exception:

  • Never let a learner handle open medicine, agricultural chemicals, bleach, drain cleaner or any pesticide container. Empty containers are still hazardous â residue remains.
  • Read packaging from a photograph, a sealed retail box, or a clean empty carton that has been rinsed and dried by an adult. Do not bring farm chemical packaging into a classroom at all.
  • Arabic-language instructions on medicine must never be interpreted by a child. Only a qualified pharmacist or clinician may advise on dosage or use.
  • Expiry dates and dosage text are safety-critical. If a learner misreads one during practice, correct it immediately and visibly.
  • Wash hands after handling any packaging, and keep all food and drink off the reading table.
  • If a learner has a known allergy, check that no packaging brought in has contained an allergen (nuts, milk, egg, wheat, shellfish).

In an emergency, contact your local poison control centre or nearest clinic immediately. In Zimbabwe, the Medicines Control Authority of Zimbabwe (MCAZ) is the authority for medicine labelling; for agricultural chemicals, follow the label and your national regulator's guidance.
