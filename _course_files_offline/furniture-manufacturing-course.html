â  SAFETY WARNING â FOOD SAFETY, PESTICIDES & AFLATOXIN

Post-harvest handling is a food safety activity, not just a logistics activity. Failure here can kill customers and destroy your business permanently.

  • Pesticide residues: Never apply crop protection products after harvest unless the label explicitly permits post-harvest use. Observe the pre-harvest interval (PHI) on every label. Buyers in the EU and UK test for maximum residue limits (MRLs) â a single failure can delist your entire supply base.
  • Aflatoxin: In maize, groundnuts, and other oilseeds, moisture above 13% at storage creates aflatoxin risk. Aflatoxin is a carcinogen and is regulated in all major markets. Dry to safe moisture, sort out mouldy grain, and never blend contaminated lots into clean ones.
  • Personal protective equipment: Anyone handling pesticides or fumigants must wear gloves, eye protection, and a respirator. Fumigation of stored grain must be done by certified operators only.
  • Water quality: Wash water for fresh produce must be potable or treated. Dirty wash water spreads pathogens across an entire batch.
  • Traceability: Keep batch records from farm to buyer. If a recall happens, traceability is the difference between a bad week and a closed business.

Reference: FAO/WHO Codex Alimentarius, General Principles of Food Hygiene (CXC 1-1969), https://www.fao.org/fao-who-codexalimentarius/codex-texts/codes-of-practice/en/.

Quick Reference Checklist

  1. Baseline your losses. Weigh produce at harvest, at storage exit, and at delivery for one full cycle. You cannot manage what you have not measured.
  2. Harvest at the right maturity and the right time of day. Early morning or late evening harvest reduces field heat and extends shelf life.
  3. Get produce out of the sun within 30 minutes. Shade is the cheapest cold chain intervention available to any African farm.
  4. Switch to rigid, clean, stackable crates. Replace woven sacks for fresh produce. Crates reduce bruising, improve airflow, and are reusable across seasons.
  5. Dry and store grains below safe moisture. Target below 13% for maize and below 9% for groundnuts. Use hermetic storage to block insect reinfestation.
  6. Grade and sort before the buyer sees the consignment. Off-grade product sold locally at a discount beats a rejected mixed load.
  7. Confirm the buyer, price, and specification before harvest. A signed order or WhatsApp confirmation with quantity, grade, and delivery date protects your margin.
  8. Keep records. Batch numbers, input dates, PHI compliance, storage conditions, and dispatch notes. Records are your insurance policy and your credit history.
