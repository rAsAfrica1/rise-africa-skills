Agro-chemicals used in crop production and post-harvest storage can cause acute poisoning, chronic illness, and contamination of food and water sources. In sub-Saharan Africa, the World Health Organization estimates that pesticide poisoning affects hundreds of thousands of agricultural workers annually, with smallholders and their families at highest risk.

  • Always read the label and follow the recommended dose. Never mix products unless the label explicitly permits it.
  • Wear personal protective equipment (PPE) â gloves, mask, long sleeves, and boots â during mixing and application. Wash PPE separately from household laundry.
  • Never store agro-chemicals in food or drink containers. Keep them locked away from children and livestock.
  • Observe pre-harvest intervals strictly. Do not harvest or sell produce before the stated waiting period has elapsed.
  • Do not eat, drink, or smoke while handling chemicals. Wash hands and face thoroughly with soap after work.
  • Dispose of empty containers safely â never reuse them for water or food. Follow national disposal guidelines.
  • If poisoning is suspected, contact a medical facility immediately and bring the product label with you.

Source: WHO (2020), Exposure to Highly Hazardous Pesticides; FAO/WHO (2019), International Code of Conduct on Pesticide Management.

Quick Reference Checklist

Use this checklist before committing to any new agribusiness enterprise or scaling an existing one. Each point is designed to be actionable within one week.

  1. Validate market demand: Identify at least three confirmed buyers and their price ranges before planting or investing. Verbal interest is not a contract.
  2. Calculate your full cost of production: Include land preparation, inputs, labour, transport, and a contingency of at least 10%. Compare against realistic farmgate prices.
  3. Secure input supply: Confirm availability and price of quality seed, fertiliser, and agro-chemicals from registered suppliers. Avoid counterfeit products.
  4. Plan post-harvest handling: Budget for storage, drying, or cold chain from the start. Post-harvest losses can exceed 30% for perishables.
  5. Arrange finance early: Explore bank loans, SACCOs, mobile money credit, or value chain financing at least one season ahead. Prepare records and a simple business plan.
  6. Check regulatory compliance: Confirm registration, certification, and tax requirements for your product and target market, especially for export.
  7. Build a risk plan: Consider weather, pest, price, and currency risks. Diversify crops or markets where possible.
  8. Track and review: Keep simple records of costs, yields, and sales. Review performance each season and adjust your plan based on actual data.
