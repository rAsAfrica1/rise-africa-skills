â  SAFETY WARNING â Cyanide and Food Safety

Fresh cassava contains cyanogenic glucosides. If you skip or shorten peeling, grating, pressing, fermentation or drying, the finished product can carry dangerous cyanide levels. Symptoms of acute cyanide poisoning include headache, dizziness, vomiting, rapid breathing and confusion â seek medical help immediately. Long-term low-level exposure is linked to konzo and tropical ataxic neuropathy, especially where cassava is the staple diet and protein intake is low. Never sell flour or garri that has not completed its full detoxification cycle. Keep children and animals away from press water and peel heaps. Wash hands and equipment between batches. If you are unsure of your product's cyanide level, do not sell it â test first. Testing services are available through national food safety authorities and IITA partner labs.

Quick Reference Checklist

  • Source roots within 24 hours. Mature (9â12 months), firm, no soft spots or rot. Old or damaged roots have higher cyanide and lower yield.
  • Peel thick. The peel carries most of the cyanogenic glucosides. A thin peel is a false economy â it costs you a rejected batch.
  • Grate fine. 1â3 mm particle size exposes more surface area for detoxification and gives better pressing.
  • Respect the press-and-ferment clock. 12â72 hours depending on product. Do not rush it because a buyer is waiting.
  • Dry to 12â14% moisture. Test with a moisture meter or the salt-and-bottle method. Wet product spoils and grows mould.
  • Sieve and grade before packing. Remove stones, fibre and oversize particles. Uniformity is what keeps bakery and institutional buyers.
  • Label every bag. Product name, batch number, production date, weight, and your phone number. Traceability wins contracts.
  • Cost every batch. Know your cost per kg before you quote a price. If you cannot state it from memory, you are guessing.
