Practical Reference

Mechanized equipment rental is one of the fastest paths to cash flow in African agribusiness because you are selling machine-hours, not harvests. A tractor, thresher, or hammer mill earns whether or not the rains come. The operators who fail are rarely the ones with the best machines; they are the ones who never matched engine size to plot size, never priced per hectare correctly, and never tracked downtime. Use the reference material below as your operating manual.

â  SAFETY WARNING â MACHINERY AND FUEL HANDLING

Rotating PTO shafts, thresher drums, and hammer mill beaters cause severe injury or death within seconds. Never operate a machine with the PTO guard removed, and never wear loose clothing, shawls, or dangling jewellery near a running driveline. Before any maintenance, clear blockages, or belt adjustment: switch off, remove the key, and wait for all moving parts to stop completely. Store petrol and diesel in approved metal containers away from living quarters, never siphon fuel by mouth, and never refuel a hot engine. Hammer mills generate combustible dust â keep the milling shed ventilated and free of naked flames. Threshing and milling dust causes chronic respiratory disease: supply and enforce the use of N95 or equivalent dust masks for every operator, every shift. If you hire out equipment, your written contract must state that the client's workers are trained on these controls before the machine is released.

Quick Reference Checklist

  • Match machine to market before you buy. Count how many hectares within a 30 km radius need ploughing, threshing, or milling each season. If the number is below 150 hectares for a tractor, start as a broker instead of an owner.
  • Price per unit of work, not per day. Quote per acre, per tonne, or per 50 kg bag. Day rates punish you for being efficient and reward clients for wasting your fuel.
  • Collect a 50% deposit at booking. Mobile money (EcoCash, M-Pesa, MTN MoMo) before the machine leaves the yard. Balance on completion, before the client's crop leaves the field.
  • Keep a machine logbook. Hours run, fuel litres, breakdowns, parts replaced, and revenue per job. This single record turns a hobby into a bankable business.
  • Set aside 15â20% of every payment for a maintenance fund. Tyres, belts, filters, and injectors fail on schedule. Operators who do not reserve cash lose a full season to a US$200 part.
  • Train one spare operator. Illness, funerals, and school terms remove labour. A second trained operator protects your booking calendar.
  • Sign a one-page rental agreement. Machine condition on release, return time, damage liability, fuel responsibility, and payment terms. WhatsApp confirmation is legally useful in most African jurisdictions.
  • Insure or self-insure the asset. Where agricultural machinery insurance is unavailable or unaffordable, hold a dedicated replacement fund equal to at least 10% of machine value.
