â  SAFETY WARNING â RAW CASSAVA AND PROCESSING CHEMICALS

Raw (bitter) cassava roots and leaves contain cyanogenic glycosides that release hydrogen cyanide. Improperly processed cassava can cause acute cyanide poisoning â konzo and ataxic neuropathy are documented in several African countries where processing is rushed. Never eat raw bitter cassava. Always peel, soak, ferment, press, and cook thoroughly until the characteristic bitter taste is gone. Discard soak water; do not use it for drinking or livestock.

If you use any agrochemical (herbicide, insecticide, fertiliser): wear gloves, boots, long sleeves, eye protection and a mask. Never mix or apply chemicals near children, water sources or food stores. Follow the label â the label is the law. Store chemicals locked away from food and feed. If someone swallows a chemical or shows dizziness, vomiting or confusion after eating cassava, contact a clinic or poison centre immediately and take the product label with you.

Cassava leaves for animal feed must be wilted, dried or ensiled before feeding to reduce cyanide load. Do not feed fresh, high-cyanide leaf material in large quantities to monogastric animals.

Quick Reference Checklist

  1. Confirm the market before you plant. Call at least three buyers (starch processor, feed maker, trader) and get indicative prices for fresh roots, chips and gari. Match variety and volume to what they actually buy.
  2. Test your soil and check drainage. Target pH 5.5â7.5 and well-drained land. Waterlogged plots rot cassava roots and destroy margin.
  3. Source certified, disease-free cuttings. 20â25 cm length, mature wood, no mosaic or brown streak symptoms. Buy from a recognised seed multiplier â cheap cuttings are the most expensive mistake in cassava.
  4. Plant at correct spacing and timing. 1 m Ã 1 m typical; plant at the start of the rains so roots bulk before dry season.
  5. Control weeds hard in months 1â4. This is the single biggest yield driver. After the canopy closes, cassava suppresses most weeds.
  6. Plan processing capacity before harvest. Roots deteriorate within 24â48 hours of lifting. Line up chipping, drying or a processor before you dig.
  7. Keep records. Log cuttings cost, labour, inputs, harvest weight and sale price per channel. Records are what turn a crop into a business and unlock finance.
  8. Run the safety protocol. Process cassava fully, store chemicals safely, and train every worker on the cyanide and agrochemical warnings above before the season starts.
