This module involves live animals, veterinary medicines, and food products. The following are non-negotiable.

  • Vaccines and veterinary drugs: Store at the temperature stated on the label (most poultry vaccines require 2â8Â°C). Never use expired vaccines. Never mix vaccines or antibiotics without written veterinary instruction. Misuse drives antimicrobial resistance, which is a growing food-safety crisis across Africa.
  • Antibiotic withdrawal periods: Observe the full withdrawal period before slaughter or egg collection. Selling meat or eggs with antibiotic residues is illegal in most jurisdictions and can destroy your buyer relationships permanently.
  • Personal protective equipment: Wear gloves, boots, and a mask when handling sick birds, disinfectants, or chemicals. Wash hands with soap after every handling session.
  • Zoonotic disease: Avian influenza and salmonella can transfer to humans. Report unusual mass mortality immediately to your local veterinary authority â do not sell or move affected birds.
  • Slaughter hygiene: Use clean water, sanitised surfaces, and separate clean/dirty zones. Never process sick birds for sale.
  • Chemical storage: Keep all agrochemicals locked away from feed, food, and children. Never decant chemicals into food or drink containers.

If in doubt, stop and call a qualified veterinarian or your national food safety authority. No margin is worth a food-safety incident.

Quick Reference Checklist

  1. Cost your inputs weekly. Chick and feed prices move. Update your unit economics every 7 days, not every season.
  2. Record three numbers daily: mortality, feed consumed, and average bird weight. If you cannot measure it, you cannot manage it.
  3. Secure the buyer before you scale. A signed off-take agreement with a hotel, school, or retailer is worth more than an extra 200 birds.
  4. Invest in cold chain before you invest in volume. Post-harvest loss of 8â15% destroys more margin than any price negotiation.
  5. Maintain a biosecurity routine: footbaths, restricted entry, clean water, and a 14-day downtime between cycles.
  6. Keep a cash reserve of at least one full cycle's feed cost. Feed price shocks are the number-one cause of smallholder failure.
  7. Benchmark quarterly. Compare your FCR, mortality, and days-to-market against Table 1. Close the gap deliberately.
  8. Document everything for finance. Records are what turn your farm from a livelihood into a bankable business.
