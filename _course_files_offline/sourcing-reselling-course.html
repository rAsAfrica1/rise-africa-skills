  • Pesticides, agrochemicals and fertilisers â require registration and certified storage. Never decant into unlabelled containers.
  • Batteries, aerosols and flammable liquids â dangerous goods for air freight; mis-declaration is a prosecutable offence under IATA rules.
  • Counterfeit branded goods â customs seizure, fines, and permanent loss of your trading record.
  • Electrical goods without a valid plug/voltage rating â fire and electrocution risk; check for recognised certification marks.

If a supplier cannot give you a certificate of conformity, an MSDS (for chemicals), or a valid import permit, walk away. Cheap stock is never cheaper than a lawsuit or a hospital bill.

Quick Reference Checklist

  1. Call three suppliers before every order. Compare unit price, MOQ, lead time and payment terms â never buy on the first quote.
  2. Calculate landed cost, not buy price. Add freight, duty, clearing, transport and packaging before you set a selling price.
  3. Set a minimum gross margin of 30%. If the numbers do not clear 30%, change product, supplier or channel â do not "make it up on volume".
  4. Test with a small first order. Buy 20â50 units, sell them, measure real demand and real returns before scaling.
  5. Price in local currency and review weekly. Exchange-rate movement can wipe out your margin in days.
  6. Keep a written stock and cash record. Every unit in, every unit out, every shilling or rand accounted for â separate business money from personal money.
  7. Verify product compliance before listing. Check certification, labelling and import permits for anything regulated.
  8. Track your top 5 SKUs monthly. Kill slow movers fast, reinvest in what turns over quickest.
