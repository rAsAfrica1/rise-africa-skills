â  SAFETY WARNING â Pesticides, Aflatoxins, and Grain Storage

Agrochemicals and mould-contaminated grain are the two most common serious hazards in African post-harvest operations. Follow these non-negotiables:

  • Never store grain with visible mould, discolouration, or a musty smell. Aflatoxin is not destroyed by cooking or milling and is a confirmed liver carcinogen.
  • Wear gloves, a face mask, and long sleeves when handling any pesticide, including "mild" ones. Never mix or apply in an enclosed space.
  • Observe the full pre-harvest interval (PHI) on the label. Selling grain with residues above the maximum residue limit (MRL) can destroy a buyer relationship permanently and trigger export rejection.
  • Store pesticides in a locked, labelled cabinet away from food, feed, seed, and children. Never decant into drink bottles.
  • If a worker shows dizziness, vomiting, or blurred vision after spraying, stop work immediately, remove contaminated clothing, wash with soap and water, and get to a clinic with the product label.
  • Test suspect grain for aflatoxin before selling to processors or exporters. Rapid test kits are available through national bureaus of standards and many agro-dealers.

Quick Reference Checklist

  1. Know your cost per unit before you plant. Build a one-page budget for every enterprise showing seed, fertiliser, labour, transport, and a 10% contingency. If you cannot state your break-even price per tonne, you are not ready to scale.
  2. Secure a buyer or a market signal before harvest. Verbal interest is not a contract. Get price, volume, quality spec, and payment terms in writing, even by WhatsApp confirmation.
  3. Invest in storage before you invest in more land. Hermetic bags and proper drying typically pay back within one to two seasons and protect the crop you already grew.
  4. Dry to safe moisture content. Maize and rice to 13â14%, pulses to 12â13%. Buy or borrow a moisture meter; do not guess by feel.
  5. Track cash weekly, not seasonally. A simple ledger or mobile money statement review every Friday will catch a margin leak before it becomes a loss.
  6. Register and formalise. Business registration, tax compliance, and, where relevant, export certification unlock formal buyers, finance, and insurance.
  7. Insure the assets that would end the business. Index-based crop insurance, livestock cover, and transit insurance are increasingly available through African insurers and mobile platforms.
  8. Train one person on safety and quality. Designate a storage and chemical safety officer. One trained person prevents most aflatoxin and pesticide incidents on a small operation.
