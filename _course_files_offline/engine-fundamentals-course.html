â  SAFETY WARNING â READ BEFORE ANY ENGINE WORK

  • Never run a petrol engine in an enclosed space. Carbon monoxide is odourless and kills within minutes. Workshop doors open, exhaust extracted to outside air.
  • Petrol is a carcinogen and ignites at low temperature. No smoking, no hot work, no mobile phones near open fuel. Store in approved metal cans, never in soft-drink bottles.
  • Diesel at 2,000 bar injection pressure penetrates skin and causes tissue necrosis. Never crack a high-pressure line with your hand. Use paper/cardboard to check for leaks â never fingers.
  • Battery electrolyte (sulphuric acid) causes permanent burns. Wear goggles and gloves. If splashed, flush with clean water for 15 minutes and seek medical help immediately.
  • Used engine oil contains heavy metals and is a suspected carcinogen. Gloves on. Never wash hands in petrol or diesel. Collect used oil and sell to a licensed recycler â it is income, not waste.
  • Antifreeze (ethylene glycol) is sweet-tasting and lethal to children and animals. Store sealed and labelled. Clean every spill.
  • Airbags and fuel systems hold pressure after the ignition is off. Wait at least 10 minutes before disconnecting fuel lines; follow the manufacturer's depressurisation procedure.

Quick Reference Checklist

  1. Quote before you touch. Use Table 1 and Table 2 to price the job â parts, labour, oil, filters and fuel estimate â and get written or WhatsApp approval from the customer.
  2. Check compression first on any misfire or hard-start job. Petrol should be within 10% across cylinders; diesel needs a leak-down or compression test at the manufacturer's spec before you remove anything.
  3. Confirm the engine code, not the vehicle model. Two identical-looking pickups can carry different engines with different torque figures. Photograph the engine plate.
  4. Torque every critical fastener to spec. Head bolts, con-rod bolts, main bearing caps and flywheel bolts are stretch or angle-torque fasteners â never "tight enough".
  5. Measure before you replace. Cylinder bore, piston-to-bore clearance, ring end gap, crank journal diameter and bearing clearance must all be recorded in your job book.
  6. Label every connector, hose and bolt as it comes off. A phone photo with a numbered tag saves hours on reassembly and protects your reputation.
  7. Use fresh oil and a new filter on every rebuild. Prime the oil system before first start; crank with the ignition disabled until oil pressure shows.
  8. Document the job. Photos, torque values, clearances and the final invoice â this is your portfolio and your proof when a customer questions the work.
