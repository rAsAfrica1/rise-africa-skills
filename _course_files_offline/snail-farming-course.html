â  SAFETY WARNING â Handling, Food Safety and Chemical Use

  • Wash hands and utensils thoroughly after handling snails, soil or pen materials. Snails can carry Angiostrongylus cantonensis (rat lungworm) and Salmonella species. Never eat raw or undercooked snail meat.
  • Cook snail meat to an internal temperature of at least 74Â°C (165Â°F) and boil for a minimum of 20 minutes before further preparation. Undercooked snail meat is a documented route for rat lungworm infection, which can cause eosinophilic meningitis.
  • Wear gloves when cleaning pens, handling sick or dead snails, or mixing agricultural lime and calcium supplements. Lime is caustic â protect eyes and skin.
  • Never use unregistered pesticides or molluscicides inside or near snail pens. If you must control predators, use physical barriers (mesh, moats, traps) and approved methods only. Misuse contaminates your stock and destroys your market.
  • Keep children away from treatment chemicals and store all inputs in labelled, locked containers away from feed and food.
  • Report unusual die-offs to your local agricultural extension officer before selling any stock from an affected pen.

Quick Reference Checklist

  1. Confirm your market before you buy stock. Call at least 5 restaurants, hotels, market traders and one exporter. Get prices in writing (WhatsApp screenshots count). No confirmed buyer, no farm.
  2. Price-check 3 suppliers for hatchlings, mesh, feed and calcium before spending. Prices vary 30â60% between regions and seasons.
  3. Build the pen before the snails arrive. Shade, ventilation, escape-proof mesh, and a moisture-retaining substrate (loose topsoil, not sand). Allow 1 pen per 100â150 adult snails.
  4. Set a calcium routine from day one. Crushed oyster shell or limestone in a shallow tray, replenished weekly. Calcium deficiency is the number one cause of slow growth and cracked shells.
  5. Keep a daily record book. Date, feed given, mortality count, temperature, humidity, and any unusual behaviour. This data is what lenders and buyers trust.
  6. Separate your nursery from your grow-out pens. Juveniles need finer feed, higher humidity and protection from adult trampling. Mixing stages slows growth and raises mortality.
  7. Harvest at target weight, not at maximum size. For A. fulica, 100â150 g live weight gives the best price-to-feed ratio. Oversized snails tie up capital and feed.
  8. Reinvest the first two cycles. Retain 20â30% of your best snails as breeders. This cuts your second-cycle stock cost to near zero and compounds your margin.
