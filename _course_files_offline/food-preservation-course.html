Agrochemical handling: If your business involves storage, distribution, or application of pesticides, herbicides, or fertilisers, you must comply with national registration and handling regulations. Always store agrochemicals in a locked, ventilated, separate area â never alongside food, feed, or seed for planting. Wear personal protective equipment (gloves, mask, eye protection, boots) during handling. Follow the label and the WHO/FAO International Code of Conduct on Pesticide Management.

Food safety: Aflatoxin contamination in maize, groundnuts, and other staples is a serious health and trade risk. Ensure adequate drying to below 13% moisture, use hermetic storage, and test for aflatoxin before selling into formal or export markets. Reject mouldy or discoloured grain.

Processing hazards: Milling, drying, and packaging equipment can cause serious injury. Guard all moving parts, train operators, and lock out equipment before maintenance. Ensure dust extraction to prevent respiratory illness and explosion risk in grain dust environments.

Cold chain and refrigeration: Ammonia and other refrigerants are hazardous. Only certified technicians should service refrigeration units. Ensure proper ventilation and leak detection in cold rooms.

Quick Reference Checklist

  • Validate your market before you invest: Confirm at least three real buyers, their volumes, quality specs, and payment terms before committing capital to equipment or storage.
  • Model your working capital cycle: Agri-businesses fail on cash flow, not margin. Map how many days pass between paying farmers and getting paid by buyers, and secure a credit line or off-taker advance to cover it.
  • Measure and reduce post-harvest loss: Set a baseline loss rate for your commodity, then invest in the single highest-impact intervention (hermetic storage, cold chain, or rapid processing).
  • Build quality and traceability systems early: Grade, document, and label. Formal buyers and exporters pay premiums for consistency and traceability â and reject undocumented supply.
  • Comply with food safety and agrochemical regulations: Register your products where required, test for aflatoxin and pesticide residues, and keep handling records.
  • Diversify your buyer base: Do not depend on a single off-taker. Aim for at least three channels (local retail, institutional buyers, export/processor contracts).
  • Invest in farmer relationships and aggregation logistics: Reliable supply at consistent quality is your competitive moat. Pay fairly and on time, and provide inputs or extension support where it strengthens supply.
  • Track unit economics monthly: Know your cost per kilogram, gross margin, and break-even volume. Review against the benchmarks in Table 2 and adjust pricing or costs accordingly.
