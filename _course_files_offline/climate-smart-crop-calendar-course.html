â ï¸ SAFETY WARNING â Agrochemicals and Seed Treatment

Fertilisers, herbicides, and pesticides are hazardous. Ammonium nitrate is both a fire and explosion risk when stored with fuels or in confined spaces. Never store agrochemicals in the same room as food, seed for consumption, or drinking water. Always wear gloves, a face mask, and long sleeves when handling or spraying; wash immediately after. Follow the label rate exactly â over-application burns crops and contaminates groundwater. Do not eat, drink, or smoke while spraying. Keep all chemicals locked away from children and animals. If someone swallows a chemical, call your nearest poison centre or clinic immediately and take the container label with you. In Zimbabwe contact the Medicines Control Authority poison information line; in Kenya call the Kenya Poisons Information Centre. Never re-use empty chemical containers for food or water.

Quick Reference Checklist

  • Confirm your agro-ecological zone and match the planting window in Table 1 â do not plant by the calendar alone, plant by soil moisture and first effective rains.
  • Test your soil before buying fertiliser. A $15 soil test can save $100 in wasted basal dressing.
  • Buy certified seed and check the germination percentage and lot date on the bag; reject torn or damp bags.
  • Budget the full hectare cost from Table 2 and secure at least 60% of input finance before land prep begins.
  • Split your top dressing into two applications (typically 4â6 weeks and 8 weeks after emergence) for better nitrogen use efficiency.
  • Scout for fall armyworm weekly from emergence to tasselling; early detection is the cheapest control.
  • Line up your buyer before harvest â a signed off-take agreement or a confirmed local market beats a full granary with no price.
  • Keep a simple record book of every input cost and every sale. Records are what turn a farmer into a bankable agribusiness.
