Practical Reference

This reference block is built for operators who intend to sell spawn, not just grow mushrooms. Spawn is the highest-margin input in the mushroom value chain because every grower needs it fresh, in volume, and repeatedly. Your job is to deliver consistent, contamination-free product on a predictable schedule. The figures below are drawn from FAO, IITA, and published African mushroom research; confirm local prices with three suppliers before you commit capital, as regional variation is significant.

â  SAFETY WARNING â Sterilisation, Chemicals, and Contamination

  • Never open a pressure cooker or autoclave before pressure has fully dropped. Scalding steam causes severe burns and is a leading injury in home spawn labs.
  • Do not use agricultural fungicides or antibiotics in spawn production. They are illegal in food production in most African countries, contaminate the product, and create resistant moulds.
  • Alcohol and flame work together dangerously. Use 70% ethanol on surfaces, but never near an open flame. Let surfaces dry before flaming tools.
  • Ventilate your work area. Mould spores and sterilisation fumes are harmful. Work in a well-ventilated room and wear a mask when handling dry grain or mouldy bags.
  • Discard contaminated bags safely. Do not compost mouldy spawn near your clean room. Bag it, remove it, and sterilise or bury it far from production.
  • Wash hands and sterilise between batches. Cross-contamination between batches is the fastest way to lose a customer base.

Quick Reference Checklist

  1. Confirm demand before you build. Call at least 10 mushroom growers within 100 km and ask what they pay for spawn, how much they buy, and how often.
  2. Price three suppliers. Grain, bags, and pressure equipment vary widely by region and season. Lock in your cost baseline before quoting customers.
  3. Set up a dedicated clean space. A small room with smooth surfaces, limited traffic, and a still-air box beats a large, dirty room every time.
  4. Master one species first. Oyster (Pleurotus) is the most forgiving entry point. Add button or shiitake only after 6 months of consistent clean batches.
  5. Track contamination rate weekly. Below 5% is professional. Above 10%, stop selling and fix the process before you lose customers.
  6. Pre-sell your first three batches. Take deposits from growers before incubation. This funds production and guarantees buyers.
  7. Package for the farmer, not for storage. Breathable filter bags, clear labelling with production date, species, and batch number.
  8. Build a repeat route. Deliver on a fixed weekly or bi-weekly schedule. Recurring revenue is the real business, not the first sale.
