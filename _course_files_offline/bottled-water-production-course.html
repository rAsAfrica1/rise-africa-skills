â  SAFETY WARNING â Aflatoxin and Chemical Handling

Food safety: Poorly dried or badly stored maize and groundnuts can develop aflatoxins, which are carcinogenic and cause stunting in children. Never sell or consume grain that is mouldy, discoloured, or smells musty. Dry grain to below 13% moisture before storage. Aflatoxin contamination cannot be removed by cooking, boiling, or milling â prevention at harvest and storage is the only reliable control.

Chemical safety: When using pesticides, herbicides, or fumigants (including phosphine tablets for storage):

  • Always wear gloves, a mask, long sleeves, and eye protection. Never handle chemicals with bare hands.
  • Never eat, drink, or smoke while applying chemicals. Wash thoroughly with soap after use.
  • Store chemicals in original labelled containers, locked away from food, feed, and children.
  • Phosphine fumigation is lethal â only trained, licensed operators should use it. Never enter a fumigated store before it has been fully ventilated and tested.
  • Observe pre-harvest intervals and follow national registration rules. Banned or unregistered products must not be used, even if cheaply available.
  • In case of poisoning, contact a health facility immediately and bring the product label.

Quick Reference Checklist

  1. Test your soil before buying fertiliser. A USD 15â30 soil test can save USD 50â100 per hectare in wasted inputs and raise yields through correct NPK and lime application.
  2. Source certified seed from registered agro-dealers. Keep receipts and batch labels. Recycled seed loses vigour and yield potential each season.
  3. Scout fields weekly for fall armyworm and other pests. Early detection cuts control costs by 50% or more compared with late intervention.
  4. Dry grain to below 13% moisture using a moisture meter before storage. This is the single most effective aflatoxin prevention step.
  5. Invest in hermetic storage bags or a metal silo. Payback is typically under one season when price arbitrage between harvest and lean season is captured.
  6. Keep simple records: inputs, labour, yields, and sales. Records are required for credit, insurance, and offtake contracts.
  7. Aggregate with neighbours or a cooperative to meet volume requirements of processors, schools, and institutional buyers â and to negotiate better prices.
  8. Check national registration and maximum residue limits (MRLs) before using any pesticide, especially for export-bound produce. Non-compliance can block entire consignments.
  9. Plan for climate risk: use drought-tolerant varieties, mulch, and consider index-based insurance where available.
