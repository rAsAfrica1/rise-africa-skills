â  Safety Warning: Aflatoxin, Cyanide, and Feed Hygiene

Never feed mouldy or damp ingredients. Groundnut cake, maize, and rice bran can carry aflatoxins â invisible poisons that cause liver damage in fish and pass into the flesh eaten by people. Reject any ingredient with a musty smell, discolouration, or clumping.

Cassava leaves and roots must be sun-dried or soaked to reduce cyanide before use. Raw cassava leaf meal can kill fish and harm workers.

Wear a dust mask and gloves when milling and mixing. Feed dust irritates lungs and eyes. Store finished feed below 12% moisture in sealed bags, off the floor, away from chemicals, fuel, and pesticides.

If fish die suddenly after a new batch, stop feeding immediately, isolate the batch, and have a sample tested. Do not sell suspect feed or fish.

Quick Reference Checklist

  1. Test moisture before milling. Target below 12% for all dry ingredients. A cheap moisture meter pays for itself in one season.
  2. Calculate the formula with the Pearson square before you buy a single bag. Write the formula, cost per kg, and expected FCR in your notebook.
  3. Grind to the correct particle size â fine for fry, coarser for grow-out. Poor grinding wastes feed and slows growth.
  4. Weigh every ingredient. Eyeballing ratios is the fastest way to lose money and fish.
  5. Mix for at least 5 minutes until the colour is uniform. Uneven mixing means some fish get too much protein and others too little.
  6. Dry finished pellets to below 12% moisture, cool completely, then bag. Warm feed sweats inside the bag and moulds.
  7. Record every batch: date, formula, ingredient costs, batch weight, and where it was fed. This is your profit data.
  8. Review FCR monthly. If FCR rises above target, check feed quality, water quality, and fish health in that order.
