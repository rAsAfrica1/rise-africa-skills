Practical Reference

This reference section is built for one purpose: to help you sell analytics as a service. Data science in an African business context is rarely about building the next global AI model. It is about helping a farm, agro-processor, distributor, clinic, or SACCO answer questions that cost them money every month. Who are my best customers? Which product line is quietly losing money? What stock will run out before the next delivery? When you can answer those questions faster and more accurately than the client can, you have a billable service.

Use the diagram and tables below as working tools. The diagram shows the standard analytics workflow you will run on every client engagement. The tables give you real regional and global benchmarks so you can frame your value in numbers a client already recognises. Everything here is designed to be copied into a proposal, a pitch deck, or a pricing conversation.

â  SAFETY WARNING â Personal and Client Data

Data science work puts you in contact with personal, financial and health information. Mishandling it can destroy your reputation and expose you to legal liability under laws such as Kenya's Data Protection Act (2019), Nigeria's Data Protection Act (2023), South Africa's POPIA (2013) and Zimbabwe's Cyber and Data Protection Act (2021). Never email raw client spreadsheets containing names, ID numbers, phone numbers or medical records. Never store client data on a shared or public device. Never publish a dashboard containing identifiable individual records without written client consent. Anonymise or pseudonymise before analysis wherever possible. If you handle health data, treat every record as confidential and keep it on encrypted storage only. When in doubt, ask the client in writing before you copy, move or share any dataset.

Quick Reference Checklist

  • Define the business question first. Write down the one decision the client will make differently after seeing your output. If you cannot name it, you are not ready to collect data.
  • Audit the data before quoting. Open the client's spreadsheet, count the rows, check for duplicates, missing values and mixed units. Quote your price only after this audit.
  • Agree data access in writing. Confirm who owns the data, how you may store it, and what happens to it when the engagement ends. Put this in the contract.
  • Anonymise personal data early. Replace names, phone numbers and ID numbers with codes before you start analysing, and keep the mapping file separately secured.
  • Build one dashboard, not ten. A single clear dashboard the client opens every morning beats a suite of reports nobody reads.
  • Tie every chart to a money number. Revenue, cost, stock value, days of cover, default rate. Charts without a currency figure rarely get renewed.
  • Document your cleaning steps. A short written log of every transformation lets a client or a successor reproduce your work â and justifies your retainer.
  • Propose the retainer at handover. When you deliver the final report, present a monthly support option on the same call. This is where the recurring revenue starts.
