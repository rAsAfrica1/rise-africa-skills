Arabic for Teens

Three consonants make a root, and a root makes a whole family of words.

Ages 12–168 modules 🪨 Starts with no device

Arabic is built on three-letter roots. Take k-t-b and you get 'he wrote', 'book', 'office', 'library' and 'writer' — five words from one root, each with the same three letters sitting inside it. Learn to see the root and Arabic stops being a memory test and becomes a system. This course takes you from dots and joins to reading real signs and writing a real message.

Twenty-eight Arabic letters are built from far fewer shapes, and dots do most of the distinguishing work.

Six Letters That Refuse to Hold Hands

Arabic letters join to both sides — except six, which join only on the right, and those six are why words look like they have gaps.

Arabic has consonants made in the throat and the back of the mouth that no European language uses, and these are what mark out a real speaker.

One Root, Five Words

Capstone: Write and Speak a Real Message

Today you write a short message in your own hand in Arabic script and deliver a full spoken introduction to a real person.

أهلا وسهلا. اسمي… أنا من… أدرس اللغة العربية. شكرا جزيلا.
For a parent or teacher

Your teenager will finish able to read and write joined Arabic script, produce the difficult pharyngeal consonants, recognise the three-letter root system that underlies the whole language, read public signage, and write and speak a short real message. Arabic is an official language across North Africa, widely used in the Sahel and the Horn, one of the working languages of the African Union, and — where it matters to the family — the language of the Qur'an.
