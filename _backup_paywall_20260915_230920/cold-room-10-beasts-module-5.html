Module 5

The Room Inside

Rails, doors, drains, lighting, and the safety rules that decide whether your cold room is a workspace or a trap. Every internal feature has a specific engineering reason behind it.

Lesson 5.1

Rails — the skeleton of the room

The rail is what the room is built around. Get the rail design right first, then size the room around it, not the other way around.

Rail height

A hanging beef carcass on a standard hook needs 2.0–2.2 m clearance from the floor to the underside of the rail. This allows for:

  • The hook itself: 30–40 cm
  • The carcass hanging length: 1.5–1.7 m for a typical 250 kg carcass
  • Floor clearance: 15–20 cm below the carcass so it does not touch anything

Any lower and the carcass drags. Any higher and you need a step to hook on the top.

Rail type

  • Stainless steel — best. Cleanable, food-safe, does not corrode. Standard for meat rooms.
  • Galvanised steel — acceptable. Cheaper. Can corrode at joints over years. Inspect annually.
  • Bare carbon steel — never. Rusts, contaminates meat, and fails within 2 years in a wet environment.

Rail diameter

Use 40 mm outside diameter minimum. Too thin and the hooks wear into the metal; too thick and standard meat hooks will not fit.

Rail support

The rail must be supported every 1.5–2 m. Support brackets must be attached to the wall or ceiling panels through proper backing plates — you cannot hang 300 kg of meat on a rail bolted through a 0.5 mm steel skin. Either run the rail on its own steel frame inside the room, or specify reinforced panel fixings.

The rail rule: the load of 10 carcasses at 250 kg is 2,500 kg. Add the hooks and dynamic load and the total design load is closer to 3,500 kg. The rail and its supports must be designed for that, not for what "looks about right".

Lesson 5.2

The door assembly

The door is covered in Module 3 (envelope). Here, the internal operating features:

Layout

  • Opens outward — mandatory.
  • Internal release handle — mandatory. Every cold room door must be openable from inside.
  • Position on the side furthest from the sun. Never face the door into afternoon sun.
  • Threshold level with the floor — no raised step inside a chilled room where trolleys must roll through.

Strip curtain

Install a plastic strip curtain on the inside face of the door. Cost $50–200. Cuts heat gain through the doorway 40–70% when the door is open. Pays back within 4–8 weeks of operation.

Door switch and light

An internal light switched by the door opening is standard. Do not leave the light on inside the closed room — the heat from a 100 W bulb is a real load on the refrigeration unit.

Lesson 5.3

Floor, drain and wash-down

Floor slope

1:100 (1 cm per metre) toward a single drain. Not steeper — you work on this floor. Not flatter — water will pool.

Drain size and position

  • Diameter — 75 mm minimum. Larger for a kill-room adjacent drain.
  • Strainer basket — mandatory. Catches bone fragments, fat, and hair before they enter the pipe.
  • Position — at the lowest point of the room, away from the door swing, accessible for cleaning.
  • Grease trap — outside the room, between the drain and the discharge point.

Wash-down water supply

A hose point inside the room, fed by a potable water line with a backflow preventer, ideally with a hot water option. Hot water dramatically improves cleaning effectiveness.

Cleaning chemicals

  • Detergent — food-safe, alkaline, foam or liquid.
  • Sanitiser — chlorine at 100–200 ppm, or quaternary ammonium, or peracetic acid.
  • Boot wash — at the door, mandatory for a food room. A simple tray of sanitiser to step through.

Lesson 5.4

Lighting and controls

Lighting

  • LED only. Warm, low-heat, long-life. Do not use incandescent bulbs in a cold room.
  • Sealed IP-rated fixtures. Moisture and food-safe. IP65 minimum.
  • 200–400 lux at working height — enough to inspect meat, cut safely, and see contamination.
  • Door-switch. The light comes on when the door opens and goes off when it closes.

Controls and displays

  • Temperature display inside and outside the room. Two displays. The outside one lets you read the room without opening the door.
  • High-temperature alarm. Audible, outside the room, and loud enough to be heard at the house or office. This is the alarm that saves the meat.
  • Door-ajar alarm. Simple switch that sounds if the door is left open for more than 2–3 minutes.
  • Power failure alarm. Sounds when the grid fails and backup is not running.

Lesson 5.5

Safety — the non-negotiables

Every cold room must satisfy these four safety rules. No exceptions.
  1. Internal door release. Anyone locked inside must be able to open the door from within, even in the dark, even if the power is out. This is not negotiable.
  2. Door opens outward. A door that opens inward can be blocked from outside by a fallen object, a trolley, or a person, trapping whoever is inside.
  3. Emergency light inside. Battery-backed LED that comes on if power fails. A person trapped in a dark cold room is a person in real danger.
  4. Alarm call point outside the door and a means of communication (whistle, bell, or phone) that works when the room is closed.

Additional safe practice

  • Never work alone in a cold room with the door closed. Always have someone outside who knows you are in.
  • Do not disable the alarm to stop nuisance trips. Fix the alarm instead.
  • If the room is over 8 m³, provide a second exit or an emergency access panel from outside.
  • Post emergency contact numbers on the outside of the door. Not inside — inside is where the person who needs them cannot see them.

Knowledge check — Module 5

1. What is the correct rail height above the floor for hanging beef carcasses?

Correct: 2.0–2.2 m. Enough for hook, carcass and floor clearance without a step.

2. Which safety feature is non-negotiable on any cold room door?

Correct: internal release. No exceptions. A door that only opens from outside is a lethal hazard.

3. What slope should the cold room floor have?

Correct: 1:100. Steeper is unsafe to work on; flatter lets water pool.

4. Where should the high-temperature alarm be heard?

Correct: outside, audible far enough. The alarm exists to summon help when the room is failing. If nobody hears it, it does not help.

5. What is the rule about working alone in a cold room?

Correct: never work alone with the door closed. Basic safety practice for any confined space.

Practical assignment — Module 5

Produce a one-page interior layout for your own cold room.

  1. Room dimensions (length × width × height, internal).
  2. Rail layout — single, two rails, or loop. State the linear rail metres and the rail height.
  3. Door position with reasoning — why that wall, that orientation.
  4. Drain position and floor slope direction.
  5. Lighting plan — number and type of fixtures, and their positions.
  6. Control panel position, display position, and where alarms will be heard.
  7. Safety features checklist — internal release, emergency light, door-ajar alarm, external alarm point.
  8. One paragraph (150 words) describing how meat will flow through the room — where it enters, where it hangs, where it is broken down, where it exits.

Marks are weighted toward points 2 and 8. The rail is the skeleton and the flow is the function. Everything else serves those two.
