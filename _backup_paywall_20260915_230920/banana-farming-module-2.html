Sucker types explained

  • Sword sucker: narrow leaves, grows fast, best for planting. Has own root system.
  • Water sucker: broad leaves, weak, slow to establish. Avoid.
  • Pepper sucker: intermediate, acceptable but slower than sword.
  • Tissue culture: produced in a lab, guaranteed disease-free, uniform, and highest yield.

Sucker preparation

  1. Choose suckers 30-60 cm tall with a healthy corm.
  2. Cut the sucker cleanly from the mother plant with a sharp knife.
  3. Trim roots to 5 cm and remove damaged tissue.
  4. Soak in hot water at 52-55 C for 20 minutes to kill weevil eggs and nematodes.
  5. Or dip in a recommended pesticide solution for 5 minutes.
  6. Plant within 48 hours - do not store.
Never plant suckers from a neighbour's field with unknown disease status. Panama disease has destroyed entire regions this way.

For most smallholders, 2.5 x 2.5 m (1,600 plants/ha) is the practical choice.

The TC rule: Tissue-culture plantlets cost 2-3x more than suckers but yield 30-50% more and are guaranteed disease-free. The payback is one crop cycle.
