Maximise 1 Hectare Vegetables

A complete, practical, 7-module course on turning one hectare of land into a profitable vegetable farm — from crop selection and nursery management to irrigation, soil fertility, pest control, and selling for the best price.

7 modules · 40+ lessons · 40+ practical exercises · quiz at the end of every module · free to read
How to use this course: Read one module, then do its practical exercises before moving on. Everything here is written for African smallholders — real input costs, real yields, real market prices. The farm teaches faster than any book.

What you will learn

  • Which 10 crops actually make money on one hectare — and which "popular" crops quietly lose it
  • How to raise seedlings with 90–95% survival using a tray instead of a gamble
  • How to cut water use 30–50% with drip irrigation, mulching, and correct scheduling
  • How to build soil fertility for free with compost, rotation, and cover crops
  • How to prevent 70% of pest and disease problems before you buy a single chemical
  • How to grade, store, price and sell for 20–50% more than a mixed pile at the market
  • How to plan a whole hectare so you have cash flow every month of the year

The 7 modules

Module 1 Planning Your Hectare The mindset, economics and planning behind a profitable hectare — why one well-managed hectare outperforms ten neglected ones. Start module → Module 2 Crops with the Best Returns Tomato, onion, cabbage, carrot, pepper, herbs — which crops actually pay, which quietly lose money, and how to pick for your market. Who this course is for

  • Smallholders with 0.5–2 hectares who want to move from subsistence to commercial vegetable production
  • New farmers who have land and water but no clear plan of what to plant and where to sell it
  • Existing growers who are getting low yields, high losses, or poor prices and want a structured system to fix it
  • Extension officers and trainers who need practical, Africa-specific teaching material with exercises and quizzes
  • Youth and women's groups starting a collective vegetable enterprise on shared land

What you need

  • Access to land — even 0.1 hectares is enough to start the exercises
  • A water source — borehole, well, dam, river, or reliable rainwater harvesting
  • Basic hand tools — hoe, rake, watering can, knapsack sprayer
  • A notebook — the practical exercises depend on writing down what you see and what you spend
  • No prior farming qualification required. If you can read this page, you can do this course.
