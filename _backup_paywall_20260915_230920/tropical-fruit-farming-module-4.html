4. Pineapple and Passion Fruit

Two crops with fast cash flow and strong year-round demand.

Module 4 of 6 · Tropical Fruit Farming for Profit

Pineapple

Pineapple produces fruit in 18-24 months and then keeps producing "ratoon" crops from the same plant. It is drought-tolerant, low-input, and prices stay firm because canned and fresh demand is universal.

MetricValue
Years to first fruit18-24 months
Yield (plant crop)50-80 t/ha
Yield (ratoon crop)40-60 t/ha
Plants per hectare50,000-70,000
Price per fruit$0.40-1.20
Productive life3-6 years from ratoons

Best varieties

VarietySizeBest for
Smooth Cayenne1.5-2.5 kgCanning, juice, export

Planting

  1. Use suckers (from the base), slips (from the stalk), or crowns (top of fruit).
  2. Crowns take longest (24 months). Slips fastest (18 months).
  3. Plant 5-10 cm deep in double rows.
  4. Spacing: 30 cm in row, 60 cm between rows, 90 cm between double-row beds.
  5. Plant at start of rainy season.

Feeding and care

StageProductRate/ha

Pineapple is sensitive to boron and zinc. Foliar sprays twice per season help.

Inducing flowering

Pineapples can be forced to flower with ethrel (ethephon) or calcium carbide. This:

  • Syncs harvest for market delivery
  • Speeds up the crop by 3-4 months
  • Produces uniform fruit size
The pineapple rule: Force flowering on half your plot only. The other half flowers naturally. You get two harvest windows and better price.

Passion fruit

Passion fruit fruits within 7-10 months. It is a climbing vine that produces 2 crops per year and prices are high because demand (juice, fresh) exceeds supply in most African cities.

MetricValue
Years to first fruit7-10 months
Yield per hectare20-40 t/year
Plants per hectare600-1,000
Price per kg$0.80-2.00
Productive life3-5 years
Harvests per year2 (main + secondary)

Trellis system

Passion fruit must have a trellis. Use one of:

  • Wire trellis: Posts every 6 m, 2-3 horizontal wires at 1.5-2 m height. Cost $600-1,200/ha.
  • Living trellis (moringa, gliricidia): Cheaper but competes for water.
  • A-frame trellis: Two posts at an angle, wire on top. Wind-resistant.

Planting

  1. Prepare holes 40 x 40 x 40 cm at 2-3 m within rows, 3 m between rows.
  2. Mix compost into each hole.
  3. Plant at start of rainy season.
  4. Water daily for 2 weeks.
  5. Train vines up a string to the trellis.
  6. Pinch the growing tip at 1 m to encourage branching.

Pruning and training

  • Train 2 main stems up to the wire.
  • Remove laterals below the wire - they produce no fruit.
  • After harvest, prune back to 2-3 buds to encourage new growth for the next crop.
  • Remove dead wood and tangled growth monthly.
