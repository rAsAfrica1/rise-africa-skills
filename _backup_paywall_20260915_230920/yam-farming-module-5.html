Lesson 1

Nematodes — the Hidden Yield Killer

In this lesson
  • Recognise nematode damage in tubers
  • Manage nematodes with clean seed, rotation and organic matter
  • Understand why nematode control is worth the cost

Nematode species

  • Scutellonema bradys: the yam nematode. Causes dry rot, brown lesions, and tuber loss.
  • Pratylenchus spp.: root-lesion nematode. Reduces root function.
  • Meloidogyne spp.: root-knot nematode. Galls on roots and tubers.

Symptoms

  • Small, cracked, or misshapen tubers
  • Brown lesions under the skin
  • Dry rot in stored tubers
  • Yellowing vines
  • General stunting

Management

  1. Clean seed yam. The single most important control. Buy certified seed or inspect the source field.
  2. Rotation. Do not plant yam after yam or after cassava or sweet potato. Rotate with cereals or legumes.
  3. Organic matter. 10–20 t/ha of manure or compost increases soil biological activity, which suppresses nematodes.
  4. Neem cake. 1–2 t/ha, incorporated at planting. Suppresses nematodes as it breaks down.
  5. Hot water treatment of setts: 50–55°C for 10–20 minutes. Kills nematodes on the sett surface.
  6. Fallow. One to two seasons fallow with a grass cover reduces nematode populations.
  7. Resistant varieties. Some yam lines show tolerance — ask your research institute.
The rule: nematodes cost 40–70% of yield if unmanaged. Clean seed and rotation are the foundation. Neem cake and organic matter are your additional lines.
Lesson 2

Anthracnose and Other Foliar Diseases

In this lesson
  • Identify anthracnose symptoms
  • Manage with resistant varieties, sanitation, and sprays
  • Recognise when spray is justified

Anthracnose (Colletotrichum gloeosporioides)

Dark brown to black lesions on leaves, stems, and vines. Leaves yellow, dry, and fall. In severe cases the vine dies back. Worse in wet, humid weather and in dense plantings.

Control

  1. Resistant varieties. The most effective control. Ask your research institute for anthracnose-tolerant lines.
  2. Sanitation. Remove and destroy infected vines. Do not compost.
  3. Wider spacing. Improves airflow, reduces humidity in the canopy.
  4. Fungicide spray. Chlorothalonil, mancozeb, or a copper-based product. Spray at 10–14 day intervals in wet weather. Usually 2–4 sprays per season.
  5. Avoid overhead irrigation. Wets leaves and spreads spores.

Other foliar diseases

  • Yam leaf spot (various fungi): circular brown spots. Manage as for anthracnose.
  • Rust (some species): orange-brown pustules. Rarely yield-limiting.
Lesson 3

Yam Mosaic Virus and Other Viral Diseases

In this lesson
  • Recognise yam mosaic virus
  • Understand why viruses cannot be cured
  • Manage with clean seed and vector control

Yam mosaic virus (YMV)

Mottled or mosaic pattern on leaves, sometimes with vein-banding. Reduces yield 20–60%. Transmitted by aphids and by infected seed yam. Widespread in West Africa.

Other viruses

  • Yam mild mosaic virus (YMMV): milder version, smaller yield loss
  • Dioscorea bacilliform virus: rare

Management

  1. Clean seed yam. The most effective control. Buy from certified producers or from fields that showed no symptoms.
  2. Rogueing. Remove and destroy infected plants within 4 weeks of emergence.
  3. Aphid control. Seed treatment with imidacloprid or thiamethoxam. Foliar spray if heavy pressure.
  4. Resistant varieties. Some tolerant lines exist.

There is no cure for yam viruses. Prevention with clean seed and roguing is the entire strategy.

Lesson 4

Insect Pests of Yam

In this lesson
  • Identify the main insect pests
  • Use cultural and biological control
  • Time chemical sprays correctly

Yam beetle (Heteroligus meles)

The main insect pest of yam in West Africa. Adults feed on the tuber surface in the soil, leaving shallow gouges that reduce market value. Worse in light soils and near rivers.

Control: harvest early, rotate to heavier soils, use of neem cake at planting.

Yam shoot borer

Larva bores into young shoots, causing wilting and death of the growing tip. Control: hand-remove affected shoots, use neem or Bt sprays if heavy.

Mealybugs

Suck sap on vine and tubers. Also transmit some viruses. Control: encourage natural enemies, use neem or soap spray if heavy.

Scale insects

Attach to tubers in storage. Control: clean storage, diatomaceous earth, or hot water treatment before storage.

Termites

Attack the tuber in the soil, especially in dry conditions. Control: keep soil moist, remove crop residue, use treated stakes.

Lesson 5

Seed Yam Certification and Quality Standards

In this lesson
  • Understand seed yam certification systems
  • Meet the standard if selling seed
  • Access the certified seed market

Why certification matters

Certified seed yam commands 2–3x food price. Certification proves the seed is free of nematodes and viruses and is true to variety. Without certification, you sell into the food market at food price.

Standards

  • Size: 100–400 g per tuber, uniform
  • Health: no visible nematode damage, no virus symptoms
  • Variety: true to named variety, uniform
  • Storage: clean, dry, no rot
  • Documentation: field inspection by certification body, followed by post-harvest inspection

Getting certified

  1. Register with your national seed certification agency
  2. Plant certified pre-basic seed yam
  3. Follow isolation, roguing and inspection requirements
  4. Pay inspection fees (typically $50–300 per cycle)
  5. Pass field and post-harvest inspections
  6. Sell certified seed with official documentation

Even without formal certification, producing clean seed yam from your own carefully managed fields earns 1.5–2x food price from neighbouring farmers.

Knowledge check — Module 5

1. What causes 40–70% of yam yield loss?

Correct: nematodes. Clean seed and rotation are the foundation of control.

2. What is the most effective control for yam viruses?

Correct: clean seed + roguing. No cure — prevention is the entire strategy.

3. What is the main insect pest of yam in West Africa?

Correct: yam beetle. Adults feed on tuber surfaces in the soil.

4. How much does certified seed yam sell for?

Correct: 2–3x. Certification proves quality and variety.

5. What treatment kills nematodes on sett surface?

Correct: hot water 50–55°C. Careful — too hot kills the sett.
