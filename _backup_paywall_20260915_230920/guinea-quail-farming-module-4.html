4. Guinea Fowl Health and Disease

Guinea fowl resist most diseases chickens get - but three problems still cost you money.

Module 4 of 12 · Guinea Fowl and Quail Farming

Why guinea fowl are hardier

Guinea fowl have natural resistance to many poultry diseases. They rarely get Newcastle, Marek's, or coccidiosis as severely as chickens. But they are not immune - and three problems regularly cost farmers money.

The three problems that matter

ProblemImpactPrevention
Keet mortality (0-6 weeks)Loses 20-40% of chicksProper brooding, dry, warm, water discipline
Worms and internal parasitesWeight loss, drop in layDeworm every 3 months
Predators (hawks, snakes, dogs, wild cats)Sudden losses of adult birdsCovered run, secure night housing, dogs trained

Newcastle disease

Guinea fowl can catch Newcastle but are much less susceptible than chickens. When they do catch it:

  • Twisted neck, paralysis
  • Gasping, nasal discharge
  • Greenish diarrhoea
  • Sudden drop in laying

Vaccinate keets at day 7, day 21, and week 8. Booster annually with killed vaccine.

Blackhead disease

Blackhead (Histomonas) affects guinea fowl more than chickens. Symptoms:

  • Yellow droppings
  • Drooping head and wings
  • Weight loss
  • Death within 1-2 weeks

Prevention: never house guinea fowl with chickens (chickens are carriers), rotate pasture, deworm regularly.

Worms

Predator control

PredatorCountermeasure
Hawks and eaglesCover the run with wire or shade cloth
Snakes1 cm mesh at base, remove tall grass around pen
Dogs and wild catsSturdy fence, locked night housing
Genets, mongoosesFirm base of run, small mesh

Daily health routine

  1. Morning: check all birds standing, alert, eating
  2. Look for limping, drooping wings, discharge
  3. Check water intake - a drop means a problem
  4. Clean drinkers and feeders
  5. Collect eggs
  6. Evening: count birds into roost - a missing bird means a predator or escape
The keet rule: 80% of guinea fowl losses happen in the first 6 weeks. Get brooding right and the rest is easy.
Practical exercise 4: Start a health log. Record daily: number of birds, any symptoms, any deaths, feed and water intake. After 3 months review the patterns.
