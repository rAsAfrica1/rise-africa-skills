6. Site, Equipment and Operations

Your yard is your factory. A well-organised yard produces more blocks with less effort. A disorganised one wastes time, breaks equipment, and loses money. This module covers layout, machines, staff, safety, and the daily routine that keeps production moving.

Module 6 of 7 · 6 lessons · ~50 minutes · 6 practical exercises · quiz at the end

Lesson 6.1 — Yard layout and workflow

A good brick yard follows a straight flow: materials in, blocks out. If workers cross paths or double back, you lose 20–30% productivity.

Ideal layout:

  1. Entrance and delivery area. Where cement and sand trucks unload.
  2. Storage. Covered cement store (dry, off the ground). Sand pile under shade cloth if possible.
  3. Mixing area. Close to storage. Hard, level surface.
  4. Moulding area. Adjacent to mixing. Space for workers and moulds.
  5. Curing area. Covered or shaded. Water access. Space for 5,000+ blocks curing.
  6. Finished stock area. Away from production, covered where possible.
  7. Loading area. Direct access to road for customer trucks.
  8. Office or shelter. Records, invoices, phone calls.

Key spacing rules:

  • Mixing to moulding: maximum 5 metres walk
  • Moulding to curing: maximum 10 metres walk
  • Curing to finished stock: 10–20 metres walk
  • Finished stock to loading: direct truck access
Practical exercise 6.1: Sketch your yard layout on paper. Mark each area. Look for crossover and bottlenecks. Redesign until materials flow in one direction.

Lesson 6.2 — Equipment inventory and maintenance

Every tool wears out. Maintenance is cheaper than replacement.

EquipmentLifespanMaintenance
Wheelbarrows6–12 monthsGrease axle monthly, patch tyres
Shovels, hoes3–6 monthsSharpen weekly, replace handles
Mixing tubs12–24 monthsRinse daily, patch cracks
Moulds1–3 yearsOil weekly, replace worn faces
Hand-press3–5 yearsGrease daily, tighten bolts weekly
Hydraulic press5–10 yearsOil change monthly, hydraulic fluid yearly
Water pump2–5 yearsClean filter weekly, service yearly
Generator (if used)3–7 yearsOil change every 100 hours

Keep a maintenance log. Every tool, every service. This tells you when to replace rather than repair.

The 50% rule: When annual repair cost exceeds 50% of replacement cost, replace. Do not keep repairing equipment that is costing you more than it earns.
Practical exercise 6.2: List every tool in your yard. Note its age and condition. Plan repairs and replacements for the next 12 months. Budget $200–$500 for maintenance across the year.

Lesson 6.3 — Hiring and managing workers

Brick making is labour-intensive. Every additional worker produces 100–600 blocks/day depending on method. Hiring well multiplies your output; hiring badly eats your margin.

RoleWage range/monthOutput
General labourer$60–$120100–300 blocks/day
Skilled moulder$120–$200400–800 blocks/day
Curing/QC worker$80–$150Oversees 2,000+ blocks/day
Loader/driver$100–$200Handles all deliveries
Supervisor$200–$350Manages the yard

Rules for hiring:

  • Start with a 1-month trial. Paid, clear expectations, review at 30 days.
  • Pay on time, every time. Late wages destroy trust and quality.
  • Written job description. What they do, hours, pay, review date.
  • Register with PAYE and social security. Legal requirement and future protection.
  • Train your standard. Show them how you want the mix, the mould, the cure.
Never hire family on relationship alone. Same rules for everyone. Written job description, trial period, review. If they cannot do the job, you cannot keep them — no matter who they are.
Practical exercise 6.3: Write the job description for your first hire. Five responsibilities, hours, pay range, review period. Even if you are not hiring yet, having this ready speeds up the process.

Lesson 6.4 — Safety and health

Brick making is dusty, heavy, and hot. Injuries and illness cost you time and money. Protect your workers and yourself.

HazardProtection
Cement dustFace masks, ventilated mixing area
Heavy liftingTeam lifting for blocks over 15 kg, back support
Slips and tripsLevel ground, no loose tools, spill cleanup
Sun exposureShade at work stations, water always available
Skin contact with cementGloves, wash hands after work, moisturiser
Dehydration5+ litres of clean water per worker per day
Eye injuriesSafety glasses when chipping or cutting

Legal requirements (in most countries):

  • Workers compensation insurance — mandatory
  • Safe working conditions — employer's duty
  • First aid kit on site
  • Access to clean drinking water and toilets
The safety rule: One serious injury costs more than a year of safety equipment. Invest in gloves, masks, and shade before you scale production.
Practical exercise 6.4: Buy 10 masks, 20 pairs of gloves, and a first aid kit. Total cost $50–$100. This is your baseline safety setup.

Lesson 6.5 — Daily operations routine

Consistency produces results. A daily routine that does not vary is what turns a brick yard into a business.

TimeActivity
6:30–7:00Open yard, brief workers on the day's target
7:00–7:30Materials check — cement, sand, water levels
7:30–12:00Production — mixing, moulding
12:00–13:00Break, water curing of yesterday's blocks
13:00–16:00Production continues, move cured blocks
16:00–16:30Final QC pass on new blocks, water curing
16:30–17:00Cleanup, tool storage, cash count, log entries

Daily records to update:

  • Blocks produced (per worker, per day)
  • Blocks sold (per customer)
  • Materials used (cement bags, sand loads, water litres)
  • Cash in / cash out
  • Issues, repairs, or deviations
Practical exercise 6.5: Write your daily routine. Post it at the yard. Follow it for 7 days without deviation. Adjust after a week, not before.

Lesson 6.6 — Weekly and monthly operations

Beyond daily, you need a weekly rhythm and a monthly review.

Weekly (every Friday afternoon):

  • Count total blocks produced for the week
  • Count total revenue
  • Count total material used
  • Check water and cement stock for the coming week
  • Pay workers if wages are weekly
  • Plan weekend maintenance

Monthly (last working day):

  • Total blocks produced
  • Total revenue
  • Cost breakdown (materials, labour, overhead)
  • Net profit
  • Customer count (new vs repeat)
  • Rejects and reject rate
  • Maintenance done and pending
  • Stock check (cement bags, cured blocks ready to sell)
  • Update price list if input costs changed
The review rule: Every month, ask 3 questions. Is revenue up or down? Is margin up or down? What is one thing to change next month? Write the answers. Track them across months.
Practical exercise 6.6: Set up a simple paper log. Daily, weekly, monthly. Review monthly. After 3 months you will know your business better than any outside consultant could.
