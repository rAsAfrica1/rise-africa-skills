6. Pest, Disease and Predator Control

Snails are slow and soft — which makes them easy targets. Ants, rats, snakes, birds and mites can wipe out a pen in days. Diseases spread fast in warm, damp conditions. This module is about the defenses that keep your snails alive and your farm profitable.

Module 6 of 7 · 6 lessons · ~50 minutes · 6 practical exercises · quiz at the end

Lesson 6.1 — Common predators and their attacks

PredatorWhat it attacksSigns
AntsHatchlings, eggs, juvenilesAnt trails, empty shells, missing hatchlings

Ants are the single biggest threat. In most snail farms, ants cause more loss than all other predators combined. You must build defenses that stop them completely.

Practical exercise 6.1: Walk around your pen every morning. Note any predators you see. Record date, type, and action. Within 2 weeks you will know your local predator load.

Lesson 6.2 — Building the defense system

Defense is layered. No single method stops everything — but combining barriers, physical protection, and hygiene covers all gaps.

Layer 1 — Physical barriers:

  • Water moat. A 10 cm wide, 5 cm deep trench around the pen, filled with water. Ants cannot cross. Refresh weekly.
  • Ash ring. Wood ash on the ground around the pen. Ants avoid it. Refresh after every rain.
  • Oil barrier. Used engine oil on a cloth strip tied around posts. Effective against crawling insects.
  • Mesh sides and top. 5 mm mesh everywhere. Buried 15 cm below ground. No gaps.

Layer 2 — Physical traps:

  • Rat traps. Set around the pen. Peanut butter bait.
  • Snake traps. Funnel traps baited with a raw egg at the entrance.
  • Sticky traps. Around the pen base for crawling insects.

Layer 3 — Environmental hygiene:

  • Short grass around pen. Rats and snakes use tall grass for cover.
  • No food scraps outside. Attracts rats and ants.
  • No standing water near pen. Breeds mosquitoes and other pests.
  • Remove dead snails immediately. Attracts ants and flies.
The layered defense rule: Every pen should have a water moat AND a mesh top AND a physical barrier around the base. If any layer fails, the others still protect. Never rely on one defense alone.
Practical exercise 6.2: Build all three defense layers around your pen. Check daily for the first week. Note which predators attempt to enter, and which layer stops them.

Lesson 6.3 — Ant control — the number one threat

Ants can kill every hatchling in your pen overnight. In some regions, they can also kill adults if the ants are large or the swarm is aggressive.

Effective ant control:

MethodEffectivenessCost
Water moatExcellent for crawling antsFree
Ash ringGood, needs refresh after rainFree
Used engine oil barrierVery goodFree
Diatomaceous earthGood, food-grade onlyLow
Ant bait stationsExcellent for colony destructionLow
Borax + sugar trapsKills whole coloniesVery low

Safe ant trap recipe:

  • 1 part borax (from hardware shop)
  • 3 parts white sugar
  • 1 part water

Mix into a paste. Place in a shallow dish OUTSIDE the pen, away from snails. Ants carry it to the colony, killing the queen and workers over 1–2 weeks.

Never place borax traps inside the snail pen. Borax kills snails. Place them outside, away from snail paths. Wait until the ants are carrying bait — you will see them gather — then leave the trap in place for 2 weeks.
Practical exercise 6.3: Set up 3 ant traps outside your pen using the borax recipe. Check daily. Replace when empty. After 2 weeks, observe whether ant activity has decreased.

Lesson 6.4 — Common diseases and their treatment

DiseaseSymptomsCauseAction
Shell rotSoft, dark patches on shellBacteria, usually from wet beddingReduce humidity. Isolate. Apply dilute iodine.

DehydrationRetracted, immobile snailsHumidity too lowIncrease spraying. Move to shaded area.
MalnutritionThin shells, slow growthPoor diet, no calciumImprove diet. Add calcium.
Respiratory infectionBubbles at shell opening, dischargeBacteria, high humidityIsolate. Reduce humidity. Vet consultation.
Parasitic wormsReduced growth, no visible causeContaminated bedding or foodReplace bedding. Source clean food.

Veterinary care for snails is rare — most vets do not treat them. Prevention is the main strategy:

  • Clean bedding monthly
  • Remove dead snails immediately
  • Quarantine new stock for 30 days
  • Do not feed contaminated food
  • Do not overcrowd
  • Maintain stable conditions
The prevention rule: Snails rarely get sick in clean, well-managed pens. If you see disease, first check conditions — the problem is usually environmental, not pathological. Fix the environment and most disease disappears.
Practical exercise 6.4: Do a monthly deep clean. Remove all bedding, wash the pen with clean water, replace with fresh bedding. Log date and any issues found.

Lesson 6.5 — Sanitation and cleaning routines

FrequencyAction
DailyRemove uneaten food, dead snails, visible waste
WeeklyCheck ant barriers, refresh water moat, inspect mesh
MonthlyPartial bedding change (top 5 cm), spot clean the rest
QuarterlyFull bedding change, wash the pen, dry thoroughly
AnnuallyFull disinfection, structural inspection, repairs

Cleaning routine steps:

  1. Move snails to a temporary holding pen (do not handle directly — use a soft spoon or cloth).
  2. Remove old bedding and dispose far from the pen.
  3. Wash the pen with clean water. If disinfecting, use a mild solution and rinse thoroughly.
  4. Let dry for 1–2 days.
  5. Add fresh bedding mix (see module 3).
  6. Dampen lightly.
  7. Return snails.
Practical exercise 6.5: Schedule quarterly deep cleans on your calendar. Prepare a holding pen before you start. Do your first deep clean this month.

Lesson 6.6 — Preventing escapes and theft

Snails can escape through surprisingly small gaps. And thieves are a real risk once your stock is valuable.

Escape prevention:

  • Mesh sides and top. 5 mm maximum opening. No gaps at corners.
  • Buried base. 15 cm below ground prevents digging.
  • Overhanging top edge. A 5 cm lip prevents climbing out.
  • Slime trail check. If you see slime trails on the outside of the pen, snails have escaped.
  • Daily count. Weigh or count a sample weekly to track population.

Theft prevention:

  • Lock or clip. Secure lid with a padlock or sturdy clips.
  • Visible location. Near your house, visible to family.
  • Fencing. If pen is away from the house, fence with thorn or wire.
  • Neighbours. Let trusted neighbours know what you are doing. Community awareness reduces theft.
  • Dogs. A farm dog deters most human intruders.
  • Don't advertise. Keep your stock levels private. Do not brag about how much your snails are worth.
The neighbor rule: In most African communities, the biggest theft risk is not strangers — it is people who know your operation. Keep your stock private. Do not tell everyone how much you have or how much it is worth.
Practical exercise 6.6: Do a security check of your pen. Are there gaps? Is it locked? Can a thief easily access it? Fix any weaknesses this week.
