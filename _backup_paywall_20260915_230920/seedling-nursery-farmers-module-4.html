4. Nursery Management and Quality

Getting seeds to sprout is easy. Getting 95% of them to grow into strong, sellable seedlings is what separates a hobby from a business. This module covers watering, feeding, hardening off, and the quality standards that make farmers come back every season.

Module 4 of 7 · 6 lessons · ~50 minutes · 6 practical exercises · quiz at the end

Lesson 4.1 — Watering schedules by crop and stage

Overwatering kills more seedlings than underwatering. Learn to read the media before you water.

StageWatering frequencyAmount
Germination (before sprouting)Once daily, morningLight mist — never soak
Week 1 after sproutingOnce daily, morningLight — surface just damp
Weeks 2–4 (growing)Every 1–2 daysDeeper — water runs through tray
Week 5–6 (maturing)Every 2 daysDeeper, less frequent
Hardening off (week 7)Every 2–3 daysDeeper, encourages strong roots

How to check before watering:

  1. Push your finger 2 cm into the media.
  2. If it feels damp — skip watering today.
  3. If it feels dry — water.
  4. In hot weather, check twice daily.
  5. In cool weather, check once.

Signs of overwatering:

  • Yellowing leaves
  • Moss or algae on the media surface
  • Fungus gnats
  • Damping-off disease
  • Slow growth despite good light

Signs of underwatering:

  • Wilting leaves (usually midday)
  • Media pulling away from tray edges
  • Brown leaf tips
  • Stunted growth
The morning rule: Always water in the morning. Evening watering leaves foliage wet overnight — perfect conditions for fungal disease. Water early and let leaves dry before night.
Practical exercise 4.1: Log water quantity and frequency for one tray for 2 weeks. Note seedling response. Adjust based on wilting, yellowing, or growth rate.

Lesson 4.2 — Feeding — when and what

Your growing media should carry seedlings through the first 3 weeks with no extra feeding. After that, seedlings may need a boost.

WeekFeedingNotes
Week 1–3NothingMedia has enough nutrition
Week 4–5Half-strength liquid fertiliserOnce per week, morning
Week 6–7Full-strength liquid fertiliserOnce per week, until hardening

Options for feeding:

  • Commercial liquid fertiliser: Cheap, reliable, easy to apply. Buy from agro-dealer.
  • Compost tea: Free, made from your own compost. Steep compost in water for 3–5 days, strain, dilute 1:10.
  • Manure tea: Same method with aged manure. Excellent for leafy crops.
  • Foliar feed: Dilute fertiliser sprayed on leaves. Fast absorption but easy to burn.
  • Seaweed extract: Excellent for root development and stress tolerance. Purchase from suppliers.

Feeding rules:

  • Never feed dry media. Water first, then feed.
  • Feed in the morning. Allows plants to use nutrients before night.
  • Do not overfeed. More is not better — it burns roots and stunts growth.
  • Stop feeding 5 days before sale. Let plants finish clean.
Never feed seedlings with dry granular fertiliser. It burns the roots and kills young plants. Only use liquid or foliar feeding for seedlings. Granular is for mature plants in the field.
Practical exercise 4.2: Buy 1 litre of liquid fertiliser. Feed half your seedlings once weekly from week 4. Keep the other half unwatered for comparison. Compare growth rates and quality at week 7.

Lesson 4.3 — Transplanting and hardening off

Hardening off is the process of accustoming seedlings to outdoor conditions. Skip it and your seedlings die within days of transplanting — after the customer pays.

Why seedlings need hardening:

  • Nursery conditions are protected: shade, steady temperature, high humidity.
  • Field conditions are harsh: direct sun, wind, temperature swings, dry air.
  • Sudden change causes shock, wilting, sunscald, and death.

The 7-day hardening schedule:

DayExposure
Day 12 hours outdoors, morning sun only
Day 24 hours, morning sun
Day 36 hours, morning and late afternoon sun
Day 48 hours, mostly sun (skip midday hottest 2 hours)
Day 5Full day, all sunlight
Day 6Overnight outdoors
Day 7Ready for sale or transplant

During hardening:

  • Reduce watering. Slightly less, to encourage root growth.
  • Stop feeding. Let plants finish clean.
  • Watch for wilting. If they wilt, move back to shade.
  • Protect from heavy rain. Harden off in dry weather if possible.
The 7-day rule: Never sell unhardened seedlings. A customer who buys soft seedlings has them die in the field. That customer never comes back. Seven days of hardening is the difference between a repeat customer and a lost one.
Practical exercise 4.3: Take 30 seedlings and harden them off using the 7-day schedule. Take 30 others directly outdoors. Compare survival after 5 days in the field.

Lesson 4.5 — Quality standards farmers expect

What makes a seedling "good" in a farmer's eyes?

StandardRequirement
Height10–15 cm for tomatoes, cabbages; varies by crop
Stem thicknessSturdy, not spindly
Leaf colourDeep green, no yellowing
Root systemWell-developed, holds the media plug together
DiseaseNo visible spots, mould, or rot
UniformityAll seedlings the same size and stage
HardenedTolerates full sun
AgeReady for transplant (varies: 4–8 weeks)

The three quality signals farmers notice first:

  1. Uniformity. If a tray has some huge plants and some tiny ones, farmers notice. Uniform trays sell faster.
  2. Root quality. If you pull one seedling, do roots hold the media together? If yes, they are ready. If they fall apart, they are not.
  3. Hardening. A hardened seedling can spend a full day in the sun without wilting. Unhardened seedlings collapse in the heat.
The buyer's test: If you were buying seedlings, would you pay full price for this tray? Be honest. Sell only trays you would buy yourself.
Practical exercise 4.5: Buy a tomato seedling from a competitor. Compare to your own. What is better or worse? Fix your weaknesses. Keep your strengths.

Lesson 4.6 — Nursery records and traceability

Records tell you what works and what does not. Every batch of seedlings should have an entry.

FieldExample
Batch numberTOM-20260915-A
Crop and varietyTomato, Roma VF
Seed source and lotEast-West Seed, lot #E-2841
Media batchMixed 14 Sept
Date sown15 September 2026
Seeds sown1,000 (8 trays)
Germination date22 September
Germination rate920/1,000 = 92%
Feeding scheduleWeeks 4, 5, 6 — liquid fertiliser
Hardening start6 October
Ready for sale13 October
Sold toMr. Moyo, 500 seedlings, $75
NotesDamping-off in 2 cells — replaced media

Why this matters:

  • Traceability. If a customer complains about germination, you can trace back to seed lot, media, or handling.
  • Patterns. Over months, you will see which crops do best, which batches fail, and why.
  • Costing. Records let you know the true cost per batch, per seedling, per season.
  • Business decisions. Without records, you are guessing. With them, you can plan.
Start recording from tray one. A simple paper log is enough. One page per batch. Even one missing field costs you a lesson.
Practical exercise 4.6: Create a paper log. Every batch gets an entry. After 6 months, review: what patterns are visible? What works, what does not?
