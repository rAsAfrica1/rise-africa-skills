4. Pricing and Costing

Pricing is where most brick businesses lose money. They sell lots of blocks and wonder where the profit went. This module teaches you to know your true cost per block, price for real profit, and never fall into a price war.

Module 4 of 7 · 6 lessons · ~50 minutes · 6 practical exercises · quiz at the end

Lesson 4.1 — Full cost per block

Cost categoryPer block (typical)
Cement$0.22
Sand$0.08
Water$0.01
Labour (moulding, curing, moving)$0.10
Site rent allocation$0.03
Tool wear and depreciation$0.02
Transport of raw materials$0.03
Admin, phone, licences$0.02
Waste factor (5–8%)$0.03
True cost per block$0.54

The waste factor is important — some blocks break, some fail QC, some never sell. Include 5–8% always.

Costing rule: Never price from materials cost alone. Include labour, overhead, transport, and waste. Most beginners undercount by 15–25%.
Practical exercise 4.1: Build your own per-block cost table with all nine categories. Post it at your mixing station. Update it monthly as input prices change.

Lesson 4.2 — Pricing for different buyers

Customer typeVolumeTypical price (per block)
Individual homeowner100–1,000$0.85–$1.10 (retail)
Contractor5,000–50,000$0.70–$0.85
Hardware shop reseller5,000–20,000$0.65–$0.80
Developer / institution50,000+$0.60–$0.75 (contract)

Pricing rules:

  • Retail = high margin, small volume. Individual buyers pay the most but slow you down.
  • Wholesale = low margin, high volume. Contractors and shops buy big, expect a discount, and pay faster.
  • Contracts = fixed terms. Written agreements, scheduled deliveries, agreed prices for the duration.
Segment your customers. Do not sell everyone at the same price. Individuals pay retail, contractors get wholesale, institutions sign contracts. Cross-selling destroys your margins.
Practical exercise 4.2: Set your three prices — retail, wholesale, contract. Test them this month. Adjust based on what sells and what does not.

Lesson 4.3 — Bulk pricing and discounts

Discounts build volume but destroy margins if not controlled.

Order sizeSuggested discount
Under 500 blocks0% (full retail)
500–2,000 blocks5–8%
2,000–10,000 blocks10–15%
10,000–50,000 blocks15–20%
50,000+20–25% (contract)

Never discount below your true cost plus a minimum 20% margin. At 10,000 blocks × $0.68 wholesale vs $0.54 cost, you are earning $0.14 per block = $1,400 profit — very healthy. At 10,000 × $0.55 you earn $0.01 per block = $100 profit — barely worth it.

Never use discounting to win a single order. The customer will expect that price forever, and other customers will find out. Set your discount structure and stick to it.
Practical exercise 4.3: Write down your discount tiers. Print them. When a customer haggles, show them the sheet. Fixed pricing is professional pricing.

Lesson 4.4 — Payment terms and deposits

You are not a bank. Your customers must pay before delivery.

Order sizeTypical terms
Under 500 blocks100% on order (full payment)
500–5,00050% deposit, 50% on delivery
5,000–50,00030% deposit, 50% on delivery, 20% net 14 days
50,000+ contract20% deposit, 40% delivery, 40% net 30 days

Rules:

  • No deposit, no production. Do not start making blocks for a customer who has not paid a deposit.
  • Full payment before loading for small orders. Once blocks leave your yard, chasing payment is nearly impossible.
  • Written invoices for every order. Even a handwritten receipt protects you in disputes.
  • Credit only to trusted repeat customers. Never to strangers or first-time buyers.
Cash flow rule: Your raw materials are cash-on-delivery. Your customers are not. Never let the gap exceed 30 days, or you will run short of stock while waiting for payment.
Practical exercise 4.4: Write a simple invoice template. Include: order number, date, customer, quantity, price, deposit, balance due, delivery date. Use it from day one.

Lesson 4.5 — Costing for different block types

Different block sizes and types have different costs. Track them separately.

Block typeCostPriceMargin
Standard 15×20×40 cm$0.54$0.8536%
Large 20×20×40 cm$0.72$1.1537%
Partition 10×20×40 cm$0.36$0.6040%
Pavers 8×10×20 cm$0.18$0.3549%
Decorative coloured$0.75$2.5070%

Pavers and decorative blocks carry much higher margins. They use less material and can be priced for value. This is the secret to real profit in the brick business.

The 20% rule for product mix: The best-performing brick businesses have 20% of revenue from high-margin premium products. Standard blocks pay the bills, but premium products build the wealth.
Practical exercise 4.5: Add one premium product line this month — pavers or decorative blocks. Price it with a 50%+ margin. Track how many you sell.

Lesson 4.6 — When to raise prices

Costs rise. Cement, sand, labour — all go up over time. When they do, prices must rise too.

Rules for raising prices:

  1. Raise when your input costs rise by 10% or more. Do not absorb sustained cost increases — they eat your profit.
  2. Give regular customers 30 days notice. "From 1 March, prices go up by 8%." Respect their planning.
  3. Hold prices on contracts. If you signed a 6-month contract, honour the price. Adjust at renewal.
  4. Raise by small increments. 5–10% increases every 6–12 months are invisible. 30% jumps trigger switching.
  5. Communicate once, calmly. No apologies, no over-explaining. "Our costs have increased and we are adjusting prices." One sentence.
Practical exercise 4.6: Review your prices against your current costs. If margin has dropped below 30%, plan your next price increase. Announce it 30 days ahead.
