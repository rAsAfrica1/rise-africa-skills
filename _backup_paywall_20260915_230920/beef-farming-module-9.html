9. Health and Reproduction Management

Advanced herd health and reproduction - the two drivers that decide whether your beef herd grows or shrinks.

The breeding season

Use a defined breeding season (60-90 days) instead of year-round breeding. Benefits:

  • Calves born in a tight group - easier management
  • Calves uniform in size - better market price
  • Nutrition matched to gestation and calving
  • Easier pregnancy diagnosis
  • Bull cost concentrated

Calving management - advanced

Pre-calving preparation

  • Move cows to a calving paddock close to the house (2-3 weeks pre-calving).
  • Vaccinate cows against enterotoxaemia 4-6 weeks before calving.
  • Deworm 4 weeks before calving.
  • Ensure high BCS (4) at calving.
  • Set up a calving pen for complications.
  • Have a calving kit ready (see below).

Calving kit

  • Obstetrical chains or straps
  • Calving jack (optional but useful)
  • Lubricant
  • Clean gloves
  • Iodine for navel
  • Antibiotics for metritis
  • Calcium solution for milk fever
  • Thermometer
  • Clean bucket and water
  • Ear tags

When to call the vet

  • Over 3 hours of active straining with no calf
  • Abnormal presentation (backwards, legs back)
  • Cow exhausted after 2 hours of straining
  • Uterine torsion suspected (cow strains but nothing presents)
  • Fetal monster (rare, but needs C-section)

Post-calving care

  • Check the placenta within 12 hours - if retained, treat with antibiotic.
  • Check the cow for tearing - especially if assisted.
  • Monitor milk production - low milk means poor nutrition or infection.
  • Observe uterus discharge - normal for 7-10 days, foul smell = infection.
  • Vaccinate calf at 3 months for the first time.
  • Record calving - date, difficulty, calf weight, sex.

Body condition and reproduction

BCS at calvingEffect on re-breeding
1-2 (thin)Long anoestrus (60-100+ days), poor conception
3 (ideal)40-60 day anoestrus, 85-90% conception
4 (good)50-70 day anoestrus, 85-90% conception

Target BCS 3-4 at calving. Cows that calve thin take 2-4 months longer to re-breed - that means one missed calf per cow per year.

The reproduction rule: A cow that does not produce a calf every 12 months is a cost to your farm. Feed cows well, breed on a defined schedule, pregnancy test, and cull open cows. This is how you build a productive herd.
Practical exercise 9: Review your cows. Pregnancy test any unclear. Score body condition of every cow. Mark calving dates for the next 3 months. Cull any cow over 12 months open.
