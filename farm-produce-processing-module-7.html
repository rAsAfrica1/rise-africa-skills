Beetroot Processing

8 lessons · 30 quiz questions · Tubers & Roots

1 Beetroot varieties

Beetroot (Beta vulgaris) is grown for its deep red root and its edible leaves. For processing, the key factor is betanin content — the natural red pigment that makes beetroot valuable as a food colourant.

Detroit Dark Red: Most common processing variety. Uniform round roots, deep red. High pigment. Good for juice and powder.

Boltardy: Bolt-resistant, reliable. Good for early crops. Medium pigment.

Chioggia (candy stripe): Pink-and-white striped inside. Lower pigment, sold as a specialty fresh vegetable, not for processing.

Golden beetroot: Yellow-orange flesh. Used for yellow food colouring (less common).

Choose Detroit Dark Red for juice, powder and natural colourant production.

2 Washing and topping

Beetroot comes out of the ground with soil, leaves and root tails. Handle carefully — bruised beets bleed red juice and lose pigment.

  1. Cut leaves off 2 cm above the root (don't cut flush, or the root bleeds)
  2. Do not cut the tail root before cooking — it prevents bleeding
  3. Soak in cold water for 15 minutes to soften soil
  4. Brush wash gently to remove soil
  5. Sort — remove any with rot, cracks or soft spots

For juice production, some processors peel before juicing. For powder, cook first then peel (skin slips off easily after boiling).

⚠ Never cut the root or tail before boiling — the beet will bleed all its colour into the water.

3 Beetroot juice extraction

Beetroot juice is a popular health drink, endorsed by athletes for nitrate content (improves blood flow).

Process:

  1. Wash, then peel or scrub
  2. Chop into 2 cm cubes
  3. Blanch in steam for 3 minutes (sets colour, softens)
  4. Cool quickly
  5. Feed into a juicer or press
  6. Strain through fine mesh
  7. Add citric acid (0.2%) and lemon juice
  8. Pasteurise at 85°C for 15 seconds
  9. Hot-fill into bottles

Yield: 100 kg beetroot → 55–65 litres juice.

Shelf life (pasteurised, sealed): 6–9 months. Refrigerated after opening: 5–7 days.

4 Pickled beetroot

Pickled beetroot is a traditional product in Europe, Australia and southern Africa.

  1. Boil whole washed beetroot for 40–60 minutes until soft
  2. Cool, then rub skins off (they slide off easily)
  3. Slice or dice into 5 mm pieces
  4. Pack into sterilised glass jars
  5. Cover with pickling liquid: 1 part vinegar (5%), 1 part water, 100 g sugar per litre, 20 g salt
  6. Add spices: cloves, cinnamon stick, black peppercorns
  7. Seal jars and store 2 weeks before eating

Shelf life: 12 months unopened. Refrigerate after opening.

5 Beetroot powder

Beetroot powder is a natural red food colourant, health supplement, and baking ingredient.

  1. Boil beetroot until soft (40–60 minutes)
  2. Cool and peel
  3. Slice into 3 mm pieces
  4. Dry in dehydrator at 55°C for 8–12 hours (or sun-dry 4–5 days)
  5. Target moisture: under 8%
  6. Mill in a spice grinder or hammer mill
  7. Sieve through 250 micron
  8. Package in airtight dark jars or foil bags

Yield: 100 kg fresh → 10–14 kg powder (beetroot is 85% water).

Retail price: $20–$45 per kg. This is one of the most profitable beetroot products.

6 Natural food colouring

Betanin from beetroot is a certified natural food colourant (E162 in the EU). The food industry pays premium prices for clean-label red colourant.

Concentrated colourant:

  1. Juice fresh beetroot
  2. Concentrate by gentle heating until 1/4 volume
  3. Add 0.5% citric acid as stabiliser
  4. Bottle in amber glass

Retail (small bottles): $15–$30 per 250 ml. Bulk to food manufacturers: $8–$15 per litre.

💰 Highest-margin beetroot product when you have a food manufacturer as a customer.

7 Beetroot crisps

Thinly sliced beetroot chips are a popular snack in the health food market.

  1. Peel and slice very thin (1–1.5 mm) — mandolin or slicer
  2. Soak in cold water 10 minutes
  3. Pat dry thoroughly
  4. Fry at 160°C for 60–90 seconds (they burn quickly)
  5. Drain and salt while hot
  6. Or bake at 150°C for 20–25 minutes, flipping once
  7. Cool completely before packing

Yield: 100 kg fresh → 8–12 kg crisps. Retail price: $40–$60 per kg.

8 Packaging and cold chain

Packaging by product:

  • Juice — glass bottles (250 ml, 500 ml) or PET bottles
  • Pickled — glass jars (350 g, 700 g)
  • Powder — dark glass jars, foil pouches, 100 g–1 kg
  • Colourant — amber glass with dropper, 50–250 ml
  • Crisps — foil pouches, 50–100 g

Cold chain:

  • Fresh juice (unpasteurised): 4°C, 5 days
  • Pasteurised juice: room temp until opened, then 4°C
  • Pickled: cool dark storage, 12 months
  • Powder: dry dark storage, 24 months
⚠ Beetroot stains everything. Use stainless steel, glass, or food-grade plastic equipment — never aluminum.
