Sound-Proof / Acoustic Block

8 lessons · 30 quiz questions · Medium difficulty

1 How sound travels through walls

Sound travels through walls in two ways:

1. Direct transmission: Sound waves hit the wall and vibrate it. Dense, heavy walls resist vibration better — that's why concrete is better than timber for sound blocking.

2. Flanking paths: Sound goes around the wall through gaps, cracks, doors, windows, or shared structural elements. A tiny gap can let as much sound through as the whole wall.

Key principle: mass blocks sound. A wall's sound-blocking ability increases with weight per m². Doubling the mass reduces sound transmission by about 6 dB (a noticeable drop).

💡 Acoustic blocks work by being very dense and having tight joints — no gaps.

2 Mass vs absorption

Two different things that people confuse:

Sound insulation (blocking): Stops sound getting from one room to another. Needs mass — heavy, dense walls.

Sound absorption (softening): Reduces echo and reverberation inside a room. Needs soft, porous surfaces — carpets, acoustic panels, curtains.

Acoustic blocks are for insulation — they stop sound passing through. They don't reduce echo inside a room; that needs absorbers.

Acoustic performance is measured in STC (Sound Transmission Class) or Rw (weighted sound reduction index). Higher is better.

  • Standard 100 mm block: STC 40–45 (normal speech audible)
  • Dense acoustic block: STC 50–55 (loud speech muffled)
  • Double wall with cavity: STC 60+ (very private)

3 Heavy aggregate choices

Acoustic blocks use heavy aggregate instead of standard gravel.

Heavy aggregates:

  • Barytes (barium sulfate): Density 4,200 kg/m³. The standard for acoustic concrete. Doubles block weight.
  • Magnetite (iron ore): Density 5,100 kg/m³. Heavier than barytes. Used in hospitals and labs.
  • Iron shot / steel punchings: Density 7,800 kg/m³. Extremely heavy. Only for specialist applications.
  • Haematite: Density 5,000 kg/m³. Common heavy aggregate, cheaper than barytes.

Standard acoustic block mix: normal sand + heavy aggregate + cement. Density target: 2,000–2,500 kg/m³ vs standard 1,800–2,000 kg/m³.

These aggregates cost more than standard gravel. Barytes: $150–$400 per tonne.

4 Dense block moulds

Acoustic blocks are made on the same machines as standard blocks, with same-size moulds.

Key differences:

  • Smaller blocks: Heavier aggregate makes bigger blocks impractical (a 440 × 215 × 100 mm block would weigh 25–30 kg). Use 390 × 190 × 90 mm.
  • Solid, not hollow: Cores reduce mass. Acoustic blocks are solid.
  • Precise faces: Tight tolerance for close-fitting joints.

Vibration needs to be strong to compact the heavy mix. Use a high-frequency vibrating table or a strong press.

Cost of an acoustic block mould: similar to a standard solid block mould, $300–$1,000.

5 Sealing joints

Even a dense block fails acoustically if joints are gaps.

Joint sealing options:

  • Thin-bed mortar: 2–3 mm of special mortar for tight joints. Best acoustic result.
  • Standard mortar: 10 mm joints — worse acoustically. Avoid if possible.
  • Acoustic sealant: Flexible mastic that fills gaps without cracking. Used at tops and edges.
  • Double wall with cavity: Two walls with a 50 mm air gap. Each wall STC 45, together STC 55+. Best result.

Any electrical socket, pipe, or vent through the wall becomes a sound leak. Use acoustic putty pads behind all electrical boxes.

⚠ A wall with one 5 mm gap can lose half its acoustic performance. Seal everything.

6 Testing acoustic performance

Acoustic testing is done in a laboratory, not on site.

Test method:

  1. A wall sample is built between two rooms in a lab.
  2. A calibrated sound source plays in one room.
  3. Sensors measure sound levels in both rooms.
  4. The difference (in dB) is the sound reduction.
  5. Results are converted to STC or Rw.

Test cost: $500–$2,000 per sample. Only needed for certification or major projects.

For most producers, published data from similar mixes is acceptable. Once you know your mix meets a spec, you can quote it without individual testing.

7 Pricing to studios

Acoustic blocks sell for premium pricing to specific markets.

Pricing (Africa, 2026):

  • Standard acoustic block (390 × 190 × 90 mm): $4.00–$7.00 each
  • Premium (higher density): $7.00–$12.00 each
  • Specialist (magnetite, iron shot): $15.00–$30.00 each

Cost to make: $1.50–$2.50 per standard acoustic block. Margin: 60–75%.

The premium is justified by the specialist application — studios, hospitals, schools, and hotels pay for quiet.

8 Specialist markets

Acoustic blocks sell to niche, high-value markets.

Who buys:

  • Recording studios and music venues
  • Hospitals (operating theatres, patient rooms)
  • Schools (classrooms near roads)
  • Hotels (party walls between rooms)
  • Cinemas and auditoriums
  • Office buildings (meeting rooms)
  • Residential party walls (between apartments)
  • Industrial (compressor rooms, generator enclosures)

Selling tools:

  • Technical data sheet with STC/Rw values
  • Sample wall section
  • Photos of completed installations
  • Partnership with acoustic consultants and specialist contractors
  • Calculations for common applications (party walls, music rooms)
💰 Acoustic blocks are a low-volume, high-margin product. Best for a yard that has barytes or magnetite locally available and a network of specialist contractors.
