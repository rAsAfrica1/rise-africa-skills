4. Feeding Horses for Condition and Performance

Horses have delicate digestive systems. Feeding them right is 60% of the skill in horse care.

Module 4 of 12 · Horse Breeding for Profit

Why feeding horses is different

Horses are trickle feeders. Their stomachs are small (only 8-15 L) but they are designed to eat small amounts continuously throughout the day. Unlike cattle, horses cannot vomit - so a bad feed choice can kill them. Getting feeding right is the single most important skill in horse care.

Forage is the foundation

Forage should make up at least 60-70% of the horse's diet by weight. Feeding too little forage causes:

  • Gastric ulcers
  • Colic
  • Behavioural vices (windsucking, cribbing, weaving)
  • Poor condition

Never feed mouldy, dusty, or old feed. Horses have no ability to vomit. Mouldy feed causes colic, which can kill within hours.

Feeding rules

  1. Feed little and often - 2-4 concentrate feeds per day, forage always available.
  2. Never change feed suddenly - introduce over 7-10 days.
  3. Feed by weight, not volume - a scoop of one feed differs from another.
  4. Never feed immediately before or after hard work - wait 1 hour before, 1-2 hours after.
  5. Water before feed - a thirsty horse that fills up on grain can colic.
  6. Feed at the same times every day - routine prevents digestive upset.
  7. Keep feed and water separate - never let hay sit in water.
  8. Weigh the feed - use a scale until you know what a scoop weighs.
  9. Store feed properly - dry, cool, rodent-proof, on pallets.
  10. Never feed dusty hay - soak it or change to haylage.

The forage rule: If you can only afford one thing, buy good hay. A horse fed only on good forage is healthier than one fed poor forage with expensive concentrate on top.

Water

  • Clean water always available.
  • Adult horse drinks 25-45 L per day.
  • Lactating mare can drink 50-70 L.
  • Never restrict water before work - the horse needs hydration.
  • Automatic drinkers are best - they keep water clean.
  • Bucket water must be changed twice daily.

Salt and minerals

  • Provide free-choice salt lick always.
  • Use a horse-specific mineral mix (not cattle or sheep mix - copper levels differ).
  • Additional salt in hot weather or after sweating.
  • Electrolytes after heavy work or in extreme heat.

Never feed a horse lawn clippings or kitchen scraps. Horses are strict herbivores with a delicate digestive system. Stick to forage, grains, and approved concentrates.
