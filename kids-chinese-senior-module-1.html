Blocks, Not Characters

Complicated characters are simple characters stacked together, and once you see the stacking you never look at a character the same way again.

🎒 What you need

  • chalk and a wall, or sand and a stick
  • twenty minutes

✋ Do this

  1. Write 木 mù — tree — large. One trunk, one crossbar, two legs spreading like roots.
  2. Now write it twice side by side: 林 lín. That means woods. Write it three times stacked: 森 sēn. Forest.
  3. Do the same with fire. Write 火 huǒ — a flame with sparks either side. Then double it: 炎 yán, a blaze.
  4. Write 人 rén — person — then put a roof over three of them. Look at 众 zhòng: three people together, meaning 'crowd'.
  5. Write all six characters in a row on your wall and draw arrows showing which simple block builds which complex one. Leave the diagram up.
  6. Explain your diagram out loud to one person. If they can follow it, you have understood it.

💡 Why it works

Chinese characters are a combining system, not a list. Once you learn to decompose a character into its parts, an unfamiliar character stops being noise and becomes a puzzle with visible pieces — which is exactly how literate Chinese readers handle characters they have not met before.

🔥 Challenge

Show someone 木, 林 and 森 without saying anything and see if they can guess the meanings in order. Most people can.
