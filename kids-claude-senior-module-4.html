Ethics and Honesty - What You Can and Cannot Ask

🧠 Brain Opener

A friend asks you to help them cheat. You say no. Claude will not say no. That is why ethics is your job, not the machine's.

  • What is the difference between using AI and cheating?
  • Who decides where the line is?
  • What happens to your reputation if you cross it?
🎁 Reveal a good answer

Cheating is using AI to avoid the work you were supposed to do. Using AI is applying it as a tool to do the work better. The line is drawn by whoever gave you the task - a teacher, a client, a boss. Your reputation is what you have when everything else is gone.

📖 Kofi Almost Cheats

Kofi, fifteen, in Accra, had an essay due the next morning. The topic was "the causes of the 1948 Accra riots." He had not read the textbook. He had not made notes. He had not planned the essay.

He opened Claude and asked: "Write a 1,500-word essay on the causes of the 1948 Accra riots." It gave him a full essay in twenty seconds. It was well written. The facts looked right. He copied it into a document and saved it.

He stared at the screen for a long time. He knew what he was about to do. He thought about his teacher, Mr Adjei. Mr Adjei had taught him for two years. Mr Adjei would know. He always knew.

Kofi deleted the essay. He opened a new document. Then he asked Claude a different question: "What are the three main causes of the 1948 Accra riots? Explain each in one paragraph at a level suitable for a 15-year-old." Claude gave him three paragraphs.

Then Kofi asked: "Give me three specific examples from the period that support each cause." Claude gave him nine examples. He checked two of them against his textbook. They were accurate.

Then Kofi asked: "What is a strong counter-argument to each cause?" Claude gave him three counter-arguments. Now he had material.

Kofi wrote the essay himself. His own intro. His own paragraphs. His own conclusion. He used Claude as a research assistant, not as a ghost writer.

He got a B+. Mr Adjei wrote at the bottom of the page: "Well argued. Your own voice is coming through." Kofi had never been prouder of a B+ in his life.

💡 The Big Idea

Tools do not have ethics - you do

AI will answer any question you ask. It will not say no. That is why the ethical decision is not the AI's - it is yours. Using AI to research, plan, learn, or check is a tool. Using it to impersonate, plagiarise, or lie is cheating. The line is drawn by whoever gave you the task, and you must ask before you cross it.

📚 Three Deep Lessons

1

The three-part test for AI use

Before using AI on any task, ask three questions. First, does the person who gave me this task allow AI? If you are not sure, ask them. Second, could I do this task without AI if I had to? If yes, AI is helping. If no, AI is doing it for you. Third, will I learn anything from using AI on this? If yes, it is education. If no, it is avoidance.

The answers tell you whether you are using a tool or cheating. The same three questions apply whether the task is a school essay, a work report, or a client project.

2

AI in real work: the honest way

In professional work, AI is used openly. Designers use AI for mood boards. Writers use AI for drafts. Coders use AI for boilerplate code. Accountants use AI for reconciliation. Nobody pretends they did it all themselves.

What professionals do not do: they do not deliver AI output as if it were their own original work without disclosure. They do not claim credit for ideas the AI had. They do not use AI to lie about facts, to fake evidence, or to impersonate someone else. The rule is simple: honest work says what it did.

3

Your reputation is your business

You are going to be judged by what you deliver. If your teacher catches you using AI to cheat once, you carry that for years. If your client discovers you delivered AI output without checking it, they leave. If your boss catches you lying about what you did, you are done.

Your reputation compounds faster than money. A student with a reputation for honesty gets better opportunities. A freelancer with a reputation for reliable work gets referred by everyone. Everything else can be rebuilt. Reputation is the one thing you cannot.

🛠 Hands-On - Ethical Review of a Real Task

You need

  • A real task you have this week - homework, project, application
  • A notebook
  • A pen
  1. Write the task in your notebook.
  2. Answer the three questions: does the task allow AI? Could I do it without? Will I learn from it?
  3. If the answers say "AI is allowed and it helps me learn," use AI. If they say "AI is not allowed" or "I would not learn anything," do not use AI.
  4. Do the task.
  5. Write two sentences about how you used AI - or why you did not.
  6. Show the two sentences to the person who gave you the task.
Why it works: Deciding before you start is ethical. Deciding after is defensive. The three questions force the decision to happen at the right time.

🎮 Game - Ethics Trials

4 to 12 players - 20 min

Split into two teams. Each team gets a scenario. Team A: "A student uses Claude to write their university application essay." Team B: "A small business owner uses Claude to write their advertising copy." Each team argues whether the AI use is ethical or not. The class votes. The team that convinces the majority wins. Half the scenarios should have no clear answer - those are the interesting ones.

A pianist can use a metronome to practice. But if the metronome plays the concert for them, the audience is hearing a machine. There is a line between using a tool and being replaced by it.

Chinese

Chengshi means honesty. Cheng means true, shi means real. In business, chengshi is worth more than skill.

🤖 AI-Era Skill

AI does not know the difference between helping and cheating. You must set the standard. AI can help you decide, but only if you ask it clearly.

Try this prompt: "I have a school essay due tomorrow. My teacher has not said whether AI is allowed. What are three ethical ways I can use AI to help me, and three unethical ways? Then help me decide which is which."

Read the answer. Then send an email to your teacher asking the question directly. The AI guess is useful. The teacher answer is the truth.

🏆 Project Challenge

Three Honest Uses and Three Dishonest Ones

Write your own ethical rulebook.

  1. List three ways you use AI that are clearly honest: research, planning, checking facts.
  2. List three ways that would clearly be dishonest: writing assignments, faking evidence, impersonating someone.
  3. List two grey areas - uses you are unsure about.
  4. For each grey area, ask the person who gave you the task.
  5. Write the answer in your notebook next to the grey area.
  6. Show your rulebook to a parent or teacher. Ask them to add one more rule.
