Lessons

1. Writing Compelling Product Titles

A product title is your first chance to grab a buyer's attention. On marketplaces like Jumia, Kilimall, or eBay, the title is what shows up in search results. A strong title includes the brand, model, size, color, and key features. For example, instead of "Phone for sale," write "Samsung Galaxy A12 64GB Black Dual SIM Smartphone - 6.5 inch Display." This tells buyers exactly what you're selling and helps search engines match your listing to their queries.

In Africa, buyers often search using specific terms. If you're selling a Nigerian-made product, include the local name. For instance, "Ankara Fabric 6 Yards - 100% Cotton Wax Print" works better than just "African fabric." Also, consider the platform. On Jumia, titles can be up to 120 characters. On eBay, 80 characters is the sweet spot. Always put the most important keywords first. Use commas or hyphens to separate details, but avoid special characters like ! or * as they can hurt search rankings.

Avoid common mistakes: don't use ALL CAPS (it looks like shouting), don't stuff keywords (e.g., "phone smartphone mobile cell"), and don't include irrelevant words. Also, check the title of similar top-selling items on the platform. For example, if you sell shoes, see how successful sellers format their titles: "Nike Air Force 1 White Sneakers Size 42 - Brand New." Notice they include brand, model, color, size, and condition.

Finally, test and tweak. After listing, monitor which titles get more views. You can use free tools like Google Keyword Planner to find popular search terms in your country. For instance, in Kenya, "sneakers" might be searched more than "trainers." Update your titles every few weeks based on what's working. A good title can increase clicks by 30% or more, so spend time on it.

2. Taking Professional Product Photos

Photos sell products. On any online marketplace, buyers can't touch or inspect the item, so your photos must build trust. You don't need an expensive camera; a modern smartphone (like a Tecno Camon or Samsung Galaxy A series) works fine. The key is lighting. Natural daylight is best. Take photos near a window between 10am and 2pm, but avoid direct sunlight which creates harsh shadows. If you're in a dark room, use a white bedsheet as a backdrop and a desk lamp with a white bulb.

Set up a simple photo studio: a clean table, a white or neutral background (a large cardboard painted white works), and your product. Remove clutter. For small items like jewelry, use a macro lens clip-on (costs about $5 on Jumia) to get close-ups. For clothing, hang the item on a hanger against a plain wall. Show multiple angles: front, back, sides, and close-ups of any flaws. Buyers appreciate honestyif there's a scratch, photograph it. This reduces returns.

Editing is minimal: use free apps like Snapseed or Canva to adjust brightness, crop, and remove blemishes. Don't over-edit to the point where the color is inaccurate. For example, if you sell a red dress, ensure it looks red, not orange. Most platforms require at least 3-5 photos per listing. On Jumia, the main image must have a pure white background. On eBay, you can use any background but a clean look performs better.

Finally, add a scale reference. For example, place a coin or a ruler next to the item so buyers understand size. For shoes, show them on a foot or with a tape measure. This is crucial in Africa where sizes can vary. Also, watermark your photos with your store name to prevent others from stealing them. Consistent, high-quality photos will make your listings stand out and boost sales.

3. Crafting Detailed Item Descriptions

Your item description is where you answer every question a buyer might have. A good description reduces messages and returns. Start with a short summary paragraph: what the item is, its condition, and why it's a great buy. For example, "This is a pre-owned iPhone XR in excellent condition. It has 64GB storage, battery health at 89%, and comes with a charger and original box." Then use bullet points for key features: brand, model, size, color, material, and any defects.

In Africa, buyers often worry about authenticity and durability. Address these directly. If you're selling a phone, mention if it's dual SIM, network compatibility (e.g., works with MTN, Airtel, Safaricom), and whether it's unlocked. For clothing, include measurements in centimeters and inches, and note if it's true to size. For electronics, list voltage (220-240V for most African countries) and plug type.

Include a section on shipping and returns. For example, "Ships within 2 days via G4S or Speedaf. Returns accepted within 7 days if item is not as described." This builds confidence. Also, add a call to action: "Message me for any questions or to request more photos." Use simple languageavoid jargon. Write as if you're explaining to a friend.

Finally, use keywords naturally throughout the description. If you're selling a generator, mention "petrol generator," "portable," "low fuel consumption," and "for home use." This helps with SEO. Don't copy descriptions from other sellers; write your own. A detailed, honest description can increase conversion rates by up to 40%. Take 10 minutes to write it properly.

4. Keyword Research for SEO

SEO (Search Engine Optimization) means using words that buyers type into search bars. If your listing uses the same words, it will appear higher in results. Start by brainstorming terms related to your product. For a handbag, think: "leather handbag," "women's purse," "shoulder bag," "tote bag." Then use free tools to find what people actually search. Google Keyword Planner (requires a Google account) shows search volume by country. For example, in Nigeria, "ankara bag" gets 1,200 searches per month, while "African print purse" gets 300. Choose the higher-volume term.

Another tool is the search bar on the marketplace itself. Type your main keyword and see what auto-complete suggests. On Jumia, typing "solar" shows "solar panel," "solar inverter," "solar battery." These are real searches. Also, look at competing listings that rank high. Note their titles and descriptions. Use a free Chrome extension like "Keywords Everywhere" (limited free credits) to see search volume on Google.

In Africa, consider local languages. In Kenya, "sneakers" is common, but "rubber shoes" is also used. In South Africa, "tekkies" means sneakers. Include synonyms in your description. For example, "Also known as takkies or trainers." This captures more searches. Don't overstuff keywordsuse them naturally. Google penalizes keyword stuffing.

Finally, create a keyword list for each product. Aim for 5-10 relevant terms. Put the most important ones in your title, then in the first paragraph of your description, and in the item specifics. Review your keywords every month. Trends change; what was popular last year may not be now. For instance, "crypto miner" was hot in 2021 but less so now. Stay updated by checking Google Trends for your country.

5. Pricing Strategies for Maximum Profit

Pricing is a balancing act. You want to cover costs, make a profit, and still be competitive. First, calculate your total cost: product cost + shipping to you + packaging + platform fees + shipping to buyer. For example, you buy a phone for 50,000 Naira, pay 2,000 for shipping, 500 for a box, and Jumia charges 10% commission. If you sell for 70,000, your profit is 70,000 - (50,000+2,000+500+7,000) = 10,500 Naira. Always know your break-even point.

Next, research competitor prices. Search for the same item on Jumia, Kilimall, and Facebook Marketplace. Note the range. If most sell for 65,000-75,000, you can price at 72,000 if your item is in better condition or includes extras. But if you're new, price 5-10% lower to attract first buyers. Use psychological pricing: 69,999 instead of 70,000. It feels significantly cheaper.

Consider offering free shipping. Buyers love it. But factor the shipping cost into your price. For example, if shipping costs 2,000, add that to your price and advertise "Free Shipping." This often increases sales. Also, use bundle pricing: sell a phone with a case and screen protector for 75,000 instead of 70,000 for just the phone. The perceived value is higher.

Finally, be flexible. Run promotions during holidays like Black Friday or Ramadan. Offer discounts for bulk purchases. Monitor your sales and adjust. If an item isn't selling after 2 weeks, reduce price by 10%. If it sells fast, you might have priced too lowraise it slightly for the next unit. Keep a spreadsheet of your costs and profits to make informed decisions.

6. Using Item Specifics and Categories

Item specifics are the detailed fields that marketplaces ask you to fill in, like brand, model, size, color, and condition. They are crucial for two reasons: they help buyers filter search results, and they improve your SEO. For example, if a buyer searches for "Nike shoes size 42," listings with those specifics filled in will appear first. If you leave them blank, your item might not show up at all.

Always fill in as many specifics as possible. On eBay, for instance, there are required and optional specifics. Required ones must be filled. Optional ones, like "Style" or "Accents," can still boost visibility. On Jumia, you select from a list. Choose the most accurate options. If you're selling a dress, select "Color: Red," "Size: M," "Material: Cotton," "Style: Casual." Don't guessif you're unsure, measure or check the label.

Categories are equally important. Putting your item in the wrong category means buyers won't find it. For example, a "phone case" should go under "Cell Phones & Accessories > Cases," not under "Cell Phones." If you're unsure, look at where similar items are listed. On Jumia, use the search bar to find your product and see its category breadcrumb. Also, some platforms allow you to suggest a new category if none fit. But usually, it's best to pick the most specific one.

In Africa, some platforms have unique categories. For example, Jumia has a "Jumia Express" category for fast delivery. Kilimall has "Kilimall Global" for imported goods. Make sure you understand these. Finally, review your specifics and categories after listing. If an item isn't getting views, the category might be wrong. Change it and see if traffic improves. Correct categorization can double your views.

7. Practical Reference

Practical Reference: Product Listing and Optimization

In the sourcing and reselling business, your product listing is your digital shopfront. Whether you sell on Jumia, Kilimall, Facebook Marketplace, Etsy, eBay, or your own WhatsApp catalogue, the quality of your listing determines whether buyers click, trust, and pay. This practical reference consolidates the six core lessons of this module into a single working document you can return to every time you list a product. It is written for African resellers operating in markets where mobile-first shopping, price sensitivity, and trust-building are decisive factors.

Optimization is not a one-time task. It is a repeatable process: research keywords, write a title that earns the click, photograph the item professionally, describe it in detail, price it for profit, and categorize it correctly. Each step reinforces the others. A great photo with a weak title fails. A strong title with a poor price fails. This reference shows you how to align all six elements.

Writing Compelling Product Titles

Your title is the first thing a buyer sees in search results. On marketplaces like Jumia and Kilimall, titles are indexed by search engines and internal search algorithms. A strong title follows a formula: Brand + Model + Key Feature + Size/Quantity + Condition. For example, instead of "Phone for sale," write "Samsung Galaxy A14 128GB Dual SIM Android Smartphone Brand New Sealed." Include the words buyers actually type. Avoid ALL CAPS, excessive punctuation, and vague terms like "nice" or "good." Keep titles between 60 and 80 characters for Google visibility, and use the full character limit on marketplaces that allow more.

Taking Professional Product Photos

In African e-commerce, where buyers cannot physically inspect goods, photos are your proof of quality. Use natural daylight, a clean neutral background (white cloth or cardboard works), and shoot from multiple angles: front, back, sides, labels, and any defects. Most smartphones today have sufficient camera quality. Avoid flash, cluttered backgrounds, and watermarks. For fashion and textiles major resale categories in Nigeria, Kenya, and Ghana show the item worn or draped, not just flat. Include a scale reference (a coin, ruler, or hand) for small items. Compress images to under 1MB without visible quality loss to speed up loading on mobile networks.

Crafting Detailed Item Descriptions

Descriptions answer buyer questions before they ask. Structure them in three parts: (1) a short summary paragraph, (2) bullet-point specifications, and (3) condition and shipping notes. Mention measurements, materials, colors, compatibility, and any flaws honestly. In markets where trust is a barrier, transparency about defects actually increases conversion because it signals credibility. Write in simple English, but include local terms buyers search for for example, "ankara fabric" alongside "African print wax cloth."

Keyword Research for SEO

Keyword research tells you what buyers type before they type it. Use free tools: Google Keyword Planner, Google Trends, and the search bars of Jumia, Kilimall, and Etsy (autocomplete suggestions reveal real buyer queries). For African markets, consider multilingual keywords Swahili, Hausa, Yoruba, French, and Pidgin terms can capture underserved traffic. Place your primary keyword in the title, first sentence of the description, and image alt text. Do not keyword-stuff; Google's spam policies penalize unnatural repetition.

Pricing Strategies for Maximum Profit

Price for profit, not just for sale. Calculate your landed cost (product + shipping + import duty + platform fees + packaging). Then apply a markup that reflects your target margin. Common strategies include psychological pricing (KSh 1,999 instead of KSh 2,000), bundle pricing (3 for the price of 2.5), and competitive anchoring (price slightly below the highest visible competitor while offering better photos or faster delivery). Monitor competitor prices monthly, but avoid race-to-the-bottom pricing that erodes margin.

Using Item Specifics and Categories

Item specifics are structured data fields brand, size, color, material, condition that marketplaces use to filter and rank listings. Filling them completely improves discoverability. Choosing the correct category matters even more: a product in the wrong category is invisible to buyers browsing that category. If unsure, search for similar products and note where they are listed. On Etsy and eBay, category selection also affects fee structures.

SAFETY WARNING: Counterfeit and Prohibited Goods

Never list counterfeit branded goods, expired products, or items prohibited by your marketplace or national law. In many African countries, selling fake pharmaceuticals, cosmetics, or electronics carries criminal penalties and platform bans. Verify supplier authenticity before sourcing. If a price seems too good to be true, it usually is. Protect your business reputation one counterfeit listing can destroy years of trust.

Quick Reference Checklist

  1. Title: Brand + Model + Feature + Size + Condition, 6080 characters, no ALL CAPS.
  2. Photos: 5+ images, natural light, clean background, include defects and scale reference.
  3. Description: Summary + bullet specifications + condition/shipping notes, written in simple English.
  4. Keywords: Primary keyword in title, first sentence, and alt text; include local-language terms.
  5. Price: Calculate landed cost, apply target margin, use psychological pricing, review monthly.
  6. Item Specifics: Fill every field brand, size, color, material, condition.
  7. Category: Match to the most specific correct category; check competitor listings if unsure.
  8. Compliance: No counterfeits, no prohibited items, no misleading claims.
