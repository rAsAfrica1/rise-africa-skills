11. Record Keeping and Business Management

Every profitable beef farm runs on numbers. Here is how to run a real business, not a hobby.

Module 11 of 12 · Beef Farming for Profit

Why records matter

Without records, you guess. With records, you know which cows make money, which buyers pay best, and how much you can grow. Most small beef farms fail not from disease, but from lack of financial control.

The six records to keep

RecordWhat to writeHow often
Herd registerDate, source, tag number, breed, sex, costEvery addition
Breeding registerCow ID, bull ID, breeding date, calving date, calf detailsEvery breeding and calving
Feed registerDate, type, kg, cost per kgDaily
Health registerVaccination, deworming, dipping, treatments, deathsEvery event
Sales registerDate, buyer, kg or number, price, paidEvery sale
Expense registerLabour, feed, vet, fencing, transportEvery purchase

Beef is a long cycle. Plan for 6-12 months of negative cash flow. Keep a reserve equal to 3 months of feed.

Never borrow to buy cattle you have not already sold or planned to finish. Debt on unsold stock destroys cash flow. Debt with a confirmed buyer is safe.

Records for decision-making

After one year, your records will tell you:

  • Which cows to keep and which to cull
  • Which months have best market prices
  • Which buyers pay best and which to drop
  • Whether your feed cost per kg gain is competitive
  • Whether you should finish cattle or sell weaners
  • Your cost per kg of beef produced

Simple bookkeeping

Use one notebook. Two columns:

DateCash inCash outBalanceNotes
1 Jan-$500-$500Feed
15 Jan$600-$100Cull cow sold
1 Feb-$300-$200Vet + vaccines

At the end of every month, total the columns. Cash in - cash out = net profit (or loss). Review at month-end.

Compliance and tax

  • Register the business once you exceed household needs.
  • VAT only above the threshold in your country.
  • Food safety certificates needed for selling beef to restaurants and shops.
  • Animal movement permits for cross-district sales.
  • Vaccination records required for auction and cross-border movement.
  • Keep receipts for all input purchases - they are deductible.
The records rule: A farm without records is a hobby. Ten minutes a day of bookkeeping tells you which cows make money, which buyers pay best, and which costs you can cut.
Practical exercise 11: Start a monthly P&L notebook. Record all revenue and expenses for 30 days. Calculate net profit at month-end. Do this for 3 months to see trends.
