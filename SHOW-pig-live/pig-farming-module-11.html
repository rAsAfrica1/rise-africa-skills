Pig Health and Disease Management

Learning Objectives

  • Identify common signs of illness in pigs, such as loss of appetite, lethargy, and abnormal breathing.
  • Implement biosecurity measures to prevent disease introduction and spread on the farm.
  • Develop a vaccination schedule for pigs based on local disease risks.
  • Treat common pig diseases using appropriate medications and dosages.
  • Manage pig waste to reduce environmental contamination and disease transmission.
  • Keep accurate health records for each pig to monitor and improve herd health.

Key Concepts

Recognizing Sick Pigs

Early detection of sick pigs is crucial for effective treatment and preventing spread. Observe your pigs daily for changes in behavior, appetite, and physical appearance. Common signs include coughing, diarrhea, skin lesions, and difficulty breathing. In Africa, diseases like African Swine Fever show sudden death and high fever. Isolate any sick pig immediately and consult a veterinarian.

Biosecurity on the Farm

Biosecurity prevents diseases from entering and spreading. Limit visitors and ensure they wear clean boots and clothing. Quarantine new pigs for at least 30 days before introducing them to the herd. Disinfect equipment and facilities regularly. In African contexts, avoid feeding pigs raw swill that may contain pathogens. Keep wild animals and rodents away from pig pens.

Vaccination and Deworming

Vaccines protect pigs from common diseases like swine fever, erysipelas, and parvovirus. Develop a schedule with your vet based on local prevalence. Deworm pigs every 3-4 months using drugs like ivermectin. In Africa, access to vaccines may be limited, so plan ahead. Proper storage of vaccines (cold chain) is essential. Record all treatments for future reference.

Common Diseases and Treatments

Common pig diseases include African Swine Fever (no treatment, cull), swine erysipelas (treat with penicillin), and mange (treat with ivermectin). Diarrhea in piglets often caused by E. coli can be treated with antibiotics and electrolytes. Always consult a veterinarian before using drugs. Use the correct dosage and withdrawal period to ensure meat safety. Prevent diseases through good hygiene and nutrition.

Waste Management and Hygiene

Proper waste management reduces disease and environmental pollution. Collect manure daily and compost it for use as fertilizer. Ensure pens have good drainage to avoid stagnant water where mosquitoes breed. Wash pens with disinfectant regularly. In Africa, using biogas from pig waste can provide energy. Keep feed and water clean and free from contamination.

Record Keeping for Health

Keeping health records helps track disease patterns and treatment efficacy. Record vaccinations, deworming, illnesses, and medications for each pig or group. Use a simple notebook or farm management app. In Africa, where mobile phones are common, apps like FarmAfric can help. Records also aid in identifying best practices and improving productivity. Share records with your vet for better advice.
