Pig Health and Disease Management

Learning Objectives

  • Identify common pig diseases in Africa and their symptoms
  • Implement biosecurity measures to prevent disease outbreaks
  • Administer basic first aid and treatments for sick pigs
  • Develop a vaccination schedule appropriate for local conditions
  • Recognize when to consult a veterinarian for pig health issues
  • Maintain records of pig health and treatments for better management

Key Concepts

Biosecurity Basics

Biosecurity involves practices that prevent diseases from entering and spreading on your farm. In African contexts, this includes limiting visitors, using footbaths with disinfectant, and quarantining new pigs for at least 30 days. Keep pens clean and avoid sharing equipment with other farms. These measures are cost-effective and crucial for healthy pigs.

Common African Pig Diseases

African Swine Fever (ASF) is a devastating viral disease with no vaccine; it causes high fever, skin hemorrhages, and sudden death. Other common diseases include swine erysipelas, mange, and worms. Early detection is key: watch for reduced feeding, lethargy, and abnormal behavior. Isolate sick pigs immediately and seek veterinary advice.

Vaccination and Deworming

Vaccinate pigs against diseases like erysipelas and leptospirosis where available. Deworm regularly using drugs such as ivermectin or albendazole, especially in free-range systems. Follow local veterinary recommendations for schedules. Keep records of all treatments to track effectiveness and avoid drug residues in meat.

Nutrition for Health

A balanced diet boosts pig immunity. Provide adequate protein, vitamins, and minerals; use locally available feeds like maize, soy, and kitchen waste, but avoid contaminated swill that can spread ASF. Ensure clean water at all times. Malnutrition leads to weak pigs prone to infections.

Housing and Sanitation

Proper housing reduces disease risk. Pens should be well-ventilated, dry, and easy to clean. Use concrete floors if possible, as they are easier to disinfect. Remove manure daily and provide bedding. In hot African climates, ensure shade and cooling to prevent heat stress, which can predispose pigs to illness.

Recognizing and Treating Sick Pigs

Learn to spot signs of illness: loss of appetite, fever, coughing, diarrhea, or skin lesions. Isolate sick pigs and consult a vet. For minor issues, use antibiotics as prescribed, but avoid overuse to prevent resistance. Supportive care includes easy access to water and soft food. Record all treatments and outcomes.

Show answer
Correct: B — There is no vaccine for ASF, so biosecurity and avoiding contaminated feed are crucial. Antibiotics are ineffective against viruses, and allowing visitors increases risk.
