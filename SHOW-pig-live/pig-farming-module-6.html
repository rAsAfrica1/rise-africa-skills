Pig Health, Diseases, and Biosecurity

Learning Objectives

  • Identify common pig diseases in Africa such as African Swine Fever and mange by recognizing their key symptoms.
  • Implement a vaccination schedule for piglets and adult pigs using locally available vaccines.
  • Apply biosecurity measures like footbaths and visitor control to prevent disease introduction.
  • Treat minor pig ailments using affordable and locally sourced remedies under veterinary guidance.
  • Maintain clean housing and waste management to reduce disease risk and improve pig health.
  • Recognize when to call a veterinarian and how to isolate sick pigs to prevent spread.

Key Concepts

Common Pig Diseases in Africa

African Swine Fever (ASF) is a devastating viral disease with no vaccine, causing high mortality; symptoms include high fever, skin hemorrhages, and sudden death. Mange, caused by mites, leads to intense itching, hair loss, and reduced growth. Worms like roundworms cause diarrhea, poor growth, and can be treated with dewormers. Pneumonia, often due to bacteria, results in coughing and labored breathing. Early detection and isolation are crucial to prevent outbreaks.

Vaccination and Deworming Schedule

Piglets should receive iron injections at 3 days to prevent anemia, and deworm at 2 weeks and then monthly until 6 months. Vaccinate against swine fever (where available) at 6 weeks and 10 weeks, and against erysipelas at 8 weeks. Adult pigs need annual booster vaccines. Use only vaccines approved for use in your country, and store them properly in a cool, dark place. Keep records of all treatments for each pig.

Biosecurity on the Farm

Biosecurity prevents diseases from entering and spreading. Limit visitors and ensure they wear clean boots and overalls. Use footbaths with disinfectant at entry points. Quarantine new pigs for at least 30 days before mixing with the herd. Control rodents and wild birds, and avoid feeding uncooked food waste, which can carry ASF. Clean and disinfect pens regularly with affordable disinfectants like bleach or lime.

Treating Sick Pigs

Isolate sick pigs immediately in a separate pen with easy access to water and soft food. For diarrhea, provide oral rehydration salts and a mix of rice water and banana pith. For wounds, clean with salty water and apply honey or iodine. Use antibiotics only when prescribed by a vet, and complete the full course. For mange, apply used engine oil or neem oil topically, and treat the whole herd. Always consult a veterinarian for severe or unknown illnesses.

Housing and Hygiene for Health

Pigs need dry, well-ventilated housing with concrete floors for easy cleaning. Remove manure daily and compost it away from the pens to reduce flies and disease. Provide clean drinking water at all times; use nipple drinkers to prevent contamination. Ensure proper drainage to avoid muddy, wet areas that harbor parasites. Whitewash walls with lime to disinfect and deter insects.

Recognizing and Reporting Notifiable Diseases

Some diseases like ASF and foot-and-mouth disease must be reported to authorities immediately. Signs include sudden deaths, blisters on snout and hooves, and high fever. Do not slaughter or sell sick pigs; instead, isolate and call veterinary services. Bury or burn carcasses deep to prevent spread. Cooperate with government officials during outbreaks to protect your community.
