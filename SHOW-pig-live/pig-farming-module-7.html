Pig Health and Disease Prevention

Learning Objectives

  • Identify common signs of illness in pigs such as loss of appetite, fever, and abnormal behavior
  • Implement biosecurity measures to prevent disease introduction and spread on the farm
  • Develop a vaccination schedule for pigs based on local disease risks
  • Treat common pig diseases using appropriate medications and dosages
  • Practice proper sanitation and disinfection procedures for pig pens and equipment
  • Recognize and manage zoonotic diseases to protect both pigs and human health

Key Concepts

Biosecurity on the Pig Farm

Biosecurity involves measures to prevent the introduction and spread of diseases. In Africa, where farms may be small and resources limited, simple steps like limiting visitors, using footbaths, and quarantining new pigs can be effective. Always isolate new or sick pigs for at least 30 days. Keep pens clean and avoid sharing equipment between farms without disinfection. These practices reduce the risk of costly disease outbreaks.

Common Pig Diseases in Africa

African swine fever (ASF) is a devastating viral disease with no vaccine; prevention is key. Other common diseases include African swine fever, swine erysipelas, and parasitic infections like mange. Bacterial diseases such as pneumonia and diarrhea are also prevalent. Early detection and treatment can save pigs. Work with local veterinary services to identify and manage diseases.

Vaccination and Deworming Schedules

Vaccines are available for diseases like swine erysipelas, pasteurellosis, and parvovirus. In Africa, vaccination schedules should be tailored to local disease prevalence. Deworm pigs every 3-4 months using drugs like ivermectin or albendazole. Always consult a veterinarian for correct dosages and timing. Keep records of all treatments for future reference.

Recognizing Sick Pigs

Healthy pigs are active, eat well, and have smooth coats. Sick pigs may isolate themselves, eat less, have dull eyes, or show abnormal breathing. Check for diarrhea, vomiting, or skin lesions. Early signs like reduced feed intake can indicate illness. Isolate sick pigs immediately and seek veterinary advice.

Sanitation and Disinfection

Regular cleaning of pens removes organic matter that harbors pathogens. Use disinfectants like bleach or quaternary ammonium compounds, following label instructions. Allow pens to dry before reintroducing pigs. Disinfect boots, tools, and vehicles entering the farm. Proper sanitation reduces disease pressure and improves pig health.

Zoonotic Diseases and Human Health

Some pig diseases can spread to humans, such as brucellosis and salmonellosis. Handle sick pigs with gloves and wash hands thoroughly after contact. Cook pork well to kill pathogens. Vaccinate pigs against diseases that can affect humans where possible. Protect farm workers and family members by practicing good hygiene.
