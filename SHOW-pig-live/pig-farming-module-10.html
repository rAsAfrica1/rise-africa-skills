Module 10: Pig Health and Disease Management

Learning Objectives

  • Identify common pig diseases in Africa, including African Swine Fever, and their symptoms.
  • Implement biosecurity measures to prevent disease introduction and spread on smallholder farms.
  • Develop a vaccination schedule for pigs based on local disease prevalence and available vaccines.
  • Recognize signs of sick pigs and take appropriate isolation and treatment actions.
  • Practice proper hygiene and sanitation in pig pens to reduce disease risk.
  • Manage pig health records and collaborate with veterinary professionals for disease control.

Key Concepts

Common Pig Diseases in Africa

African Swine Fever (ASF) is a highly contagious viral disease with no vaccine, causing high mortality. Other common diseases include swine erysipelas, pasteurellosis, and parasitic infections like mange. Early detection and reporting to veterinary authorities are crucial to prevent outbreaks. Smallholder farmers should be aware of symptoms such as fever, loss of appetite, skin lesions, and sudden death.

Biosecurity on Smallholder Pig Farms

Biosecurity involves measures to prevent pathogens from entering and spreading. Key practices include limiting visitors, disinfecting boots and equipment, and quarantining new pigs for at least 30 days. Avoid feeding uncooked food waste, which can transmit ASF. Keep pens clean and control rodents and wild birds that may carry diseases.

Vaccination and Parasite Control

Vaccines are available for diseases like erysipelas, pasteurellosis, and classical swine fever (where permitted). Follow a vaccination schedule recommended by local veterinarians. Regular deworming with drugs like ivermectin controls internal parasites. External parasites like ticks and lice can be controlled with acaricides and good hygiene.

Recognizing and Isolating Sick Pigs

Observe pigs daily for signs of illness: reduced feeding, lethargy, coughing, diarrhea, or skin abnormalities. Isolate sick pigs immediately in a separate pen to prevent spread. Consult a veterinarian for diagnosis and treatment. Keep records of symptoms and treatments for future reference.

Hygiene and Sanitation Practices

Clean pens regularly, removing manure and soiled bedding. Disinfect with appropriate chemicals like bleach or lime. Ensure proper drainage to avoid wet, muddy areas that harbor pathogens. Provide clean drinking water and feed from clean troughs. Practice handwashing after handling pigs.

Working with Veterinary Services

Establish a relationship with local veterinary officers for advice and emergency support. Report unusual deaths or disease outbreaks immediately to authorities. Participate in community disease surveillance programs. Use only approved drugs and follow withdrawal periods for meat safety.
