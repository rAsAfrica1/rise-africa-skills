Pig Housing, Feeding, and Health

Learning Objectives

  • Design and construct a cost-effective pig pen using locally available materials like bamboo, mud, and thatch.
  • Formulate balanced pig rations using affordable feed ingredients such as maize, cassava, and local protein sources.
  • Implement a routine health program including vaccination, deworming, and hygiene practices to prevent common diseases.
  • Identify signs of common pig diseases early and apply appropriate treatment or seek veterinary assistance.
  • Manage pig waste to produce compost and biogas, reducing environmental impact and generating extra income.
  • Keep simple records of feed consumption, growth rates, and health treatments to monitor pig performance and profitability.

Key Concepts

Affordable Pig Pen Construction

A good pig pen protects pigs from predators, thieves, and extreme weather while allowing easy cleaning and feeding. In rural Africa, you can build pens using bamboo, wooden poles, mud bricks, and thatched roofs. The floor should be slightly sloped and made of concrete or packed earth for easy drainage, and walls should be at least 1 meter high with a secure door. Ensure proper ventilation to reduce ammonia buildup and provide space of about 1.5 to 2 square meters per pig. Locate the pen away from homes to minimize odors and flies.

Local Feed Formulation

Pigs need a balanced diet with energy, protein, vitamins, and minerals for fast growth. You can mix your own feed using locally available ingredients like maize, cassava peels, sweet potato vines, and protein sources such as soybean meal, fish meal, or sunflower cake. A typical ration for growing pigs might include 60% energy feeds, 25% protein feeds, and 15% mineral and vitamin supplements. Always provide clean water and add kitchen leftovers or garden vegetables to reduce feed costs. Avoid moldy or spoiled feed to prevent poisoning.

Routine Health Management

Preventing diseases is cheaper than treating them, so establish a health routine from the start. Vaccinate pigs against common diseases like African Swine Fever (where vaccines exist), swine erysipelas, and pasteurellosis, following local veterinary guidelines. Deworm every 3-4 months using affordable dewormers like albendazole or ivermectin. Clean pens daily, disinfect with lime or bleach solution, and isolate new or sick pigs for at least two weeks. Watch for signs of illness such as loss of appetite, diarrhea, coughing, or skin lesions, and act quickly.

Recognizing and Treating Common Diseases

Common pig diseases in Africa include African Swine Fever (ASF), swine flu, mange, and worm infestations. ASF has no cure and spreads rapidly, so report sudden deaths to veterinary authorities immediately. For treatable bacterial infections like pneumonia or diarrhea, use antibiotics such as oxytetracycline or penicillin under veterinary guidance. Mange, caused by mites, can be treated with ivermectin injections or topical acaricides. Always complete the full course of treatment and observe withdrawal periods before selling meat.

Waste Management and Biogas

Pig waste can be a valuable resource rather than a nuisance. Collect manure and urine in a concrete pit or directly into a biogas digester to produce methane for cooking and lighting. The slurry from the digester is an excellent organic fertilizer for crops, increasing yields without chemical inputs. Alternatively, compost manure with crop residues to create nutrient-rich compost. This reduces odors, flies, and environmental pollution while saving money on fuel and fertilizer. Selling excess compost or biogas can generate additional income.

Record Keeping for Profitability

Keeping simple records helps you track expenses, income, and pig performance to make informed decisions. Record daily feed consumption, weight gain, health treatments, and breeding dates in a notebook or on a mobile app. Calculate feed conversion ratio (feed consumed divided by weight gained) to assess efficiency. Monitor costs of feed, medicine, and labor against sales of pigs or pork. This data helps you identify problems early, set realistic prices, and plan for expansion. Good records also make it easier to access loans or grants.
