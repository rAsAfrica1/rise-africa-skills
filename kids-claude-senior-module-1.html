🧠 Brain Opener

A real client does not want a conversation. They want a finished thing: a poster, a letter, a spreadsheet, a plan. Claude is a tool for making finished things - if you know how to steer it.

  • What is the difference between a chat with Claude and a deliverable made with Claude?
  • Why does the client care about the deliverable and not the process?
  • What makes the difference between a professional user and an amateur user of AI?
🎁 Reveal a good answer

A chat is a conversation. A deliverable is a file or document you hand over. The client pays for the deliverable, not the chat. A professional user of AI takes the raw output, checks it, formats it, and delivers it as their own work - and takes responsibility for it.

📖 Tanaka Delivers the Poster

Tanaka was fifteen, in Bulawayo, and had been using Claude for a year. He had asked it a hundred questions - how to fix a bicycle, why the sky was blue, what to write for a school essay. He thought he knew how to use it.

Then his neighbour Mrs Ncube asked him to make a poster for her hair salon. She paid him five dollars. "Something modern," she said. "Something that makes people stop and look."

Tanaka sat down with the phone and asked Claude to write a poster. Claude gave him four paragraphs of text. He copied them into a document, printed it out, and took it to Mrs Ncube.

She looked at it for three seconds. "This is not a poster," she said. "This is a letter. A poster has a big headline. It has the shop name huge. It has the prices. It has a phone number you can see from across the road."

Tanaka went home embarrassed. Then he sat down and thought properly.

He asked Claude: "What are the six things a small business poster must contain?" Claude gave him a list. He asked: "Write me three headline options for a hair salon called Glow, in Bulawayo, aimed at women aged 20 to 40." Claude gave him three. He chose one. He asked: "Now write six short service lines and prices." Claude did.

Then Tanaka built the poster himself. He put the headline at the top, huge. He put the services in a column. He put the phone number at the bottom. He added a border. He printed it.

Mrs Ncube looked at the new poster. She smiled. "Now that," she said, "is a poster." She paid him the five dollars and asked him to make fifty copies.

Tanaka learned the real lesson that day. Claude is not the deliverable. Claude is the assistant that helps you make the deliverable.

💡 The Big Idea

A chat is not a deliverable

Anyone can open Claude and ask it questions. The professional user opens Claude with a specific finished thing in mind, extracts exactly what is needed from the AI, checks every fact and every word, and then builds the final deliverable with their own judgement. The client pays for the deliverable, not the chat.

📚 Three Deep Lessons

1

What is a deliverable?

A deliverable is a finished thing you hand to someone else. A poster. A letter. A spreadsheet. A five-page report. A three-minute video. It has a beginning, an end, and a purpose. It is not a conversation and it is not a draft.

A chat with Claude is useful for thinking. A deliverable is useful for doing. Professional users always know which one they are working toward. If you are chatting without a deliverable in mind, you are just talking to a machine. If you have a deliverable in mind, every prompt becomes a tool toward it.

2

The three-step loop

Every AI-assisted deliverable follows the same loop: extract, check, build. First you extract - get the raw material from Claude: ideas, structures, words, data. Then you check - every fact, every name, every number, every claim. Then you build - you assemble the deliverable in your own document, with your own layout, using your own judgement.

Most people skip the check step. This is the difference between a professional and an amateur. The professional checks the names, the phone numbers, the spelling, the facts. The amateur copies and pastes. Guess which one keeps the client.

3

Own the output

When you hand a deliverable to a client, you are saying: this is correct and this is mine. If a phone number is wrong, that is your fault, not Claude's. If the spelling is wrong, that is your fault. If the client wanted a poster and you delivered a letter, that is your fault.

This is the most important rule of professional AI use: take full responsibility for the output, even though you did not write every word. Your name is on the deliverable. Your reputation is on the line. This is the difference between an assistant and a business.

🛠 Hands-On - Turn One AI Chat into a Real Deliverable

You need

  • A phone or laptop with Claude open
  • A piece of A4 paper
  • A pen
  • One small real-world task (poster, letter, invitation, price list)
  1. Pick one real thing someone actually needs - ask a family member.
  2. Write a one-line brief: "Make a [thing] for [person] that [purpose]."
  3. Ask Claude for the six things that thing must contain.
  4. Ask Claude for three versions of the main text.
  5. Pick the best one. Check every fact. Fix every mistake.
  6. Build the deliverable by hand or on the phone. Print it. Hand it over.
Why it works: The whole point is to make something real. Practice with fake tasks teaches nothing. Practice with real tasks teaches everything.

🎮 Game - Deliverable Sprint

2 to 6 players - 25 min

Give every player the same brief - "make a one-page poster for a school event." Everyone has 20 minutes. At the end, all posters are laid out. The class votes on the winner using three criteria: (1) can you read the headline from across the room, (2) is the phone number visible, (3) does it say what the event is. The winner gets a paper crown and the losing players copy the winning layout into their notebooks.

🤖 AI-Era Skill

Every AI output is a draft. The professional eye is what turns a draft into a deliverable.

Try this prompt: "Here is a poster text I made with an AI. Read it as a client would. List every problem, every unclear word, and every missing piece of information. Then rewrite it as a finished poster."

Compare the AI rewrite with your own rewrite. If yours is better, keep yours. If the AI one is better, keep the AI one. Either way, you are now the editor.

🏆 Project Challenge

Deliver Three Real Things

Do not practice on fake briefs. Deliver three real things to three real people this week.

  1. Ask three family members or neighbours what they need: a poster, a letter, a price list, an invitation.
  2. For each one, run the extract-check-build loop.
  3. Deliver the finished thing by hand.
  4. Ask the person one question: "What would make this better?"
  5. Write down their answers. Each answer is a lesson.
  6. Do the second deliverable better than the first. The third better than the second.
