Permeable Paving Block

8 lessons · 30 quiz questions · Easy difficulty

1 Why permeability matters

Standard concrete paving is impermeable — water runs off the surface, overloading storm drains and causing urban flooding. Many cities now require permeable paving on driveways, car parks and public spaces.

What permeable paving does:

  • Allows water to infiltrate into the ground below
  • Reduces stormwater runoff by 50–90%
  • Recharges local groundwater
  • Reduces urban flooding
  • Lowers heat island effect (wet pavement is cooler)

Permeable blocks have wider gaps between them or open cells through which water drains. The blocks themselves can also be porous (pervious concrete).

💡 Regulation-driven market. As cities enforce permeable paving rules, demand grows automatically.

2 Gap-width design

Permeable paving works through gaps between blocks. Two design approaches:

Wide-joint permeable paving:

  • Blocks are standard shape, laid with 10–20 mm gaps
  • Gaps filled with gravel or grass
  • Simplest system — uses standard block moulds
  • Open area: 15–25%

Open-cell permeable paving:

  • Blocks have cells or holes through the body
  • Similar to grass pavers (Module 13)
  • Higher infiltration rate
  • Requires custom moulds
  • Open area: 20–40%

For small producers: wide-joint paving is easier — same moulds as standard pavers, just different laying.

3 Concrete mix for permeable pavers

The mix must be strong enough for traffic but open enough for water.

Two mix options:

Option 1 — Pervious concrete mix (block itself is porous):

  • Cement: 1 part
  • Single-size aggregate (6–10 mm): 4 parts
  • Water: 0.3 (very low, just to coat aggregate)
  • No sand
  • Density: 1,600–1,900 kg/m³

Water drains through the block itself. Strength: 10–20 N/mm².

Option 2 — Solid block with wide joints (recommended for small producers):

  • Same mix as standard pavers (Module 9)
  • Standard block density and strength
  • Water drains through gaps, not through the block

Option 2 is what most small producers do. Simpler, cheaper, works.

4 Layer installation

Permeable paving is a system — not just blocks.

Layers from bottom up:

  1. Subgrade: Native soil, compacted. Must be permeable (not clay).
  2. Sub-base: 150–300 mm of clean 20–40 mm crushed stone. This is the water storage layer.
  3. Bedding layer: 30–50 mm of 2–6 mm clean gravel. Blocks sit on this.
  4. Paving blocks: Laid with 10–20 mm gaps.
  5. Joint fill: 2–6 mm gravel swept into gaps, or soil + grass seed.

Compaction of each layer is critical — uncompacted layers sink and cause uneven paving.

⚠ Never use sand or fine material in the base layers. It clogs and stops the drainage.

5 Sub-base preparation

The sub-base is the foundation. Get it wrong and the whole paving fails.

Steps:

  1. Excavate to required depth (typically 300–500 mm total)
  2. Compact subgrade with a plate compactor (4–6 passes)
  3. Install a geotextile separation layer (prevents soil from clogging the stone)
  4. Add 150–300 mm of 20–40 mm clean crushed stone in 100 mm layers
  5. Compact each layer (4 passes with plate compactor)
  6. Check final level with a string line

Sub-base thickness depends on load:

  • Footpath: 100 mm
  • Driveway: 200 mm
  • Car park: 250 mm
  • Truck access: 300–400 mm

6 Testing infiltration rates

Permeability is measured in mm/hour — how fast water drains through the paving.

Simple field test:

  1. Cut a 300 mm square piece of plastic or metal with sides (like a shallow box)
  2. Place on the paving, seal edges with clay or sand
  3. Pour 5 litres of water into the box
  4. Time how long it takes to disappear
  5. Calculate: infiltration rate = (volume × 1000) / (area × time in hours)

Example: 5 litres draining through 0.09 m² in 60 seconds = 3,333 mm/hour. That's excellent.

Target infiltration rates:

  • Footpath: 500+ mm/hour
  • Driveway: 1,000+ mm/hour
  • Car park: 2,000+ mm/hour

If infiltration drops, the joints are clogged. Clean with a leaf blower or pressure washer.

7 Local drainage regulations

Permeable paving is often required by local regulation. Know your local rules.

Typical regulations:

  • New driveways over 5 m² must be permeable (UK, parts of US)
  • Commercial car parks must include permeable surfaces (many cities)
  • Stormwater management plans required for new builds
  • Runoff coefficient limits (e.g. 0.3 max for new development)

Permeable paving meets these rules. Standard concrete paving often doesn't.

This creates a captive market — homeowners and developers must buy permeable products to get planning approval. Price accordingly.

8 Marketing to councils

Councils specify permeable paving for:

  • Public footpaths
  • Council-owned car parks
  • Parks and public spaces
  • School playgrounds
  • Roundabout surrounds and verges

How to win council contracts:

  • Get on the council supplier list
  • Provide test data showing infiltration rates meet spec
  • Offer competitive pricing at volume
  • Reliable delivery and quality
  • Offer installation service or partner with installers

Pricing (Africa, 2026):

  • Standard permeable paver: $10–$18 per m²
  • Premium/coloured: $15–$25 per m²
  • Delivered + laid: add $5–$10 per m²

Cost to make: $5–$9 per m². Margin: 40–55%.

💰 Permeable paving is the fastest-growing category in landscaping. Regulation-driven demand means steady orders once you're established with councils and landscapers.
