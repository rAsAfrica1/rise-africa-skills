11. Record Keeping and Costing

Rice is a business. Every kg and every dollar has to be tracked - this is how you know if you are making money.

Module 11 of 12 · Rice Farming for Profit

Why records matter

Rice farming involves significant investment - seed, fertiliser, water, labour, milling. Without records, you cannot tell if your crop is profitable or a hobby. Most smallholder rice farms lose money and do not know it.

The eight records every rice farm must keep

Field registerPlot ID, area, variety, planting date, harvest dateEvery season

Break-even price

Break-even price = total cost per hectare ÷ yield per hectare.

ScenarioCost/haYieldBreak-even paddy price
Irrigated, good management$1,3606 t$0.227/kg
Irrigated, average$1,3004 t$0.325/kg
Rain-fed, low input$7003 t$0.233/kg
Upland, low input$5001.5 t$0.333/kg

If the market price for paddy is $0.40/kg and your break-even is $0.23/kg, you are profitable. If it falls below $0.30/kg, you are losing money on low-yield plots.

Know your break-even before you plant. Never sell below break-even, even in a glut. Learning your numbers protects you from selling at a loss.

If you cannot finance the whole season, reduce area instead of cutting inputs. Cutting inputs reduces yield more than the cost saved.

Bank and loan documentation

If you seek credit for rice farming, be ready with:

  • Field records from previous seasons (yield, revenue)
  • Cost of production calculation
  • Sales records or buyer contracts
  • Collateral (land title, or group guarantee)
  • Business plan for expansion

Banks lend more easily to farmers with records. This is one of the biggest advantages of good bookkeeping.

Tax considerations

  • Smallholders under the VAT threshold usually pay no VAT.
  • Register with the tax authority when you sell commercially.
  • Keep receipts - they are deductible against income.
  • Depreciate equipment (mill, pump) over 5-15 years.
  • Consult a local accountant for country-specific rules.
The record rule: Ten minutes a day of bookkeeping tells you which fields make money, which buyers pay best, and whether to expand. Without records, you are farming blind.
Practical exercise 11: Start a rice field notebook. Record every cost and every sale for one season. At harvest, calculate your cost per kg and compare to your sale price. That single number tells you if your farm is profitable.
