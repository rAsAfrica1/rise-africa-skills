Zucchini & Courgette Processing

8 lessons · 30 quiz questions · Cucurbits

1 Variety selection

Zucchini and courgette are the same vegetable — "zucchini" is Italian-American, "courgette" is French-British. Both refer to Cucurbita pepo harvested young.

Dark green: Classic supermarket variety. Best for fresh sale and pickling.

Yellow/golden: Sweeter, thinner skin. Premium fresh market.

Round (Ronde de Nice): Popular for stuffing. Specialty market only.

Romanesco: Ribbed, nutty flavour. Italian markets.

For processing, choose dark green varieties harvested at 15–20 cm. Larger zucchini become watery and seedy.

2 Washing and topping

Zucchini has soft, edible skin — no peeling needed.

  1. Rinse under cold running water
  2. Rub gently to remove soil and spines
  3. Cut off both ends by 5 mm
  4. Cut to size for your product

Handle carefully — zucchini bruises easily and bruises turn mushy in processing.

⚠ Use zucchini within 48 hours of harvest for best results. They lose moisture and firmness fast.

3 Zucchini noodles (zoodles)

Zoodles are a popular low-carb alternative to pasta in health-conscious markets.

  1. Wash and trim zucchini
  2. Use a spiralizer to cut into noodle shapes
  3. For fresh sale: pack in sealed containers, refrigerate, sell within 3 days
  4. For longer shelf life: blanch 30 seconds, cool, pack in vacuum bags, refrigerate 10 days
  5. For frozen: blanch 1 minute, cool, freeze in bags — 6-month shelf life

Yield: 1 kg zucchini → 700–800 g zoodles.

Retail: $6–$12 per kg fresh zoodles. Popular with meal-kit companies and supermarkets.

4 Pickled courgette

Pickled courgette is a deli and farmers' market product.

  1. Slice zucchini 5 mm thick (or cut into ribbons with a peeler)
  2. Salt for 1 hour, then drain and rinse
  3. Pack into jars with garlic, dill, mustard seed, chilli flakes
  4. Cover with brine: 250 ml white vinegar + 250 ml water + 50 g sugar + 15 g salt
  5. Heat brine to 80°C and pour over
  6. Seal, refrigerate 1 week before eating

Shelf life: 3 months refrigerated. Retail $8–$15 per jar.

5 Zucchini bread and muffins

Zucchini bread is a classic American product — sweet, moist, and uses up large zucchini.

Recipe per loaf: 300 g grated zucchini + 250 g flour + 200 g sugar + 2 eggs + 100 ml oil + cinnamon + baking soda + salt.

Bake at 175°C for 55 minutes. Wrap tightly and refrigerate. Sells at bakeries, farmers' markets, cafés.

Retail: $6–$12 per loaf.

Muffins use the same batter in cups. Retail $2–$3 each.

6 Dried zucchini chips

Zucchini chips are a healthy snack — but tricky to make well because zucchini has high water content.

  1. Slice 3 mm thick (mandolin for consistency)
  2. Salt for 30 minutes, then pat dry thoroughly
  3. Optional: toss with oil and seasoning
  4. Dehydrate at 55°C for 8–10 hours
  5. Chips should be crisp, not chewy
  6. Cool completely, pack in foil bags with oxygen absorbers

Yield: 100 kg fresh → 5–8 kg chips (zucchini is 95% water).

Retail $50–$80 per kg — but low yield means pricing must be high.

7 Freezing

Frozen zucchini is sold to food service and used in soups, stews and casseroles.

  1. Wash and trim zucchini
  2. Slice or cube to size
  3. Blanch for 2 minutes (stops enzymes)
  4. Cool immediately in ice water
  5. Drain and pat dry
  6. Spread on trays and freeze individually (IQF)
  7. Pack in bags once frozen

Shelf life: 12 months at −18°C.

Wholesale: $1.50–$3 per kg to food service.

8 Restaurant supply

Zucchini is a staple vegetable for restaurants, hotels and caterers.

What chefs want:

  • Consistent size (15–18 cm for grilling, 5 cm baby for premium)
  • Firm, no soft spots
  • Dark green, glossy skin
  • Delivered chilled
  • Reliable weekly supply
  • Clean and trimmed

Direct supply to 5–10 restaurants is often more profitable than selling through a wholesaler. You get better prices and steady orders.

💰 Restaurants pay $2–$5 per kg for premium zucchini year-round. Contract growing with them provides stable income.
