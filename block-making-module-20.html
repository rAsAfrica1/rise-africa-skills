Sand-Lime (Calcium Silicate) Brick

8 lessons · 30 quiz questions · Hard difficulty

1 What is sand-lime brick

Sand-lime brick (also called calcium silicate brick or "white brick") is made from sand and lime, not clay or cement. It's shaped, then cured in a high-pressure steam autoclave — the same process used for AAC (Module 3).

Key properties:

  • Very uniform white or light grey colour
  • Sharp, precise edges
  • Higher compressive strength than concrete brick (15–25 N/mm²)
  • Good acoustic insulation
  • No firing needed (lower carbon than clay)

Popular in Europe (Germany, Netherlands, Poland) and increasingly in Africa where the raw materials are available. Major producers: Xella, Tarmac.

⚠ Sand-lime bricks are NOT the same as concrete bricks. Different chemistry, different process, different equipment.

2 Lime handling and safety

Lime is the second key ingredient after sand. Two forms are used:

  • Quicklime (calcium oxide, CaO): The traditional raw material. Reacts violently with water. Store in sealed containers away from moisture.
  • Hydrated lime (calcium hydroxide, Ca(OH)₂): Safer, pre-reacted with water. Standard for small producers.

Safety rules:

  • Wear goggles, gloves, dust mask when handling lime
  • Never add water to quicklime in a confined space (releases heat + steam)
  • Lime burns skin and eyes — wash immediately with clean water if contact occurs
  • Store hydrated lime in sealed bags in a dry shed

Hydrated lime costs 3–5× more than quicklime per kg but is much safer and easier to use.

3 Sand grading

Sand-lime brick sand must be clean, well-graded, and silica-rich.

Specification:

  • Silica content: 85%+ (quartz)
  • Grain size: 0–2 mm, mostly 0.1–0.6 mm
  • Clay content: under 3% (clay blocks the reaction)
  • Organic matter: under 0.5%
  • No salt (chlorides) — check for coastal sand

Wash sand if needed to remove clay. Screen to remove oversize. Test in a lab before committing to a supplier — sand quality determines everything in this process.

4 Mixing lime and sand

Standard sand-lime brick mix by weight:

  • Sand: 90–93%
  • Hydrated lime: 6–8%
  • Water: 3–5% (just enough to make the mix cohesive)

There's no cement — the strength comes from the lime-silica reaction in the autoclave.

Mixing time: 5–8 minutes in a pan mixer. The mix must be uniform and slightly damp — it should hold together when squeezed, like wet sand for a sandcastle.

Note the low water content. Sand-lime mix is much drier than concrete. Don't add more water "to help" — it weakens the brick.

5 Pressing the green brick

Sand-lime bricks are pressed at very high pressure — much higher than concrete bricks.

Press parameters:

  • Pressure: 15–25 MPa
  • Dwell time: 2–5 seconds at full pressure
  • Cycle time: 8–15 seconds per press
  • Output: 400–1,200 bricks per hour (multi-cavity press)

High pressure is essential — it compacts the sand and lime so the reaction can reach every grain during autoclaving. Hand-pressed sand-lime bricks are weak and don't meet standards.

Press cost: $30,000–$200,000 for a professional sand-lime press. Fibreglass or small steel moulds won't work — this is factory-scale.

⚠ Sand-lime brick is a factory business. Minimum investment $250,000 to be competitive.

6 Autoclave steam curing

After pressing, bricks are loaded into autoclave trolleys and cured in a pressure steam chamber.

Curing cycle:

  • Temperature: 175–200°C
  • Pressure: 8–12 bar
  • Cycle time: 6–12 hours
  • Full cure in one autoclave cycle

The autoclave turns the sand + lime into calcium silicate hydrate (C-S-H) — a strong, permanent bonding compound. Bricks come out of the autoclave fully cured and ready to sell.

Autoclaves cost $100,000–$400,000 depending on size. Certified operators and annual safety inspections required.

7 Why sand-lime looks clean

Sand-lime bricks have a distinctive look that drives their market:

  • Very uniform white or cream colour — no variation between bricks
  • Sharp, precise edges — thin mortar joints possible (2–3 mm)
  • Smooth faces — no plaster needed for interior walls
  • Clean, modern aesthetic — popular in contemporary architecture

Markets for sand-lime brick:

  • Interior feature walls (exposed brick look)
  • High-end residential (clean modern aesthetic)
  • Educational and healthcare buildings (acoustic insulation)
  • Industrial buildings with strict noise requirements

Pricing: sand-lime bricks sell for 1.5–3× the price of concrete bricks, similar to high-end clay brick.

8 Factory-scale economics

Sand-lime brick is not a small-business product. Here are the real numbers:

Minimum economic plant:

  • Investment: $250,000–$600,000
  • Building: 800–2,000 m²
  • Press: $80,000–$200,000
  • Autoclave: $100,000–$300,000
  • Mixers, conveyors, trolleys: $50,000–$100,000

Operating scale:

  • 30,000–60,000 bricks per day
  • 8–15 staff
  • Power: 300–800 kW
  • Steam boiler: 2–5 tonnes/hour

Return: Break-even at 40–60% capacity, payback 4–7 years at full operation.

Recommended path: Do NOT attempt sand-lime as your first block business. Start with concrete blocks (Modules 1–2). Once you have 3–5 years of experience and cash reserves, consider sand-lime as an expansion — usually with a partner or investor.

💰 Sand-lime is the "next level up" from concrete. Best as a second or third product for an established block manufacturer.
