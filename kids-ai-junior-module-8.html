🧠 Brain Opener

You can build a real AI model with just paper and a pencil. It is called a decision tree. It is what many real AI systems are built on. By the end of this module you will have one in your hands.

  • What is a decision tree?
  • How does it make a decision?

🎁 Reveal a good answer

A decision tree is a set of questions arranged like the branches of a tree. Each question has two or three possible answers, and each answer leads to another question. You follow the branches until you reach the end, which is the decision. Yes, it can be wrong - but with good questions, it is right most of the time.

📖 Tsitsi Builds a Decision Tree

Tsitsi was twelve. Her aunt ran a small clinic in Bindura and was always overwhelmed. Patients came with fevers, coughs, and stomach pains. The nurse had to decide who was serious and who could wait.

Tsitsi asked her aunt: "How do you decide who to see first?"

"I ask questions," her aunt said. "How long has the fever been there? Is there a cough? Has the patient eaten today? Have they been near someone sick?"

"Show me the questions. All of them."

Her aunt wrote them on a page. Tsitsi drew a tree - questions as branches, decisions at the ends.

The first question: does the patient have a fever above 39? If yes, see them first. If no, ask the next question. Is there a cough and a fever below 39? If yes, see them within an hour. If no, ask the next question.

Within one afternoon, Tsitsi had a decision tree. The nurse used it as a reminder. It was not a perfect system. But it helped. Patients who needed help urgently got it. The rest waited.

Tsitsi understood something: this was exactly what many real AI systems did. They looked at a few measurements and made a decision. It was not magic. It was a tree of questions, and Tsitsi had built one.

Her aunt hung it on the wall of the clinic. It is still there.

💡 The Big Idea

A decision tree is an AI model you can hold

Decision trees are one of the oldest and most useful AI models. They work by asking questions - one at a time - and leading you down a branch toward a decision. Real AI systems use them for medical diagnosis, bank lending, and customer support. You can build one with paper and a pencil. And once you have built one, you have built a machine.

📚 Three Deep Lessons

1

Building a decision tree

Start with the final decisions you need to make. For Tsitsi: see immediately, see within an hour, can wait. Those are the leaves of the tree.

Then work backwards. What question separates "see immediately" from the other two? It was fever above 39. What question separates "see within an hour" from "can wait"? A cough and a low fever. Each question is a branch. Each answer leads to another branch or to a leaf.

2

How the tree makes a decision

To make a decision, you follow the tree. Start at the top question. Look at your patient, your customer, your example. Answer yes or no. Follow the branch. Ask the next question. Continue until you reach a leaf. The leaf is your decision.

Real decision trees can have ten or twenty levels of questions. Some of them are built automatically by a computer from data. Others are built by hand by experts. Both work. The one you build by hand is yours.

3

When the tree is wrong

A decision tree is only as good as its questions and its thresholds. If the threshold is wrong - fever above 39 instead of 38 - the tree will misjudge some patients. If a question is missing, the tree cannot see something that matters.

This is why real AI teams test their trees on real examples. They find the mistakes, adjust the questions, and test again. A tree is not a one-time creation. It is a living thing that gets better with testing.

🛠 Hands-On - Build Your Own Decision Tree

You need

  • A large piece of paper
  • A pencil and eraser
  • A ruler (optional)
  1. Pick a decision you make often - what to eat, what to wear, which game to play.
  2. Write the possible decisions at the bottom of the page (the leaves).
  3. Above them, write the first question that separates them.
  4. Add more questions to separate the smaller groups.
  5. Draw lines connecting each question to its answers.
  6. Test your tree on five real examples. Does it always give the right answer? Fix it if not.
Why it works: A decision tree is a real AI model. Yours is made of paper, but the logic is exactly the same as a tree running on a computer.

🎮 Game - Tree Test Tournament

3 to 12 players - 20 min

Every player builds a decision tree for the same task (for example: what to eat for lunch). Then every tree is tested against 10 real examples. The tree with the most correct answers wins. Discuss: what made the winning tree better? Was it more questions, or better questions?

🤖 AI-Era Skill

AI builds decision trees from data automatically. You can too, with paper and testing.

Try this prompt: "Help me build a decision tree for [your decision]. What questions should I ask first, and what decisions should be at the end?"

Build the tree on paper. Then test it on 5 real cases. If the tree fails, adjust the questions until it works.

🌍 Real Stories from Africa

Zimbabwe clinic triage

Small rural clinics in Zimbabwe use simple decision trees to sort patients - who needs the doctor now, who can wait. The trees are written on paper and pinned to walls. They save lives by making the sorting faster and more consistent.

Kenyan bank lending tree

A Kenyan microfinance bank uses a decision tree to decide who gets a small loan. Questions: do you have a regular income? Do you have a business? Have you repaid before? Simple questions, useful decisions, thousands of loans a month.

🏆 Project Challenge

Build a Real Decision Tree

Build a decision tree that someone else will actually use.

  1. Find a real decision someone in your family or school makes often.
  2. Watch them make the decision five times. Write down every question they ask themselves.
  3. Build a tree from those questions.
  4. Test the tree on ten real cases with them.
  5. Fix the tree until it gets at least nine out of ten right.
  6. Give them the tree. Ask them to use it for one week.
  7. Write 3 sentences about what worked and what did not.
