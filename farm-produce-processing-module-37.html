Pineapple Processing

8 lessons · 30 quiz questions · Fruits

1 Pineapple varieties

Pineapple (Ananas comosus) is native to South America but grows widely in tropical Africa (Kenya, Uganda, Nigeria, South Africa).

Smooth Cayenne: The classic canning variety. Large, cylindrical, high sugar. Best for canning and juice.

MD-2 (Gold): Modern premium variety. Extra sweet, low acid, golden flesh. Best for fresh export.

Queen: Small, very sweet, crisp. Best for fresh premium markets.

Sugarloaf (local African): Large, very sweet, fibrous. Best for local juice and jam.

For processing, Smooth Cayenne is the standard. For premium juice, MD-2.

2 Peeling and coring

Pineapple processing is a multi-step operation.

  1. Wash the pineapple thoroughly
  2. Cut off the crown (top) and base (bottom)
  3. Stand upright and slice off the skin in strips
  4. Remove the "eyes" (brown spots) with a V-cut or spiral cut
  5. Cut into slices or quarters, then remove the fibrous core

Yield: 100 kg whole pineapple → 45–55 kg usable flesh (crown, skin, core, and eyes make up the rest).

💡 The core isn't wasted — it has bromelain and juice. Blend cores into juice.

3 Pineapple juice

Pineapple juice is a top-10 juice globally. Sold fresh, pasteurised, or frozen concentrate.

  1. Cut pineapple into chunks (peel and core removed)
  2. Blend or press
  3. Strain through fine mesh
  4. Add 0.1% citric acid and 0.05% ascorbic acid
  5. Add sugar to taste (pineapple is usually sweet enough without)
  6. Pasteurise at 88°C for 30 seconds
  7. Hot-fill into bottles

Yield: 100 kg fresh → 65–75 litres juice.

Retail: $2–$4 per litre. Premium fresh-pressed: $5–$8 per litre.

4 Canned slices and chunks

Canned pineapple is a global commodity — millions of cans sold every year.

  1. Peel and core pineapples
  2. Slice into rings (1 cm thick) or chunk into cubes
  3. Fill cans/jars with fruit and hot syrup (18–22 brix sugar solution)
  4. Add citric acid (0.3%)
  5. Exhaust air or vacuum-seal
  6. Process in boiling water bath: 20–25 minutes for 500 ml jars

Yield: 100 kg fresh → 55 kg canned fruit.

Retail: $1.50–$3 per 400 g can. Big volume, thin margin.

5 Dried pineapple

Dried pineapple is a premium snack — sweet, chewy, and long-lasting.

  1. Peel, core and slice 5 mm thick
  2. Optional: osmotic pre-treatment in 30% sugar syrup for 4 hours
  3. Drain and pat dry
  4. Dry at 55°C for 10–14 hours (or sun-dry 3–5 days)
  5. Target moisture: 15–18%
  6. Cool and pack in foil pouches

Yield: 100 kg fresh → 12–16 kg dried.

Retail: $25–$45 per kg.

6 Pineapple jam

Pineapple jam is a value-added product with strong retail appeal.

  1. Chop pineapple flesh finely
  2. Mix with sugar (1 kg sugar per 1.2 kg fruit)
  3. Add lemon juice (for pectin)
  4. Add commercial pectin (0.5%)
  5. Simmer until thick (65 brix)
  6. Fill jars at 90°C

Yield: 100 kg fresh → 120–130 kg jam.

Retail: $6–$12 per 350 g jar.

7 Bromelain extraction

Bromelain is an enzyme (protease) extracted from pineapple — used in meat tenderizers, digestive supplements, and cosmetics.

  1. Extract juice from pineapple stems (highest bromelain content)
  2. Add acetone or ammonium sulfate to precipitate proteins
  3. Filter and dry
  4. Mill into powder

Yield: 100 kg stems → 200–500 g bromelain powder.

Price: $30–$150 per kg depending on purity. Advanced processing — most small processors don't do this.

⚠ Bromelain extraction requires chemistry equipment and lab skills. Start with juice and jam first.

8 Export market

Pineapple is a major export crop for Africa — mostly fresh to Europe and the Middle East, but also processed.

Export markets:

  • Fresh pineapple: EU (Netherlands, Germany), Middle East (UAE)
  • Canned pineapple: EU, US, Japan
  • Pineapple juice concentrate: EU, US food industry
  • Dried pineapple: EU, US health food shops

Compliance: Phytosanitary certificate, pesticide residue test, GlobalG.A.P. for fresh export.

💰 Highest-margin products: dried pineapple ($25–45/kg) and premium fresh juice. Bulk canned is high volume, thin margin.
