Critical risk in African feed production: Groundnut cake, cottonseed cake, maize bran, and cassava peel meal are highly susceptible to Aspergillus flavus mould growth and aflatoxin contamination, especially under humid tropical storage conditions.

  • Never use mouldy or discoloured ingredients. Aflatoxin B1 is a potent hepatocarcinogen and causes liver damage, reduced growth, and mortality in fish.
  • Maximum safe level: Total aflatoxin should not exceed 20 ppb in complete fish feed (FAO/WHO Codex Alimentarius).
  • Testing: Use rapid aflatoxin test kits (available from agricultural extension offices) before incorporating suspect ingredients.
  • Storage: Keep all feed ingredients dry (below 12% moisture), in ventilated spaces, off the floor, and away from walls. Use pallets and moisture barriers.
  • Binders: Consider adding commercial mycotoxin binders (e.g., bentonite clay at 0.5â1%) when using high-risk ingredients.
  • Regulatory compliance: Check national standards (e.g., NAFDAC in Nigeria, KEBS in Kenya, FDA Ghana) for maximum permissible limits.

Contaminated feed can cause fish mortality, reduce market value, and pose food safety risks to consumers. When in doubt, discard the ingredient.

Quick Reference Checklist

  1. Match protein to species and stage: Use Table 1 as your baseline. Fry and fingerlings need 5â10% higher protein than grow-out fish. Adjust downward as fish approach market size to reduce cost.
  2. Source ingredients locally first: Before importing fishmeal or soybean meal, survey local markets for groundnut cake, cassava peel, palm kernel cake, and black soldier fly larvae. Local sourcing can cut feed costs by 30â50%.
  3. Test every batch of high-risk ingredients for aflatoxin: Groundnut cake, cottonseed cake, and maize bran must be tested before use. Keep test records for traceability and regulatory compliance.
  4. Calculate cost per kilogram of fish produced, not cost per bag of feed: A cheaper feed with a higher FCR may cost more per kg of fish. Always evaluate feeds on cost-per-kg-fish-gain basis.
  5. Match particle size to fish mouth size: Feed that is too large is wasted; too small is not efficiently consumed. Refer to Table 1 for species-specific particle size ranges.
  6. Use a premix: Vitamin and mineral premixes cost 0.5â1% of total feed cost but prevent deficiency diseases and improve growth by 10â15%. Never skip the premix.
  7. Store feed properly: Keep feed in a cool, dry, ventilated space. Use within 4â6 weeks of manufacture. Never store feed on bare concrete floors or against exterior walls.
  8. Keep formulation records: Document ingredient sources, batch numbers, inclusion rates, and feeding trial results. This data is essential for troubleshooting, scaling, and accessing finance.
