Training vs Testing - How to Know It Really Works

~14 min read20 min gameIntermediate
No device needed

🧠 Brain Opener

A student who studies only the exact questions on last year's exam might pass if the questions repeat. But if the teacher changes the questions, the student fails. AI has the same trap.

  • Why is a test different from practice?
  • What is the danger of scoring 100 percent on training data?
  • How do you know if a model will work in the real world?
🎁 Reveal a good answer

Practice is when you learn. A test is when you prove you learned. If you only ever practise and never test on new examples, you cannot know whether you understood or just memorised. The danger of 100 percent on training data is that it often means 60 percent on new data - a sign of overfitting.

📖 Ropafadzo Exam Trick

Ropafadzo was fifteen and had a system for passing exams. She read last year's past paper. Then she read the year before. Then the year before that. She memorised every question and every answer.

It worked. She scored 92 percent on her first exam.

Then her teacher, Mrs Chikafu, changed the format. No more questions from past papers. New questions only. Real-world problems.

Ropafadzo scored 48 percent.

She was furious. "I studied so hard," she told her mother.

"You did not study," her mother said. "You memorised. That is different."

"What is the difference?"

"If I ask you the answer to a question you memorised, you know it. If I ask you something new that uses the same idea, you do not. You memorised the answers. You did not learn the rules."

Ropafadzo changed her study method. She stopped reading past papers. She started doing problems she had never seen before. She tested herself on new examples every day.

Her next exam score: 84 percent. Not 92. But this time it was real.

Years later, when she learned about AI training, she recognised what had happened. She had been an overfit model. Then she had learned to generalise.

💡 The Big Idea

Test on data you never trained on

Training is showing examples with answers. Testing is checking on examples the model has never seen. If your model does well on training and badly on testing, it has memorised, not learned. If it does well on both, it has learned something real.

📚 Three Deep Lessons

1

The three data sets

Every real AI project splits its data into three sets. Training set - usually 70 to 80 percent of the data. The model learns from this. Validation set - 10 to 15 percent. Used during training to tune the model. Test set - 10 to 15 percent. Used only once, at the very end.

The test set must never be shown to the model during training. It is the final exam. If you sneak it in, you are cheating yourself - you will not know how the model will perform in the real world.

2

Why 100 percent is a warning sign

If your model scores 100 percent on training data, one of two things is true. Either the problem is very simple, or the model has memorised the answers. The second is much more common.

When a model has memorised the training set, it usually scores far lower on the test set. This is overfitting. The fix is more data, fewer parameters, or stopping training earlier.

3

What test scores mean

A test score of 95 percent on a real problem is often excellent. A test score of 99 percent is suspicious - check the data for leaks. A test score of 60 percent means the model needs more work, or more data, or a different approach.

Real AI teams report both training and test scores. If someone only reports training scores, ask about testing. The one that matters is the one the model has never seen.

🛠 Hands-On - Split Your Own Data

You need

  • A notebook
  • A pen
  • A set of 30 to 50 examples of any pattern
  1. Pick a pattern you want to test - predicting your mum mood, sorting laundry, guessing whether it will rain.
  2. Collect 40 real examples. Write each one down with its correct answer.
  3. Split them: 30 for training, 5 for validation, 5 for testing.
  4. Look only at the 30 training examples. Write a simple rule that fits them.
  5. Test the rule on the 5 validation examples. Adjust if needed.
  6. Test on the 5 test examples - but only once.
  7. Write down your test score. This is the real measure of your rule.
Why it works: This is the exact process real AI teams use. The numbers change. The logic does not.

🎮 Game - Cheat on the Test

2 to 10 players - 20 min

One player writes down a hidden rule. They give ten examples of the rule to the other players. Then they give five new examples and ask for predictions. Score by how many new examples they get right. Then swap. Discuss: did the winner memorise the ten examples, or figure out the underlying rule?

🤖 AI-Era Skill

AI can help you design the split and check your data for leaks.

Try this prompt: "I have 100 examples of a pattern. How should I split them into training, validation, and test sets for a small AI project? What mistakes should I avoid?"

Check the answer. The standard split is 70/15/15 or 80/10/10. The main mistake is leaking test data into training. Make sure your split is clean.

🌍 Real Stories from Africa

Kaggle competition winners, 2015

A famous Kaggle competition was won by a team whose model scored almost perfectly on the training set. When tested on fresh data, it failed. The team had accidentally included test-set information in training. They were disqualified. Leaking test data is one of the most common AI mistakes.

Kenyan crop yield model

A Kenyan startup built an AI to predict crop yield. Their first model scored 95 percent on training data and 60 percent on test data. They had overfit. They collected 5 times more data, re-trained, and got 82 percent on test. That model is now used by 20,000 farmers.

🏆 Project Challenge

Build, Split, and Test a Rule

Design a decision rule, split your data carefully, and report the real score.

  1. Pick a pattern you can observe.
  2. Collect 50 examples. Write them all down with the correct answer.
  3. Split 35 training, 7 validation, 8 test. Do not look at the test set.
  4. Study only the training set. Write a simple rule.
  5. Check on validation. Adjust the rule if needed.
  6. Test on the test set once. Write down the score.
  7. Then test on 10 new real examples from the next week. Compare with the test score.
