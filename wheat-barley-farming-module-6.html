Lesson 1

The Wheat and Barley Price Calendar

In this lesson
  • Map price seasonality
  • Use storage to sell into the peak
  • Understand the import-linked price floor

The price pattern

  • Harvest months: lowest prices
  • 3–6 months after harvest: rising as imports become the marginal supply
  • Late dry season / pre-harvest: peak, often 20–40% above harvest

Import parity pricing

Wheat and barley prices in most African markets track the import parity price — the cost of imported grain landed in the country, plus tariff. If local prices fall below import parity, millers buy local. If local prices rise above import parity, millers import and local sales slow.

This means local wheat and barley prices have a floor (below which you should not sell) and a ceiling (above which imports displace you). Understanding the parity price is the single most important market intelligence for a wheat grower.

Lesson 2

The Five Buyer Types

In this lesson
  • Name and price the five buyers
  • Match grade to buyer
  • Understand payment terms

1. Local market vendors

Small lots, cash. Rare for wheat — wheat is not a household commodity in most of Africa. Sold as flour, not grain.

2. Flour mills

The main buyer for wheat. 10–100 tonne lots, 30–60 day payment. Test moisture, protein, foreign matter, mycotoxins. Prices track imports.

3. Breweries and maltsters

The main buyer for malting barley. Contract-based, strict quality. 20–50 tonne lots, 30–90 day payment. The best price for barley when spec is met.

4. Feed mills

Buy feed wheat and feed barley. Prices track maize. Cash or 30 days.

5. Seed distributors and aggregators

Certified seed producers and aggregators buy for regional trade. 2–3x food price for certified seed.

Lesson 3

Contract Farming & Out-Grower Schemes

In this lesson
  • Understand out-grower contracts
  • Read contracts for the risky clauses
  • Decide when a contract is worth signing

What a wheat contract covers

  • Quantity to deliver
  • Quality specification (protein, moisture, foreign matter, mycotoxins)
  • Price (fixed, or formula-linked to import parity)
  • Delivery date and location
  • Input provision (seed, fertiliser) or not
  • Payment terms
  • Penalties for failing to deliver or failing specification

Risky clauses

  • Input credit at inflated prices — effect is a high-interest loan
  • One-sided rejection — buyer rejects without right of appeal
  • No price floor — follows the market but with a heavy discount clause
  • Exclusive delivery — you must deliver even if price collapses
  • Delayed payment without interest — 90 days with no late penalty

When a contract is worth signing

  • Inputs at market price or below with clear accounting
  • Price has a floor or is competitive at signing
  • Specification is written and testable
  • Dispute resolution exists
  • Payment terms reasonable (30 days or less, or with interest)

Read the contract. If you cannot get a clause changed, walk away. There is always another buyer.

Revenue at 5 t/ha

  • At $0.35/kg (harvest, feed) = $1,750
  • At $0.45/kg (milling) = $2,250
  • At $0.60/kg (malting barley premium) = $3,000
  • At $0.70/kg (high protein premium) = $3,500

Net margin

$500–1,500 per hectare for wheat. Higher for malting barley when the contract is in place. Wheat and barley are lower-margin per hectare than vegetables or potato, but they are lower-risk, mechanisable, and have government-backed markets.

Lesson 5

The One-Page Business Plan

In this lesson
  • Complete your one-page plan
  • Set year 1 and year 2 targets
  • Decide scale triggers

Before next season, fill in this one page:

  1. Business type: milling wheat, malting barley, feed grain, or seed?
  2. Capital tier: how much will you invest?
  3. Land and water: hectares, irrigation method, altitude
  4. Variety and why
  5. Target yield: 4 t/ha in year 1
  6. Buyers: three named contacts (miller, maltster, feed mill)
  7. Revenue target: price × yield
  8. Total cost: every line
  9. Net margin: revenue − cost
  10. Value step: contract farming or seed multiplication in 12 months
  11. Scaling trigger: condition before expanding

Knowledge check — Module 6

1. What sets the price floor for local wheat?

Correct: import parity. If local prices rise above parity, millers import.

2. Who is the main wheat buyer?

Correct: flour mills. 10–100 tonne lots, 30–60 day payment.

3. What is a red flag in an out-grower contract?

Correct: inflated input credit. Effectively a high-interest loan.

4. What is the total cost per hectare for irrigated wheat?

Correct: $900–2,000. The dominant costs are irrigation, labour and fertiliser.

5. When should you scale up?

Correct: two profitable cycles with a buyer contract. Contract first, then scale.
