10. Selling Kittens and Building Reputation

A kitten sale is the beginning of a relationship. Here is how to sell well and build a name.

Module 10 of 12 · Cat Breeding for Profit

Why selling well matters

Most catteries lose money because they cannot sell kittens at the right price. Two years of work collapses if a litter goes unsold or sells at half price. The skill of selling is as important as the skill of breeding.

Pricing strategy

  1. Know your cost per kitten - feed, vet, registration, cattery share, time.
  2. Research the market - what are similar kittens selling for?
  3. Set three prices - asking price, target price, floor price.
  4. Never sell below floor - it damages your brand forever.
  5. Offer payment plans - some serious buyers cannot pay all at once.
  6. Take a deposit - typically 20-30% to reserve a kitten.
  7. Premium for show quality - 2-3x for show-potential kittens.
  8. Discount for older kittens - after 16 weeks, price drops.

Photographing kittens

  • Natural light - outdoors in shade, or near a window.
  • Clean background - plain blanket, or a cat tree.
  • Close-up face shots - clear, sharp, well-lit.
  • Full body shots - standing profile.
  • Play shots - kittens with a toy, showing personality.
  • Video - 1-2 minute clip of kittens playing, following a toy, exploring.
  • Consistent - same setup for each kitten in a litter.
  • Regular - new photos weekly as kittens grow.

Writing a good kitten listing

Every listing should include:

  • Breed and sex - clear and up front.
  • Date of birth and age - exact.
  • Parents'' names and health tests - HCM, PKD, DNA clearances.
  • Vaccination status - which vaccines, when given, next due.
  • Deworming history - which product, how often.
  • FeLV/FIV status - tested negative.
  • Microchip number - if applicable.
  • Temperament notes - each kitten is different.
  • Ideal home - family, single, indoor-only, active, quiet.
  • What is included - kitten pack, food sample, blanket with familiar smell, contract.
  • Price - clear, with deposit details.

Screening buyers

You have the right to refuse a sale. Ask:

  • Why this breed? - a serious buyer has reasons.
  • Where will the cat live? - house, apartment, indoor-only?
  • Who will care for the cat daily? - avoid "the children will".
  • How many hours alone per day? - cats are more independent but need attention.
  • Have you owned cats before? - experience matters.
  • What is your vet''s name? - serious buyers have one.
  • Are you prepared for 15-20 years of commitment? - cats live long.
  • What will you do if the cat is ill or you move? - red flag if no plan.
  • Will the cat be indoor-only or have outdoor access? - depends on breed.

Red flags: no questions, wants the kitten "today", will not give a vet reference, wants to pay cash only, refuses a home visit, plans to resell, wants to breed unregistered.

Home visits and handovers

  • Home visits - visit the buyer''s home where possible, or ask for photos of the intended living area.
  • Handover - meet in person, hand over vaccination cards, kitten pack, and contract.
  • Do not ship a kitten unaccompanied by an adult human.
  • Do not sell multiple kittens from the same litter to the same household unless they have experience with littermate syndrome.

Littermate syndrome

Two kittens from the same litter raised together often become overly dependent on each other, harder to socialise with humans, and prone to behavioural issues. If you sell two to the same home:

  • Advise separate sleeping areas and separate play times.
  • Encourage separate human bonding for each kitten.
  • Warn about the risks in writing.

The kitten pack

Every kitten leaves with:

  • Vaccination card
  • Deworming record
  • FeLV/FIV test result
  • Microchip certificate (if applicable)
  • Sample of current food (3-7 days)
  • Blanket or toy with littermate scent
  • Care guide (feeding, litter, health, first weeks)
  • Contract with health guarantee
  • Your contact details for follow-up

Legal and ethical obligations

  • Disclose all known health issues. Hiding problems destroys reputation and can be illegal.
  • Do not sell kittens under 12 weeks. They need littermates and mother for social skills and vaccinations.
  • Vaccinate and deworm before sale. It is a welfare obligation.
  • Provide a contract. It protects both buyer and breeder.
  • Follow local laws on cattery registration, breeding licences, and sales.
  • Refuse sales to buyers who clearly should not own a cat.
  • Take back any kitten you bred if the buyer cannot keep it. This is what reputable breeders do.
The reputation rule: One dishonest sale destroys 10 years of work. In the cat world, word travels fast. Sell honestly, price fairly, support your buyers, and you will never struggle to sell kittens.
