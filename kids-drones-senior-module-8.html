🧠 Brain Opener

You have learned aerodynamics, batteries, GPS, missions, photos, mapping, and law. This final module is about turning all of it into a business that pays you every month.

  • What are the three pieces of a successful drone business?
  • How do you find your first ten clients?
  • What makes a drone business fail?
🎁 Reveal a good answer

The three pieces are: capability (what you can deliver), clients (who pays you), and systems (how you run it). Your first ten clients come from family, friends, and neighbours. Drone businesses fail when they rely on one client, undercharge, or ignore the rules.

📖 Rudo Builds a Real Business

Rudo was eighteen and had been flying drones for two years. She had done jobs for family and friends. She had earned a few hundred dollars total. But she did not have a business - she had a hobby that occasionally paid.

Then she met Mrs Mhaka, a property developer in Harare who owned twelve houses. Mrs Mhaka asked Rudo to shoot a video for each property. Twelve videos over six weeks. She would pay 200 dollars each.

Rudo said yes. Then she sat down and planned.

First, she wrote down the deliverable: a 45 to 60 second video tour of each house, shot in the morning or evening light, with music, delivered as an MP4 and a set of ten still photos.

Second, she wrote down the flight plan for each property: arrival, exterior establishing shot, garden overview, interior entry through main door, walkthrough of main rooms, exterior closing shot.

Third, she calculated: 8 minutes of flight per house. 2 hours of shooting including travel and setup. 1 hour of editing. 3 hours per house. 36 hours total. At 200 dollars per house, that was 2,400 dollars.

She registered with the Civil Aviation Authority. She paid for the licence. She had insurance.

She shot the first three houses in one weekend and sent them to Mrs Mhaka on Monday. Mrs Mhaka was delighted. She paid within a week.

By the end of the six weeks, Rudo had delivered all twelve videos and made 2,400 dollars. Her expenses: licence, insurance, transport, and phone data. Net profit: 1,600 dollars.

Mrs Mhaka referred her to two other developers. Both hired her. Within three months, Rudo had a business - not a hobby.

She hired her cousin to help with editing. She wrote standard contracts. She set a fixed price list. She printed business cards. She stopped saying "I do drone stuff" and started saying "I shoot property videos for developers."

Her second-year income was 8,000 dollars. Her third-year target was 20,000.

💡 The Big Idea

A business is a system, not a skill

Skill is what you can do. A business is a system for finding clients, delivering reliably, pricing fairly, and asking for referrals. Every drone business that succeeds has all four. Every one that fails is missing at least one.

📚 Three Deep Lessons

1

The four pieces of a drone business

Service list: three to five specific services with prices. "Property videos at 200 dollars, farm maps at 150 dollars, event coverage at 300 dollars." Portfolio: five to ten examples of past work, one line each on what it solved.

Client tracker: name, brief, quote, answer, delivery date, follow-up date. A notebook is fine. Referral system: after every delivery, ask one question - "Do you know anyone else who needs this?" Most of your business will come from this question.

2

Finding your first ten clients

Your first clients are people you already know. Family. Family friends. Parents colleagues. Neighbours. Small business owners on your street. Ask every one of them the same question: what do you need that I could do with a drone?

Your next ten clients come from referrals. After every delivery, ask for one. Your next hundred come from a reputation. Every finished job adds to it. Every late delivery takes away from it. The compounding is slow and then fast.

3

Why drone businesses fail

Four common failures. One client: if 80 percent of your income comes from one client, you do not have a business. Undercharging: if you charge too little, you cannot afford to do the work well. Ignoring the law: one fine can wipe out a year of profit. No systems: if you do not track clients, follow up, or ask for referrals, the business does not grow.

Rudo did none of these. She had twelve clients. She charged fairly. She was licensed. She had a system. That is why she built a real business in six months.

🛠 Hands-On - Build Your Drone Business Starter Kit

You need

  • A notebook
  • A phone or computer
  • A printer or a shop that prints
  • Your five best past projects
  1. Write your service list. Three to five services, each with a price.
  2. Write five case studies of past work. One paragraph each.
  3. Create a client tracker in a notebook or spreadsheet: name, brief, quote, answer, delivery, follow-up.
  4. Print 20 flyers. Name, phone number, three services, one line on what makes you different.
  5. Hand them out at a market, a church, a school gate, a business area.
  6. Track every call. Follow up every three days.
  7. After every delivery, ask for one referral.
Why it works: A drone pilot with no system earns as much as a drone pilot with a great skill and no clients. The kit turns skill into a business.
