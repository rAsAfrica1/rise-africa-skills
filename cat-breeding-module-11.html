11. Advanced Breeding and Genetics

The best breeders are students of genetics. Here is how to improve your cattery every generation.

Module 11 of 12 · Cat Breeding for Profit

Why genetics matters

Genetics determine 30-60% of a cat''s traits. Health, temperament, conformation, and coat are all inherited. Good genetics produce healthy, sound, easy-to-live-with cats that sell easily. Bad genetics produce sick, aggressive, or defective cats that ruin reputations.

Every generation you breed with attention to genetics improves your cattery. Every generation you breed carelessly degrades it.

The key genetic concepts

ConceptWhat it means
HeritabilityHow much of a trait is genetic vs environment. High heritability = easier to select
Dominant genesOne copy is enough to show the trait
Recessive genesTwo copies needed to show the trait
CarriersHave a recessive gene but do not show it
InbreedingMating related cats. Increases genetic defects
Line breedingMating cats from the same line, but not closely related
OutcrossingMating unrelated cats. Increases vigour but can reduce consistency
Hybrid vigourCrossbred kittens are often healthier and stronger

The three breeding strategies

StrategyMethodProsCons
InbreedingFather-daughter, brother-sister, close relativesFixed type quicklyGenetic defects, low fertility, weakened immunity
Line breedingGrandparent-grandchild, cousinsConsistent type without extremesRisk of concentrating defects if not managed
OutcrossingTwo unrelated catsHybrid vigour, genetic diversityLess predictable offspring

Best practice for small catteries: line breeding with regular outcrosses every 2-3 generations. This gives consistency without accumulating problems.

The coefficient of inbreeding (COI)

COI measures how related a cat''s parents are. Lower is better.

COIMeaning
Below 5%Very low - healthy genetic diversity
5-10%Low-moderate - acceptable
10-20%Moderate - watch for problems
20-30%High - likely to see defects
Over 30%Very high - avoid

Tools like PawPeds COI calculator or the ICB calculator estimate COI from pedigrees. Aim for below 10%.

Blood typing - critical for breeding

QueenStudRisk to kittens
Type AType ANone
Type AType BNone
Type BType BNone
Type BType AHigh - kittens may be type A and die from mother''s antibodies in colostrum

Test every queen before mating. If a type B queen must be bred to a type A stud, remove kittens from the mother for the first 24 hours and hand-rear them on kitten milk replacer, then return them to the queen after her colostrum has dried up.

ElementWhat to do

Genetic diversity - the long game

Small closed breeding populations lose genetic diversity over time. Prevent with:

  • Import a stud from a distant line every 2-3 generations
  • Use multiple stud males instead of one male for the whole cattery
  • Do not overuse one popular male - even if he is excellent
  • Track COI on every mating plan
  • Test for carriers and mate carrier-to-clear, never carrier-to-carrier
  • Consider crossbreeding programs (breed-specific) to introduce vigour
  • Use frozen semen from distant lines if available

The genetics rule: Health-test every breeding cat. Never breed an untested cat. Never breed a cat with a known genetic problem. Never breed two carriers of the same recessive gene. Genetics is the long game - play it right.
Practical exercise 11: Research the health tests required for your breed. Contact a vet or genetic testing lab and price the tests. Plan to test your current breeding stock over the next 6 months.
