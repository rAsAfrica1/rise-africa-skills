Chilli Pepper Processing

8 lessons · 30 quiz questions · Fruit Vegetables

1 Chilli varieties and heat levels

Chillies (Capsicum spp.) range from mild to explosive. Heat is measured in Scoville Heat Units (SHU).

  • Bell peppers: 0 SHU — no heat
  • Poblano/Ancho: 1,000–2,000 SHU — mild
  • Jalapeño: 2,500–8,000 SHU — medium
  • Cayenne: 30,000–50,000 SHU — hot
  • Bird's eye (African): 50,000–100,000 SHU — very hot
  • Habanero/Scotch Bonnet: 100,000–350,000 SHU — extremely hot
  • Carolina Reaper: 1.5M+ SHU — super hot

African markets prefer bird's eye and scotch bonnet. European markets prefer milder chillies.

2 Washing and destemming

Chillies are handled carefully — capsaicin (the heat compound) irritates skin and eyes.

  1. Wear gloves throughout processing
  2. Wash chillies in cold water
  3. Cut off stems and green calyx
  4. Remove seeds if making mild products (most heat is in the pith and seeds)
  5. Keep seeds if making hot sauce
⚠ Never touch your face, eyes or mouth while handling chillies. Wash hands thoroughly with soap and cold water after.

3 Drying whole chillies

Dried chillies are the base of most chilli products.

  1. Wash and destem
  2. Spread on drying racks in single layer
  3. Sun-dry 5–10 days (or solar-dry 2–3 days)
  4. Or dehydrate at 55°C for 10–12 hours
  5. Target moisture: 8–10%
  6. Store whole (better flavour retention than powder)

Yield: 100 kg fresh → 20–25 kg dried (chillies have 75–80% water).

Bulk: $4–$10 per kg. Higher for premium single-variety.

4 Chilli powder and flakes

Chilli powder is a global commodity.

  1. Dehydrate dried chillies
  2. Remove any remaining stems
  3. Mill in a hammer mill
  4. Sieve through 250 micron for powder; coarse (1–2 mm) for flakes
  5. Package in dark airtight containers

Yield: 100 kg dried → 90–95 kg powder.

Retail: $12–$25 per kg for standard; $30–$60 per kg for single-origin premium.

⚠ Chilli powder is a respiratory irritant. Wear a dust mask when milling and packing.

5 Chilli sauce and paste

Chilli sauce is one of the highest-margin products you can make — $8–$20 per 250 ml bottle in Western markets.

Basic hot sauce:

  1. Blend chillies + vinegar (equal parts) + garlic + salt
  2. Simmer 15 minutes
  3. Strain (for smooth sauce) or leave chunky
  4. Add 0.1% potassium sorbate as preservative
  5. Hot-fill at 90°C into bottles
  6. Cap and cool

Yield: 100 kg fresh chillies → 150–180 L sauce (vinegar adds volume).

Variations: mango chilli sauce, pineapple chilli, peri-peri (with lemon and herbs).

6 Chilli oil

Chilli oil is used in Asian and African cooking. High margin, long shelf life.

  1. Dry chillies fully
  2. Lightly crush into flakes
  3. Warm oil (sunflower or peanut) to 90°C
  4. Pour over flakes (1 part flakes to 4 parts oil)
  5. Cool and bottle in dark glass
  6. Let sit 1 week before use

Retail: $15–$35 per 250 ml bottle.

7 Pickled chillies

Pickled chillies are popular in Mexico, Asia and Africa.

  1. Wash and destem whole small chillies (or slice)
  2. Pack into jars with garlic, peppercorns
  3. Cover with brine: 1 part vinegar + 1 part water + salt + sugar
  4. Seal and refrigerate 2 weeks before eating

Retail: $6–$12 per 350 g jar. Shelf life: 12 months refrigerated.

8 Export standards

Chilli is one of the most-traded spices globally. To export:

  • Aflatoxin test: Critical for EU — aflatoxin levels must be under 5 ppb
  • Pesticide residue test: EU has 500+ monitored compounds
  • Microbiological test: Salmonella, E. coli must be absent
  • Steam sterilisation: Often required to reduce microbial load
  • Phytosanitary certificate
  • Certificate of origin

Main export markets: EU, US, Japan, Middle East. Prices vary widely: standard $4–$10/kg dried whole; premium organic single-origin $15–$30/kg.

💰 Chilli sauce is the highest-margin chilli product. Retail $8–$20 per bottle with simple equipment. Build a brand and sell direct.
