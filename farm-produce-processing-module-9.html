Pumpkin Processing

8 lessons · 30 quiz questions · Cucurbits

1 Pumpkin varieties

Pumpkins (Cucurbita spp.) come in many shapes and sizes. For processing, choose varieties with thick, dry flesh, small seed cavity, and sweet flavour.

Butternut (Cucurbita moschata): Small, pear-shaped, dense orange flesh. Best for puree, soup and baby food.

Kabocha: Japanese pumpkin. Sweet, dry, thin-skinned. Popular for roasting and soup.

Connecticut Field: Large, classic orange pumpkin. Good for juice and dicing.

Hubbard: Huge, grey-blue skin, sweet orange flesh. Best for large-scale puree production.

Flesh yield matters — some varieties are 70% flesh, others 45%. Butternut and Hubbard have the best flesh-to-weight ratio.

2 Washing and cutting

Pumpkins are tough and require proper tools.

  1. Wash the outside thoroughly — you'll be cutting through the skin
  2. Cut in half lengthwise with a heavy knife
  3. Scoop out seeds and stringy flesh with a spoon (save seeds for roasting)
  4. Cut into manageable chunks
  5. Peel with a knife or peeler (skip for butternut — skin is edible)
  6. Cube to size for your product

Use stainless steel knives and cutting boards. Pumpkin stains wood.

💡 Save the seeds! Roasted pumpkin seeds sell for $15–$30 per kg as a snack.

3 Pumpkin puree

Pumpkin puree is the base for pies, soups, baby food and pasta fillings. In the US, canned pumpkin puree is a $200M+ market.

  1. Cut and clean pumpkins
  2. Steam or roast (roasting gives sweeter flavour)
  3. Roast at 190°C for 45 minutes until soft
  4. Scoop flesh from skin
  5. Blend smooth
  6. Adjust consistency with water (thicker for pie, thinner for soup)
  7. Heat to 90°C
  8. Hot-fill into jars or cans
  9. Process in pressure canner (low-acid food)

Yield: 100 kg whole pumpkin → 50–65 kg puree.

⚠ Pumpkin is low-acid — must be pressure-canned, not water-bath canned. Botulism risk if done wrong.

4 Pumpkin soup concentrate

Pumpkin soup is a popular retail product in cooler markets.

  1. Make pumpkin puree as above
  2. Add vegetable stock (onion, carrot, celery)
  3. Add cream or coconut milk (optional)
  4. Season with salt, pepper, nutmeg, ginger
  5. Simmer to concentrate (reduce volume 30%)
  6. Pasteurise at 90°C
  7. Hot-fill into jars or retort pouches

Retail: $5–$10 per 500 ml jar. Sells year-round, higher in winter.

5 Roasted pumpkin seeds

Pumpkin seeds (pepitas) are a premium snack with high protein and zinc. Sell for 3–5× the price of fresh pumpkin per kg.

  1. Wash seeds thoroughly to remove pulp
  2. Boil in salted water for 10 minutes
  3. Drain and pat dry
  4. Toss with oil and seasoning (salt, paprika, garlic)
  5. Roast at 160°C for 20 minutes, stirring once
  6. Cool completely
  7. Pack in foil pouches

Yield: 100 kg pumpkin → 2–3 kg raw seeds → 1–1.5 kg roasted.

Retail: $15–$30 per kg.

6 Pumpkin flour

Pumpkin flour is used in gluten-free baking and as a natural orange colourant.

  1. Peel and cube pumpkin
  2. Steam 10 minutes
  3. Dry at 60°C for 8–12 hours
  4. Mill to fine powder
  5. Sieve through 250 micron
  6. Package in dark airtight bags

Yield: 100 kg fresh → 8–12 kg flour.

Retail: $15–$25 per kg.

7 Pumpkin jam and chutney

Pumpkin jam is a value-added product with strong local demand.

Pumpkin jam: Cook 1 kg pumpkin puree + 800 g sugar + juice of 1 lemon + cinnamon + cloves. Simmer 40 minutes until thick. Jar hot.

Pumpkin chutney: Pumpkin + vinegar + sugar + onion + ginger + chilli. Simmer 1 hour. Good with cheese and meat.

Retail: $6–$12 per 350 g jar.

8 Packaging and freezing

Packaging by product:

  • Puree — glass jars, cans, retort pouches
  • Soup — glass jars, retort pouches
  • Seeds — foil pouches (50 g, 100 g, 250 g)
  • Flour — dark bags, 500 g–5 kg
  • Jam/chutney — glass jars (350 g)

Freezing: Cooked pumpkin puree freezes well at −18°C for 12 months. Raw cubed pumpkin freezes for 8 months but becomes soft on thaw.

💰 Pumpkin is cheap raw material (often $0.20–$0.50 per kg). Processed into puree it becomes $3–$6 per kg. Highest value-add of any crop in this course.
