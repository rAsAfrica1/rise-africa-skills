Compressed Stabilised Earth Block (CSEB)

8 lessons · 30 quiz questions · Easy difficulty

1 Soil selection and testing

CSEB (also called "soil block" or "earth block") is made from local soil + 5–10% cement, compressed in a manual or electric press. It's cheap because soil is nearly free.

Good CSEB soil:

  • 45–75% sand, 25–55% silt+clay
  • Low organic matter (no topsoil — get subsoil)
  • No large stones
  • Not too much clay (cracks when drying)

Simple field test (jar test):

  1. Fill a jar 1/3 with soil, 2/3 with water
  2. Shake hard, then let settle for 8 hours
  3. Measure layers: sand at bottom, silt in middle, clay on top
  4. Aim for 30–40% sand, 30–40% silt, 20–30% clay

If sand is too low: add sand. If clay is too high: add sand and cement.

💡 Test soil from 3 different sites before committing. Small differences in clay content give very different block quality.

2 Cement percentage basics

Cement stabilises the soil — it stops the block swelling with water and adds strength.

Cement percentage by soil type:

  • 5% cement: Sandy soil, non-load-bearing walls
  • 7% cement: Standard mix, load-bearing single-storey walls
  • 10% cement: Clayey soil, two-storey walls, foundations
  • 12%+ cement: Heavy loads, poor soil, or waterproof blocks

By weight of dry soil. Example for 100 kg dry soil at 8%: add 8 kg cement.

Do not confuse cement % with cement:sand ratio. 8% by weight is roughly 1 cement : 12 soil by weight — but the block is still strong because the soil does most of the work.

3 Manual CSEB press

The manual CSEB press (like the CINVA-Ram or Terstaram) uses a lever to compress soil-cement into a block.

How it works:

  1. Load the mould with 8–10 kg of soil-cement mix
  2. Lower the compression piston with the lever
  3. Pull hard — compression pressure is 2–4 MPa (much higher than a vibrating machine)
  4. Lift the piston
  5. Eject the green block from the mould
  6. Move to curing area

Cycle time: 30–60 seconds per block. Output: 60–120 blocks per day with one operator.

Cost: $1,500–$3,500 for a solid manual press. Cheapest entry into block making.

4 Electric CSEB press

The electric version uses a hydraulic ram instead of a lever. Same principle, 10× faster.

Advantages:

  • Cycle time: 8–15 seconds per block
  • Output: 500–1,200 blocks per day
  • More consistent pressure — better quality control
  • Less operator fatigue

Disadvantages:

  • Cost: $8,000–$25,000
  • Needs reliable power (or a generator)
  • Bigger operation to justify the investment

Move from manual to electric when you're selling 300+ blocks/week consistently and turning away customers.

5 Soil-cement mixing

Mixing is done in two stages:

Dry mixing:

  1. Pulverise soil to break up lumps (screen through 10 mm sieve)
  2. Add cement and mix dry for 2 minutes — until uniform colour

Wet mixing:

  1. Add water slowly while mixing
  2. Target moisture: 8–12% (soil-cement should hold a ball shape but crumble at edges when squeezed)
  3. Mix 2 more minutes

Total mix time: 4–5 minutes. Over-mixing wastes time; under-mixing gives weak blocks.

Add no more than needed — extra water reduces compression strength. CSEB mix is much drier than regular concrete.

6 Compression pressure

CSEB strength comes from pressure, not cement. Higher pressure = stronger block.

Pressure ranges:

  • Manual press: 2–4 MPa
  • Electric press: 6–12 MPa
  • High-end hydraulic: 15–25 MPa

At 4 MPa with 8% cement, a CSEB block reaches 3–5 N/mm². At 10 MPa with the same cement, it reaches 6–9 N/mm².

Never try to compensate for low pressure with more cement. Cement can't replace compaction.

⚠ Don't use concrete mixers for CSEB. They add too much water. Use a pan mixer or a tiller converted to mixing.

7 Stack curing CSEB

Fresh CSEB blocks are more fragile than concrete blocks. Special curing:

  • Leave the block on the press pallet for 5 minutes
  • Move to a flat curing surface — do not stack yet
  • Cover with plastic sheet for 24 hours
  • Stack 6–8 high on pallets after 24 hours
  • Keep covered with plastic or damp cloth for 14 days (longer than concrete blocks)
  • Spray with water 2× daily

Full strength at 28 days. CSEB blocks are often sold at 21 days for lack of storage — don't rush, blocks keep gaining strength for months.

8 Selling to low-cost housing

CSEB is the cheapest block you can make — soil is nearly free. Ideal markets:

Low-cost housing: Government housing projects, NGO self-build programs. CSEB is accepted in most African building codes (unlike adobe).

Rural construction: Farmhouses, storage buildings, schools.

Eco-builders: "Earth-friendly", "low carbon", "natural building" marketing.

Pricing (Africa, 2026):

  • Standard CSEB (295 × 140 × 100 mm): $0.30–$0.60 per block
  • Premium pressed CSEB (stronger): $0.50–$0.90 per block
  • Interlocking CSEB: $0.70–$1.20 per block

Production cost: $0.10–$0.25 per block. Margin: 40–60%.

Selling angles:

  • Cheapest wall material in Africa
  • Made on-site or locally (low transport)
  • Low carbon footprint
  • Good thermal mass (cool in summer, warm in winter)
  • Natural look, no plastering needed
💰 CSEB is the entry point to block making. Manual press + local soil = $2,000 to start. Perfect for rural areas and low-cost housing markets.
