1. Introduction and Why It Matters

When you sell gold, the buyer will test it. If you don't know the purity, you will be cheated. In Africa, many small-scale miners sell raw gold at 50-70% of its true value because they cannot prove its fineness. Assaying is the process of determining how much pure gold is in your sample. It matters because a 1-gram difference in purity on a 100-gram sale can mean hundreds of dollars lost. For example, if gold is 18 karat (75% pure) and you sell it as 22 karat (91.6% pure), you lose about $300 on a 100-gram sale at $60 per gram. Assaying also helps you control your smelting process. If you smelt and the assay shows 85% purity, you know you need to improve your flux or remove more base metals. In Africa, common impurities are silver, copper, and iron. These affect color, hardness, and value. A simple assay can be done with a touchstone and acids, or more accurately with a fire assay or electronic tester. This module will teach you practical methods that work in a small workshop, using locally available materials. You will learn to take a proper sample, test it, and calculate purity. You will also learn to avoid common mistakes like testing dirty metal or using wrong acids. By the end, you will be able to determine the purity of your gold within 1-2% accuracy using basic tools. That knowledge protects your income and helps you negotiate better prices. Remember: never sell gold without knowing its purity. Always assay before you sell or smelt.

2. Tools and Materials

To assay gold, you need a few basic tools. For the touchstone method, you need a black touchstone (basalt or slate), a set of test acids (nitric acid, hydrochloric acid, and aqua regia), and gold test needles of known purity (e.g., 8K, 14K, 18K, 22K, 24K). In Africa, you can buy these from jewelry supply shops in cities like Nairobi, Accra, or Johannesburg. A small acid test kit costs about $50-$100. For fire assay, you need a crucible, furnace (or charcoal forge), lead foil, cupel (bone ash cup), and a balance accurate to 0.01 grams. A digital pocket scale costs $20-$40. You also need safety gear: goggles, gloves, and a respirator. For electronic testing, a gold tester (e.g., Kee Gold Tester) costs $200-$500 and uses a probe and gel. It gives a digital reading. For sampling, you need a small hammer, chisel, and file. Always use a clean file to avoid contamination. For weighing, a 0.01g scale is enough for small lots. For larger lots, use a 0.1g scale. You also need a notebook to record results. In rural areas, you can improvise: use a dark ceramic tile as a touchstone, and buy acids from chemical suppliers. But be careful: acids are dangerous. Store them in glass bottles with tight caps, away from children. For fire assay, you can build a small furnace from a metal bucket and charcoal, but you need a crucible that withstands 1200C. Clay crucibles work. Cupels can be made from bone ash or bought. A balance can be a simple beam scale, but digital is better. Always calibrate your scale with a known weight. These tools will let you assay gold accurately without a lab.

3. Step by Step Method

Here is a practical step-by-step method for assaying gold using the touchstone method, which is cheap and works in the field. First, clean your gold sample by rubbing it with a file or sandpaper to remove dirt and oxide. Then, rub the gold firmly on the touchstone to leave a clear streak. Do this for your sample and for each test needle. Now, apply a drop of nitric acid to the streaks. Nitric acid dissolves silver and base metals but not gold. If the streak disappears, it is not gold. If it remains, gold is present. Next, apply aqua regia (3 parts hydrochloric acid to 1 part nitric acid) to the streaks. Aqua regia dissolves gold. Compare how fast the streak dissolves with the test needles. The needle that dissolves at the same rate as your sample indicates the purity. For example, if your sample dissolves like the 18K needle, it is about 18K (75% pure). For more accuracy, use the fire assay method. Weigh 0.5g of your sample and 2g of lead foil. Wrap the sample in lead and place in a cupel. Heat in a furnace at 1000C for 1 hour. Lead oxidizes and absorbs into the cupel, leaving a gold-silver bead. Weigh the bead. Then, separate silver by boiling in nitric acid. The remaining gold is pure. Calculate purity: (weight of pure gold / original sample weight) x 100. For example, if 0.5g sample yields 0.45g pure gold, purity is 90%. For electronic testing, clean the sample, apply a drop of gel, and touch the probe. The reading shows karat or percentage. Always test three spots on the sample and average. Record all results. This method gives you reliable purity data.

4. Common Mistakes and Fixes

Many learners make mistakes that lead to wrong assay results. Here are the most common and how to fix them. Mistake 1: Testing dirty gold. Dirt, oil, or oxide gives false readings. Fix: Always clean the sample with a file or sandpaper until it shines. Mistake 2: Using old or contaminated acids. Acids absorb moisture and lose strength. Fix: Store acids in tight glass bottles and replace every 6 months. Mistake 3: Not using a control. Without a known purity needle, you cannot compare. Fix: Always use at least three test needles (e.g., 14K, 18K, 22K) and include a known sample. Mistake 4: Rubbing too hard on touchstone. This creates a thick streak that dissolves slowly. Fix: Use light, even pressure to make a thin, uniform streak. Mistake 5: Ignoring temperature in fire assay. Too hot or too cold gives incomplete separation. Fix: Use a furnace with a thermostat set to 1000C. If using charcoal, use a pyrometer or watch for cherry-red heat. Mistake 6: Not weighing accurately. A 0.01g error on a 0.5g sample is 2% error. Fix: Use a calibrated digital scale and weigh three times. Mistake 7: Testing only one spot. Gold can be uneven. Fix: Test three spots and average. Mistake 8: Using wrong acid ratio for aqua regia. Too much nitric acid dissolves gold too fast. Fix: Mix 3 parts HCl to 1 part HNO3 by volume. Mistake 9: Not recording results. You forget and repeat mistakes. Fix: Keep a logbook with date, sample ID, method, and result. Mistake 10: Skipping safety gear. Acids burn skin and eyes. Fix: Always wear goggles, gloves, and apron. By avoiding these mistakes, your assay will be accurate and safe.

5. Safety and Best Practice

Assaying gold involves dangerous chemicals and high heat. Safety is not optional. Always wear personal protective equipment (PPE): safety goggles, nitrile gloves, and a rubber apron. Work in a well-ventilated area, preferably outdoors or with a fume hood. Nitric acid and aqua regia produce toxic fumes. Never breathe them. If you smell fumes, step away and improve ventilation. Store acids in glass bottles with tight caps, in a locked cabinet away from metals and organic matter. Never store acids in metal containers. When mixing aqua regia, always add acid to water, never water to acid. Mix slowly in a glass beaker. Have a bucket of clean water and baking soda nearby to neutralize spills. If acid touches skin, rinse with plenty of water for 15 minutes. If it gets in eyes, rinse and seek medical help. For fire assay, use tongs and heat-resistant gloves. The furnace is 1000C. Keep flammable materials away. Never leave a hot furnace unattended. Use a proper crucible and cupel; cracked ones can explode. After use, let everything cool before handling. Best practice: work in pairs, never alone. Keep a first aid kit with burn cream and eye wash. Label all chemicals clearly. Do not eat, drink, or smoke in the assay area. Wash hands after work. Dispose of waste properly: neutralize acids with baking soda before disposal. Never pour acids down the drain. For electronic testers, follow the manufacturer's instructions and replace gel regularly. Finally, keep good records: date, sample, method, result, and any issues. This helps you improve and stay safe. Remember, a safe assay is a good assay.

6. Practical Exercise

Now it's time to practice. This exercise will guide you through assaying a gold sample using the touchstone method. You will need: a gold sample (at least 0.5g), a touchstone, test acids (nitric and aqua regia), test needles (14K, 18K, 22K), a file, a digital scale (0.01g), and safety gear. Step 1: Clean your gold sample with a file until it is shiny. Weigh it and record the weight. Step 2: Rub the sample on the touchstone to make a streak about 2cm long. Do the same with each test needle. Step 3: Apply one drop of nitric acid to each streak. Wait 30 seconds. The streaks that remain are gold. The ones that disappear are not. Step 4: Apply one drop of aqua regia to each remaining streak. Watch how fast they dissolve. The streak that dissolves at the same rate as the 18K needle indicates your sample is about 18K. Step 5: Repeat the test three times on different spots of your sample. Average your observations. Step 6: Calculate purity. If your sample matches 18K, purity is 75%. If it matches 22K, purity is 91.6%. Record your result. For a more accurate result, use the fire assay method: weigh 0.5g sample, wrap in 2g lead foil, cupel at 1000C for 1 hour, weigh the bead, then boil in nitric acid to remove silver, weigh pure gold, and calculate percentage. Do this exercise with three different gold samples (e.g., from different mines or batches). Compare results. Write a short report: sample ID, weight, method, purity, and any observations. This exercise will build your confidence. Remember, practice makes perfect. The more you assay, the better you get. Share your results with your mentor or classmates for feedback.

<h2>Practical Reference: Assaying and Purity Testing for African Gold Businesses</h2>

<p>This practical reference consolidates the working knowledge a small-to-medium African gold business needs to test purity, price fairly, and avoid disputes. It is written for operators in Ghana, Kenya, Tanzania, Uganda, Nigeria, South Africa, Zimbabwe, and the wider continent where artisanal and small-scale mining (ASM) supplies most dor and scrap gold. The goal is commercial: know what you are buying and selling, document it, and get paid correctly.</p>

<p>In African gold trading, purity determines price. A 22K (91.6%) chain and a 18K (75%) chain look similar but differ by roughly 16.6% in gold content. On a 100 g transaction at USD 65/g gold value, that is about USD 1,080 in value difference. Assaying is therefore not a laboratory luxury it is a cash-flow tool. Buyers who cannot verify purity either overpay, underpay, or lose deals to competitors who can.</p>

<h3 style="color:#c0392b;margin-top:0;"> SAFETY WARNING Fire Assay and Acid Testing Hazards</h3>
<p style="margin-bottom:8px;"><strong>Lead and mercury poisoning:</strong> Fire assay uses lead oxide and can release lead fumes. Mercury amalgamation, still used in some ASM operations, is highly toxic. Work only in a fume hood or well-ventilated space. Never heat lead or mercury indoors or near food.</p>
<p style="margin-bottom:8px;"><strong>Acid burns:</strong> Nitric acid and aqua regia cause severe burns and blindness. Wear splash goggles, nitrile gloves, and a rubber apron. Always add acid to water never water to acid.</p>
<p style="margin-bottom:0;"><strong>Legal and environmental:</strong> In Ghana, Tanzania, Kenya, and South Africa, unlicensed use of mercury or open burning of amalgam is illegal and carries fines or imprisonment. Dispose of acid and lead waste through licensed handlers. Your business licence, export permit, and bank financing can be revoked for non-compliance.</p>
</div>

<h3>Quick Reference Checklist</h3>
<ol>
<li><strong>Collect a representative sample.</strong> Drill, cut, or melt a portion never test only the surface. Surface gold is often higher purity than the core.</li>
<li><strong>Weigh on a calibrated 0.0001 g balance.</strong> Record sample mass and gold mass before and after assay. Calibrate daily with certified weights.</li>
<li><strong>Run two methods when value is high.</strong> Use XRF for speed and fire assay for final pricing on transactions above USD 5,000.</li>
<li><strong>Retain a split sample.</strong> Keep half the sample sealed, labelled, and stored for at least 90 days in case of dispute.</li>
<li><strong>Document every transaction.</strong> Record date, seller ID, gross mass, purity, method, spot price, deductions, and net payment. Issue a signed receipt.</li>
<li><strong>Use the correct spot price.</strong> Use LBMA or a recognised benchmark for the day, and state the currency and exchange rate used.</li>
<li><strong>Apply a written refining charge.</strong> Publish your deduction schedule (e.g., 38% for dor) so sellers know the rules before they arrive.</li>
<li><strong>Re-test on any dispute over 0.5%.</strong> Send the retained split to an independent lab such as SGS, Bureau Veritas, or a national assay office.</li>
</ol>
