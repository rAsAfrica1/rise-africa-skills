1 Why staff quality matters

Blocks are made by people, not just machines. A trained, motivated team produces more, wastes less, and stays safe.

Impact of staff quality:

  • Poorly trained staff — more rejects, accidents, downtime
  • Well-trained staff — consistent quality, higher output, less waste
  • Attitude matters — block making is repetitive; reliable people are gold

Invest in hiring and training. It pays back in weeks.

2 Roles on a block-making team

Even a small yard needs clear roles.

  • Machine operator: runs mixer and block machine
  • Loader/mixer: feeds sand, cement, aggregate
  • Curing yard worker: moves, waters, stacks blocks
  • Quality checker: measures blocks, records results
  • Maintenance person: greases, checks, repairs
  • Supervisor: schedules, solves problems, checks safety
  • Sales/loader: serves customers, loads trucks

3 Hiring: finding reliable workers

Good hiring prevents many problems.

Where to find workers:

  • Referrals from current staff
  • Local radio, notice boards, WhatsApp groups
  • Vocational training centres
  • Labour offices

What to look for:

  • Punctuality and honesty
  • Willingness to learn
  • Safety awareness
  • Physical fitness for lifting

Always check references. Use a paid trial day.

4 Interviewing and testing skills

Ask practical questions and test skills.

  • “What would you do if the machine jams?” — correct answer: stop, lock out, then clear
  • Practical test: start machine, mix, demould
  • Numeracy test: measure a block with tape
  • Safety test: identify hazards in a photo

Hire for attitude; train for skill.

5 Training new operators

Use a step-by-step training plan.

  1. Safety induction — PPE, emergency stops, LOTO
  2. Shadow experienced operator
  3. Supervised operation — simple tasks first
  4. Independent operation with spot checks
  5. Sign-off checklist when competent

Document each step. Do not rush.

6 Cross-training and multiskilling

Train everyone on at least two roles.

  • Covers sickness, holidays, sudden absences
  • Increases flexibility
  • Reduces boredom
  • Builds a stronger team

Use a skills matrix: names down, tasks across. Tick when trained.

7 Performance and motivation

Motivated staff work harder and stay longer.

  • Set fair daily targets
  • Pay wages on time
  • Recognise good work publicly
  • Bonus for quality, not just speed
  • Deal with poor performance quickly and fairly

8 Retaining good staff

Replacing staff is expensive. Keep the good ones.

  • Provide training and progression
  • Keep the workplace safe and clean
  • Treat people with respect
  • Offer consistent work
  • Listen to their ideas

Good staff are your best competitive advantage.
