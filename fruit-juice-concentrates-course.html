â  SAFETY WARNING â PESTICIDES, FERTILISERS AND FOOD SAFETY

Agrochemicals can cause acute poisoning, chronic illness and death, and can leave illegal residues that destroy an export contract overnight. The following are non-negotiable:

  • Never eat, drink, smoke or chew khat/chat while handling or spraying agrochemicals.
  • Always wear the label-specified PPE: gloves, boots, long sleeves, eye protection and a respirator for fumigants and fine sprays.
  • Respect the pre-harvest interval (PHI) on every label. Harvesting inside the PHI can leave residues above Codex maximum residue limits (MRLs) and trigger rejection.
  • Store pesticides in a locked, ventilated, labelled cabinet away from food, feed, seed and children. Never decant into beverage bottles.
  • Follow the WHO Recommended Classification of Pesticides by Hazard. Avoid WHO Class Ia and Ib products where a safer alternative exists.
  • Triple-rinse empty containers, puncture them, and dispose of them through an approved collection scheme. Never reuse them for water or food.
  • If poisoning is suspected, stop work, move the person to fresh air, remove contaminated clothing, and call your national poison centre or nearest clinic immediately. Bring the product label.
  • Keep a written spray log: date, product, batch number, rate, plot, operator, weather and PHI. This log is your legal and commercial defence.

Source: WHO/FAO International Code of Conduct on Pesticide Management (who.int); FAO/WHO Codex Alimentarius MRL database (fao.org/fao-who-codexalimentarius).

Quick Reference Checklist

  1. Test before you spend. Take a composite soil sample (15â20 cores, 0â20 cm depth) and send it for analysis before buying fertiliser. A USD 30â60 test can redirect hundreds of dollars of input spend.
  2. Calculate the marginal return. For every input, estimate the extra yield it buys and multiply by your farm-gate price. If the marginal value is not clearly above the marginal cost, do not buy it.
  3. Apply the 4Rs. Right source, right rate, right time, right place. Split nitrogen on maize and rice rather than applying it all at planting.
  4. Integrate organic matter. Apply 2â5 t/ha of well-decomposed manure or compost where available, and retain crop residues. This raises fertiliser use efficiency and buffers drought.
  5. Record every application. Maintain a spray and fertiliser log with date, product, batch, rate, plot and operator. This is mandatory for certification, export and most agricultural finance.
  6. Enforce PPE and PHI. No spraying without protective equipment, and no harvest inside the pre-harvest interval. Post the PHI for each product in the store and at the field gate.
  7. Know your break-even. Write your break-even yield on the store wall. If the crop is trending below it, act early â reduce cost, find a better market, or cut losses.
  8. Reinvest a fixed share of surplus. Allocate at least 10â20% of gross margin to soil health, water infrastructure or storage. Farms that reinvest outperform farms that consume all surplus.
