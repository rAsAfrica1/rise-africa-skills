Good Training, Bad Training - Learning Too Well

~12 min read15 min gameIntermediate
No device needed

🧠 Brain Opener

A student who memorises the answers but does not understand the subject will fail the exam when the questions change. AI can have the same problem. It is called overfitting.

  • What is memorising?
  • What is understanding?
  • Which is better for a machine?
🎁 Reveal a good answer

Memorising is storing facts exactly. Understanding is knowing how to use them in new situations. AI needs to understand - not just memorise. If it only memorises, it will fail on anything new. This is called overfitting.

📖 Dumi and the Guessing Game

Dumi was ten and had a game he played with his friends. He would think of a number between one and ten. His friends would guess. Most got it wrong.

Then Dumi told them the secret. "The number is always three."

The next day, his friends got it right every time. They felt clever. Dumi laughed.

"Now I am going to change the game," he said. "The number can be any number between one and ten."

His friends got it wrong again. They had memorised the answer. They had not learned the game.

"This is what machines do wrong," Dumi said. "They remember the answer from yesterday, but when today changes, they are stuck."

His friend Chipo thought about it. "So what should we have done?"

"You should have asked questions," Dumi said. "Is it bigger than five? Is it even? Is it small? Real learning is asking the right questions, not just remembering the answers."

His friends tried the new way. It took longer, but they got more right. Not because they had memorised, but because they had learned to think.

💡 The Big Idea

Overfitting - learning too much of the wrong thing

When a machine memorises every example perfectly, it does great on those examples. But give it a new example and it fails. This is overfitting. It means the machine did not learn the real pattern - it learned the specific examples. Good training is not about perfect scores on the examples you taught. It is about good scores on examples you did not.

📚 Three Deep Lessons

1

Memorising vs learning

A student can memorise "2 plus 2 equals 4" without understanding addition. They pass one question and fail the next. A student who understands addition can work out any sum.

AI works the same way. If a machine memorises every cat photo, it only recognises those exact cats. If it learns what "cat" means, it recognises cats it has never seen. The first is memorising. The second is learning. Good training aims at the second.

2

Why overfitting happens

Overfitting happens when the machine has too much time and too few examples. It finds tiny details in the examples - a specific lighting, a specific background - and thinks those details are the pattern.

The cure is more examples and less time. Give the machine thousands of examples and stop training early. A slightly weaker machine that has learned the real pattern is better than a perfect machine that has memorised the training set.

3

How you check for it

Always keep some examples hidden. Train the machine on 80 percent. Test it on the other 20 percent. If it scores 100 percent on the training set but 60 percent on the test set, it is overfitting.

Real AI teams keep a third set too - a final test set that nobody touches until the very end. If the machine passes this, it is ready for real use.

🛠 Hands-On - The Overfitting Test

You need

  • 20 pieces of paper
  • A pen
  • A friend
  1. Write 20 numbers, one on each paper. Make them all different.
  2. Show your friend the first 15 numbers and tell them to memorise.
  3. Then ask: what is the next number? They will guess. It will be wrong.
  4. Now write 15 new numbers on paper and let your friend see the pattern (for example: increasing by 3 each time).
  5. Ask them to predict the next number from the pattern. They will get it right.
Why it works: The first friend memorised. The second one learned. Real learning finds the pattern, not the examples.

🎮 Game - Overfit or Learn

4 to 12 players - 15 min

One player writes ten examples of a rule (for example: "animals with four legs: dog, cow, cat"). The others guess the rule. If they say "dog, cow, cat," they memorised. If they say "any animal with four legs," they learned. Score by which answer was given.

🤖 AI-Era Skill

AI can overfit just like a student. Test it with a pattern question.

Try this prompt: "If I train an AI to recognise dogs using 100 photos of golden retrievers, what will happen when I show it a black poodle?"

The answer should say: the AI will probably fail, because it has only seen golden retrievers. More diverse training data would fix this.

🌍 Real Stories from Africa

Medical AI overfitting

A hospital trained an AI to spot pneumonia from chest X-rays. It scored perfectly in testing. In a new hospital, it failed badly. The problem: it had learned to spot the X-ray machine model, not the disease. Different machines took different photos, and the AI was confused.

Face recognition across Africa

Early face recognition AI was trained mostly on European faces. When used in Africa, it made mistakes. The AI had overfit on European faces and could not generalise. Newer systems use diverse training data from many countries.

🏆 Project Challenge

Catch an Overfit

Build a friend-trainer and test for overfitting.

  1. Pick a simple rule - fruit with seeds, or animals with fur.
  2. Show 15 examples to a friend, one at a time, and let them guess.
  3. Test them on 5 new examples they have never seen.
  4. Score their answers.
  5. Write 3 sentences about whether they learned the rule or memorised the examples.
  6. Try again with 30 examples and see if the score improves.
