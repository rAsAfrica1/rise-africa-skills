â  SAFETY WARNING â Pesticides, Post-Harvest Chemicals and Food Safety

If you use any pesticide, fumigant, wax, or post-harvest treatment, the following are non-negotiable:

  • Never apply a pesticide that is not registered for that specific crop in your country. Check your national regulator (e.g. KEPHIS in Kenya, NAFDAC in Nigeria, DALRRD in South Africa).
  • Always observe the pre-harvest interval (PHI) printed on the label. Selling produce before the PHI expires can cause buyer rejection, export bans, and acute consumer poisoning.
  • Wear gloves, boots, eye protection, and a respirator when handling concentrates or fumigating stores. Never fumigate a sealed store without a trained operator and a gas monitor.
  • Store chemicals in a locked, labelled, ventilated store away from food, feed, seed, and children. Never decant into drink bottles.
  • Wash hands and change clothing after handling. If poisoning is suspected, contact a poison centre immediately and bring the product label.
  • Residue limits are commercial risk too: exceeding EU or local maximum residue limits (MRLs) can destroy an entire export consignment and your buyer relationship.

Quick Reference Checklist

  1. Measure your loss before you fix it. Weigh in and weigh out for two weeks per commodity. You cannot manage what you have not counted.
  2. Harvest at the right maturity and the right time of day. Cooler hours reduce field heat and extend shelf life by days, not hours.
  3. Get produce off the ground immediately. Crates, pallets, or clean tarpaulins â never heap produce directly on soil or hot concrete.
  4. Shade and ventilate. Even a simple shade net and cross-ventilated store cuts losses measurably at near-zero cost.
  5. Grade and pack for the buyer, not for the farm. Uniform count, uniform size, clean labels. Grading raises price even when volume is unchanged.
  6. Plan the cold chain as one unbroken line. A single gap â a hot hour at the market gate â can undo a week of careful handling.
  7. Keep records that a lender will accept. Volumes, loss rates, prices, and costs, dated and signed. This is your credit history.
  8. Verify chemical use and PHI before every dispatch. Check the label, check the date, check the buyer's MRL requirement.
