Typing Chinese With a Phone Keyboard

You type Chinese by typing Pinyin and choosing the character — which means your Pinyin and your tones are what unlock the writing.

🎒 What you need

  • the shared phone, about 20 minutes
  • permission to add a keyboard, or the course's browser typing pad

✋ Do this

  1. Open the typing pad in the browser. Type 'nihao' and watch 你好 appear in the options. Select it.
  2. Type 'shouji' and look at the list of candidates. Several different words share that sound. Find 手机 — mobile phone — and choose it.
  3. Type each of the five words of this module by their Pinyin and select the correct character from the list every time.
  4. Now type your own name in Chinese if you know it, or type 我是学生 as a full sentence.
  5. Write down on paper which words the keyboard offered you that were NOT the one you wanted. That list is your homework — those are words that sound identical to ones you know.
  6. Show one person the sentence you typed on the screen and read it aloud to them.

💡 Why it works

Pinyin input is how almost every Chinese person actually writes on a device, and it flips the skill you need: you must recognise characters rather than produce every stroke. Practising recognition against a list of similar-sounding options is one of the fastest ways to sharpen it.

🔥 Challenge

Type a short message in Chinese and show it to someone. Have them ask you what each character means.
