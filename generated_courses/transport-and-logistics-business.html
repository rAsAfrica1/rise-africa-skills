Overview of Logistics Business Models

A logistics business model is simply how you make money moving goods. In Africa, the most common models are: asset-based (you own trucks, bikes, or warehouses), asset-light (you broker loads to other owners), and hybrid (you own some assets and subcontract the rest). For example, a boda boda rider in Kampala who owns his motorcycle is asset-based; a broker who connects 50 riders to clients via WhatsApp is asset-light. Your model determines your costs, risks, and profits..Trim()

.Trim()

Start by calculating your break-even point. If you buy a used 7-tonne truck for $25,000, spend $500/month on insurance, $1,200/month on fuel, and $300/month on maintenance, your monthly fixed cost is about $2,000. If you charge $0.10 per tonne-km and drive 10,000 km per month, you earn $7,000. Subtract variable costs (fuel, driver) of $3,000, leaving $4,000 gross profit. That covers fixed costs and gives $2,000 net. But if you only get 5,000 km of work, you lose money. Asset-light models avoid this risk: you take a 10-15% commission on each load, with almost no fixed costs..Trim()

.Trim()

In Africa, successful models often blend both. For instance, Kobo360 started asset-light (connecting truckers to cargo) but later added its own fleet for reliability. Sendy in Kenya began as asset-light for deliveries, then built warehouses. The key is to match your model to local demand: in landlocked countries like Rwanda, cross-border trucking is high-value but requires customs expertise. In cities like Lagos, last-mile delivery by motorcycle is low-cost but competitive..Trim()

.Trim()

To choose your model, answer: What can I afford to own? What can I control? Where is the biggest pain point? If clients complain about unreliable trucks, asset-based gives you control. If they complain about high prices, asset-light lets you cut costs. Many African logistics startups fail because they copy models from Europe or Asia without adapting to local fuel costs, road conditions, or payment delays. Test small: rent one truck or broker one load before scaling..Trim()

In logistics, risk is anything that can stop you from moving goods from point A to point B on time and without loss. Crisis Management and Emergency Response

A crisis is any sudden event that threatens your business, like a serious accident, hijacking, fire, or natural disaster. Your response in the first hour can save lives and money. Start by creating an emergency response team: a leader (maybe you), a communications person, and a logistics person. Give them clear roles.
