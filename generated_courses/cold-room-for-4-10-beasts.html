Positioning for Ventilation in Hot Climates

How a Compressor Works in Simple Terms

A compressor is the heart of your cold room. It pumps refrigerant gas through pipes, squeezing it to raise its pressure and temperature. Think of a bicycle pump: when you push the handle, air gets hot and compressed. The compressor does the same with refrigerant. The hot, high-pressure gas then flows to a condenser (the outdoor unit), where it releases heat and turns into a liquid. That liquid goes through an expansion valve, which drops its pressure and makes it very cold. Finally, the cold liquid enters the evaporator (inside the cold room), where it absorbs heat from the room and turns back into a gas. The gas returns to the compressor, and the cycle repeats. For a cold room for 4-10 beasts, you need a compressor that can handle the heat load. A typical 4-beast room might use a 1.5 HP compressor, while 10 beasts need about 3 HP. In Africa, ambient temperatures are high, so compressors work harder. Choose a model rated for high ambient, like those with 'tropical' or 'high ambient' specifications. Common brands in Africa include Copeland, Bitzer, and Tecumseh. Local technicians can repair these. Keep the compressor clean and well-ventilated. Never block airflow around it. If you hear unusual noises or it runs constantly without cooling, call a technician. Regular maintenanceâchecking oil levels, cleaning coilsâextends its life. A well-maintained compressor can last 10-15 years..Trim()
