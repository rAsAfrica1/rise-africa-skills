Understanding ISPM 15 for Wood Packaging

If you export goods on wooden pallets, crates, or dunnage, you must comply with ISPM 15 (International Standards for Phytosanitary Measures No. 15). This regulation prevents the spread of pests like the pine wood nematode and Asian longhorn beetle through wood packaging. There are two approved treatments: heat treatment (HT) and methyl bromide fumigation (MB). Heat treatment requires heating the wood core to 56Â°C for at least 30 minutes. Methyl bromide fumigation uses a gas that is being phased out in many countries due to ozone depletion, so HT is preferred. After treatment, the wood must be marked with the IPPC logo (a wheat symbol), the country code (e.g., KE for Kenya, NG for Nigeria), a unique producer code, and the treatment code (HT or MB). You must ensure your pallet supplier is certified by your national plant protection organization (NPPO). If you use untreated wood, your shipment can be refused entry, fumigated at your cost, or destroyed. In the EU, non-compliant wood packaging is often rejected outright. The cost of re-treatment or return shipping can exceed the value of your goods..Trim()

To comply, always ask your supplier for the IPPC mark and keep records of treatment certificates. If you manufacture your own crates, contact your NPPO to get certified. Budget for treated wood: it costs about 20-30% more than untreated, but the alternative is losing your export market. For small exporters, consider using plastic pallets or corrugated cardboard instead of wood, as they are exempt from ISPM 15. Corrugated Fiberboard: Grades and Selection

Corrugated fiberboard is the workhorse of export packaging. It is made of a fluted layer (the wavy part) glued between flat linerboards. In Africa, you will find it in three common grades: single wall (one flute), double wall (two flutes), and triple wall (three flutes). .Trim()

The key number is the Edge Crush Test (ECT) rating. This measures how much force a box can take on its edge before crushing. In Africa, many suppliers sell boxes by 'bursting strength' (Mullen test), but ECT is more reliable for stacking. Ask your supplier for the ECT value. If they cannot provide it, test a sample: stack boxes 2 meters high in a humid room for 24 hours and see if they deform..Trim()

.Trim()

Humidity is your enemy. Corrugated board loses up to 50% of its strength at 90% relative humidity. .Trim()

For example, if you stack 5 boxes of 20 kg each, the bottom box must support 100 kg. Choosing the right packaging material depends on two things: the product and the route. Start by listing product characteristics: weight, fragility, moisture sensitivity, value, and shape. How you stack boxes on a pallet determines whether your load arrives intact or as a pile of broken goods. The goal is to interlock boxes so they don't shift during transport.
