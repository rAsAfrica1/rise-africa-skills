Module 1 · Introduction to PVC Pipes and Plumbing Systems

Identifying Common PVC Pipe Sizes Used in African Homes (20mm to 110mm)

In African homes, PVC pipes come in standard metric sizes measured by outside diameter (OD). The most common sizes you will see are 20mm, 25mm, 32mm, 40mm, 50mm, 63mm, 75mm, 90mm, and 110mm. These are used for different purposes: 20mm and 25mm are for small supply lines to taps and toilets; 32mm and 40mm are for sink and basin waste; 50mm and 63mm are for shower and bath drains; 75mm and 90mm are for main soil stacks; and 110mm is for underground sewer lines and toilet outlets. In many markets, pipes are sold in 4-meter or 6-meter lengths. You can identify the size by looking at the markings printed on the pipe: it will say something like 'PVC 110mm x 4m' or 'uPVC 32mm'. If the markings are worn off, use a measuring tape to measure the outside diameter. For example, a 110mm pipe will measure about 110mm across the outside. Be careful: some older pipes use imperial sizes like 1/2 inch or 2 inch. A 1/2 inch pipe is about 21mm, which is close to 20mm but not exactly the same. Always match the pipe size to the fittings you have. If you are replacing a section, take a small piece of the old pipe to the shop to compare. In rural areas, you might find pipes sold without labels; in that case, measure the inside diameter (ID) as well, because the wall thickness affects flow. For example, a 20mm pipe with thick walls might have an ID of only 15mm, reducing water pressure. Always buy pipes from a reputable supplier who stores them out of direct sunlight, as UV light makes PVC brittle. When cutting, use a hacksaw and then deburr the edges so fittings slide on easily. Remember: the size number on the pipe refers to the outside diameter, not the inside. So a 110mm pipe will not fit into a 110mm fitting if the fitting is meant for a different standard. Always test-fit before gluing..Trim()

Understanding the Difference Between PVC, uPVC, and CPVC for Hot and Cold Water

PVC, uPVC, and CPVC are all types of plastic pipes, but they are made differently and used for different jobs. PVC (polyvinyl chloride) is the general term. It often contains plasticizers that make it flexible. You will see grey or white PVC pipes used for drainage and cold water. uPVC (unplasticized PVC) has no plasticizers, so it is rigid and strong. It is the most common pipe for cold water supply and drainage in African homes. uPVC is usually white or grey and comes in sizes from 20mm to 110mm. It cannot handle hot water; above 60Â°C it softens and can burst. CPVC (chlorinated PVC) is PVC that has been chlorinated, making it resistant to higher temperatures. CPVC can handle water up to 90Â°C, so it is used for hot water lines from geysers or solar heaters. CPVC is usually cream or light yellow and is more expensive. In many African markets, CPVC is less common and may need to be ordered. For cold water, always use uPVC. For hot water, use CPVC or PPR (another plastic). Never use ordinary PVC for hot water. If you are unsure, check the pipe marking: it will say 'uPVC' or 'CPVC' and may show a temperature rating. Also, solvent cement for uPVC is different from CPVC cement. You must use the correct cement. For example, uPVC cement will not properly bond CPVC. When buying, ask the supplier for the right cement. In rural areas, you might find only uPVC. For hot water, you can use copper or PPR if available. But for most cold water plumbing, uPVC is your best choice. It is cheap, easy to cut, and lasts for decades if installed correctly. Remember: uPVC and PVC are often used interchangeably in shops, but uPVC is the rigid type you want for plumbing. CPVC is for hot water only. Always read the label..Trim()

Tools You Need to Start: Hacksaw, Measuring Tape, and Deburring Tool

To start working with PVC pipes, you need a few basic tools. The most important are a hacksaw, a measuring tape, and a deburring tool. A hacksaw with a fine-tooth blade (24 teeth per inch) is best for cutting PVC. A coarse blade will chip the plastic. You can buy a good hacksaw for about $5 to $10 in most African markets. Always use a mitre box or a steady hand to make straight cuts. A measuring tape, at least 3 meters long, is essential for measuring pipe lengths and marking cut lines. Use a pencil or a permanent marker to mark the pipe. A deburring tool (also called a reamer) removes the burrs inside and outside the pipe after cutting. If you don't have a deburring tool, you can use a pocket knife or sandpaper, but a proper tool is faster and safer. You will also need a solvent cement applicator (a small brush), safety gloves, and safety glasses. For cutting larger pipes (50mm and above), a pipe cutter or a mitre saw can be used, but a hacksaw works fine. When cutting, secure the pipe in a vice or hold it firmly. Cut slowly to avoid cracking. After cutting, deburr both inside and outside. This step is critical: burrs can cause turbulence and leaks. Also, you need a tape measure that locks, so you can measure alone. A small spirit level helps to ensure pipes are straight. For gluing, you need clean rags to wipe dirt. A bucket of water and soap for cleaning hands is also good. In rural areas, you might not find a deburring tool, so use a file or sandpaper. But invest in one if you can; it costs about $3. Always keep your tools clean and dry. A hacksaw blade will last longer if you don't force it. Replace blades when they become dull. With these tools, you can cut and fit PVC pipes for any home repair. Practice on scrap pieces first. Remember: a straight cut and a clean deburr are the secrets to leak-free joints..Trim()

How Water Flows: Gravity vs. Pressure Systems in Rural and Urban Settings

Water flows in two main ways: gravity and pressure. In rural areas, many homes use gravity systems. Water is stored in a tank on a roof or a hill, and it flows down through pipes to taps. The higher the tank, the stronger the flow. For every 1 meter of height, you get about 0.1 bar of pressure. So a tank 5 meters above a tap gives about 0.5 bar, which is enough for a slow flow. Gravity systems are simple and don't need pumps, but pressure is low. You must use larger pipes (e.g., 25mm or 32mm) to reduce friction loss. In urban areas, water comes from a mains supply under pressure, often 2 to 4 bar. This pressure pushes water through pipes to your home. You can use smaller pipes (20mm) for supply lines. But high pressure can cause water hammer and leaks, so you need pressure reducers if it's above 4 bar. In both systems, water always flows from high pressure to low pressure. In gravity systems, the height difference creates pressure. In pressure systems, a pump or the municipal supply creates pressure. When designing a system, you must consider flow rate (liters per minute) and pressure. For a gravity system, use a tank at least 3 meters above the highest tap. For pressure systems, ensure pipes are rated for the pressure (uPVC is usually rated for 6 to 10 bar). Also, avoid sharp bends and use elbows to reduce friction. In rural areas, if you have a borehole, you might need a pump to fill the tank. In urban areas, if the mains pressure is low, you might need a booster pump. Always install a stop valve at the tank and at the main entry. For gravity systems, use a float valve in the tank to prevent overflow. For pressure systems, install a pressure gauge to monitor. Remember: water flow is affected by pipe size, length, and fittings. A 20mm pipe can only carry so much water. If you need more flow, use a larger pipe. Test your system by opening a tap and measuring how long it takes to fill a 10-liter bucket. That gives you flow rate in liters per minute. Adjust as needed..Trim()

Safety First: Handling PVC Solvent Cement and Cutting Tools

PVC solvent cement is a dangerous chemical. It contains solvents like tetrahydrofuran (THF) and cyclohexanone, which are flammable and toxic. Always work in a well-ventilated area, preferably outdoors or with open windows and fans. Never use it near an open flame or a water heater. Wear safety glasses and chemical-resistant gloves (nitrile or neoprene). Avoid breathing the fumes; if you feel dizzy, move to fresh air immediately. Keep the cement container closed when not in use. Do not smoke while working. Solvent cement can burn your skin, so wash it off with soap and water if you get it on you. If it gets in your eyes, flush with water for 15 minutes and seek medical help. When cutting pipes with a hacksaw, wear safety glasses to protect your eyes from plastic chips. Secure the pipe in a vice or clamp so it doesn't slip. Cut away from your body. Keep your fingers clear of the blade. A dull blade can jump and cut you. After cutting, deburr carefully; the cut edge can be sharp. Use a deburring tool or a file, not your fingers. When using a knife to deburr, cut away from yourself. Store tools safely when not in use. Keep solvent cement out of reach of children. Do not store it in a hot vehicle. The cement has a shelf life; check the date. If it becomes thick or stringy, throw it away. When gluing, work in a well-ventilated space and avoid inhaling deeply. Use a small brush to apply cement, not your fingers. If you spill cement, wipe it up with a rag and dispose of it properly. Do not pour it down the drain. Always have a first aid kit nearby with eye wash and bandages. In rural areas, if you cannot get proper gloves, use thick plastic bags as a temporary barrier, but get proper gloves as soon as possible. Remember: safety is not optional. A few minutes of preparation prevents serious injury..Trim()

Module 2 · Measuring, Cutting, and Preparing PVC Pipes

Accurate Measurement for Pipe Runs in Bathrooms and Kitchens

In bathroom and kitchen plumbing, a wrong measurement means wasted pipe, extra joints, and leaks. Start by understanding what you are measuring: pipe runs go from fitting to fitting, not wall to wall. For example, if you are connecting a sink P-trap to a wall drain, measure from the center of the trap outlet to the center of the drain pipe. Use a steel tape measure, not a cloth tape, because cloth stretches. Write down each measurement in millimeters. In many African markets, PVC pipes come in 3-meter or 6-meter lengths, so plan cuts to minimize waste. For a typical bathroom sink, the horizontal run from the wall to the P-trap might be 400 mm. Always measure twice: once at the actual location, and once after checking that the pipe will sit straight without forcing. Use a pencil or marker to mark the pipe where it will enter each fitting. Remember that fittings add length: a 90-degree elbow adds about 30-40 mm to the run. If you forget that, your pipe will be too short. For kitchen sinks, the run from the cabinet floor to the sink basket might be 600 mm vertical. Measure from the floor flange to the bottom of the basket strainer. Account for the slope: drain pipes need a fall of about 1 cm per 30 cm of horizontal run. So if your horizontal run is 900 mm, the end should be 30 mm lower than the start. Use a spirit level to check slope. In Africa, many homes have concrete walls, so you cannot easily move pipes inside walls. Measure from the outside of the wall to the fixture. Always add 10-20 mm extra on each end for insertion into fittings, then cut to final length after dry-fitting. Dry-fit all pipes and fittings before gluing. If a pipe is too long, you can cut more; if too short, you must start over. Keep a notebook with a sketch of the run, showing each measurement and fitting. This saves time and money..Trim()

Using a Hacksaw or Pipe Cutter for Clean Cuts on PVC

PVC pipe must be cut square, meaning the cut face is at exactly 90 degrees to the pipe length. A crooked cut causes leaks because the pipe will not seat fully in the fitting. You have two main tools: a hacksaw or a pipe cutter. A hacksaw is cheap and available everywhere in Africa. Use a 24-tooth per inch blade for PVC; too many teeth clog, too few chip the plastic. Secure the pipe in a vise or clamp it to a workbench. If you don't have a vise, use a G-clamp to hold it against a sturdy table. Mark the cut line all the way around the pipe with a pencil or marker. Then use a square (or a piece of paper folded to 90 degrees) to draw a line around the pipe. Start the cut with light strokes to make a groove, then use full strokes. Do not force the saw; let the teeth do the work. For a clean cut, rotate the pipe slightly after every few strokes. A pipe cutter (also called a plastic pipe cutter) works faster. It has a ratcheting mechanism and a sharp blade. Place the pipe in the cutter, tighten the handle until the blade touches the pipe, then rotate the cutter around the pipe while tightening slightly after each turn. Do not over-tighten or you will crack the pipe. The cutter makes a square cut with minimal burrs. For large diameters (50 mm and above), use a hacksaw or a mitre box. A mitre box helps keep the saw straight. After cutting, check squareness by standing the pipe on a flat surface; it should not wobble. If it wobbles, recut. Always wear safety glasses and gloves. PVC chips are sharp. In many African workshops, a hacksaw with a new blade costs less than a pipe cutter, but a pipe cutter saves time on many cuts. For a simple bathroom job, a hacksaw is fine. For production work, invest in a good pipe cutter. Remember: a clean cut saves you 10 minutes of deburring and prevents leaks..Trim()

Deburring and Chamfering Pipe Ends for Smooth Joints

After cutting PVC pipe, the cut edge has burrs: small ridges of plastic that stick out. If you don't remove them, the pipe will not slide fully into the fitting, and the solvent cement will not seal properly. Deburring means removing those burrs. Chamfering means making a slight bevel (angle) on the outside edge. Both are quick and essential. Use a deburring tool, a pocket knife, or a file. A deburring tool has a curved blade that shaves the inside and outside edges. If you don't have one, use a sharp knife to scrape the inside and outside edges at a 45-degree angle. Then use a file or sandpaper (80-120 grit) to smooth the edge. For chamfering, you want a bevel of about 1-2 mm on the outside edge. This helps the pipe slide into the fitting without pushing the cement out. A simple way: hold the pipe at a 45-degree angle and rub it on a piece of sandpaper or a file a few times. Do not over-chamfer; you don't want to reduce the wall thickness. After deburring and chamfering, wipe the pipe end with a clean rag to remove dust and plastic shavings. Then dry-fit the pipe into the fitting. It should go in easily but not loosely. If it is too tight, chamfer more. If too loose, the pipe may be out of round; you may need to cut a new end. In Africa, many plumbers skip deburring, but that leads to leaks. A leak in a wall means breaking the wall to fix it. Take 30 seconds to deburr and chamfer every pipe end. Use a rag to clean the inside of the fitting too. Solvent cement needs clean surfaces to melt and fuse the plastic. Any dust or oil weakens the joint. Keep a small deburring tool in your pocket. It costs little and saves hours..Trim()

Marking Insertion Depth for Solvent-Weld Joints

Solvent-weld joints (also called glue joints) work by melting the PVC surfaces and fusing them together. For a strong joint, the pipe must be inserted to the full depth of the fitting socket. If you insert too little, the joint is weak; too much, and the pipe bottoms out and may crack. So you must mark the insertion depth on the pipe. First, measure the depth of the fitting socket. For example, a 20 mm (1/2 inch) PVC elbow typically has a socket depth of about 15-20 mm. A 50 mm (2 inch) fitting has a depth of about 30-40 mm. Use a tape measure or a small ruler. Then, using a pencil or marker, draw a line around the pipe at that distance from the end. For example, if the socket depth is 20 mm, mark a line 20 mm from the cut end. When you insert the pipe into the fitting, you should see that line just at the edge of the fitting. This ensures full insertion. Some plumbers mark the pipe with a wrap of masking tape or a permanent marker. Do not use a grease pencil because it leaves residue. After marking, dry-fit the pipe and check that the mark aligns with the fitting rim. Then remove the pipe and apply solvent cement. Apply cement to both the pipe end (up to the mark) and the inside of the fitting. Push the pipe into the fitting with a quarter-turn twist. Hold for 30 seconds. The mark should disappear into the fitting. If the mark is still visible, you did not push hard enough. If the pipe bottoms out before the mark, the socket is shallower than you thought; recut and remark. In Africa, many leaks come from under-inserted pipes. A simple pencil mark prevents that. Keep a marker in your tool bag. Also, mark the pipe for orientation: draw a line along the pipe and fitting to align them. This helps when you have multiple joints. Always work quickly; solvent cement sets in seconds..Trim()

Avoiding Common Cutting Mistakes That Cause Leaks

Many PVC leaks are not caused by bad glue but by bad cuts. Here are the most common mistakes and how to avoid them. First, cutting at an angle. If your cut is not square, the pipe will not seat fully in the fitting. The cement will only touch part of the surface, leaving a gap. Always use a square or mitre box. Check squareness by standing the pipe on a flat surface. Second, not deburring. Burrs push the pipe out of the fitting or create a channel for water to leak. Always deburr and chamfer. Third, using the wrong blade. A hacksaw blade with too many teeth clogs and overheats the PVC, causing rough edges. Use 24 TPI. A dull blade smears the plastic. Change blades often. Fourth, cutting without measuring twice. If you cut too short, you waste pipe and may need a coupling. Always dry-fit before cutting. Fifth, not accounting for fitting depth. If you cut a pipe to the exact distance between two fittings, it will be too short because each fitting adds depth. Add the socket depth (e.g., 20 mm) at each end. Sixth, cutting on a dirty surface. PVC shavings and dirt get inside the pipe and contaminate the joint. Cut over a clean cloth or bucket. Seventh, using a wood saw. Wood saws have teeth that tear PVC. Use a hacksaw or pipe cutter. Eighth, cutting without clamping. If the pipe moves, the cut is crooked. Clamp it securely. Ninth, not marking the cut line all the way around. A partial mark leads to a crooked cut. Tenth, cutting pipe that is cold. In cold weather, PVC is brittle and cracks. Warm it slightly with your hands or leave it in the sun. In Africa, many of these mistakes happen because of rushing. Take your time. A clean, square, deburred cut is the foundation of a leak-free joint. Replace your blade when it starts to snag. Keep a file handy. And always test your joints with water before closing walls..Trim()

Practice Project: Cutting and Preparing a Simple Pipe Assembly

Now you will practice everything from this module by making a simple pipe assembly: a 90-degree elbow connecting two straight pipes. This is the most common joint in bathroom and kitchen plumbing. You will need: a 1-meter length of 20 mm PVC pipe, two 20 mm 90-degree elbows, a hacksaw or pipe cutter, a deburring tool or knife, sandpaper, a pencil, a tape measure, and PVC solvent cement. First, plan your assembly. Let's say you want a pipe that goes 300 mm up, then turns 90 degrees, then goes 200 mm horizontally. Measure and mark the first pipe: cut a piece 300 mm long. Remember to add insertion depth: each elbow socket is 20 mm deep, so cut the pipe 300 mm + 20 mm = 320 mm. Mark the cut line all the way around. Cut with a hacksaw, keeping the cut square. Deburr and chamfer both ends. Mark the insertion depth (20 mm) on each end. Repeat for the second pipe: 200 mm + 20 mm = 220 mm. Cut, deburr, chamfer, and mark. Now dry-fit the assembly: insert the first pipe into one elbow, then the second pipe into the other end of that elbow. Check that the marks align with the fitting rims. The assembly should look like an L shape. If any pipe is too long, recut. If too short, start over. Once dry-fit is correct, disassemble. Clean all surfaces with a rag. Apply solvent cement to the pipe end and inside the fitting. Push together with a quarter turn. Hold for 30 seconds. Wipe off excess cement. Repeat for the other joint. When to Use Threaded Fittings vs. Solvent-Weld

In PVC plumbing, you have two main ways to join pipes: solvent-weld (glue) and threaded fittings. Solvent-weld is permanent: you apply PVC primer and cement, push the pipe into the fitting, and it fuses into one piece. Threaded fittings screw together and can be taken apart later. .Trim()

Use solvent-weld for most of your piping runs, especially underground or inside walls. It is cheaper, faster, and less likely to leak because there are no threads to seal. .Trim()

Use threaded fittings when you need to connect to metal pipes, pumps, filters, or valves that have threaded ports. Also use them where you might need to disassemble for cleaning or repair, like a drain line cleanout. For instance, connecting a PVC pipe to a galvanized steel pump outlet requires a threaded adapter. In Africa, many water systems mix PVC and galvanized pipes, so threaded transitions are common..Trim()

.Trim()

Never use threaded PVC fittings underground without sealing them well, because soil movement can loosen them. Using Teflon Tape or Pipe Dope on Threads

Teflon tape is cheap and clean. Buy a roll of white tape (for water lines) about 12mm wide. Pipe dope is a paste that you brush onto the male threads. It fills gaps better than tape and is preferred for larger fittings (25mm and above) or where threads are rough. .Trim()

Never use both tape and dope togetherâit can cause the joint to crack. For plastic-to-plastic threaded joints, tape is usually enough. Mistake 1: Overtightening. PVC threads are soft. If you crank too hard, the fitting cracks or the threads strip. Always hand-tighten first, then use a wrench for only 1 to 1.5 turns. Mistake 7: Forgetting to leak-test. Always test with water pressure before burying or closing walls. If you see a drip, tighten a quarter turn. If it persists, disassemble, clean, and reapply sealant. By avoiding these mistakes, your threaded joints will last for years..Trim()

Understanding Drain, Waste, and Vent (DWV) Systems

A DWV system has three jobs: drain wastewater away, carry solid waste to a sewer or septic tank, and vent sewer gases safely outside. In a typical African home, PVC pipes are used because they are cheap, smooth inside (so waste flows easily), and resist corrosion. The drain pipes slope downward so gravity does the work. Waste pipes carry toilets, sinks, and showers. Vent pipes go up through the roof and let air in, so water flows smoothly and gases escape. Without a vent, a toilet flush can suck the water out of a nearby sink trap, letting sewer smell into the house..Trim()

.Trim()

Start by mapping your system. Use a pencil and paper to sketch where the toilet, sinks, showers, and outside tank or sewer line are. Mark the main stack (the big vertical pipe) and where it exits the building. In most homes, the main stack is 110 mm PVC for the toilet, and branch lines are 50 mm or 40 mm for sinks and showers. Every fixture needs a trap (P-trap or bottle trap) to hold water and block gas. The vent pipe is usually 50 mm and must go above the roof by at least 300 mm. If you cannot vent through the roof, an air admittance valve (AAV) can work, but check local rules..Trim()

.Trim()

When installing, cut PVC with a fine-tooth saw or pipe cutter. Deburr the cut with a file or sandpaper. Dry-fit everything first. Use PVC solvent cement: apply primer to both pipe and fitting, then cement, push together with a quarter turn, and hold for 30 seconds. Slope horizontal drain pipes at least 1:60 (about 1 cm per 60 cm) and no more than 1:40 (1.5 cm per 40 cm). Too little slope causes clogs; too much slope lets water run away and leave solids behind. Support pipes every 1.2 m with plastic clips or straps. Test by pouring 20 litres of water down each fixture and checking for leaks. Vent pipes must be clear and open to air. Never connect a vent to a chimney or use a vent as a drain. Keep it simple, slope it right, and vent it well..Trim()

Using the wrong pipe diameter is a top cause of clogs and slow drains. In PVC DWV systems, the size is measured by the outside diameter (OD) but named by nominal bore: 40 mm, 50 mm, 75 mm, 110 mm. For a toilet, you need 110 mm (4-inch) pipe. A toilet discharges about 6â9 litres per flush with solid waste. A smaller pipe will block. The toilet's waste outlet is usually 100 mm or 110 mm, so the pipe must match. Never reduce below 110 mm for a toilet branch..Trim()

.Trim()

For sinks (kitchen and bathroom), use 40 mm (1.5-inch) pipe. That handles soapy water and small food particles. If the sink is far from the main stack (more than 3 metres), use 50 mm to reduce clogging. For showers and bathtubs, use 40 mm or 50 mm. A shower drain sees hair and soap scum; 50 mm is safer if multiple fixtures share the line. For floor drains (common in wet rooms), use 50 mm. For a washing machine, use 50 mm because it pumps water fast..Trim()

.Trim()

When combining fixtures, the branch pipe must be large enough for the total flow. A bathroom group (toilet, sink, shower) often uses a 110 mm branch for the toilet and 50 mm branches for sink and shower, all connecting to a 110 mm main stack. Proper Slope for Drainage Pipes (1:60 to 1:40)

Slope, or fall, is the angle at which a drain pipe drops. If the pipe is too flat, water moves slowly and solids settle, causing clogs. If it is too steep, water runs away too fast, leaving solids behind. The correct slope for PVC drainage pipes is between 1:60 and 1:40. That means for every 60 units of horizontal run, the pipe drops 1 unit (1:60), up to 1 unit per 40 units (1:40). In practical terms: 1 cm drop per 60 cm length (minimum) to 1.5 cm drop per 40 cm length (maximum). For a 5-metre run, the total drop should be between 8.3 cm (at 1:60) and 12.5 cm (at 1:40)..Trim()

.Trim()

To set the slope, use a spirit level and a measuring tape. Mark the start and end heights. A simpler method: use a 1-metre level. For 1:60, the bubble should show a drop of about 1.7 cm per metre (since 100 cm / 60 = 1.67 cm). For 1:40, drop 2.5 cm per metre. Mark the pipe supports accordingly. Support the pipe every 1.2 m so it does not sag. Sagging creates a belly where water and waste collect..Trim()

In Africa, many homes have sandy or clay soils. Dig the trench with a slight slope, then compact the bottom. Lay a 50 mm bed of sand or fine gravel to protect the pipe. Heat-bending PVC lets you create custom angles without fittings, saving money and reducing joints where leaks can occur. Pressure Testing Your System with a Hand Pump

After you finish installing a PVC water line, you must pressure test it before covering trenches or connecting fixtures. A hand pump (hydrostatic test pump) is the standard tool.
