Overview of Pest Control Methods: Chemical, Biological, and Cultural

Pest control methods fall into three main categories: chemical, biological, and cultural. Chemical control uses pesticidesâsubstances that kill pests. Common examples include insecticides (e.g., permethrin, deltamethrin) for cockroaches and mosquitoes, rodenticides (e.g., brodifacoum) for rats, and termiticides (e.g., imidacloprid) for termites. These come as sprays, dusts, baits, and fumigants. Chemical control is fast and effective but must be used safely: follow label instructions, wear protective gear, and keep children and pets away. Biological control uses living organisms to reduce pests. For example, Bacillus thuringiensis israelensis (Bti) kills mosquito larvae without harming other wildlife. Ladybugs eat aphids, and nematodes can control termites. This method is eco-friendly but slower and may require specific conditions. Cultural control changes the environment or human behavior to prevent pests. For homes: seal cracks, fix leaking pipes, store food in airtight containers, and remove trash. For farms: rotate crops, plant resistant varieties, and clear brush around fields. Cultural methods are cheap and sustainable but need consistent effort. In practice, a good pest control business uses integrated pest management (IPM): start with cultural and biological methods, and use chemicals only when necessary. For example, to control mosquitoes, you might first drain stagnant water (cultural), introduce Bti (biological), and then spray adulticides (chemical) if needed. This approach reduces costs, protects the environment, and lowers the risk of pests developing resistance. As a technician, you'll assess each situation and choose the best mix of methods for your customer..Trim()

Types of Pesticides: Insecticides, Rodenticides, and Fumigants

Pesticides are chemicals used to kill or control pests. In pest control, you will mainly work with three types: insecticides, rodenticides, and fumigants. Insecticides target insects like cockroaches, mosquitoes, and termites. Common active ingredients include permethrin, deltamethrin, and cypermethrin.
