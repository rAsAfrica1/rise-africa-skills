Creating Balanced Menus

Scaling Recipes for Groups

Packing and Transporting Food

When you cook from home, packing and transporting food safely is critical. Use insulated coolers or thick cardboard boxes lined with newspaper or bubble wrap. For hot food, wrap pots in towels and place in a cooler. For cold food, use ice packs or frozen water bottles. Never mix hot and cold items in the same container. Label each container with the dish name and date. For a 50-person event, you might need 4 large coolers: one for hot mains, one for cold salads, one for drinks, and one for desserts. Pack sauces and dressings separately in leak-proof jars. Use sturdy plastic containers with tight lids rather than foil, which tears. Transport in a vehicle with a flat boot or back seat. Secure containers with seatbelts or rope so they don't slide. If travelling more than 30 minutes, avoid opening coolers. In hot African climates, keep food out of direct sun. For weddings or funerals, where food may sit for hours, use chafing dishes with sterno fuel. Always carry a basic toolkit: spare lids, cling film, paper towels, and a thermometer. Check that hot food stays above 60Â°C and cold below 5Â°C. If you must hold food, use a insulated bag. Plan your route to avoid potholes and traffic. For large orders, make two trips rather than overloading. Remember: food safety is your reputation. One case of food poisoning can end your business. So invest in good coolers and never cut corners on temperature control..Trim()

On-Site Setup and Organization

Arrive at the venue at least 2 hours before serving time. First, walk the space: identify where guests will sit, where the buffet will go, and where you can set up a prep area. You need a table for food, a separate table for drinks, and a waste bin. If there is no kitchen, bring a folding table and a clean cloth. Set up a 'backstage' area for your bags, empty containers, and extra supplies. Arrange food in the order guests will serve themselves: plates first, then mains, sides, sauces, and cutlery last. Use risers (upside-down boxes) to create height and save space. For a 50-person event, use two serving lines to avoid queues. Label each dish clearly with a card. If serving hot food, set up chafing dishes with water and fuel. For cold items, nest bowls in larger bowls filled with ice. Keep a trash bag tied to the table for scraps. Have a hand sanitizer station. Organize your staff: one person per serving line, one to replenish, one to clear tables. If you are alone, pre-plate salads and desserts so you only serve mains. Use a checklist: tables, chairs, linen, plates, cutlery, glasses, napkins, serving spoons, cooler, fuel, matches, bin bags, cleaning cloths. Test everything before guests arrive. In many African events, guests arrive late, so keep food hot or cold properly. Do not leave food uncovered. A clean, organized setup shows professionalism and makes service smoother..Trim()

Serving and Staffing Considerations

For small events (20-100 guests), you need 1 server per 25 guests for buffet, or 1 per 15 for plated. If you are the only staff, choose buffet. Train helpers on portion sizes: a main is 150-200g of meat, 1 cup of rice, 2 tablespoons of sauce. Use a standard serving spoon (60ml) for sides. For plated service, serve from the left, clear from the right. In African settings, many guests prefer generous portions, so plan 10% extra. Serve hot food hot: keep chafing dishes at 60Â°C. Cold food at 5Â°C. If using local servers, pay them fairly: in Kenya, a server might earn KES 800-1500 per event. Brief them on allergies and dietary needs. Have a designated person for drinks so the buffet line moves. For outdoor events, use food covers to keep flies off. If serving alcohol, check local laws and have non-alcoholic options. For children, offer smaller plates and simple foods. For elderly, ensure soft options. Always serve yourself last. Monitor food levels; when a dish is half empty, replace it with a full one. Do not let guests serve themselves with their own cutlery. Provide tongs or spoons for each dish. Keep a clean cloth to wipe spills. After the event, pack leftovers in clean containers within 2 hours. If you have excess, offer to staff or donate. Good serving keeps guests happy and your business recommended..Trim()
