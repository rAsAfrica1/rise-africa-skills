Ghanaian Bead Making

CRAFTS & ARTISANRY · 12 modules · 72 lessons

Master the ancient art of Ghanaian bead making, from recycled glass powder to finished ceremonial jewelry. Learn traditional Krobo techniques, kiln firing, decorative surface design, and the business skills to turn your craft into a sustainable income.

12Modules
72Total Lessons

Module 1 · Introduction to Ghanaian Bead Culture

  • History and cultural significance of Ghanaian beads
  • Types of Ghanaian beads: Krobo, Ashanti, Ewe, and Fante
  • Symbolism and meanings of bead colors and patterns
  • Traditional uses in rites of passage and ceremonies
  • Overview of bead-making regions in Ghana
  • Ethical sourcing of materials and cultural appreciation

Module 2 · Tools and Materials for Bead Making

  • Identifying and sourcing recycled glass bottles
  • Essential tools: kilns, mandrels, and bead reamers
  • Cassava starch and other binders for powder beads
  • Natural colorants: oxides, ochres, and plant dyes
  • Safety equipment: respirators, gloves, and eye protection
  • Setting up a safe and efficient workspace

Module 3 · Glass Preparation and Powder Creation

  • Cleaning and sorting glass by color
  • Crushing glass into frit using mortar and pestle
  • Grinding glass to fine powder with a mill
  • Sieving powder for consistent particle size
  • Storing and labeling glass powder
  • Color blending techniques for custom shades

Module 4 · Core Bead Forming Techniques

  • Hand-rolling powder beads with cassava binder
  • Using wooden molds for uniform bead shapes
  • Creating layered and patterned beads
  • Forming beads on mandrels for wound glass
  • Drying and hardening raw beads
  • Troubleshooting cracks and deformities

Module 5 · Kiln Firing and Annealing

  • Loading beads into the kiln for firing
  • Understanding firing schedules and temperature curves
  • Annealing for strength and durability
  • Kiln safety and maintenance
  • Cooling and unloading fired beads
  • Assessing firing results and quality control

Module 6 · Finishing and Polishing Beads

  • Reaming beads to clean holes
  • Initial grinding with coarse grit
  • Progressive polishing with finer grits
  • Using a tumbler for bulk polishing
  • Final buffing for high gloss
  • Inspecting beads for defects

Module 7 · Decorative Techniques and Surface Design

  • Applying powdered glass for color accents
  • Using stringers and murrini for patterns
  • Etching and sandblasting for matte finishes
  • Painting with glass enamels and firing
  • Incorporating metallic foils and leaf
  • Creating textured surfaces with molds

Module 8 · Traditional Krobo Bead Making

  • History and styles of Krobo beads
  • Preparing clay molds for powder beads
  • Layering colors in molds
  • Firing Krobo beads in a traditional kiln
  • Polishing Krobo beads with sand and water
  • Stringing Krobo beads for ceremonial use

Module 9 · Advanced Bead Shapes and Forms

  • Making large focal beads
  • Creating hollow and sculpted beads
  • Shaping beads with carving tools
  • Combining glass with other materials
  • Producing beads with intricate internal designs
  • Experimenting with organic and abstract forms

Module 10 · Bead Stringing and Jewelry Assembly

  • Selecting cords, threads, and findings
  • Basic stringing and knotting techniques
  • Designing necklaces and bracelets
  • Incorporating beads into earrings and accessories
  • Using spacers and clasps for professional finish
  • Creating traditional Ghanaian bead jewelry

Module 11 · Marketing and Entrepreneurship

  • Pricing beads and jewelry for profit
  • Photographing beads for online sales
  • Selling at local markets and craft fairs
  • Building an online presence and social media
  • Packaging and shipping beads safely
  • Understanding export regulations and fair trade

Module 12 · Capstone Project: Create a Bead Collection

  • Planning a cohesive bead collection
  • Sourcing materials sustainably
  • Producing a series of beads using learned techniques
  • Documenting the creative process
  • Presenting the collection to peers and instructors
  • Receiving feedback and refining designs
