Module 1 · Introduction to Pet Nutrition and Feed Formulation

The Role of Nutrition in Dog and Cat Health

Good nutrition is the foundation of your pet's health. In dogs and cats, the right balance of protein, fats, carbohydrates, vitamins, and minerals supports strong muscles, a shiny coat, a robust immune system, and healthy digestion. For example, a dog needs about 22% protein in its diet for maintenance, while a cat requires 26% protein. In Africa, where many pets are fed table scraps or homemade diets, deficiencies are common. A lack of calcium can cause bone deformities in puppies, and insufficient taurine in cats leads to blindness and heart disease. You can prevent these issues by using locally available ingredients like fish meal, chicken by-products, and maize. For instance, a simple homemade diet for a 10 kg dog might include 200g of cooked chicken, 100g of cooked rice, and 50g of vegetables, but this alone may lack essential vitamins. That's why commercial or properly formulated diets are recommended. As a formulator, your goal is to meet the animal's nutritional requirements at every life stageâpuppy, adult, pregnant, or senior. In Africa, where commercial pet food can be expensive, you can create affordable, balanced recipes using local grains, legumes, and animal proteins. Remember, cats are obligate carnivores and need meat-based diets, while dogs are omnivores and can thrive on a mix of plant and animal ingredients. By understanding these basics, you can improve the health of pets in your community and build a business around quality pet food..Trim()

A Brief History of Commercial Pet Food

Commercial pet food began in the mid-1800s when James Spratt, an American electrician in London, noticed dogs eating ship biscuits and created the first dog biscuit. By the 1920s, canned horse meat was popular in the US, and after World War II, dry kibble became the norm due to convenience and cost. In Africa, commercial pet food arrived much later, often imported and expensive. For decades, many African pet owners relied on homemade diets or leftovers. Today, however, local production is growing. For example, in South Africa, companies like Montego and Bob Martin produce affordable kibble using local ingredients like maize, chicken, and fish. In Nigeria and Kenya, small-scale entrepreneurs are starting to make their own pet food, often using extrusion machines. Understanding this history helps you see that pet food is not a new conceptâit has evolved with technology and consumer needs. The key lesson: early pet foods were simple, and you can start small too. You don't need a factory; you can begin with a mixer, a grinder, and an oven. Many successful African pet food businesses started in home kitchens. The industry is now worth billions globally, and Africa's pet population is rising, creating opportunities for local formulators who understand local ingredients and prices. By learning from history, you can avoid past mistakes, such as using poor-quality by-products or neglecting nutritional balance, and build a brand that pet owners trust..Trim()

Key Differences Between Dog and Cat Nutritional Needs

Dogs and cats have distinct nutritional requirements. Dogs are omnivores, meaning they can eat both plant and animal matter. Cats are obligate carnivores, meaning they must eat meat to survive. This difference affects everything from protein to amino acids. Cats need taurine, an amino acid found only in animal tissue. Without it, cats can go blind or develop heart disease. Dogs can produce taurine themselves, so it's not essential in their diet. Cats also require arachidonic acid, an omega-6 fatty acid found in animal fats, while dogs can make it from linoleic acid. In terms of protein, cats need more: 26% of dry matter for adult cats, versus 18% for adult dogs. Cats also have a higher requirement for vitamin A, which they cannot convert from beta-carotene like dogs can. So, if you're formulating for both, you can't use the same recipe. For example, a chicken-based dog food might have 22% protein and 10% fat, while a cat food needs 30% protein and 15% fat. In Africa, where fish is abundant, you can use fish meal for cats to meet their taurine needs, but for dogs, you can include more plant proteins like soybean meal. Also, cats are less tolerant of carbohydrates; they lack salivary amylase, so they digest carbs less efficiently. Dogs can handle up to 50% carbs, but cats do better with less than 30%. These differences mean you must formulate separate diets. Never feed dog food to catsâit can lead to malnutrition over time. As a formulator, always check the species-specific requirements before mixing ingredients..Trim()

Overview of the Feed Formulation Process

Feed formulation is the process of selecting ingredients and mixing them in the right proportions to meet an animal's nutritional needs. It involves several steps: first, identify the nutrient requirements for the specific animal (species, age, activity level). Second, list available ingredients and their nutrient composition. Third, use a method like Pearson's Square or computer software to balance the recipe. Fourth, mix and test the feed. For example, to make 100 kg of dog food with 22% protein, you might use 40 kg maize (8% protein), 30 kg soybean meal (44% protein), 20 kg fish meal (60% protein), and 10 kg vitamins/minerals. Calculate the protein: (40*0.08)+(30*0.44)+(20*0.60)=3.2+13.2+12=28.4 kg protein, which is 28.4%âtoo high, so adjust. You can use a simple spreadsheet or free software like WinFeed or FeedSoft. In Africa, where ingredients vary, always test your ingredients for nutrient content using local labs or kits. The process also includes considering cost, availability, and palatability. After mixing, you may extrude or bake the feed to improve digestibility and kill pathogens. Then, package and label with guaranteed analysis. Start small: make 10 kg batches, test on your own pets, and get feedback. Many African entrepreneurs start with a hammer mill, a mixer, and a pelletizer. The key is consistency and accuracy. Weigh ingredients precisely; a 1% error in salt can harm pets. Document every batch. Over time, you can scale up and even sell to local pet shops. This overview gives you a roadmap to create your own recipes..Trim()

Regulatory Bodies and Standards (AAFCO, FEDIAF)

Regulations ensure pet food is safe and nutritious. In the United States, AAFCO (Association of American Feed Control Officials) sets nutrient profiles and labeling rules. In Europe, FEDIAF (European Pet Food Industry Federation) provides similar guidelines. These bodies define minimum and maximum levels for nutrients like protein, fat, vitamins, and minerals. For example, AAFCO requires adult dog food to have at least 18% protein and 5.5% fat, while cat food needs 26% protein and 9% fat. They also specify feeding trials or lab analysis to prove the diet is complete. In Africa, many countries lack specific pet food regulations, but you can still follow AAFCO or FEDIAF standards to ensure quality. For instance, if you export to Europe, you must meet FEDIAF. If you sell locally, you can use AAFCO as a guide. Key labeling requirements include the product name, guaranteed analysis (protein, fat, fiber, moisture), ingredient list, and feeding directions. You must also include a statement like "complete and balanced for all life stages" only if it meets the standards. In Africa, some countries like South Africa have their own regulations under the Department of Agriculture. As a formulator, you should test your product at a lab to confirm nutrient levels. Many African universities, like the University of Nairobi, offer testing services. Following these standards builds trust with customers and avoids legal issues. Even if not enforced, voluntary compliance sets you apart from informal sellers. Start by downloading AAFCO or FEDIAF guidelines free online and use them as your checklist..Trim()

Module 2 · Essential Nutrients for Dogs and Cats

Water: The Most Critical Nutrient

Water is the nutrient your animal needs in the largest amount and the one it can survive without for the shortest time. A dog can lose almost all its fat and half its protein and still live, but losing just 10 percent of its body water causes serious illness, and 15 percent loss usually kills. A healthy dog drinks roughly 50 to 60 ml of water per kilogram of body weight each day. A 20 kg dog therefore needs about 1 to 1.2 litres daily. Cats are desert-adapted animals and drink less by nature, only about 45 to 50 ml per kg per day, so a 4 kg cat needs roughly 180 to 200 ml. Cats evolved to get most of their water from prey, so a cat on dry kibble is often chronically under-hydrated. This is a major cause of urinary and kidney problems in African homes where dry food is the cheap, easy option..Trim()

.Trim()

Water quality matters as much as quantity. In many parts of Africa, borehole, well and river water carries high levels of salts, iron, nitrates or bacteria. Never assume your animal can drink what you drink without checking. If your own water is salty or smells of iron, filter it, boil it and let it cool, or buy treated water for your animals. Change drinking water at least twice a day in hot weather. Bowls must be scrubbed daily because biofilm slime grows fast in warm climates and causes diarrhoea..Trim()

.Trim()

You can boost water intake cheaply without buying anything. Add warm water to kibble at a ratio of one part water to two parts kibble and let it soak for ten minutes. Feed wet food, or mix in cooked pumpkin, carrot or bone broth with no salt and no onion. Cats in particular respond to running water, so a small solar-powered pet fountain or a dripping tap in a clean basin encourages drinking..Trim()

.Trim()

Watch for dehydration signs: skin that stays tented when you pinch the neck, dry tacky gums, sunken eyes, and concentrated dark yellow urine. A quick test is to lift the skin between the shoulder blades; it should snap back in under two seconds. If it stays up, get water into the animal immediately and see a vet. In hot northern Nigerian, Sahelian or Kalahari conditions, increase water allowance by 20 to 30 percent and always provide shade. Clean, cool, fresh water is the cheapest health intervention you will ever give your dog or cat..Trim()

Protein and Amino Acid Requirements

Protein is built from about 20 amino acids, and your animal's body cannot make 10 of them, so these must come from food. For dogs the essential ones include arginine, histidine, isoleucine, leucine, lysine, methionine, phenylalanine, threonine, tryptophan and valine. Cats are stricter: they also cannot make taurine, arachidonic acid or enough arginine, so a cat fed dog food will go blind and develop heart failure from taurine deficiency within months. Never feed cat food to dogs as a substitute for dog food, and never feed dog food to cats at all..Trim()

.Trim()

Numbers you can work with. A healthy adult dog needs a diet containing at least 18 percent crude protein on a dry matter basis, and 22 to 25 percent for growth, pregnancy or heavy work. A cat needs at least 26 percent crude protein for maintenance and 30 percent for growth and reproduction, though most quality cat foods sit at 32 to 40 percent. These are minimums, not targets. Protein quality matters more than quantity. Egg, fish, poultry, milk and meat are complete proteins with all essential amino acids. Maize, cassava, rice and sorghum are incomplete, low in lysine and tryptophan, so they must be combined with animal or legume protein..Trim()

.Trim()

Local and affordable protein sources in Africa include fishmeal from Lake Victoria or the Niger Delta, dried sardines and kapenta, poultry offal, blood meal, soybean meal, groundnut cake, cottonseed meal, and insect meal such as black soldier fly larvae. Black soldier fly larvae contain 40 to 45 percent protein and can be farmed on food waste in a plastic drum, which cuts feed cost dramatically for small producers. Cook all animal proteins thoroughly. Raw poultry carries Salmonella and Campylobacter, and raw freshwater fish in parts of Africa carries parasites. Boiling for 20 minutes is enough..Trim()

.Trim()

Signs of protein deficiency include poor growth in puppies and kittens, dull coat, muscle wasting along the spine, slow wound healing, and a swollen belly from low blood albumin. Excess protein is not harmful to healthy adult animals but is wasteful and expensive, and it burdens kidneys already damaged by disease. A practical rule for a small producer: aim for 25 percent protein in dog food and 35 percent in cat food, and always include at least two protein sources so that amino acid gaps in one are filled by the other. Weigh ingredients with a kitchen scale in grams, not by eye, because a 5 percent error in protein can mean the difference between a healthy litter and stunted pups..Trim()

Fats and Essential Fatty Acids

Fats are the most concentrated energy source in feed, giving about 8.5 kilocalories per gram compared with 3.5 for protein and carbohydrate. A dog needs a minimum of 5.5 percent fat in the diet, and 8 to 15 percent is normal for active animals. Cats need at least 9 percent fat, and 15 to 20 percent is common in good cat food. Fat also carries the fat-soluble vitamins A, D, E and K, makes food palatable, and supplies the essential fatty acids your animal cannot make: linoleic acid for dogs and cats, plus arachidonic acid for cats, and alpha-linolenic acid which converts to EPA and DHA..Trim()

.Trim()

Good fat sources available across Africa include poultry fat, beef tallow, lard, sunflower oil, groundnut oil, palm oil in moderation, and fish oil. Fish oil from sardines, anchovies or mackerel is the best source of EPA and DHA. For a 20 kg dog, half a teaspoon to one teaspoon of fish oil daily supports skin, coat and joints. For a 4 kg cat, a quarter teaspoon is enough. Never exceed this because too much fish oil thins the blood and can cause vitamin E deficiency. Red palm oil is rich in vitamin A and is widely available, but use no more than 2 percent of the diet because excess saturated fat causes pancreatitis..Trim()

.Trim()

Essential fatty acid deficiency shows as dry flaky skin, dull brittle coat, excessive shedding, slow wound healing, and in cats poor reproductive performance. These signs are common in African dogs fed mostly maize porridge and scraps. Adding one tablespoon of sunflower oil per kilogram of cooked food for dogs, or a few drops for cats, corrects mild deficiency within three to four weeks..Trim()

Rancid fat is dangerous. In hot, humid climates fats oxidise quickly and produce peroxides that destroy vitamins and cause diarrhoea and liver damage. Store oils in dark bottles, keep them out of the sun, and smell them before use; a bitter or paint-like smell means throw it away. Never use oil that has been used for frying because it contains free radicals and burnt particles. When mixing your own feed, add fat last after the food has cooled to below 40 degrees Celsius, since heat destroys essential fatty acids. Weigh fat accurately: it is easy to over-pour. A simple tool is a 5 ml medicine spoon or a small kitchen scale. Balanced fat gives glossy coats, healthy skin, strong puppies and good milk in nursing mothers, all of which raise the market value of your animals..Trim()

Carbohydrates and Fiber: Roles and Sources

Carbohydrates are not strictly essential for dogs and cats because the body can make glucose from protein and fat, but they are the cheapest energy source and they make feed affordable. Cooked starch from maize, rice, sorghum, millet, cassava, sweet potato and potato supplies 3.5 kilocalories per gram. Dogs digest cooked starch well, up to 90 percent, but cats have low amylase enzyme and digest starch less efficiently, so cat food should stay below 30 percent carbohydrate and preferably under 20 percent. Raw starch is poorly digested by both species and causes gas and loose stool, so always cook grains thoroughly, ideally by boiling or pressure cooking until soft..Trim()

A practical African ration for a 20 kg dog might contain 50 percent cooked maize or rice, 25 percent animal protein, 10 percent legumes, 8 percent fat and 7 percent vegetables and minerals. For a cat, reduce carbohydrate to 15 to 20 percent and raise animal protein to 40 to 50 percent. Cassava must be properly processed because raw cassava contains cyanogenic glycosides that are toxic. Peel, soak for 24 hours, and boil thoroughly or ferment into gari before use. Never feed mouldy maize or groundnuts; aflatoxin causes liver cancer and is common in poorly stored African grain..Trim()

Fiber is the carbohydrate your animal cannot digest, and it has three jobs: it feeds good gut bacteria, it regulates stool consistency, and it slows sugar absorption. Soluble fiber such as pumpkin, sweet potato, oats, psyllium and beet pulp ferments in the colon and supports gut health. Insoluble fiber such as wheat bran, rice husk and cellulose adds bulk and speeds transit. Dogs do well with 2.5 to 4.5 percent crude fiber, and cats with 1 to 3 percent. For diarrhoea, add one tablespoon of cooked pumpkin per 10 kg body weight twice daily. For constipation, add one teaspoon of psyllium husk or wheat bran per 10 kg body weight mixed into wet food with extra water..Trim()

Too much fiber is as bad as too little. Excess fiber binds minerals like calcium, zinc and iron so they pass out in the stool, and it reduces calorie intake, which is dangerous for growing puppies and kittens. Signs of excess fiber are large soft stools, frequent defecation and weight loss despite eating. Weigh fiber sources with a scale. A useful local trick for small producers is to keep a record book of every batch: date, ingredients, weights, and how the animals responded. This turns ordinary cooking into real formulation and lets you repeat what works..Trim()

Vitamins: Fat-Soluble and Water-Soluble

Vitamins are needed in tiny amounts, measured in milligrams or micrograms, but missing one can cripple or kill your animal. They split into two groups. Fat-soluble vitamins A, D, E and K are stored in the liver and body fat, so they can build up to toxic levels if overfed, especially A and D. Water-soluble vitamins, the B group and vitamin C, are not stored. Excess passes out in urine, so they must be supplied daily and deficiency appears faster..Trim()

.Trim()

Vitamin A is essential for vision, immunity and skin. Dogs and cats cannot convert carotene from plants efficiently, so they need preformed vitamin A from liver, egg yolk, fish oil or fortified premix. A cat needs about 900 micrograms and a dog about 1,500 micrograms per 1,000 kilocalories. Liver is the richest source but feed it only once or twice a week because too much causes bone pain and deformities. Vitamin D controls calcium and phosphorus absorption. Dogs and cats do not make vitamin D from sunlight the way humans do, so it must come from fish liver oil, egg yolk or premix. Deficiency causes rickets in puppies and kittens; excess causes kidney calcification..Trim()

.Trim()

Vitamin E protects cell membranes and works with selenium. Sunflower oil, wheat germ and green leafy vegetables supply it. Deficiency causes muscle weakness and in cats a painful condition called steatitis from too much fish oil without enough E. Vitamin K is made by gut bacteria and is rarely deficient unless the animal eats warfarin-type rat poison or has prolonged diarrhoea. B vitamins include thiamine, riboflavin, niacin, pyridoxine, B12, folate, biotin and pantothenic acid. They drive energy metabolism and nerve function. Cats need far more niacin and B6 than dogs because they cannot convert tryptophan efficiently. Thiamine deficiency, common when fish is fed raw and unrefrigerated because an enzyme destroys thiamine, causes head tilt, circling and seizures. Cooking the fish destroys the enzyme and protects the vitamin..Trim()

In practice, most African home-made diets are short on vitamins A, D, E and several B vitamins, especially when based on maize and scraps. The simplest fix is a commercial vitamin-mineral premix for dogs or cats, dosed exactly per the label, usually 1 to 2 grams per kilogram of finished feed. For a small producer, buy premix in sealed foil packs, keep it in a cool dark place, and use it within three months of opening because vitamins degrade. Never guess doses of vitamin A or D. Use a gram scale or the measuring scoop supplied. A balanced premix plus fresh liver once a week, egg yolk twice a week, and a variety of vegetables will keep most animals healthy without expensive supplements..Trim()

Minerals: Macro and Trace Elements

Minerals are divided into macro minerals needed in grams per day and trace minerals needed in milligrams. The macro group is calcium, phosphorus, magnesium, sodium, potassium, chloride and sulphur. The trace group includes iron, zinc, copper, manganese, iodine, selenium, cobalt and molybdenum. The most important balance in any dog or cat diet is calcium to phosphorus. For dogs the ideal ratio is between 1.2 to 1 and 1.4 to 1. For cats it is 1.1 to 1 to 1.3 to 1. Puppies and kittens are very sensitive: a wrong ratio causes bone deformities that cannot be reversed. Meat and offal are high in phosphorus and low in calcium, so a diet of only meat and rice will cause rubber jaw and fractures in growing animals..Trim()

.Trim()

Calcium sources available in Africa include bone meal, limestone flour, oyster shell, eggshell powder and dairy. To make eggshell powder, wash shells, boil them, dry them in the sun, and grind to a fine powder with a pestle. One teaspoon of eggshell powder contains about 2,000 mg of calcium. Phosphorus comes from meat, fish, bone and cereal bran. Sodium and chloride come from salt; a dog needs about 0.3 percent salt in the diet and a cat about 0.2 percent, but excess salt causes kidney damage, so weigh it. Potassium is plentiful in meat, potato and banana. Magnesium is in bone, cereal and green vegetables and is rarely deficient..Trim()

.Trim()

Trace minerals matter in tiny amounts. Iron from liver, blood meal, red meat and dark green vegetables prevents anaemia. Zinc from meat, egg and shellfish supports skin and immunity; deficiency causes hair loss and poor wound healing. Copper from liver and shellfish works with iron and zinc; deficiency causes anaemia and coat colour change, while excess causes liver damage, especially in Bedlington terriers. Manganese from cereal and bone supports joints. Iodine from iodised salt or seaweed prevents goitre and stillbirths. Selenium from fish, meat and eggs works with vitamin E; deficiency causes muscle weakness and in some African regions where soil selenium is low, white muscle disease in pups. Cobalt is needed only as part of vitamin B12..Trim()

Practical formulation rules: always add a calcium source when feeding meat and rice, use a commercial trace mineral premix rather than guessing, and never add extra of any single trace mineral without veterinary advice because the safe range is narrow. Keep a simple record of the mineral mix used per batch. If animals show lameness, bone pain, poor coat or anaemia despite adequate protein, suspect mineral imbalance and correct the calcium to phosphorus ratio first. Weigh minerals on a jeweller's scale accurate to 0.01 grams, because a 1 gram error in a trace mineral can poison a small cat..Trim()

Fiber Sources and Their Functional Properties

Fiber is not a nutrient but it is essential for gut health. It comes from plant cell walls. In dog and cat feed, fiber helps with digestion, stool quality, and weight management. Common sources in Africa include beet pulp, wheat bran, rice bran, maize bran, cassava peel, and pumpkin. Soluble fiber ferments in the gut and feeds beneficial bacteria. Growth and Reproduction Requirements

Extrusion Technology Overview

Extrusion is the heart of dry pet food production. It's a process where you take a mix of ingredients, add water and heat, and push the dough through a machine that cooks and shapes it into kibble. In Africa, small-scale extruders are becoming more available, from simple single-screw models to more advanced twin-screw extruders. A basic extruder for a small business can cost between $2,000 and $10,000, while industrial ones run into hundreds of thousands. The key steps: first, grind your ingredients into a fine meal (particle size under 1 mm). Then mix with water to about 25-30% moisture. This mix goes into the extruder barrel, where friction and steam heat it to 120-150Â°C. The pressure cooks the starches and proteins, and when the dough exits the die, it expands and forms the kibble shape. You can adjust the die to make different shapes and sizes. After extrusion, kibble is dried to about 10% moisture to prevent mold. In Africa, humidity can be high, so drying is critical. Use a hot air dryer at 100-120Â°C for 10-20 minutes, or sun-dry if you have reliable sun, but that's risky. The extruder's screw speed, temperature, and moisture affect the final product's density, texture, and digestibility. For beginners, start with a single-screw extruder and simple formulas. Remember, extrusion is not just cooking; it's also a way to create appealing textures. Local ingredients like maize, cassava, and soy can be used, but you need to balance nutrients. Always test your extruder with small batches before scaling up. Proper maintenance is key: clean the die and screw after each use to prevent buildup. With practice, you can produce consistent, high-quality kibble that meets local market needs..Trim()

Ingredients and Their Roles in Kibble

In dry pet food, ingredients are chosen for nutrition, cost, and how they behave in the extruder. The main categories are proteins, carbohydrates, fats, and additives. Proteins: common animal proteins include chicken meal, fish meal, and meat and bone meal. In Africa, fish meal from local fisheries is often affordable and high quality. Plant proteins like soybean meal and cowpea are also used. Protein levels for dogs: 18-25% for maintenance, 25-30% for growth. Cats need more: 26-30% maintenance, 30-40% growth. Carbohydrates: maize, rice, wheat, and cassava are staple energy sources. They also help bind the kibble. Maize is widely available and cheap, but must be ground fine. Cassava is drought-resistant and grows in many African countries, but it's low in protein, so combine with protein sources. Fats: added as oils or animal fats. They provide energy and palatability. Poultry fat and vegetable oil are common. Fat content typically 8-15% for dogs, 10-20% for cats. Add fats after extrusion via coating to avoid rancidity. Additives: vitamins, minerals, amino acids (like taurine for cats), and preservatives. Taurine is essential for cats; without it, they can go blind or have heart problems. Use a premix to ensure correct levels. Also, antioxidants like vitamin E or BHA/BHT to preserve fats. Palatability Enhancement in Dry Foods

Palatability is how much a pet likes the taste, smell, and texture of the food. If your kibble isn't palatable, pets won't eat it, no matter how nutritious. Third, control texture: kibble that's too hard or too soft can reduce intake. Adjust extrusion parameters to get a crunchy but not rock-hard texture. Fourth, consider aroma: pets rely on smell. Canning and Retort Processing Basics

Canning pet food means sealing food in a container and heating it to kill all bacteria, yeasts, and moulds. The key tool is a pressure retort, which is like a large pressure cooker. In Africa, many small producers start with a simple vertical retort that can hold 50-100 cans. You need a retort that reaches 121Â°C (250Â°F) and holds that temperature for a set time. For a 400g can of pet food, typical retort time is 60-90 minutes at 121Â°C, depending on the can size and recipe. Always use a calibrated thermometer and pressure gauge. A common mistake is not venting the retort properly at the start; you must let steam escape for 5-10 minutes to remove all air, or heat will not penetrate evenly. After processing, cool the cans quickly in chlorinated water to below 40Â°C. Test your process by sending cans to a lab for sterility checks, or use a simple incuba- tion test: keep a can at 37Â°C for 7 days and check for swelling. If it swells, your process failed. Start with small batches and record every step: time, temperature, pressure, and can size. This record helps you repeat success and fix problems. In Africa, power cuts are common, so have a backup generator or use a gas-fired retort. Never skip the cooling step; slow cooling can cause thermophilic bacteria to grow. Remember, canning is a critical control point for food safety. If you cannot afford a retort, you cannot safely make canned pet food. Do not try to use a normal pot; it will not reach 121Â°C. Your goal is commercial sterility: no harmful bacteria and no spoilage organisms that can grow at room temperature..Trim()

Raw feeding means giving dogs and cats uncooked meat, bones, organs, and sometimes vegetables. In Africa, many owners already feed raw meat scraps, but a balanced raw diet needs precise ratios. A common starting point is the BARF model: 70% muscle meat, 10% raw edible bone, 5% liver, 5% other organs (like kidney or spleen), and 10% vegetables or fruit. For a 20 kg dog, that's about 400g of food per day. Use a digital kitchen scale (available in most markets for under $20) to weigh each component. Benefits include shinier coats, smaller stools, and better dental health from chewing bones. However, risks are serious. Raw meat can carry Salmonella, E. coli, and parasites like Toxoplasma. In Africa, where refrigeration is unreliable, bacterial growth is a major concern. Always source meat from inspected butcheries, freeze at -20Â°C for at least 3 weeks to kill parasites (except for some tapeworms), and handle with gloves. Never feed raw pork or wild game without freezing. Cats are obligate carnivores and need taurine from raw heart or supplements; without it, they can go blind or die. Dogs can survive on raw but need calcium from bones or eggshell powder. Watch for choking on bones; never feed cooked bones as they splinter. Signs of illness: vomiting, diarrhea, lethargy. If you see these, stop raw feeding and see a vet. For beginners, start with one protein source like chicken and gradually add organs. Keep a feeding diary to track weight and stool quality. Freeze-Dried and Dehydrated Formulations

Freeze-drying removes 98% of water from raw meat, organs, and bones by freezing then sublimating ice under vacuum. The result is a light, shelf-stable product that rehydrates with warm water. In Africa, freeze-dried pet food is expensive and imported, but you can make a similar product using a solar dehydrator. Dehydrating at 60-70Â°C for 8-12 hours removes moisture but does not kill all bacteria. To be safe, pre-cook meat to 70Â°C internally before dehydrating, or use a vinegar (5% acetic acid) dip for 10 minutes. Vegetarian and Vegan Pet Diets
