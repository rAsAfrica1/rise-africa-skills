What Is Social Media Reselling?

Social media reselling means buying products at a low price and selling them for a profit through platforms like Facebook, Instagram, WhatsApp, and TikTok. You don't need a physical shop. You need a smartphone, internet data, and a good eye for what people want. In Africa, this model works because social media is where people already spend time and trust recommendations from friends and local sellers..Trim()

Legal and Tax Basics for Resellers

For example, in Kenya, you can register a business name with the eCitizen portal for about 1,000 shillings. In Nigeria, you can register with the Corporate Affairs Commission (CAC) for around 10,000 naira. In Ghana, the Registrar General's Department charges about 50 cedis. Registration gives you a legal identity, which helps you open a business bank account and avoid fines..Trim()

Module 2 · Sourcing Products for Resale

Finding Wholesale and Liquidation Suppliers

To make money reselling, you need a reliable source of cheap products. Wholesale suppliers sell in bulk at lower prices, while liquidation suppliers sell off excess stock, customer returns, or bankrupt inventory at even deeper discounts. In Africa, start by visiting local wholesale markets like Balogun Market in Lagos, Kantamanto in Accra, or Toi Market in Nairobi. Walk the aisles, ask traders where they get their goods, and note prices. Many will source from importers who bring in containers from China, Dubai, or Turkey. Ask if you can buy a mixed pallet or a bale of assorted items. For example, a bale of 50 mixed-brand t-shirts might cost 500 Ghana cedis and resell for 20-30 cedis each, giving you 500-1000 cedis profit. Online, try Alibaba, but beware of scams; use Trade Assurance. Also check local Facebook groups like 'Lagos Wholesale and Liquidation' where sellers post clearance lots. For liquidation, look for stores closing down, or contact companies that sell off returned electronics. Always ask for a sample before committing to a large order. Build relationships: a good supplier will give you credit after a few cash purchases. Start small with 200-500 cedis, test what sells, then scale. Remember, your profit margin depends on buying low. A 30% margin is common; aim for 50% or more on trending items..Trim()

Thrift Store and Garage Sale Sourcing Strategies

Thrift stores (called 'bend-down' or 'second-hand' markets in many African countries) and garage sales are goldmines for resellers. In Kenya, Gikomba Market is famous for second-hand clothes; in Nigeria, Yaba and Oshodi have thrift stalls. Go early in the morning when new bales are opened. The best items are grabbed within the first hour. Bring cash and a large bag. Focus on quality brands: Zara, H&M, Nike, and Levi's sell fast on social media. Check for stains, tears, or missing buttons; minor flaws can be fixed and still profitable. A typical bale of 100 mixed clothes might cost 300-500 Kenyan shillings per piece, but you can sell individual pieces for 500-1500 shillings. For garage sales, scan local Facebook Marketplace, WhatsApp groups, and community notice boards. Estate sales often have household items, books, and electronics at low prices. Negotiate: offer to buy multiple items for a discount. For example, if a seller wants 2000 naira for a used blender, offer 1500 naira cash. Also, attend church jumble sales and school fairs. Always test electronics before buying. Clean and repair items before reselling; a little polish can double the price. Keep a notebook of what you paid and what you sold it for, so you know which thrift sources are most profitable..Trim()

Using Online Marketplaces for Sourcing

Online marketplaces let you source products without leaving your home. In Africa, popular platforms include Jumia (Nigeria, Kenya, Egypt), Kilimall (Kenya), and Takealot (South Africa). But for resellers, the best deals often come from Facebook Marketplace, Facebook groups, and WhatsApp Business catalogs. Join groups like 'Wholesale Suppliers Nigeria' or 'Nairobi Buy and Sell'. Post a 'wanted' ad: 'Looking for bulk phone cases, 100 pieces, budget 20,000 naira.' You'll get responses from suppliers. For international sourcing, use Alibaba, AliExpress, or DHgate. Always check seller ratings and reviews. Start with a small trial order of 10-20 units to test quality and shipping time. Shipping to Africa can take 2-6 weeks and incur customs duties; factor that into your cost. For example, a phone accessory that costs $1 on AliExpress might land at $2 after shipping and duty, and you can sell it for $5 locally. Also, use local classifieds like OLX (now Jiji in Nigeria) to find clearance lots. When buying online, use secure payment methods like PayPal or escrow. Never send money via Western Union to a stranger. Meet local sellers in person if possible, and inspect goods before paying. Keep records of all transactions for tax and profit tracking..Trim()

Negotiating Bulk Purchases

Negotiation is a skill that directly increases your profit. Before you negotiate, know the market price. Research online and visit multiple suppliers. If you're buying 50 units, ask for a 20-30% discount from the single-unit price. For example, if a supplier quotes 1000 naira per unit, offer 700 naira per unit for 50 units. Be polite but firm. Use phrases like 'I'm a regular buyer' or 'I can pay cash today.' Cash is king; many suppliers will lower the price for immediate payment. If they won't budge on price, ask for free delivery or extra items. Another tactic: buy in larger quantities. A supplier might give 40% off if you buy 200 units instead of 50. But don't overbuy if you can't sell that much. Start with a small order to build trust, then negotiate better terms on the next order. Also, consider group buying: team up with other resellers to pool money and buy a whole pallet at a lower price. Always get the agreement in writing via WhatsApp or email, including the price, quantity, and delivery date. If a supplier refuses to negotiate, walk away; there are many others. Remember, your goal is to get the lowest cost per unit without sacrificing quality..Trim()

Evaluating Product Quality and Profit Potential

Not every cheap product is a good deal. Before you buy in bulk, evaluate quality and profit potential. For quality, check materials, stitching, and functionality. For clothes, look at fabric thickness, seams, and zippers. For electronics, test charging, buttons, and screen. Ask for a sample if buying online. A common mistake is buying a large lot of low-quality items that won't sell. Instead, test a small batch first. For profit potential, calculate your total cost: purchase price + shipping + customs + packaging + social media ads. Then research the selling price on Facebook, Instagram, or Jumia. A good rule: your selling price should be at least double your total cost. For example, if a handbag costs 2000 naira landed, sell for 4000-5000 naira. Check demand: search hashtags like #handbagnigeria to see what's popular. Also consider seasonality: umbrellas sell in rainy season, Christmas decorations in December. Use a simple spreadsheet to track cost, price, and profit margin for each product. If an item doesn't sell within 2 weeks, discount it to recover cash. Focus on products with high turnover and repeat customers, like phone accessories, hair, or skincare. Avoid fads that die quickly..Trim()

Managing Inventory and Storage

Once you start buying in bulk, you need to manage inventory and storage to avoid losses. First, keep a record of every item: date bought, cost, quantity, and location. Use a notebook or a free app like Google Sheets or Zoho Inventory. Label boxes with purchase date and product name. Store items in a dry, clean space away from sunlight and pests. For clothes, use plastic bins or hang them to avoid wrinkles. For electronics, keep them in anti-static bags. If you don't have a spare room, use a locked trunk or rent a small storage unit (in Lagos, a 10x10 ft unit might cost 20,000 naira per month). But for most beginners, a corner of your bedroom works. Rotate stock: sell older items first (FIFO). Set a reorder point: when stock drops to 10 units, reorder. Track sales daily to know what's moving. If an item hasn't sold in 30 days, bundle it with a popular item or run a flash sale. Also, insure high-value inventory if possible. Keep your storage organized so you can find items quickly when an order comes in. A messy inventory leads to lost sales and wasted money..Trim()

Deciding between local pickup and shipping depends on your product, location, and customer base. Local pickup is free for you and fast for the buyer. Managing Finances and Profit Margins

Many resellers mix personal and business money. That is the fastest way to fail. You need to know your profit margins and manage cash flow. First, separate your accounts. Open a dedicated bank account or mobile money wallet for your business. In Nigeria, use a separate Opay or PalmPay account. In Kenya, use a separate M-Pesa till or paybill. Deposit all sales there. Pay yourself a small salary (e.g., $100 per month) and leave the rest for restocking. Second, calculate your profit margin. Formula: (Selling Price - Cost Price - Expenses) / Selling Price x 100. For example, you buy a dress for $10, sell for $25. Expenses per item: packaging $1, delivery $2, ads $2. Total cost = $15. Profit = $10. Margin = 10/25 = 40%. Aim for at least 30% margin. If it's lower, increase price or reduce costs. Third, track every expense. Use a simple app like 'Expense Manager' or a notebook. Record: stock purchases, data, transport, packaging, ads, and fees (e.g., M-Pesa charges). Fourth, set aside money for taxes and emergencies. In many African countries, small businesses pay presumptive tax. Check your local rules. Save 10% of profit for a rainy day. Fifth, reinvest. If you make $500 profit, don't spend it all. Use $300 to buy more stock, $100 for ads, $50 for savings, and $50 for yourself. Sixth, review weekly. Every Sunday, calculate your sales, expenses, and profit. Use a Google Sheet template. If you see a product with low margin, stop selling it. Focus on high-margin items. Finally, avoid debt unless you can repay from sales. If you must borrow, keep it small. Financial discipline is the difference between a hobby and a real business..Trim()

Avoiding Scams and Chargebacks

Scams are common in social media reselling. One major scam is the fake payment notification. A buyer sends a screenshot of a bank transfer, but the money never arrives. Always confirm payment in your bank app or mobile money before releasing goods. Another scam is the overpayment scam: a buyer sends extra money and asks you to refund the difference. The original payment is fake, and you lose the refund. Never refund without confirming the original payment has cleared. Chargebacks happen when a customer disputes a card payment. To avoid chargebacks, use secure payment methods like PayPal Goods and Services or local options like M-Pesa, Airtel Money, or Flutterwave. Always keep proof of delivery: photos, videos, or signed receipts. For high-value items, use a courier with tracking. Communicate clearly with buyers about your return policy. If a buyer threatens a chargeback, try to resolve it by offering a replacement or refund. Document all conversations. In Africa, mobile money is safer than cash on delivery because it's traceable. But beware of fake mobile money messages. Always check your balance directly. Consider using escrow services for large transactions. For international sales, use platforms like Payoneer or Wise to receive payments. Never share your bank OTP or PIN with anyone. Educate your customers about scams too. A reputable reseller builds trust and reduces disputes..Trim()
