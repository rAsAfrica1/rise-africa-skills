.Trim()

.Trim()

.Trim()

.Trim()

Selection and Preparation of Stem Cuttings

Selecting the right stem cuttings is the first step to a healthy cassava crop. Choose stems from mature plants that are 8 to 12 months old, free from disease, and at least 2.5 cm thick. Avoid stems that are too young (soft, green) or too old (woody, dry). A good stem has prominent nodes (buds) and is firm. Cut stems into pieces 20 to 30 cm long, each with at least 5 to 7 nodes. Use a sharp, clean machete or pruning shears to make clean cuts; jagged cuts invite rot. .Trim()

.Trim()

After cutting, treat the cuttings to prevent disease. Soak them in a fungicide solution (e.g., 1% benomyl) for 5 minutes, or use wood ash as a natural alternative. In dry areas, dip the cut ends in melted wax to reduce moisture loss. Let the cuttings dry in a shaded, well-ventilated area for 1 to 2 days before planting. This allows the cut surfaces to heal and reduces the risk of rot. .Trim()

.Trim()

Proper storage is crucial if you cannot plant immediately. Store cuttings upright in a cool, shaded place, with the basal ends (the end that was closer to the ground) buried in moist sand or soil. Never store them in plastic bags or in direct sunlight. They can be stored for up to 2 weeks this way. .Trim()

.Trim()

When planting, ensure the basal end is planted downwards. The buds on the stem will sprout into new shoots. If you plant upside down, the shoots will struggle to emerge. Always plant fresh cuttings; old or shriveled cuttings have low sprouting rates. With good selection and preparation, you can achieve over 90% sprouting. Remember, healthy cuttings mean a healthy crop..Trim()

Planting Methods: Horizontal, Vertical, and Slanting

Cassava can be planted in three main ways: horizontal, vertical, and slanting. Each method suits different soil and climate conditions. Horizontal planting: Lay the cutting flat in a furrow 5-10 cm deep, then cover with soil. This method is good for dry areas because the cutting is fully covered, reducing moisture loss. Nutrient Requirements of Cassava

Cassava is a heavy feeder. A crop of 30 tonnes of fresh roots per hectare removes about 120 kg nitrogen (N), 30 kg phosphorus (P2O5), and 150 kg potassium (K2O) from the soil. That is more potassium than most cereals. If you harvest roots and remove stems, you are mining the soil. Without replacement, yields drop every season..Trim()

.Trim()

Cassava needs nitrogen for leaf and stem growth. Early in the season, N drives canopy formation. A shortage shows as pale, small leaves and thin stems. Phosphorus is critical for root development. It helps the plant build a strong root system and improves starch content. Potassium is the most important nutrient for root bulking and starch formation. It also helps the plant resist diseases. Cassava also needs calcium, magnesium, sulphur, zinc, and boron, but in smaller amounts..Trim()

.Trim()

In Africa, most smallholder soils are sandy or degraded. They are low in organic matter, nitrogen, and phosphorus. Potassium is often low in sandy soils but can be adequate in clay soils. Continuous cassava cropping without fertilizer will exhaust even good soil. A farmer who plants cassava after cassava for three years without inputs may see yields fall from 20 tonnes per hectare to 8 tonnes or less..Trim()

.Trim()

To meet these needs, you must combine organic and inorganic sources. Farmyard manure, poultry litter, and compost supply multiple nutrients and build soil organic matter. Mineral fertilizers give a quick boost. The exact amount depends on your soil test, but a general recommendation for cassava in Africa is 60-90 kg N, 30-45 kg P2O5, and 60-90 kg K2O per hectare. That translates to about 4-6 bags of NPK 15-15-15 per hectare, plus additional potassium if needed..Trim()

.Trim()

Remember: cassava yields best when nutrients are available steadily. Split applications and place fertilizer correctly. Do not guess. Test your soil if possible. If not, use the general rates and adjust based on leaf colour and root size..Trim()

Organic Manure Application Techniques

Organic manure is the backbone of cassava fertility in Africa. It adds nutrients slowly, improves soil structure, and feeds soil life. Common types are farmyard manure (cattle, goat, sheep), poultry litter, compost, and green manure from legume cover crops like mucuna or cowpea..Trim()

.Trim()

Fresh manure can burn plants and carry weeds. Compost or age manure for at least 4-6 weeks before use. Poultry litter is rich: one tonne contains about 20 kg N, 15 kg P2O5, and 10 kg K2O. Cattle manure is weaker: one tonne has about 5 kg N, 2 kg P2O5, and 5 kg K2O. So you need more cattle manure to get the same effect..Trim()

.Trim()

Apply manure 2-4 weeks before planting. Broadcast it evenly over the plot, then incorporate it into the top 15-20 cm of soil with a hoe or plough. For cassava, a good rate is 5-10 tonnes per hectare of cattle manure, or 2-4 tonnes per hectare of poultry litter. If you have little manure, concentrate it in the planting holes. Dig a hole 30 cm wide and 20 cm deep, mix a handful (about 200-300 g) of well-rotted manure with the soil, then plant the cutting. This 'micro-dosing' gives each plant a good start..Trim()

.Trim()

You can also make compost from crop residues, kitchen waste, and animal dung. Build a heap 1.5 m wide and 1 m high. Turn it every 2 weeks. Keep it moist but not soaked. In 6-8 weeks, it becomes dark, crumbly compost. Apply 2-3 tonnes per hectare..Trim()

.Trim()

Green manure: plant mucuna or cowpea in the off-season. When they flower, cut them down and incorporate into the soil. This adds 40-60 kg N per hectare. Do not burn residues; that loses nitrogen. Organic manure works best when combined with small amounts of mineral fertilizer. Do not rely on manure alone if your soil is very poor..Trim()

Inorganic Fertilizer Types and Application Rates

Inorganic fertilizers are concentrated and fast-acting. They are sold in bags marked with three numbers, like 15-15-15. These numbers are the percentage of nitrogen (N), phosphorus (P2O5), and potassium (K2O). A 50 kg bag of 15-15-15 contains 7.5 kg N, 7.5 kg P2O5, and 7.5 kg K2O..Trim()

.Trim()

For cassava, common fertilizers are NPK 15-15-15, NPK 20-10-10, urea (46% N), and muriate of potash (60% K2O). Cassava needs more potassium than many crops. A general recommendation for a 20-30 tonne per hectare crop is 60-90 kg N, 30-45 kg P2O5, and 60-90 kg K2O per hectare. That means about 4-6 bags of NPK 15-15-15 per hectare, plus 1-2 bags of muriate of potash if your soil is low in K..Trim()

.Trim()

Apply fertilizer in a ring or band 10-15 cm from the plant base, not touching the stem. For a single plant, use about 10-15 g of NPK 15-15-15 at planting, and another 10-15 g at 6-8 weeks after planting. If you use urea, apply 50 kg per hectare (about 1 bag) split into two doses: half at 4 weeks, half at 8 weeks. Urea must be covered with soil to avoid losing nitrogen to the air..Trim()

.Trim()

Do not over-apply. Too much nitrogen gives big leaves but small roots. Too much fertilizer can burn young plants. Always read the label. Store fertilizer in a dry place, off the floor. Wear gloves and wash hands after use. If you cannot afford full rates, use half rates and combine with manure. A soil test will tell you exactly what you need. Without a test, start with 4 bags of NPK 15-15-15 per hectare and adjust based on plant growth..Trim()

Fertilizer Timing and Placement Methods

When and how you apply fertilizer matters as much as what you apply. Cassava has a long growing season (8-12 months). It needs nutrients early for root establishment, and again during root bulking (3-5 months after planting)..Trim()

.Trim()

At planting: Apply a starter dose of phosphorus and potassium. Mix 5-10 g of NPK 15-15-15 per planting hole with the soil before placing the cutting. This gives the young roots immediate access to nutrients. Do not let fertilizer touch the cutting directly; it can burn the buds..Trim()

.Trim()

First top-dressing: 4-6 weeks after planting, when shoots are 15-20 cm tall. Apply 10-15 g of NPK 15-15-15 per plant, or 50 kg of urea per hectare. Place fertilizer in a ring 10-15 cm from the stem, then cover lightly with soil. This is the most critical application for canopy development..Trim()

.Trim()

Second top-dressing: 8-12 weeks after planting, just before the roots start bulking. Apply 10-15 g of NPK 15-15-15 per plant, or 50 kg of muriate of potash per hectare. Potassium at this stage increases root size and starch content. If you use urea, split it: half at 4 weeks, half at 8 weeks..Trim()

.Trim()

Placement methods: Broadcasting is easy but less efficient; you need higher rates. Banding or ring placement puts fertilizer near the roots, so you can use less. For ring placement, make a shallow trench 10-15 cm from the plant, spread fertilizer, and cover with soil. Do not place fertilizer in a heap; it can damage roots. On slopes, place fertilizer on the uphill side to reduce runoff..Trim()

.Trim()

If you use manure, apply it 2-4 weeks before planting and incorporate it. Then supplement with mineral fertilizer at the times above. Always apply when soil is moist, not dry. If it is dry, wait for rain or water the soil lightly after application..Trim()

Integrated Soil Fertility Management

Integrated Soil Fertility Management (ISFM) means combining organic and inorganic nutrient sources with good agronomic practices. It is the best way to keep cassava yields high without destroying the soil. ISFM has three pillars: use organic matter, use mineral fertilizer wisely, and improve crop management..Trim()

.Trim()

First, organic matter. Apply 5-10 tonnes of cattle manure or 2-4 tonnes of poultry litter per hectare. Use compost from crop residues. Plant legume cover crops like mucuna or cowpea in rotation or intercropped. These add nitrogen and improve soil structure. Keep crop residues on the field; do not burn them..Trim()

.Trim()

Second, mineral fertilizer. Use the right type and rate based on soil test or general recommendations. For cassava, apply 4-6 bags of NPK 15-15-15 per hectare, split into planting and top-dressing. Add muriate of potash if potassium is low. Do not apply fertilizer without organic matter; the combination works better than either alone..Trim()

.Trim()

Third, good agronomic practices. Use improved varieties that respond to fertilizer. Plant on time at the start of the rains. Control weeds, especially in the first 3 months. Use proper spacing (1 m x 1 m or 1 m x 0.8 m). Practice crop rotation with cereals or legumes to break pest cycles and improve soil. Avoid continuous cassava on the same plot for more than 3 years without heavy organic inputs..Trim()

.Trim()

ISFM also includes soil conservation: contour ploughing, mulching, and cover crops to reduce erosion. On sandy soils, add clay or organic matter to increase water and nutrient holding capacity. Test your soil every 2-3 years if possible. If not, observe your plants: pale leaves mean nitrogen shortage; purple leaves mean phosphorus shortage; yellow leaf edges mean potassium shortage..Trim()

.Trim()

A simple ISFM plan for one hectare: apply 5 tonnes of manure before planting. At planting, apply 2 bags of NPK 15-15-15. At 6 weeks, apply 1 bag of urea. At 10 weeks, apply 1 bag of NPK 15-15-15 plus 1 bag of muriate of potash. This gives about 60 kg N, 30 kg P2O5, and 60 kg K2O, plus organic matter. Yields can reach 25-30 tonnes per hectare..Trim()

Diagnosing Nutrient Deficiencies in Cassava

Cassava plants tell you what they need. Learn to read the leaves. Nutrient deficiencies show specific symptoms. Early diagnosis saves your harvest..Trim()

.Trim()

Nitrogen deficiency: Older leaves turn pale green, then yellow. The whole plant looks stunted. Stems are thin. Leaves may drop early. This happens in sandy soils with low organic matter, or after heavy rains that leach nitrogen. To fix: apply urea or NPK 15-15-15 at 50 kg per hectare. Split applications..Trim()

.Trim()

Phosphorus deficiency: Leaves become dark green with a purple or reddish tint, especially on older leaves. Stems may be thin and short. Roots are small. This is common in acidic soils. To fix: apply NPK with phosphorus, or bone meal. Lime acidic soils to pH 5.5-6.5..Trim()

.Trim()

Potassium deficiency: Leaf edges turn yellow, then brown and dry (scorch). Older leaves are affected first. Roots are small and have low starch. This is common in sandy soils and after continuous cropping. To fix: apply muriate of potash at 50-100 kg per hectare, or wood ash..Trim()

.Trim()

Magnesium deficiency: Older leaves have yellow patches between green veins (interveinal chlorosis). Leaf edges may turn reddish-purple. To fix: apply magnesium sulphate (Epsom salt) at 50 kg per hectare, or dolomitic lime..Trim()

.Trim()

Zinc deficiency: Young leaves are small and narrow, with yellowing between veins. Internodes are short. To fix: spray zinc sulphate at 5 g per litre of water..Trim()

.Trim()

Boron deficiency: Growing points die. Leaves are thick and brittle. Roots are cracked. To fix: apply borax at 10 kg per hectare..Trim()

.Trim()

How to tell deficiency from disease? Nutrient deficiencies appear on many plants uniformly, often on older or newer leaves in a pattern. Diseases show spots, wilting, or rot. If you are unsure, test your soil. A simple soil test costs little and saves money on wrong fertilizers. Keep a notebook: record symptoms, what you applied, and the result. Over time, you will know your farm's needs..Trim()

Determining Harvest Maturity Indices

Knowing when to harvest cassava is critical. Harvest too early and roots are small, watery, and low in starch. Wait too long and roots become woody, fibrous, and may rot in the ground. Most cassava varieties mature between 9 and 18 months after planting, depending on the variety and growing conditions. For example, early-maturing varieties like TMS 30572 can be ready in 9â12 months, while late varieties like local bitter types may take 15â18 months. You can also do a simple starch test: cut a root and press the cut surface; if it feels sticky and milky, starch is high. Another method is the 'crack test': bend a root; if it snaps cleanly, it's mature; if it bends without breaking, it's immature. In Africa, farmers often use the 'calendar method' based on planting date, but this can be off by months. Combine calendar with root sampling. For processing into gari or fufu, harvest when roots are 10â12 months for best starch yield. For fresh market, harvest slightly earlier when roots are tender. Always harvest during the dry season when possible, as wet soil makes harvesting harder and roots rot faster. If you must harvest in the rainy season, do it quickly and process immediately. Remember, maturity indices are not just about time; they are about the plant's physical signs and your market need. Train yourself to check roots every month after 8 months. Detoxification of Cyanogenic Glycosides

Cassava contains cyanogenic glycosides, mainly linamarin and lotaustralin, which release hydrogen cyanide (HCN) when the plant cells are broken. Combine methods: soak, ferment, and cook. For industrial processing, use a screw press to remove water, then dry in a flash dryer at 150Â°C for 10 seconds. Cost of Production and Profitability Analysis

To know if your cassava farm is making money, you must track every cost and every income. Start by listing all costs for one hectare. Land preparation: if you hire a tractor, it costs about $80. Cuttings: you need 10,000 stems, which cost around $50 if you buy them. Planting labour: 10 people for 2 days at $5 per day = $100. Weeding: two rounds at $60 each = $120. Fertilizer: if you use NPK, 4 bags at $30 = $120. Harvesting: 15 people for 3 days at $5 = $225. Total cost: $695 per hectare. Now, yield: a good farm produces 20 tonnes of fresh roots per hectare. If you sell at $50 per tonne, revenue is $1,000. Profit = $305 per hectare. But if you process into garri: 20 tonnes roots give about 5 tonnes garri. Selling garri at $300 per tonne gives $1,500. Subtract processing costs (grating, pressing, frying) of about $200, and you get $1,300 revenue, profit $605. That's double the profit. Always calculate your break-even point: total costs divided by price per unit. If your cost is $695 and you sell at $50 per tonne, you need 13.9 tonnes to break even. Keep records of every expense and sale in a notebook. Use a simple tool like a cash book or a mobile app like FarmDrive. This helps you know which activity is profitable and where to cut costs. Remember, family labour should be valued at market rate to see true profit. Without records, you are farming blindly..Trim()

Market Research and Consumer Preferences

Before you plant or process, find out what people want to buy. Start by visiting local markets, talking to traders, and observing what sells. In many African countries, fresh cassava roots are sold for direct consumption, but processed products like garri, fufu, and starch have higher demand in cities. For example, in Nigeria, garri is a staple; in Ghana, fufu and banku are popular. Ask questions: How much do you pay for a 50kg bag of garri? What quality do you prefer (fine or coarse)? Do you buy white or yellow garri? Note that yellow garri (with palm oil) often sells at a premium. Also, check packaging: buyers may prefer sealed plastic bags with labels for hygiene. In East Africa, cassava chips and flour are growing markets. Use simple surveys: talk to 20 potential customers. Record their answers. Look at competitor prices: if a trader sells garri at $1 per kg, you can price yours at $0.90 to attract buyers, but ensure you cover costs. Also, consider seasonality: during the lean season, prices rise. You can store garri for months if kept dry. For processed products, target institutions like schools, restaurants, and hotels. They buy in bulk and pay well if quality is consistent. A simple tool: use a notebook to track daily prices at three different markets. After one month, you'll see patterns. This research reduces risk and helps you decide whether to sell fresh roots or process. Remember, consumer preferences vary by region, so test your product in small quantities first..Trim()

Value Chain Financing and Credit Access

Cassava farming needs money for inputs, labour, and processing. Many farmers lack cash, so they need credit. Climate-Smart Cassava Farming Practices

Climate-smart cassava farming means growing more cassava while adapting to changing weather and reducing greenhouse gases. In Africa, rainfall is becoming unpredictable, and droughts are more common. To be climate-smart, start with the right variety. Improved varieties like TME 419, TMS 30572, and TMS 98/0581 are drought-tolerant and give 20-30 tonnes per hectare with good management. Plant at the onset of rains, using healthy stem cuttings of 20-30 cm with at least 5 nodes. Space 1m by 1m for sole cassava, or 1m by 0.5m if you want to intercrop with legumes like cowpea or groundnut. Intercropping covers the soil, reduces evaporation, and fixes nitrogen. Use minimum tillage to keep soil carbon. Instead of burning, mulch with cassava leaves and crop residues. If you have irrigation, use drip lines to apply water directly to roots. On flat land, build contour ridges to harvest rainwater. In dry areas, plant early maturing varieties like TMS 91/02324 that mature in 9-12 months. Climate-smart also means reducing methane from livestock and using organic fertiliser. Apply 5-10 tonnes of compost or poultry manure per hectare. This improves soil water holding capacity. Rotate cassava with maize or legumes to break pest cycles. After harvest, leave some residues and plant cover crops like mucuna. These practices increase yields, reduce risk, and help fight climate change. Start small: test on one plot, then expand..Trim()

Soil Health and Environmental Sustainability

Cassava is tough, but it still needs healthy soil. Continuous cassava farming without adding nutrients depletes the soil. In Africa, many farmers get only 8-10 tonnes per hectare because soils are poor. To sustain production, you must feed the soil. First, test your soil. Contact your local agricultural extension officer or use a simple soil test kit (available from agro-dealers for about $20). Test for pH, nitrogen, phosphorus, and potassium. Cassava grows best at pH 5.5-6.5. If pH is below 5.0, apply agricultural lime at 1-2 tonnes per hectare. Add organic matter: compost, farmyard manure, or green manure. A good practice is to apply 5 tonnes of manure per hectare before planting. After planting, apply 300 kg per hectare of NPK 15-15-15 at 8 weeks. But don't rely only on chemical fertiliser. Use crop rotation: plant cassava after a legume like soybean or groundnut. Legumes fix nitrogen, reducing the need for fertiliser. Also, avoid burning. Burning destroys organic matter and kills beneficial microbes. Instead, incorporate residues into the soil. Control erosion by planting on contours or using vetiver grass barriers. Keep a cover crop like mucuna between rows. For sustainable harvesting, don't uproot all plants at once; harvest selectively. After 3-4 years, rotate to another crop. These practices maintain soil fertility, reduce costs, and protect the environment. Healthy soil means higher yields and better income..Trim()

Waste Utilization and Circular Economy

Cassava processing generates a lot of waste: peels, wastewater, and bagasse (fibrous residue). Instead of throwing it away, turn it into value. Cassava peels can be dried and milled into animal feed. Bagasse can be used as fuel for cooking or as substrate for mushroom farming. In a circular economy, nothing is wasted. For example, a medium-scale garri processor producing 1 tonne per day generates about 200 kg of peels and 500 litres of wastewater. By converting peels to feed and starch, you can earn an extra $20-30 per day. Also, use cassava leaves as a protein-rich vegetable for human consumption (after proper cooking to remove cyanide).
