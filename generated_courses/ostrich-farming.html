Module 1 · Introduction to Ostrich Farming

History and Global Industry of Ostrich Farming

Ostrich farming began in South Africa in the 1860s when farmers in the Karoo and Eastern Cape started domesticating wild ostriches for their feathers. By 1913, South Africa was exporting over 1 million kilograms of feathers annually, mostly to European fashion houses. The industry collapsed after World War I when feather fashion died, but revived in the 1980s with demand for ostrich meat and leather. Today, South Africa remains the world leader, processing about 200,000 birds per year. Other major producers include Namibia, Botswana, Zimbabwe, Kenya, and Nigeria. Global ostrich meat production is estimated at 15,000 to 20,000 tonnes per year, with Europe importing most of it. Leather is used for luxury bags, shoes, and car interiors. Feathers are sold for dusters and fashion. In Africa, small-scale farmers often start with 10 to 20 birds. The key markets are local butcheries, hotels, and export abattoirs. For example, in Kenya, Kajiado and Laikipia counties have successful ostrich farms supplying Nairobi restaurants. To enter the industry, you need a reliable abattoir within 300 km, because live birds cannot travel long distances without stress. South Africa's Ostrich Business Chamber provides price data. Your first step is to visit a working farm and ask about their costs and buyers. Real numbers: A day-old chick costs $30-$50, a 12-month-old bird sells for $300-$500, and a pair of breeding birds costs $1,000-$2,000. Start small, learn the ropes, and scale up as you find markets..Trim()

Overview of Ostrich Products: Meat, Leather, Feathers, and Oil

Ostrich farming gives you four main products: meat, leather, feathers, and oil. Meat is the biggest earner. A mature ostrich yields 35-40 kg of lean red meat. It tastes like beef but is lower in fat and cholesterol. In South Africa, ostrich steak sells for $15-$25 per kg in supermarkets. In Nigeria, upscale restaurants in Lagos pay $20 per kg. Leather comes from the hide. One hide produces 1.2-1.5 square meters of leather. Top-grade ostrich leather has raised quills and is used for luxury handbags, wallets, and car seats. A single hide can sell for $150-$300. Feathers are collected from live birds every 8-12 months. A bird gives about 1 kg of feathers per year. White feathers are most valuable for dusters and costumes; they sell for $50-$100 per kg. Oil is rendered from the fat around the carcass. It is used in cosmetics for its moisturizing properties. One bird yields 1-2 liters of oil, selling for $30-$50 per liter. To succeed, you need to process or sell to processors. For meat, you must have a licensed abattoir. For leather, you need to salt and dry hides immediately after slaughter. For feathers, pluck cleanly and store in dry bags. For oil, render fat at low heat. In Africa, many farmers sell live birds to abattoirs because they lack processing skills. But value addition doubles your income. Example: A farmer in Botswana who sells live birds gets $300 per bird. If he slaughters and sells meat, leather, and feathers separately, he can earn $600-$800 per bird. Start by learning from a local abattoir or tannery..Trim()

Legal and Regulatory Requirements for Ostrich Farming

Ostrich farming is regulated in most African countries. You need permits to keep, transport, and slaughter birds. Start by visiting your local department of agriculture or livestock office. In South Africa, you must register with the Department of Agriculture, Land Reform and Rural Development (DALRRD). You need a permit to keep ostriches, and your farm must be inspected for biosecurity. In Kenya, the Kenya Wildlife Service (KWS) regulates ostrich farming because ostriches are wild animals under the Wildlife Conservation and Management Act. You need a license from KWS, which costs about KSh 10,000 per year. In Nigeria, the Federal Ministry of Agriculture and Rural Development issues permits. You also need a veterinary health certificate for movement. For slaughter, you must use a licensed abattoir. If you want to export, you need additional certification from the World Organisation for Animal Health (WOAH) and your country's export authority. For example, South Africa's export abattoirs are approved by the EU. Other requirements include land use permits if you are on communal land, environmental impact assessments for large farms, and waste disposal plans. You must also comply with animal welfare laws. Ostriches need space: at least 1,000 square meters per 10 birds. Fencing must be at least 1.8 meters high. You cannot keep ostriches in residential areas. Record-keeping is mandatory: you must keep a register of births, deaths, and movements. Fines for non-compliance can be heavy. In Kenya, farming without a KWS license can lead to imprisonment. So, before you buy your first chick, get your paperwork in order. Visit your local agriculture office and ask for a checklist. It may take 2-3 months to get all permits, so start early. Basic Ostrich Biology and Behavior

Ostriches are the world's largest birds. They cannot fly, but they can run up to 70 km/h. An adult male stands 2.1-2.8 meters tall and weighs 100-160 kg. Females are slightly smaller. They have long necks, large eyes, and two-toed feet. Ostriches are hardy and adapt to hot, dry climates. They can live 30-40 years, but commercial farms keep them for 10-15 years. Ostriches are omnivores; they eat plants, seeds, insects, and small reptiles. On a farm, you feed them alfalfa, maize, soybean meal, and mineral supplements. A mature bird eats 2-3 kg of feed per day. They need fresh water at all times. Ostriches are social animals. In the wild, they live in groups of 5-50 birds. On a farm, keep them in groups of 10-20 to reduce stress. They have a pecking order; mixing new birds can cause fighting. Breeding season is from March to September in southern Africa. A male mates with several females. A female lays 40-60 eggs per year, but on a farm, you collect eggs for incubation. Eggs weigh 1.2-1.8 kg and take 42 days to hatch. Chicks need warmth (30-35Â°C) for the first two weeks. Ostriches have strong legs and can kick hard enough to kill a lion. They also have excellent eyesight and can see predators from far away. When threatened, they may lie flat on the ground or run. On a farm, you must handle them calmly to avoid injuries. They are curious and may peck at shiny objects. Never corner an ostrich; always leave an escape route. For breeding, select healthy birds with good conformation. Keep records of egg production and hatch rates. Understanding their behavior helps you design better housing and feeding systems. For example, provide shade and dust baths for cooling. Ostriches are resistant to many diseases, but they can get avian influenza, Newcastle disease, and worms. Vaccinate according to your vet's advice..Trim()

Designing Ostrich Pens and Shelters

Ostriches need space to run, but also shelter from sun, wind, and rain. A good pen design prevents injury and stress. For a breeding pair, provide at least 250 square meters (about 2,700 sq ft) of outdoor run. For growing chicks, allow 5 square meters per bird in the first month, increasing to 20 square meters by six months. Pens should be rectangular, not square, so birds can run in straight lines without hitting fences. Use a 2-meter high fence with small mesh at the bottom to stop chicks from slipping through. The shelter should be at least 3 meters by 4 meters per adult bird, with a solid roof and open sides for airflow. In hot African climates, orient the shelter east-west so the roof shades the midday sun. Flooring inside the shelter can be packed earth or concrete; concrete is easier to clean but needs bedding like wood shavings or sand for comfort. Provide a dust bath area: a shallow pit with dry sand or ash helps ostriches clean their feathers and control parasites. Always include a catch pen or small holding area attached to the main pen for easy handling. Avoid using wire mesh with sharp edges; ostriches have long legs and can easily cut themselves. Use smooth, rounded posts and rails. In areas with predators like jackals or lions, reinforce the fence with strong posts and buried wire. Remember, ostriches are curious and will peck at shiny objects; keep feeders and waterers simple and sturdy. A well-designed pen reduces stress, injuries, and escapes, leading to healthier birds and better production..Trim()

Fencing Requirements and Materials

Fencing is the most important part of ostrich housing because ostriches are strong and can run fast. A fence must be at least 1.8 to 2 meters high. Use woven wire mesh with small openings (5 cm by 10 cm) at the bottom to prevent chicks from escaping and predators from digging in. The mesh should be at least 1.5 meters high, with two or three strands of smooth wire above it to reach 2 meters. Wooden posts (10 cm diameter) or steel posts should be placed every 3 meters. Corner posts need extra bracing. In Africa, treated eucalyptus or pine posts last longer against termites. Do not use barbed wire; ostriches can tear their skin on it. For internal partitions, use the same mesh but lighter gauge. Gates should be 1.5 meters wide and swing inward or slide, made of metal or strong wood. In predator-prone areas, bury the bottom of the fence 30 cm deep and add an apron of wire on the ground. Repair immediately. A good fence keeps birds in, predators out, and reduces stress. Remember, ostriches can jump up to 1.5 meters, so never leave gaps at the top.
