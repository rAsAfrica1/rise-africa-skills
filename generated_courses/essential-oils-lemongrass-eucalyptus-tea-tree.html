Identifying Eucalyptus Trees in Your Area and Harvesting Leaves

Eucalyptus trees are common in many African countries, especially in South Africa, Kenya, Ethiopia, and Zimbabwe. Tea Tree: How to Recognize and Source It in Africa

Tea tree (Melaleuca alternifolia) is native to Australia, but it is now grown in several African countries, including South Africa, Kenya, Zimbabwe, and Zambia. It is a small tree or shrub (up to 6 meters) with narrow, needle-like leaves and papery, layered bark. The leaves are soft, aromatic, and have a strong medicinal, camphor-like smell. Tea tree grows best in warm, humid areas with 800-1200 mm annual rainfall and well-drained, acidic soil. It cannot tolerate frost. If you are in a high-altitude region, you may need to grow it in a greenhouse or under shade cloth. To source tea tree, contact commercial essential oil farms in South Africa (e.g., in Mpumalanga or KwaZulu-Natal) or Kenya (e.g., around Mount Kenya). Some nurseries sell tea tree seedlings. For oil production, harvest the leafy tips (top 30 cm of branches) when the plant is 12-18 months old. Harvest in the morning after dew has dried.
