Understanding Solar Cells and Their Functions

Selecting and Preparing Aluminum Frames

Aluminum frames protect the edges of your solar panel, hold the glass and backsheet together, and make mounting easy. In Africa, you'll find two common frame types: mill finish (plain silver) and anodized (coated for corrosion resistance). For coastal or humid areas, always choose anodized. Frames come in standard sizes for 60-cell and 72-cell panels, but you can cut them to length. You'll need a tape measure, hacksaw or mitre saw with a metal blade, file, and a rubber mallet. First, measure your laminated panel's length and width. Add 2 mm clearance on each side for the sealant. Cut four frame pieces: two long (length + 4 mm) and two short (width + 4 mm). Use a mitre box to cut 45-degree angles on both ends of each piece. After cutting, file the ends smooth to remove burrs. If you're reusing old frames, scrape out old silicone with a putty knife. Lay out the four pieces on a clean, flat surface like a plywood board. Make sure the mitred corners meet tightly. Diagnosing Common Panel Failures

Start by checking the obvious: look at the panel for cracks, burns, or discoloration. Use a multimeter set to DC volts. Measure the open-circuit voltage (Voc) at the junction box.
