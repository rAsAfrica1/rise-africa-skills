Module 2 · Dairy Ingredients and Their Functions

Milk Composition and Quality Parameters

Milk is about 87% water, 3.5% fat, 3.4% protein, 4.8% lactose, and 0.7% minerals. For ice cream, you need to know these numbers because they affect texture and sweetness. In Africa, fresh cow milk is common, but you can also use camel, goat, or sheep milk. Each has different fat and protein levels. For example, goat milk has smaller fat globules, which can make ice cream creamier but also more prone to goaty flavor. Always test milk quality before use. A simple way is to check smell and appearance: fresh milk should smell clean, not sour, and look white to creamy. Use a lactometer to check density; it should read between 1.026 and 1.032 at 20Â°C. If it's lower, the milk may be watered down. Also, do a clot-on-boiling test: boil a small sample; if it curdles, the milk is acidic and will ruin your ice cream. For ice cream, you want milk with low bacteria count. If you don't have a lab, pasteurize milk at 72Â°C for 15 seconds, then cool quickly. In many African settings, milk is often unpasteurized, so this step is critical. Also, consider using milk powder if fresh milk is scarce. But fresh milk gives better flavor. Remember, milk quality directly affects the final product: high-fat milk makes richer ice cream, while high-protein milk can make it too icy if not balanced. So, measure your ingredients carefully. Use a thermometer and a lactometer; these are cheap tools available in agricultural supply shops. Always source from healthy animals and keep milk cold (below 4Â°C) until use. If you're buying from a local farmer, ask about animal health and feed. Good milk is the foundation of good ice cream..Trim()

Cream: Types, Fat Content, and Sourcing

Cream is the fat-rich part of milk that rises to the top when milk is left to stand. In ice cream, cream gives richness, smoothness, and helps trap air. The fat content of cream varies: light cream has 18-30% fat, whipping cream has 30-36%, and heavy cream has 36-40%. In Africa, you can buy cream from dairy processors or make your own by skimming the top of settled milk. To make cream at home, let fresh milk sit in a cool place for 12-24 hours, then skim the top layer with a ladle. This gives you a cream with about 20-30% fat, depending on the milk. For consistent results, you can also use a cream separator, but these are expensive. Instead, many small-scale producers use a simple method: heat milk to 40Â°C, then let it cool and skim. The fat content affects your ice cream's texture: more fat means creamier, but too much can make it heavy and coat your mouth. A typical ice cream mix has 10-16% fat. So, if you use heavy cream (36% fat), you'll need less than if you use light cream. When sourcing cream, check for freshness: it should smell sweet, not sour. If you're buying from a market, ask for unpasteurized cream and pasteurize it yourself at 72Â°C for 15 seconds. In hot African climates, cream spoils quickly, so keep it refrigerated. If you can't get fresh cream, you can use butter or ghee, but these change the flavor. For a local twist, try using cream from zebu cattle, which has higher fat content. Always measure fat content using a butyrometer if available, or use the formula: fat% = (cream volume / milk volume) x 100. This helps you adjust recipes. Remember, cream is expensive, so use it wisely. You can also blend cream with milk to get the right fat level. For example, to make 1 liter of 12% fat mix, mix 333 ml of 36% cream with 667 ml of skim milk. This saves money and gives consistent results..Trim()

Concentrated Milks: Evaporated, Condensed, and Powdered

Concentrated milks are milk with some water removed. They are useful in Africa because they last longer without refrigeration and are easy to transport. Evaporated milk is unsweetened and has about 7.5% fat and 25% milk solids. Condensed milk is sweetened with sugar (about 40-45% sugar) and has 8% fat. Powdered milk is dried milk with less than 5% moisture; it can be whole (26% fat) or skim (1% fat). In ice cream, these ingredients add milk solids, fat, and flavor. They also help control ice crystal formation because they bind water. For example, using 100g of skim milk powder in a 1-liter mix can improve texture and reduce iciness. But you must rehydrate them properly: for powdered milk, mix with warm water (40Â°C) at a ratio of 1 part powder to 4 parts water for whole milk, or 1:8 for skim. Let it sit for 20 minutes to fully dissolve. Evaporated milk can be used straight from the can, but if you want to substitute for fresh milk, mix equal parts evaporated milk and water. Condensed milk is already sweet, so reduce the sugar in your recipe. For instance, if a recipe calls for 200g sugar and you use 300g condensed milk (which contains about 120g sugar), add only 80g extra sugar. In Africa, these products are often imported, so check expiry dates and seals. If you open a can, use it within 3 days and refrigerate. Powdered milk is the most shelf-stable; keep it dry and cool. For best results, use a blender to mix powders into your liquid to avoid lumps. Also, note that concentrated milks can have a cooked flavor, especially evaporated milk. To minimize this, choose brands with no additives. You can also make your own evaporated milk by simmering fresh milk down to half its volume, but this takes time and fuel. For small-scale ice cream makers, powdered milk is often the most cost-effective. It allows you to adjust fat and solids easily. For example, to increase milk solids without adding fat, use skim milk powder. To add fat, use whole milk powder or cream. Always calculate your mix to balance fat, sugar, and solids. A simple formula: total solids should be 36-40% of the mix. This includes fat, protein, lactose, and added sugar. Concentrated milks help you hit that target..Trim()

Whey and Dairy By-Products in Ice Cream

Whey is the liquid left after cheese-making. It contains about 0.8% protein, 5% lactose, and little fat. In Africa, whey is often discarded, but it can be used in ice cream to reduce cost and add solids. However, whey has a high lactose content, which can cause sandiness (lactose crystals) if too much is used. A safe limit is 2-3% whey powder in the mix. Whey protein concentrate (WPC) is a better option: it has 35-80% protein and improves texture by binding water and stabilizing air bubbles. You can buy WPC from dairy suppliers or use it as a by-product from local cheese makers. Other by-products include buttermilk (from butter-making) and skim milk. Buttermilk adds a tangy flavor and helps emulsify fat. In ice cream, using 5-10% buttermilk can enhance creaminess. Skim milk is essentially milk without fat; it adds protein and lactose but no fat. When using by-products, always pasteurize them first. For whey, heat to 72Â°C for 15 seconds, then cool. If you're using whey from a local cheese maker, ask if it's been pasteurized; if not, do it yourself. Also, whey can have a salty taste if from salted cheese, so taste before adding. In terms of cost, whey is often free or cheap, making it attractive for small businesses. But don't overuse it: too much whey can make ice cream icy and weak-flavored. A typical recipe might use 10% whey powder, 5% buttermilk powder, and the rest from milk and cream. You can also use whey to replace some of the water in your mix. For example, if a recipe calls for 1 liter of milk, use 800 ml milk and 200 ml whey. This reduces cost and adds nutrients. However, whey has a high mineral content, which can affect flavor. So, start with small amounts and adjust. In Africa, whey is also used in animal feed, but with proper handling, it can be a valuable ice cream ingredient. Always store whey refrigerated and use within 2 days. If you can't get fresh whey, whey powder is a good alternative. It has a long shelf life and is easy to measure. Use 1 part whey powder to 9 parts water to reconstitute. Then treat it like fresh whey. Remember, by-products are not waste; they are resources. With creativity, you can make delicious ice cream while saving money..Trim()

Butterfat Alternatives and Their Impact on Texture

Butterfat is the gold standard for ice cream, but it's expensive and not always available in Africa. Alternatives include vegetable oils (coconut, palm, sunflower), butter, ghee, and emulsifiers. Each affects texture differently. Coconut oil is solid at room temperature in cooler climates but melts quickly in the mouth, giving a cool sensation. It has a distinct flavor, so use refined coconut oil if you want neutral taste. Palm oil is semi-solid and cheap, but it can leave a waxy mouthfeel. Sunflower oil is liquid and makes ice cream softer; it's best used in small amounts (2-5% of mix). Butter and ghee are dairy fats, so they give a rich flavor, but they contain water and milk solids that can affect freezing. Ghee is clarified butter, so it's pure fat and has a high smoke point; it's great for ice cream but expensive. When replacing butterfat, you need to consider the fat's melting point. Role of Sweeteners in Freezing Point Depression

Sweeteners do more than make ice cream sweet. They lower the freezing point of your mix, which controls how hard your final product gets and how smoothly it scoops. In continuous freezers, ice cream mix is frozen dynamically as it is pumped through a barrel while a dasher scrapes the frozen mix off the walls. Overrun is the amount of air incorporated into ice cream, expressed as a percentage of the volume of the mix. For example, if 1 liter of mix becomes 1.5 liters of ice cream, overrun is 50%. Overrun affects texture, cost, and scoopability. Too little overrun (less than 30%) makes ice cream dense and heavy; too much (over 100%) makes it fluffy and airy, melting quickly. Commercial ice cream typically has 50-100% overrun. To measure overrun, weigh a fixed volume of mix (e.g., 1 liter) and the same volume of finished ice cream. In Africa, where ingredients can be costly, controlling overrun helps maximize profit. To increase overrun, increase dasher speed, reduce mix pump speed, or open the air inlet valve more. To decrease overrun, do the opposite. In batch freezers, overrun is controlled by the amount of mix and churning time; more mix and longer churning can increase overrun. For continuous freezers, adjust the air valve and pump settings. Keep records of overrun for each batch to maintain consistency. High overrun is not always better; for premium ice cream, aim for 30-50% overrun for a rich, creamy texture. In hot climates, lower overrun (30-40%) helps the ice cream resist melting.
