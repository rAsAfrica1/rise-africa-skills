Module 1 · Introduction to EV Motorbike Technology

Overview of Electric Motorbike Components

An electric motorbike has five main parts: the battery, motor, controller, throttle, and frame. The battery stores energy, usually lithium-ion, with a capacity of 1-3 kWh for a typical African commuter bike. For example, a 48V 20Ah battery gives about 960Wh, enough for 40-60 km. The motor is usually a brushless DC hub motor, rated 500W to 3000W, mounted in the rear wheel. It converts electrical energy into rotation. The controller is the brain: it takes signals from the throttle and regulates power to the motor. It also protects against overcurrent and low voltage. The throttle is a twist-grip or thumb lever that sends a 0-5V signal to the controller. The frame is often a modified petrol bike frame, but EV-specific frames have stronger dropouts and battery mounts. Other components include the DC-DC converter (steps 48V down to 12V for lights and horn), the charger, and the wiring harness. In Africa, many builders start with a second-hand petrol frame and convert it. You must check that the frame can hold the battery weightâoften 10-15 kg. Use a tape measure and angle grinder to adapt mounts. The battery is the most expensive part, so protect it from vibration and water. A simple test: connect the battery to the controller, twist the throttle, and see if the wheel spins. If not, check the throttle signal with a multimeter. This overview gives you the map; later lessons detail each part..Trim()

Understanding Electric vs. Internal Combustion Engines

An internal combustion engine (ICE) burns petrol or diesel to create expanding gases that push a piston. It has hundreds of moving parts: pistons, valves, crankshaft, camshaft, and more. It needs oil changes every 1,000-3,000 km, air filters, spark plugs, and a exhaust system. An electric motor has one moving part: the rotor. It uses electromagnetic fields to spin. No oil, no spark plugs, no exhaust. For a learner, the key difference is maintenance. An ICE bike in Africa might need a full service every month if used daily for deliveries. An EV motor needs almost no maintenanceâjust check bearings and bolts every 6 months. Efficiency also differs: ICE converts only about 25-30% of petrol energy into motion; EV converts 80-90% of battery energy. That means an EV can go 60 km on the same energy that an ICE bike uses for 20 km. But EVs have limitations: range is limited by battery capacity, and charging takes 4-8 hours from a wall socket. ICE bikes can refuel in 5 minutes at any petrol station. In Africa, where grid power can be unreliable, many EV owners use solar panels or swap batteries. Torque is another difference: electric motors give full torque from zero RPM, so they accelerate faster off the line. ICE engines need to rev up. For assembly, you must understand that an EV drivetrain is simpler: battery, controller, motor, and throttle. No clutch, no gearbox, no fuel tank. That means fewer parts to break and fewer skills needed for repair. But you must respect high voltageâlater lessons cover safety..Trim()

Safety Protocols for High-Voltage Systems

EV batteries run at 48V, 60V, or 72V DC. That is high enough to cause serious burns or stop your heart. Always treat any battery above 50V as lethal. Before you touch any wiring, turn off the battery isolator switch, then disconnect the main negative terminal first. Use a multimeter to confirm zero voltage between the positive and negative cables. Never assume a switch is off. Wear insulated gloves rated for 1000V, and use tools with insulated handles. Remove metal jewelleryârings, watches, necklacesâbecause they can short a battery and cause an explosion. Work on a dry rubber mat, not a concrete floor. If you need to test a live circuit, use one hand only (keep the other in your pocket) to avoid a path across your heart. Battery fires are different from petrol fires: they are chemical and need water or a Class D extinguisher, not a standard ABC extinguisher. Keep a bucket of sand and a fire blanket nearby. If a battery starts to smoke or swell, move away and let it burn in a safe open areaâdo not try to smother it. For assembly, never short the battery terminals with a wrench. Use tape on terminals when not connected. When charging, use the correct charger and never leave it unattended overnight. In Africa, where power surges are common, use a surge protector. Teach everyone in your workshop these rules. A single mistake can kill. Practice lockout/tagout: put a padlock on the isolator and keep the key in your pocket. That way no one can turn power on while you work..Trim()

Tools and Equipment for EV Assembly

You need a basic set of hand tools: metric spanners from 8mm to 19mm, a set of screwdrivers (flat and Phillips), pliers, wire cutters, and a crimping tool for making battery cables. A digital multimeter is essentialâget one that measures DC volts up to 200V, resistance, and continuity. A clamp meter that reads DC amps is useful for checking motor current. For battery work, you need a torque wrench to tighten terminals to the manufacturer's spec (often 5-8 Nm). A soldering iron (60W or more) for splicing wires, with lead-free solder and heat shrink tubing. For mechanical work, a socket set with extensions, a rubber mallet, and a hacksaw or angle grinder for cutting brackets. A bench vice and files help with fitting. For electrical safety, insulated gloves, safety glasses, and a rubber mat. For testing, a 48V/72V battery load tester is expensive, but you can make a simple test with a 100W light bulb in series. For assembly flow, you need a workbench at waist height, good lighting (LED floodlight), and a parts organizer. In Africa, many tools are available in local marketsâbuy good quality, not the cheapest. A cheap multimeter can give wrong readings and cause a short. Keep a notebook to record torque values and wiring colours. With these tools, you can assemble a complete EV motorbike..Trim()

Reading Electrical Schematics and Diagrams

A schematic is a map of how electricity flows. It uses symbols: a long line for a wire, a circle with a letter for a component, and a switch symbol for a switch. You must learn the common symbols: battery (two parallel lines, one long one short), resistor (zigzag), capacitor (two parallel lines), motor (circle with M), controller (rectangle with pins), and ground (three decreasing horizontal lines). In EV diagrams, the main power circuit goes from battery positive to a fuse, then to the controller, then to the motor, then back to battery negative. The throttle connects to the controller with three wires: +5V, ground, and signal. The brake switch cuts power to the controller when pressed. Colour codes vary by manufacturer, but common: red for positive, black for negative, green for signal. Always trace a circuit from the battery to the component and back. Use a multimeter in continuity mode to check if a wire is broken. For assembly, you will get a wiring diagram with your kit. Study it before connecting anything. Label each wire with tape and a marker. If you don't have a diagram, you can draw your own by following the wires. Start with the battery, then controller, then motor. Test each section with a multimeter before connecting the next. A common mistake is reversing positive and negativeâthat can destroy the controller. Double-check with a multimeter: red probe on positive, black on negative should read battery voltage. If it reads negative, swap probes. Practice reading simple diagrams from online sources. In Africa, many kits come with Chinese diagrams, so learn to interpret symbols, not words..Trim()

Workplace Organization and Assembly Flow

A clean, organized workshop saves time and prevents accidents. Start with a dedicated assembly area: a concrete floor, a sturdy table 1m high, and shelves for parts. Use the 5S method: Sort, Set in order, Shine, Standardize, Sustain. Sort means remove everything not needed for EV assembly. Set in order means each tool has a marked placeâpaint outlines on a pegboard. Shine means clean up spills immediately, especially battery acid or oil. Standardize means write a checklist for each assembly step. Sustain means do it every day. For assembly flow, break the build into stations: Station 1: frame preparation (grinding, painting). Station 2: motor installation (mount wheel, torque axle nuts). Station 3: battery box mounting and wiring. Station 4: controller and throttle connection. Station 5: testing and final checks. Each station has its own tools and parts. Move the bike from station to station, not the tools. This reduces walking and mistakes. Use a Kanban card or a simple whiteboard to track progress. In Africa, space may be limited, so use vertical storage: hang cables on hooks, put small parts in clear jars. Keep a fire extinguisher and first aid kit visible. Label all bins: 'M8 bolts', 'Anderson connectors', 'heat shrink'. At the end of each day, spend 10 minutes tidying. A well-organized shop means you can build a bike in 4-6 hours instead of 2 days. And you will make fewer errors, like crossing wires or losing screws. Good organization is a skill you can learn..Trim()

Module 2 · Frame and Chassis Preparation

Inspecting and Cleaning the Frame

Start by placing the frame on a clean, flat surface like a wooden pallet or a sturdy table. You need good lightâwork outdoors or use a bright LED lamp. Put on safety glasses and gloves. First, check for any bends, cracks, or deep rust. Look closely at the steering head tube, the swingarm pivot area, and the rear shock mounts. A cracked frame is dangerous; if you find one, set it aside and get a replacement. For minor rust, use a wire brush or sandpaper (80-grit then 120-grit) to remove it. In humid coastal areas like Mombasa or Lagos, rust spreads fast, so treat any bare metal immediately with a rust converter like phosphoric acid. Next, clean the frame with a degreaser. Mix dish soap with warm water if you don't have a dedicated degreaser. Use a stiff nylon brush to scrub away dirt, oil, and grease. Pay attention to welds and tight corners where grime hides. Rinse with clean water and dry with a cloth. Then wipe down with acetone or methylated spirits to remove any residue. This step is critical because paint and threadlocker won't stick to oily surfaces. After cleaning, inspect all threaded holes. Run a tap (M6, M8, M10) through them to clear paint or rust. If a thread is stripped, you can install a Helicoil insert. Finally, check the frame's alignment by placing a straight edge along the main tube. Any gap greater than 2 mm means the frame is bent. For African workshops, a simple plumb bob and string can check vertical alignment. Once clean and straight, apply a thin coat of primer if you plan to paint, or leave bare for assembly. Always store the frame on a padded surface to avoid scratches..Trim()

Mounting the Frame on Assembly Jig

An assembly jig holds the frame steady while you install heavy components. You can build a simple jig using square steel tubes (40x40 mm) welded to a base plate. The jig must be level; use a spirit level to check both directions. If you don't have a welder, bolt together wooden beams (100x100 mm) with M12 bolts. Place the frame onto the jig so the steering head and swingarm pivot are supported. The frame should not rock. Use C-clamps or U-bolts to secure it. For a typical EV motorbike frame, the steering head tube should be about 800-900 mm from the ground, and the swingarm pivot about 300-350 mm. Measure these heights with a tape measure. If your jig is adjustable, set these dimensions. Next, check that the frame is horizontal side-to-side. Place a spirit level across the top tubes. If it's off, shim the low side with metal plates. Once level, tighten all clamps. Now you can test fit the swingarm and rear shock. The jig should allow you to remove and install parts without the frame moving. In many African workshops, a jig made from scrap metal and a wooden pallet works fine. Just ensure it's stable. After mounting, mark the jig with a paint pen for future frames. This saves time on the next build. Remember, a wobbly frame leads to misaligned wheels and poor handling. Take 10 minutes to get it right..Trim()

Installing Steering Head Bearings

The steering head bearings let the front fork turn smoothly. Most EV motorbikes use tapered roller bearings. You need a bearing driver, a hammer, and grease. First, clean the steering head tube inside. Use a rag with solvent. Then apply a thin layer of high-temperature grease to the inside of the tube. Take the lower bearing cone (the inner race) and press it onto the steering stem using a pipe or bearing driver. Tap evenly until it seats fully. You'll hear a change in sound. Next, pack the bearing with grease. Work grease into the rollers by hand. For the upper bearing, place it into the top of the head tube. Then insert the steering stem through the head tube. Install the upper cone and dust seal. Thread on the adjusting nut. Tighten it by hand until snug, then use a spanner to turn it another 1/8 to 1/4 turn. You want zero play but no binding. Check by turning the fork side to side; it should move smoothly with slight resistance. If it feels gritty, the bearings are not seated or are damaged. In dusty African conditions, use sealed bearings if available. Otherwise, pack extra grease. After adjustment, install the lock nut and tighten it while holding the adjusting nut. Torque the lock nut to 25-30 Nm. Recheck play after tightening. A common mistake is overtightening, which causes flat spots and dangerous handling. Test ride at low speed to confirm..Trim()

Attaching Swingarm and Rear Suspension

The swingarm connects the rear wheel to the frame. Start by greasing the swingarm pivot bolt and the bearings inside the swingarm. Use a waterproof grease like lithium complex. Slide the swingarm into the frame's pivot brackets. Insert the pivot bolt from the left side. Apply threadlocker (blue Loctite) to the bolt threads. Thread on the nut and tighten to 60-70 Nm for a typical M14 bolt. Do not overtighten; you'll crush the bearings. Check that the swingarm moves up and down freely without side play. If it's tight, the bearings are misaligned. Next, install the rear shock absorbers. Most EV bikes have twin shocks. Mount the top eye to the frame and the bottom eye to the swingarm. Use new mounting bolts and nuts. Torque the shock bolts to 40-45 Nm. For the shock preload, set it to the middle position for a rider of 70-80 kg. In Africa, roads can be rough, so you might increase preload by 2 clicks. Check that the shock springs don't rub on the swingarm or frame. Finally, install the rear wheel temporarily to check alignment. The wheel should be centered between the swingarm legs. Use a string line from the front wheel to the rear wheel to check tracking. Adjust the chain adjusters if needed. After everything is tight, bounce the rear suspension a few times. Listen for clunks. If you hear any, something is loose. Recheck all bolts..Trim()

Front Fork Installation and Alignment

Front forks hold the wheel and absorb bumps. Begin by sliding the fork tubes into the triple clamps. The lower triple clamp holds the tubes; the upper clamp secures them. Leave the upper clamp bolts loose for now. Push the tubes up until they protrude about 10-15 mm above the upper clamp. This height affects steering. For most EV motorbikes, 10 mm is a good starting point. Tighten the lower clamp bolts to 20-25 Nm. Then tighten the upper clamp bolts to 20-25 Nm. Do not overtighten; you can crack the aluminum clamp. Next, install the front wheel. Slide the axle through the fork dropouts and wheel hub. Tighten the axle nut to 50-60 Nm. Check that the wheel spins freely. Now check alignment. Stand in front of the bike and look at the fork tubes. They should be parallel. If one tube is forward or backward, loosen the clamps and adjust. Use a straight edge against the fork tubes to check. Also, ensure the handlebar is straight. With the wheel pointing forward, the handlebar should be perpendicular to the frame. Adjust the handlebar clamps if needed. Finally, check the fork oil. Most forks need 150-200 ml of 10W fork oil per leg. If you don't know the history, drain and refill. In dusty conditions, change fork oil every 5000 km. After installation, compress the forks a few times to settle the springs. Then recheck all bolts..Trim()

Torque Specifications for Chassis Fasteners

Torque is the tightness of a bolt, measured in Newton-meters (Nm). Too loose, parts fall off. Too tight, bolts break or threads strip. You need a torque wrench. The click-type is best for beginners. Always use the correct size socket. For chassis fasteners on an EV motorbike, here are common specs: Steering stem lock nut: 25-30 Nm. Triple clamp bolts (M8): 20-25 Nm. Swingarm pivot bolt (M14): 60-70 Nm. Rear shock bolts (M10): 40-45 Nm. Front axle nut (M12): 50-60 Nm. Rear axle nut (M14): 80-90 Nm. Motor mount bolts (M10): 45-50 Nm. Battery box bolts (M8): 15-20 Nm. Always clean threads and apply threadlocker (blue) unless specified dry. Tighten in a crisscross pattern for multiple bolts, like on a wheel. After torquing, mark the bolt and nut with a paint pen so you can see if it loosens. Recheck torque after the first 50 km and then every 1000 km. In African heat, metal expands, so torque can change. Keep a torque chart in your workshop. If you don't have a torque wrench, tighten until snug plus a quarter turn for small bolts, but this is risky. Save up for a good wrench; it's cheaper than replacing a stripped frame. Always use a calibrated wrench. Store it at its lowest setting..Trim()

Torque Check of Critical Fasteners

After assembling an EV motorbike, you must check the tightness of every critical bolt and nut. Loose fasteners can cause accidents. Use a calibrated torque wrench. For this module, you need a torque wrench that reads from 5 Nm to 100 Nm. Start with the front and rear axle nuts. The front axle nut should be tightened to 60 Nm. The rear axle nut to 80 Nm. Fluid and Lubricant Top-Off

EV motorbikes have fewer fluids than petrol bikes, but you still need to check and top off several. First, brake fluid. Most EV bikes use DOT 3 or DOT 4 brake fluid. Show them how to charge: plug into a standard 220V socket, using the charger provided. Charge for 6-8 hours for a full charge. Do not leave it plugged in for more than 24 hours. Show them how to check tire pressure and brake fluid. Teach them the daily checks: tires, brakes, lights, battery level. Show them how to adjust the mirrors and handlebar. Explain the regenerative braking: when you release the throttle, the motor slows the bike and charges the battery. This saves energy. Warn them not to touch the battery terminals or open the battery box. If they have a problem, they should call you, not try to fix it themselves. Give them your phone number and the workshop address. Do a test ride with them: you ride first, then they ride in a safe area. Watch them start, stop, and turn. Correct any mistakes. Finally, give them the keys, documents, and a small toolkit.
