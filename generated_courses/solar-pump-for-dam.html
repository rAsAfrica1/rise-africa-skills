MPPT and PWM are two different ways a controller pulls power from your solar panels. PWM, or pulse width modulation, simply connects the panels to the pump in short pulses. The panel voltage is pulled down to the pump or battery voltage, so if you have a 48V pump and 60V panels, you lose the extra 12V as heat. .Trim()

MPPT, or maximum power point tracking, is smarter. The controller then converts that higher voltage down to what the pump needs, gaining up to 30% more water per day. In a dam application, that means you can pump longer in the morning and evening, and you get more litres on cloudy days. .Trim()

For pumping, MPPT is almost always the better choice when your array voltage is higher than your pump voltage. A solar pump controller has several protection features that keep your pump and panels safe. Overvoltage protection stops the controller when the DC input from the panels is too high. Overview of Pump Types: Centrifugal, Submersible, Surface

When pumping from a dam, you have three main pump types to choose from: centrifugal, submersible, and surface. Each has strengths and weaknesses for African conditions..Trim()

.Trim()

Centrifugal pumps are the most common for surface applications. They sit above water and use an impeller to create flow. They work best when the suction lift is less than 7 meters (at sea level) and are ideal for moving large volumes at low to medium heads. In Africa, you'll see them on many farms because they're cheap and easy to repair. However, they lose prime easily if the suction pipe leaks or the water level drops..Trim()

.Trim()

Submersible pumps are designed to be submerged in the dam or a borehole. They push water rather than pull it, so they can lift from deep depths. They are more efficient and reliable for high-head applications, but they cost more and require a waterproof electrical connection. In a dam, a submersible can be placed on a float or suspended to avoid silt. They are excellent for solar because they can run directly off DC power without an inverter..Trim()

.Trim()

Surface pumps (also called self-priming or jet pumps) are a subset of centrifugal but can handle some air in the suction line. They are useful when the water level fluctuates, but they are less efficient than submersibles for deep lifts..Trim()

.Trim()

For a typical African dam with a water level 3-5 meters below the pump and a total head of 10-20 meters, a centrifugal pump is often the best choice. But if your dam is deep or you need to push water uphill over 30 meters, a submersible is better. Always check the pump's maximum suction lift and head capacity before buying. Local availability of spare parts matters: in many rural areas, centrifugal pumps are easier to repair than submersibles..Trim()

Matching Pump Curves to Dam Head and Flow Requirements

A pump curve is a graph that shows how much water a pump can deliver at different heads. The head is the total vertical distance plus friction losses. .Trim()

Start by calculating your daily water need. For example, irrigating 1 hectare of maize in Zimbabwe might need 30,000 liters per day. .Trim()

Now, look at pump curves. Manufacturers like Grundfos, Lorentz, and Dayliff provide curves for their solar pumps. Find the curve where your flow (3.75 mÂ³/h) intersects your head (10 m). In Africa, many pumps are sold without curves. Insist on seeing the curve or use online tools like the Grundfos Product Center. For solar pumps, the curve changes with solar irradiance. .Trim()
