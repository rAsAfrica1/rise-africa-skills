Module 1 · Introduction to Sunflower Farming

History and Global Importance of Sunflower

Sunflower (Helianthus annuus) was first domesticated in North America over 4,000 years ago, but it found its way to Africa through trade routes in the 16th century. Today, Africa produces about 2.5 million tonnes of sunflower seed annually, with major growers including Tanzania, South Africa, Uganda, and Zambia. In Tanzania alone, over 1.5 million smallholder farmers grow sunflowers, mostly in the central and northern regions. The crop is valued for its oil, which is used for cooking, and its seed cake, a high-protein animal feed..Trim()

.Trim()

Globally, sunflower is the fourth most important oilseed after palm, soybean, and rapeseed. The world produces around 50 million tonnes of sunflower seed each year. Major producers include Ukraine, Russia, Argentina, and China. In Africa, sunflower is a strategic crop because it thrives in dry areas where other crops fail, and it matures in just 90-120 days, allowing farmers to harvest before the rains end. This makes it ideal for smallholder farmers in semi-arid regions..Trim()

.Trim()

Sunflower was introduced to Africa by Portuguese traders who brought it from the Americas. It quickly spread because it grows well in poor soils and requires little attention. However, for many years, it was grown mainly as a ornamental plant. Only in the 20th century did its commercial oil production take off. Today, countries like Tanzania have built oilseed processing plants, and organizations like the Tanzania Sunflower Development Trust support farmers with seeds and training..Trim()

.Trim()

For you as a new farmer, understanding this history matters because it shows sunflower is a proven crop with a ready market. The global demand for cooking oil is rising, and Africa imports over 2 million tonnes of edible oil annually. By growing sunflower, you can tap into this demand and earn good money. The crop also improves soil health and can be rotated with maize or beans. So, you are not just growing a plant; you are joining a global industry with deep roots in Africa..Trim()

Sunflower Varieties and Their Uses

Choosing the right sunflower variety is key to a successful harvest. In Africa, you will find two main types: open-pollinated varieties (OPVs) and hybrids. OPVs like 'Record' and 'Kibaha' are cheaper and you can save seeds for next season, but they yield less, around 800-1,200 kg per hectare. Hybrids like 'Pannar 7355' and 'Seed Co's PAN 7049' cost more but yield 1,500-2,500 kg per hectare and are more resistant to diseases. For a beginner, start with a hybrid if you can afford it, because higher yields mean more profit..Trim()

.Trim()

Sunflower varieties also differ in oil content and maturity period. High-oil varieties contain 40-50% oil, while confectionery types have 25-30% oil but larger seeds for eating. In Africa, the most common oilseed varieties include 'Hysun 33', 'Fedha', and 'Kenya Fedha'. These mature in 90-110 days. Confectionery varieties like 'Mammoth' have large black-and-white seeds and are sold for roasting and snacks. If you are near a town, you can sell confectionery seeds directly to consumers at a premium price..Trim()

.Trim()

When selecting a variety, consider your local climate. In dry areas like Dodoma in Tanzania, choose drought-tolerant varieties such as 'Record' or 'Hysun 33'. In cooler highlands like Njoro in Kenya, 'Kenya Fedha' performs well. Always buy certified seeds from registered agro-dealers, not from the local market, to avoid fake seeds. A 10 kg bag of hybrid seed costs about $30-50 and is enough for one hectare. You can also get seeds from research stations like the Tanzania Agricultural Research Institute (TARI) or the Kenya Agricultural and Livestock Research Organization (KALRO)..Trim()

.Trim()

Remember, variety choice affects your entire season. Plant at the right time, and use the recommended spacing of 75 cm between rows and 30 cm between plants. This gives about 44,000 plants per hectare. With good management, a hybrid can give you 2 tonnes per hectare, which at $500 per tonne earns you $1,000. That is a solid return on your investment. So, do your homework before planting..Trim()

Major Sunflower Producing Regions

Sunflower is grown across Africa, but production is concentrated in a few countries. Tanzania is the largest producer in East Africa, with over 1 million hectares planted annually, mainly in the central regions of Dodoma, Singida, and Tabora. In these areas, rainfall is 500-700 mm per year, which is perfect for sunflower. Other important producers include South Africa (Free State and North West provinces), Uganda (Eastern and Northern regions), Zambia (Central and Eastern provinces), and Ethiopia (Oromia and Amhara regions)..Trim()

.Trim()

In West Africa, sunflower is less common but gaining ground in Nigeria (Kano and Kaduna states) and Ghana (Northern Region). The crop is also grown in Malawi, Zimbabwe, and Mozambique. Each region has its own challenges: in Tanzania, pests like birds and diseases like rust are common; in South Africa, drought is a major risk. But farmers in these areas have learned to cope by using early-maturing varieties and planting on time..Trim()

.Trim()

Why does location matter? Because sunflower yields depend heavily on climate and soil. In Dodoma, Tanzania, the sandy loam soils and reliable sunshine give some of the best yields in Africa. In the highlands of Kenya, cooler temperatures slow growth, so you need longer-maturing varieties. In the dry savannah of northern Nigeria, you must plant with the first rains and harvest before the dry season sets in. Knowing your region helps you pick the right variety and management practices..Trim()

.Trim()

If you are in a major producing region, you have advantages: better market access, more agro-dealers, and nearby processing plants. So, before you plant, find out where you can sell. Talk to other farmers, visit local markets, and contact cooperatives. The best regions have strong farmer organizations that help with marketing. Even if you are not in a top region, you can still grow sunflower successfully with good practices..Trim()

Economic and Nutritional Value of Sunflower

Sunflower is a money-making crop. In Africa, a farmer can harvest 1-2 tonnes per hectare, and the farm-gate price ranges from $300 to $600 per tonne, depending on the country and quality. In Tanzania, for example, a tonne of sunflower seeds sells for about TZS 800,000-1,200,000 ($350-520). If you grow one hectare and get 1.5 tonnes, you earn around $600. With costs of about $200 for seeds, fertilizer, and labour, your profit is $400. That is better than many other crops. Plus, you can press the seeds into oil and earn even more: 1 tonne of seeds gives about 350-400 litres of oil, which sells for $1.5-2 per litre, plus the cake for animal feed..Trim()

.Trim()

Nutritionally, sunflower oil is one of the healthiest cooking oils. It is rich in vitamin E and polyunsaturated fats, which help lower cholesterol. The seeds are also a good source of protein (20-25%), fibre, and minerals like magnesium and selenium. In many African households, sunflower oil is used for cooking, and the seed cake is fed to poultry and livestock. The cake contains 30-40% protein, making it an excellent feed for chickens and dairy cows. So, sunflower contributes to both human nutrition and animal health..Trim()

.Trim()

Beyond the farm, sunflower supports jobs in processing, transport, and retail. In Tanzania, the sunflower value chain employs over 2 million people. Women are particularly involved in processing and selling oil. If you start a small oil press, you can create income for yourself and your community. A manual press costs about $500, and a motorized one $2,000. With a motorized press, you can process 100 kg of seeds per hour, producing 35 litres of oil. That is a viable business..Trim()

.Trim()

For you as a learner, the economic value means you should treat sunflower as a business, not just a crop. Keep records of your costs and sales. Look for contracts with oil mills or cooperatives before you plant. And remember, quality matters: clean, dry seeds fetch a higher price. So, invest in proper drying and storage. With good management, sunflower can double your income compared to maize or beans..Trim()

Sunflower Farming Systems: Rainfed vs Irrigated

In Africa, most sunflower is grown under rainfed conditions, meaning it relies entirely on rainfall. This is the cheapest system because you don't need pumps or pipes. However, it is risky: if the rains fail, you lose everything. Rainfed sunflower works best in areas with 500-700 mm of rainfall during the growing season. In Tanzania's Dodoma region, farmers plant with the first rains in December and harvest in April. They use drought-tolerant varieties and plant early to escape dry spells. But yields are often low, around 0.8-1.2 tonnes per hectare, because of unpredictable rains..Trim()

.Trim()

Irrigated sunflower, on the other hand, gives you more control. You can grow sunflower year-round if you have water. In South Africa, many commercial farmers use centre-pivot irrigation to produce 2.5-3 tonnes per hectare. In Kenya, smallholders along rivers use bucket irrigation or drip kits. A simple drip irrigation system for one hectare costs about $1,500-2,500, but it can double your yield. You need about 500-600 mm of water per season, applied through furrows or drip lines. The best time to irrigate is at flowering and seed filling, when water stress hurts yields the most..Trim()

.Trim()

Which system should you choose? If you have reliable rainfall and limited capital, go rainfed. But you must plant on time and use good agronomic practices. If you have access to water (a well, river, or dam), consider irrigated sunflower. It costs more but gives higher and more stable yields. You can also do supplemental irrigation: grow rainfed but give one or two watering at critical stages. This is a middle path that many African farmers use. For example, in Ethiopia's Awash Valley, farmers combine rainfed and irrigated sunflower to spread risk..Trim()

.Trim()

Before you decide, check your water source. A small petrol pump costs $200-300 and can irrigate one hectare. But you also need pipes and labour. Calculate your costs and expected returns. If water is scarce, use mulching to conserve moisture. Remember, sunflower is drought-tolerant but not drought-proof. Even in rainfed systems, you can improve yields by planting in basins or using conservation agriculture. So, choose the system that fits your resources and climate..Trim()

Overview of the Sunflower Value Chain

The sunflower value chain starts with you, the farmer, and ends with the consumer. It includes input supply, production, harvesting, processing, and marketing. As a farmer, you are at the production stage, but you need to understand the whole chain to maximize your profit. The chain begins with seed and fertilizer suppliers. In Africa, companies like Seed Co, Pannar, and local agro-dealers sell certified seeds. You also need fertilizers like DAP or urea, though sunflower can grow with little fertilizer if the soil is good. A 50 kg bag of DAP costs about $30 and can be used on one hectare..Trim()

.Trim()

After production, you harvest and dry the seeds to 10-12% moisture. Then you thresh and clean them. This is where many farmers lose money: poor drying leads to mould and low prices. You can sell your seeds to three main buyers: local traders, cooperatives, or oil mills. Local traders often pay cash but low prices. Cooperatives like the Tanzania Sunflower Farmers Association (TASUFA) negotiate better prices and sometimes provide seeds on credit. Oil mills, such as Mount Meru Soyco in Tanzania, buy in bulk and may contract you before planting. A contract gives you a guaranteed price, which reduces risk..Trim()

.Trim()

Processing is the next step. Small-scale oil pressing is done by manual or motorized presses. A manual press costs $500 and processes 20 kg per hour, yielding 7 litres of oil. Motorized presses cost $2,000-5,000 and process 100-200 kg per hour. The oil is sold in local markets or bottled for supermarkets. The seed cake is sold as animal feed. In Kenya, companies like Bidco Africa and Elianto process sunflower oil on a large scale. They buy seeds from farmers and cooperatives. If you can add value by pressing oil, you earn more. For example, selling 1 tonne of seeds as raw material gives $400, but pressing it into oil gives $700 (350 litres at $2) plus $100 for cake..Trim()

.Trim()

Marketing is the final link. Your success depends on finding reliable buyers. Attend agricultural shows, join farmer groups, and use mobile phones to check prices. In Tanzania, you can use the Tigo Pesa or M-Pesa platforms to receive payments. Also, consider organic certification if you want to export. The global demand for organic sunflower oil is growing. But start small: focus on producing quality seeds and building relationships with buyers. The value chain is long, but every step you control adds to your income..Trim()

Module 2 · Sunflower Botany and Growth Stages

Plant Morphology: Roots, Stems, Leaves, and Head

Sunflower is a deep-rooted crop. The taproot can reach 1.5 to 2 meters in good soil, with lateral roots spreading up to 1 meter. This deep root system helps the plant survive dry spells. In poor, shallow soils, roots may only go 50 cm deep, making the plant vulnerable to drought. To encourage deep rooting, plough to 30 cm before planting. Avoid compacted layers..Trim()

.Trim()

The stem is thick and sturdy, often 2 to 3 meters tall in local varieties, but hybrid varieties may be 1.5 to 2 meters. The stem carries leaves and supports the head. It is green when young, turning brown at maturity. A single plant can have 20 to 30 leaves. Leaves are large, heart-shaped, and arranged alternately on the stem. They capture sunlight for photosynthesis. In dense plantings, lower leaves may die from shading..Trim()

.Trim()

The head is actually a composite flower, made of hundreds of tiny flowers called florets. The outer yellow petals are ray florets; they attract pollinators. The inner disc florets are where seeds form. Each disc floret can produce one seed. A single head may contain 1,000 to 2,000 seeds. The head follows the sun when young (heliotropism), but once mature, it faces east permanently..Trim()

.Trim()

Understanding morphology helps you manage the crop. For example, deep roots mean you can apply fertiliser before planting. Tall stems may need wider spacing to avoid lodging. Large heads need adequate water during flowering. When you scout your field, check root depth by digging a small pit, inspect leaves for pests, and monitor head development. This basic knowledge guides all your decisions..Trim()

Germination and Seedling Development

Sunflower seeds germinate when soil temperature is at least 8Â°C, but optimum is 20-25Â°C. In most of Africa, you can plant with the first rains. Germination takes 5-10 days. The seed absorbs water, the root emerges first, then the shoot. A good seed lot should have over 85% germination. Test by placing 100 seeds in moist cloth for 7 days; count how many sprout..Trim()

.Trim()

Seedling emergence: The hypocotyl (stem below seed leaves) pushes up, pulling the seed coat above ground. The seed leaves (cotyledons) open and turn green. These are not true leaves; they provide food for the young plant. The first true leaves appear about 10-14 days after planting. At this stage, the plant is very vulnerable to pests like cutworms, birds, and rodents. Keep the field clean and monitor daily..Trim()

.Trim()

Spacing matters. For local tall varieties, plant 75 cm between rows and 30 cm within rows. For dwarf hybrids, 60 cm x 25 cm. This gives 40,000 to 60,000 plants per hectare. Thin to one plant per station when seedlings are 10-15 cm tall. Weeding should be done early; weeds compete for water and nutrients. A hoe or hand pulling works well..Trim()

.Trim()

Water: Seedlings need consistent moisture but not waterlogging. If dry spells occur, water lightly every 2-3 days. Avoid overhead irrigation in the evening to prevent fungal diseases. Fertiliser: Apply a starter fertiliser like DAP (18-46-0) at planting, 100 kg per hectare. Place it 5 cm to the side of the seed, not directly on it. This gives seedlings a strong start. By 3-4 weeks, the plant has 4-6 true leaves and enters rapid growth..Trim()

Vegetative Growth and Leaf Area Development

From 4 weeks to about 8 weeks after planting, sunflower grows rapidly. This is the vegetative phase. The stem elongates, and new leaves appear every 2-3 days. Leaf area increases, capturing more sunlight. By the end of this phase, the plant may be 1-2 meters tall and have 20-25 leaves. This is when the plant builds the factory that will produce seeds..Trim()

.Trim()

Nutrient demand is high. Sunflower needs nitrogen (N), phosphorus (P), and potassium (K). A soil test is best. If not available, apply 100 kg/ha of NPK 20-10-10 at 4 weeks, then top-dress with 50 kg/ha urea at 6 weeks. Place fertiliser 10 cm from the plant and cover with soil. Lack of nitrogen causes pale, small leaves; phosphorus deficiency gives purplish stems; potassium shortage causes leaf edge burn..Trim()

.Trim()

Water: The crop needs 500-600 mm of water over the season. During vegetative growth, it uses 3-5 mm per day. In dry areas, irrigate every 7-10 days. Avoid water stress; it reduces leaf area and final yield. Mulching with crop residues helps conserve moisture and suppress weeds..Trim()

.Trim()

Pest and disease scouting: Check weekly for aphids, whiteflies, and leaf-eating caterpillars. Aphids cluster on young leaves; spray with soapy water (10 g soap per litre) or neem extract. Diseases like downy mildew show yellow patches on upper leaf surface and white fungus underneath. Remove infected plants. Keep field free of weeds; they host pests. By 8 weeks, the growing point changes from leaf production to flower bud initiation. This is the shift to reproductive stage..Trim()

Reproductive Phase: Bud Formation to Flowering

At about 8 weeks, a small bud appears at the top of the stem. This is the reproductive phase. The bud grows rapidly, and the head forms. From bud initiation to flowering takes 2-3 weeks. During this time, the plant needs plenty of water and nutrients. Water stress now can reduce seed number and size. Keep soil moist but not soggy..Trim()

.Trim()

Flowering begins when the outer ray petals open. The disc florets open from the outside inward over 5-10 days. Each floret is fertile for a short time. Pollination is mainly by bees, but wind helps too. Encourage bees by avoiding pesticides during flowering. If few bees, you can hand-pollinate by gently rubbing heads together in the morning. Flowering lasts 2-3 weeks. A single head may produce pollen for 1-2 weeks..Trim()

.Trim()

Nutrient needs: At flowering, the plant takes up most of its nitrogen and potassium. If leaves are pale, apply 50 kg/ha urea at early flowering. But avoid excess nitrogen, which delays maturity and reduces oil content. Boron deficiency can cause empty seeds; apply 10 kg/ha borax if soil is deficient. Check with local extension officer..Trim()

.Trim()

Pests: During flowering, watch for head-eating caterpillars (Helicoverpa). They bore into the head and eat seeds. Scout early morning; handpick and destroy. For severe infestations, spray Bacillus thuringiensis (Bt) at 2 g/litre. Diseases like head rot (Sclerotinia) appear as white mould on the back of the head. Remove infected heads. Also, birds can be a problem; use scarecrows or reflective tape. After flowering, petals drop, and seeds begin to fill..Trim()

Seed Development and Maturity Stages

After flowering, seeds develop inside the head. This takes 30-45 days, depending on variety and weather. The stages are: seed filling (milk stage), dough stage, and physiological maturity. At milk stage, seeds are soft and white; you can squeeze them and a milky liquid comes out. At dough stage, seeds are firmer and dough-like. At physiological maturity, seeds are hard and black or striped, and the back of the head turns yellow-brown..Trim()

.Trim()

During seed filling, the plant needs water. Drought stress now reduces seed size and oil content. Irrigate if rainfall is less than 20 mm per week. But too much water near maturity can cause lodging and head rot. Stop irrigation when the back of the head turns brown. Birds are a major threat; they eat seeds as they mature. Use bird scarers, netting, or plant early to avoid peak bird season..Trim()

.Trim()

Harvesting: Harvest when the back of the head is brown and seeds are hard (about 20% moisture). If you leave too long, seeds shatter and are lost. Cut heads with a sickle or knife, leaving 10-15 cm of stem. Dry heads in the sun on a tarpaulin for 3-5 days. Then thresh by beating with a stick or rubbing by hand. Winnow to remove chaff. Store seeds in dry, cool place in sacks. For oil extraction, seeds should be below 10% moisture..Trim()

.Trim()

Yield: A good crop can yield 1.5 to 2.5 tonnes per hectare for local varieties, up to 3 tonnes for hybrids. Record your yields to improve next season. Also, save some seeds from the best plants for planting, but be aware that hybrids may not breed true..Trim()

Growth Stage Identification and Its Management Implications

Knowing the growth stage helps you apply the right management at the right time. Sunflower growth can be divided into vegetative (V) and reproductive (R) stages. Vegetative stages: VE (emergence), V1 (first true leaf), V2 (second leaf), up to Vn (n leaves). Reproductive stages: R1 (bud visible), R2 (bud 2 cm above nearest leaf), R3 (bud 10 cm above), R4 (inflorescence opening), R5 (flowering), R6 (flowering complete), R7 (back of head yellow), R8 (back of head brown), R9 (physiological maturity)..Trim()

.Trim()

Management by stage: At V stages, focus on weeding, thinning, and fertiliser. At V4-V6, apply nitrogen. At R1, ensure adequate water; this is critical for seed number. At R5, protect pollinators and scout for head pests. At R7-R8, reduce irrigation and prepare for harvest. At R9, harvest promptly to avoid shattering..Trim()

.Trim()

Tools for stage identification: A notebook, a measuring tape, and a hand lens. Walk your field weekly. Randomly select 10 plants, record their stage. Calculate the average. This tells you the field's progress. For example, if 50% of plants are at R5, you know flowering is peaking. Then you can time pesticide application (if needed) to avoid harming bees..Trim()

.Trim()

Common mistakes: Applying nitrogen late (after R5) causes excessive leaf growth and delays maturity. Irrigating after R8 causes head rot. Harvesting too early gives low oil and shrivelled seeds. Harvesting too late causes shattering. By matching actions to stages, you maximise yield. Share this knowledge with neighbours; collective timing helps control pests and diseases..Trim()

Choosing the Right Sunflower Variety for Your Region

Choosing the right sunflower variety is the first step to a successful harvest. In Africa, varieties are bred for different climates: some tolerate drought, others resist diseases common in humid areas. You must match the variety to your local rainfall, temperature, and soil. For dry regions like northern Nigeria or Kenya's semi-arid areas, choose short-season varieties such as 'PAN 7351' or 'Sungold' that mature in 85-100 days. These need less water and escape late-season drought. In wetter areas like Uganda or Malawi, select taller, longer-season varieties like 'Record' or 'Hybrid 66' that can reach 120 days but yield more oil. Always check with your local agricultural extension officer or research station for recommended varieties in your district. They often have trial data showing which varieties perform best in your agro-ecological zone. Avoid planting varieties that are not adapted to your area, even if they are cheap or donated. A wrong variety can fail completely. Also consider your market: if you sell to oil processors, high-oil hybrids like 'Hysun 33' are best; for bird feed or local consumption, larger-seeded open-pollinated varieties like 'Fedha' work well. Seed labels should state the variety name, maturity days, and disease resistance. If you save seed from hybrid varieties, the next generation will not be true to type and yields will drop. So buy fresh hybrid seed each season. For open-pollinated varieties, you can save seed but must select the best plants and store properly. Remember, variety choice affects everything: planting date, spacing, fertiliser needs, and pest management. Spend time researching before you buy. Visit a nearby farmer who grows sunflower and ask what works. Your choice today determines your harvest tomorrow..Trim()

Hybrid vs Open-Pollinated Varieties

Sunflower varieties come in two main types: hybrids and open-pollinated (OP). Understanding the difference helps you decide which to plant. Hybrids are created by crossing two parent lines. They give higher yields, more uniform plants, and better disease resistance. For example, hybrid 'PAN 7049' can yield 2.5 tonnes per hectare, while OP 'Local' may yield only 1 tonne. Hybrids also have stronger stalks and uniform maturity, making harvest easier. But hybrid seed is more expensive and must be bought every season. If you save seed from hybrids, the next crop will be a mix of weak and strong plants, reducing yield by 30-50%. Open-pollinated varieties are naturally pollinated and stable. You can save seed from your best plants and replant next season. They are cheaper and good for low-input farming. Examples include 'Fedha', 'Sungold', and many local landraces. However, OP varieties often yield less and vary in height and maturity. They may also be more susceptible to diseases like rust and downy mildew. In Africa, many smallholders use OP varieties because they cannot afford hybrid seed each year. But if you can access hybrid seed through subsidy or credit, it often pays off. Consider your goal: if you sell to a processor demanding uniform seed, hybrids are better. If you grow for home use and want to save seed, OP is practical. You can also plant both: a hybrid for cash and an OP for seed. Always buy certified seed from reputable agrodealers. Avoid grain from the market, as it may be treated with chemicals or be a non-adapted variety. Check the label for germination percentage and purity. Sunflower needs different amounts of water as it grows. From planting to emergence, the seed needs enough moisture to sprout, but too much water can rot it. From emergence to the vegetative stage (about 20 to 40 days after planting), the plant grows leaves and roots. Water use increases. The crop needs about 3-4 mm of water per day. .Trim()

The flowering stage (40 to 70 days after planting) is the most critical. Sunflower uses 5-7 mm of water per day. A shortage now means fewer seeds and smaller heads. Do not let the plant wilt at midday, as this reduces seed set..Trim()

.Trim()

During seed fill (70 to 100 days), water use drops slightly to 4-5 mm per day, but it is still important. Lack of water now causes empty seeds and low oil content. The crop needs about 100-150 mm during this stage. Irrigate every 6-7 days with 25-30 mm if rains fail. As the plant matures and heads turn brown, reduce watering. Too much water late can delay maturity and cause head rot..Trim()

.Trim()

In total, a sunflower crop needs 500-700 mm of water from planting to harvest. In dry African regions like the Sahel or northern Kenya, you must irrigate to reach this. In wetter areas, rainfall may supply most of it. Always check your soil: sandy soils need more frequent, smaller amounts; clay soils hold water longer but can become waterlogged. Irrigation Scheduling Based on Soil Moisture and ET

Irrigation scheduling means deciding when to water and how much. You can do this by checking soil moisture or by using evapotranspiration (ET) data. ET is the water lost from soil and plants to the air. In hot, dry African conditions, ET can be 5-8 mm per day. If you know ET, you know how much water your crop has used and needs to be replaced..Trim()

.Trim()

A simple way to check soil moisture is the feel method. Take a handful of soil from 15 cm deep. If it forms a ball and leaves a slight moisture mark on your hand, it has enough water. .Trim()

You can also use a simple tensiometer, a tool that measures how tightly soil holds water. Install it at 20 cm depth. When the reading is between 30 and 50 centibars (cb), it's time to irrigate. .Trim()

ET-based scheduling uses local weather data. Many African meteorological departments provide daily ET estimates. If ET is 6 mm today, your crop used 6 mm of water. .Trim()

Combine both methods: check soil moisture to confirm ET estimates. Keep a simple record: date, ET, rainfall, irrigation amount. After a season, you'll know your fields better. Sunflower hates waterlogged soil. It can survive short floods, but if water stands for more than 2-3 days, roots suffocate, and the plant turns yellow, wilts, and dies. Pest resistance happens when insects survive a pesticide that used to kill them. It develops when you use the same chemical repeatedly. For example, if you spray lambda-cyhalothrin every week for head borers, within two seasons many borers will be resistant. To slow resistance, rotate insecticides with different modes of action. The mode of action is how the chemical kills the pest. Group 3A (lambda-cyhalothrin) affects the nervous system; Group 11 (Bt) damages the gut; Group 5 (spinetoram) affects the nervous system differently. Never use the same group more than twice in a season. Also, rotate with non-chemical methods like Bt and neem. If you use Bt, you reduce the selection pressure for chemical resistance..Trim()

Determining Harvest Maturity: Moisture and Seed Color

Sunflower is ready to harvest when the back of the flower head turns from green to yellow-brown and the seeds are plump with a moisture content of about 20-25%. In many African countries like Tanzania, Nigeria, and South Africa, sunflower is harvested from May to July in the southern hemisphere and October to December in the northern hemisphere. Delaying harvest after the head turns brown can lead to losses of up to 30% due to shattering. So, monitor your field daily as the heads turn downward.
