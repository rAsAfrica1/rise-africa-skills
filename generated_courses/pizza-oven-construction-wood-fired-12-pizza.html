Overview of Wood-Fired Oven Types

Wood-fired pizza ovens come in three main types: brick, cob, and metal. For a 12-inch pizza, you need an oven with a cooking floor of at least 60 cm x 60 cm. In Africa, brick ovens are most common because clay bricks are locally made and cheap. A simple brick oven uses firebricks for the floor (they withstand high heat) and regular clay bricks for the dome. You can build one for about 200 USD if you gather materials yourself. Cob ovens use a mix of clay, sand, and straw. They are cheap (under 100 USD) but crack easily in heavy rain, so they need a roof. Metal ovens are made from steel drums or custom welded boxes. They heat fast but lose heat quickly, so you need more wood. For a beginner, a brick oven with a firebrick floor and a clay dome is best. It holds heat for hours and can bake 12-inch pizzas in 90 seconds. In many African towns, you can find firebricks at building supply stores or pottery factories. If not, use dense clay bricks and line the floor with a layer of sand mixed with clay to protect from thermal shock. The dome shape matters: a low dome (about 40 cm high) reflects heat onto the pizza. A high dome wastes heat. The Science of Wood-Fired Cooking

Wood-fired cooking works because of three things: high heat, radiant heat, and wood smoke. When you burn dry hardwood like acacia or eucalyptus, the oven floor reaches 400Â°C to 500Â°C. A 12-inch pizza cooks in 90 seconds at this temperature. The heat comes from three directions: the fire on one side, the hot floor below, and the dome above reflecting heat back down. This is called radiant heat. Unlike a gas oven, wood fire also produces smoke that flavors the crust. The key is to keep the fire small and to the side. You want a rolling flame, not a roaring bonfire. Use small pieces of wood, about 30 cm long and 5 cm thick. Hardwoods burn longer and hotter than softwoods like pine. In Africa, acacia, mopane, and eucalyptus are excellent. Avoid treated wood or painted wood â they release toxic fumes. The oven needs to be preheated for 45 to 60 minutes. Start with a small fire, then add wood gradually. The dome should turn white-hot (about 450Â°C). You know it's ready when the soot burns off the dome. For a 12-inch pizza, the floor temperature should be around 350Â°C to 400Â°C. If the floor is too hot, the bottom burns before the top cooks. If too cold, the crust is pale and dry. Use an infrared thermometer to check. You can also throw a pinch of flour on the floor â if it browns in 2 seconds, it's ready. The science also involves moisture. Pizza dough contains water. In a hot oven, that water turns to steam and puffs the crust. That's why you get air pockets. A wood-fired oven with a low dome traps steam better than a tall one. Finally, remember that heat moves from hot to cold. The oven walls absorb heat and release it slowly. That's why you can bake bread after the pizza fire dies. Site Selection and Foundation Requirements

Understanding Oven Dimensions for 12-Inch Pizzas

A 12-inch pizza is about 30 cm in diameter. Your oven must be bigger than that to allow for turning and airflow. The cooking floor should be at least 60 cm x 60 cm. If too low, you cannot fit a pizza peel or turn the pizza. The oven mouth (the opening) should be about 30 cm wide and 20 cm high. This is wide enough to slide a 12-inch pizza in and out. The mouth should be at the same level as the floor. The ratio of mouth height to dome height is important: mouth height should be about half the dome height. So if dome is 40 cm, mouth is 20 cm. This creates a good draw for smoke and heat. The floor should be made of firebricks laid flat. Each firebrick is about 23 cm x 11 cm x 6 cm. You need about 12 to 15 firebricks for a 60 cm x 60 cm floor. If you cannot get firebricks, use dense clay bricks and cover with a 1 cm layer of clay and sand mix. The dome is built from bricks or cob. For a 60 cm floor, the dome diameter at the base is about 70 cm. The walls should be 10 cm thick for good heat retention. The chimney opening should be at the front of the dome, above the mouth. A 10 cm diameter metal pipe works well. The total oven footprint (including walls) is about 90 cm x 90 cm. This fits on a 1 meter x 1 meter base. For a 12-inch pizza, you can bake 2 pizzas at a time if your floor is 60 cm x 60 cm, but leave space between them. Do not overcrowd. The oven should also have a landing (a flat area in front of the mouth) to rest the peel. Make it 30 cm deep. These dimensions are tested and work well in African conditions. Planning Your Build: Timeline and Budget

Building a wood-fired pizza oven for 12-inch pizzas takes about 3 to 5 days of work, spread over a week. Day 1: gather materials and build the foundation. You need cement, sand, gravel, and water. This takes 4 to 6 hours. Day 3: insulate the base and lay the firebrick floor. Mix clay and sand for insulation. Lay firebricks flat with a thin clay slip. This takes 3 to 4 hours. Day 4: build the dome. This is the slowest part. Use a brick or cob mix. Shape the dome using a sand mound as a guide. Cover with a layer of clay and straw for insulation. This takes 6 to 8 hours. Day 5: build the chimney and let the oven dry. Do not fire it immediately. Let it air-dry for 3 days, then light small fires for 3 days to cure the clay. A full cure takes a week. So total timeline: 1 week of building plus 1 week of curing. Budget: For a basic brick oven, expect to spend 150 to 250 USD. Also, check local regulations â some areas require permits for outdoor fires. Finally, keep a contingency of 20% for unexpected costs.
