Module 1 · Understanding the Tissue & Paper Business in Africa

Why Tissue and Paper Products Sell in African Markets

Tissue and paper products sell because they are cheap, needed every day, and used by everyone. In most African cities, a family of five uses at least 4 rolls of toilet paper per week. That is 16 rolls a month. If you sell each roll at 50 cents, that family spends $8 a month on toilet paper alone. Multiply that by 100 families in your neighbourhood and you have a $800 monthly market. Add facial tissues, kitchen towels, and serviettes, and the numbers grow fast..Trim()

Population growth is another reason. Africa's population is expected to reach 2.5 billion by 2050. More people means more homes, schools, offices, and events. Each new home needs toilet paper. Each new school needs serviettes for lunch programs. Each new office needs facial tissues and hand towels. The demand is not going away..Trim()

.Trim()

Urbanisation also drives sales. When people move from villages to cities, they change habits. In rural areas, some people use water or leaves. In cities, they buy tissue. A 2022 study in Kenya found that urban households spend 3 times more on tissue products than rural households. If you set up near a growing town or city, you are in the right place..Trim()

Finally, tissue is a fast-moving consumer good. People do not buy one roll and keep it for a year. They buy every week or every month. That means repeat customers. If your product is good and your price is fair, customers come back. You do not need to find new customers every day. You just need to keep the ones you have. This repeat business is what makes tissue a stable income, even in tough economic times..Trim()

Toilet roll is the most common tissue product. It comes in different ply (layers). 1-ply is thin and cheap, used in public toilets and low-income homes. 2-ply is softer and stronger, used in most homes. 3-ply is premium, used in hotels and offices. In Africa, 2-ply sells best because it balances quality and price. A standard roll has 200 to 300 sheets. You can sell single rolls or packs of 4, 10, or 24. Sourcing Raw Materials Locally vs. Importing

You need about 1.2 kilograms of waste paper to make 1 kilogram of tissue. So for 100 kilograms of tissue, you need 120 kilograms of waste paper. That costs about $18 to $30. Costing Raw Materials per Roll: A Simple Formula

To know if you're making a profit, you must calculate raw material cost per finished roll. Start with the cost per tonne of jumbo rolls. This depends on the weight of your finished roll. For example, a standard toilet roll weighs 100 grams (0.1 kg). So from 1 kg, you get 10 rolls. But there's waste: trimming, core, and startup losses. Assume 5% waste, so you get 9.5 rolls per kg. Therefore, raw material cost per roll = 0.70 / 9.5 = USD 0.074 (about 7.4 cents)..Trim()

Now add other direct costs: core (USD 0.005 per roll), packaging (USD 0.01 per roll), and labor (USD 0.01 per roll). Total variable cost = 0.074 + 0.005 + 0.01 + 0.01 = USD 0.099 per roll. Calculating Cost per Roll: Materials, Labour, and Overheads

To make a profit, you must know exactly what each roll of tissue costs you to produce. Start by calculating your material cost per roll. Suppose you buy waste paper at 10,000 shillings per tonne. If you produce 1,000 rolls from one tonne, your paper cost per roll is 10 shillings. Add chemicals (bleach, dye) at 2,000 shillings per tonne, so 2 shillings per roll. Packaging: a plastic wrapper costs 1 shilling per roll. Total material cost = 13 shillings per roll..Trim()

.Trim()

Next, calculate labour. If you pay two workers 15,000 shillings each per day, and they produce 1,000 rolls per day, labour cost per roll = (2 x 15,000) / 1,000 = 30 shillings per roll. If you work yourself, decide a fair wage and include it. Overheads include electricity, water, rent, machine maintenance, and transport. Suppose these total 20,000 shillings per day for 1,000 rolls, so 20 shillings per roll. Total cost per roll = 13 + 30 + 20 = 63 shillings..Trim()

.Trim()

Now you know your break-even price. Any price above 63 shillings gives profit. But remember, production isn't always perfect: some rolls may be defective. If 5% are wasted, your effective cost per good roll rises. To account, divide total cost by (1 - waste rate). With 5% waste, cost per good roll = 63 / 0.95 = 66.3 shillings. Always round up to 67 shillings for safety..Trim()

.Trim()

Use a simple notebook to record daily expenses. At the end of each day, sum materials, labour, and overheads, then divide by the number of good rolls produced. This gives your true cost per roll. Update it weekly because material prices change. Knowing your cost per roll is the foundation for all pricing decisions..Trim()

.Trim()

At the end of the week, total all Money In and all Money Out. The difference is your profit or loss. .Trim()
