Types of Onions: Bulb, Bunching, and Shallots

There are three main types of onions you can grow in Africa: bulb onions, bunching onions (also called spring onions or scallions), and shallots. Bulb onions are the most common, forming a large, round bulb. They are used in cooking and can be stored for months. Popular varieties include Red Creole, Texas Grano, and Bombay Red. Bunching onions are harvested young for their green leaves and mild flavor. They are ready in 60-80 days and sell well in urban markets. Shallots are small, mild-flavored bulbs that grow in clusters. They are prized in West African cuisine, especially in Ghana and Nigeria. Each type has different management. Bulb onions need well-drained soil, full sun, and 4-6 months to mature. Bunching onions can be grown densely and harvested earlier. Shallots are planted from small bulbs and multiply. For beginners, start with bulb onions because of high demand and storage life. But if you have a market for fresh greens, bunching onions give quick cash. Shallots fetch premium prices but have a smaller market. Choose based on your local demand and climate. In dry areas, bulb onions do well with irrigation. In humid areas, bunching onions are less prone to rot. Always buy certified seed or sets from reputable suppliers. Avoid using onions from the market as seedâthey may carry diseases. Test a small plot before scaling up..Trim()

Basic Botany of the Onion Plant

Understanding the botany of the onion plant helps you grow it better. Onions (Allium cepa) are biennial plants, meaning they complete their life cycle in two years. In the first year, they grow leaves and form a bulb. In the second year, they flower and produce seeds. The plant has a shallow root system, mostly in the top 30cm of soil, so it needs frequent, light irrigation. The leaves are tubular and hollow, and they manufacture food through photosynthesis. The bulb is actually a modified underground stem made of fleshy scales. When days get longer (more than 12 hours of daylight), the plant starts forming bulbs. This is why day length matters: varieties are classified as short-day, intermediate, or long-day. In Africa, short-day varieties like Red Creole are best for equatorial regions. The flower stalk emerges from the center of the bulb, bearing a cluster of small white flowers. Seeds are small, black, and angular. Onions also have a pungent smell due to sulfur compounds. These compounds are released when cells are damaged, making you cry. Pests like thrips and diseases like purple blotch attack the leaves. Proper spacing (10-15cm between plants) ensures good air circulation. Crop rotation with cereals or legumes helps prevent soil-borne diseases. Knowing these basics will guide your management decisions..Trim()

Career Opportunities in Onion Farming

Onion farming offers many career paths beyond just growing. You can become a seed producer, a nursery specialist, or a pest management expert. Seed production is lucrative: a kilogram of certified onion seed can sell for $50-$100. To produce seed, you need to grow onions for two years and manage flowering. Nursery operators raise seedlings for farmers who prefer transplants. They can earn $0.02 per seedling, and with 100,000 seedlings, that's $2,000 per cycle. As a farm manager, you can oversee large onion farms for absentee owners, earning a monthly salary. Agricultural extension officers advise farmers on best practices. Trading and brokerage: buy onions from farmers and sell to urban markets or processors. A trader can make a 20-30% margin. Processing: turn onions into powder, paste, or pickles. This requires a grinder, dryer, and packaging machine. Storage: build ventilated stores and charge farmers for storage. Transport: own a truck and move onions from farms to markets. Agribusiness consultants help farmers with business plans and market linkages. If you love teaching, become a trainer or write guides. Many NGOs and government programs hire onion experts. You can also start a cooperative to aggregate produce and negotiate better prices. With skills in record-keeping and marketing, you can scale any of these. The key is to start small, learn, and reinvest. Onion farming is not just a jobâit's a business ecosystem with room for everyone..Trim()

Module 3 · Soil Preparation and Fertility

Soil Requirements for Onions

Onions grow best in loose, well-drained soil that is rich in organic matter. The ideal soil is sandy loam or loam, which allows the bulbs to expand easily and prevents waterlogging. Avoid heavy clay soils because they hold water and can cause bulb rot. If your soil is clay, you must improve it by adding compost, sand, or planting on raised beds. Onions are shallow-rooted, so they need soil that is at least 30 cm deep and free of rocks and hard clods. Before planting, test your soil drainage: dig a hole 30 cm deep, fill it with water, and see how long it takes to drain. If water remains after 2 hours, drainage is poor. In that case, you must plant on raised beds or install drainage channels. Onions also prefer a soil pH between 6.0 and 6.8. If your soil is too acidic (below 5.5), add agricultural lime at 2-4 tonnes per hectare. If too alkaline (above 7.5), add gypsum or elemental sulphur. In many parts of Africa, soils are sandy and low in nutrients. You can still grow onions by adding plenty of compost and fertilizing regularly. Always choose a field that has not grown onions, garlic, or leeks for at least 2-3 years to avoid soil-borne diseases like white rot. Good soil preparation is the foundation of a healthy onion crop..Trim()

Soil Testing and pH Adjustment

Before you plant onions, you must know your soil's pH and nutrient levels. You can buy a simple soil test kit from an agricultural supply store or send a sample to a local extension office. To take a sample, use a clean spade to dig 15-20 cm deep in at least 10 spots across your field. Mix the soil in a bucket, then take about 500g for testing. If you cannot afford a lab test, you can use a DIY pH test kit with colour indicators. Onions grow best at pH 6.0-6.8. If your soil is too acidic (pH below 6.0), apply agricultural lime. The amount depends on your soil type: for sandy soil, apply 2 tonnes per hectare; for loam, 3 tonnes; for clay, 4 tonnes. Spread lime evenly and mix it into the top 15 cm of soil at least one month before planting. If your soil is too alkaline (pH above 7.5), add gypsum at 2 tonnes per hectare or elemental sulphur at 500 kg per hectare. You can also add organic matter like compost or manure, which helps buffer pH. Remember that onions are sensitive to high acidity, which can cause poor growth and small bulbs. Adjusting pH takes time, so test early and correct before planting. In Africa, many soils are acidic due to heavy rainfall and erosion. Regular testing every 2-3 years will help you maintain the right pH for good yields..Trim()

Organic Matter and Compost Incorporation

Organic matter is the key to healthy onion soil. It improves soil structure, holds water, and provides nutrients. The best organic matter for onions is well-rotted compost or manure. Fresh manure can burn plants and introduce weeds, so always compost it for at least 3 months. To make compost, pile layers of dry leaves, grass, kitchen scraps, and animal manure. Turn the pile every 2 weeks and keep it moist. In 2-3 months, it will turn dark and crumbly. Apply compost at 10-20 tonnes per hectare (about 1-2 kg per square metre). Spread it evenly over the soil and mix it into the top 15-20 cm with a hoe or fork. If you don't have compost, you can use well-rotted cow, goat, or poultry manure. Poultry manure is rich in nitrogen, so use it at 5 tonnes per hectare. In sandy soils, organic matter helps retain water and nutrients; in clay soils, it improves drainage. You can also grow green manure crops like cowpeas or mucuna, then plough them into the soil before planting onions. This adds nitrogen and organic matter. Remember that onions have shallow roots, so they benefit from a loose, crumbly soil. Incorporate organic matter at least 2-3 weeks before planting to allow it to settle. In Africa, where synthetic fertilizers may be expensive, compost is a cheap and effective way to boost soil fertility and get bigger onion bulbs..Trim()

Fertilizer Recommendations: N-P-K

Onions need balanced fertilizers, but they are heavy feeders of nitrogen (N), phosphorus (P), and potassium (K). A common recommendation is to apply a basal fertilizer at planting, such as NPK 10-20-10 or 12-24-12, at 300-400 kg per hectare. This provides phosphorus for root development and potassium for bulb formation. Nitrogen is needed for leaf growth, but too much nitrogen late in the season can cause thick necks and poor storage. Apply half of the nitrogen at planting and the other half as a side-dress 4-6 weeks later. Use ammonium sulphate (21% N) or urea (46% N) for side-dressing. For example, apply 100 kg of ammonium sulphate per hectare at side-dressing. Potassium is crucial for bulb size and quality; if your soil is low in potassium, apply muriate of potash (0-0-60) at 100-150 kg per hectare. In sandy soils, split applications are better because nutrients leach away. You can also use organic fertilizers like poultry manure or compost tea. A simple rule: for every 10 tonnes of compost, you get about 50 kg N, 20 kg P, and 50 kg K. Always base your fertilizer application on soil test results. Over-fertilizing can burn plants and pollute water. Under-fertilizing leads to small bulbs. In Africa, where fertilizers may be costly, combine small amounts of synthetic fertilizer with compost for best results. Apply fertilizers when the soil is moist, and water after application to dissolve nutrients..Trim()

Micronutrient Management

Onions need small amounts of micronutrients like zinc, boron, manganese, copper, and iron. These are needed in tiny quantities but are essential for healthy growth. Deficiencies can cause poor bulb formation, yellowing leaves, and reduced yields. In many African soils, zinc and boron are the most common deficiencies. Zinc deficiency shows as yellowing between the veins of younger leaves and stunted growth. To correct, apply zinc sulphate at 20-30 kg per hectare before planting, or spray a 0.5% zinc sulphate solution on leaves. Boron deficiency causes hollow stems, thick necks, and brown spots on bulbs. Apply borax at 10-15 kg per hectare, but be careful: too much boron is toxic. Manganese deficiency causes grey speck on leaves; spray with manganese sulphate at 0.5%. Copper deficiency leads to pale, twisted leaves; apply copper sulphate at 10 kg per hectare. Iron deficiency shows as yellow leaves with green veins; spray with chelated iron. The best way to manage micronutrients is through organic matter. Compost and manure contain a wide range of micronutrients. Regular addition of compost reduces the need for synthetic micronutrients. If you suspect a deficiency, send leaf samples to a lab for testing. In Africa, where lab services may be limited, you can use visual symptoms as a guide. Always follow recommended rates; excess micronutrients can harm plants and soil. Foliar sprays are quick fixes, but soil application with organic matter is a long-term solution..Trim()

Raised Bed and Flatbed Preparation

Onions can be grown on flatbeds or raised beds, depending on your soil and climate. Flatbeds are common in dry areas with good drainage. To prepare a flatbed, plough or dig the soil to a depth of 20-30 cm, remove rocks and weeds, and level the surface. Make beds 1-1.2 metres wide so you can reach the centre from both sides. Leave paths of 30-40 cm between beds. Raised beds are better in wet areas or where drainage is poor. They are also easier to manage and reduce soil compaction. To make a raised bed, dig soil from the paths and pile it onto the bed to a height of 15-30 cm. Mix in compost and fertilizer. The bed should be 1-1.2 metres wide and any length. Raised beds warm up faster in cool weather and drain excess water. In Africa, raised beds are especially useful during the rainy season. For either bed type, break up clods to a fine tilth so onion seeds or seedlings can emerge easily. Onions are often transplanted, so prepare the bed 2-3 weeks before transplanting. Apply a pre-planting fertilizer and mix it into the top 10 cm. Water the beds thoroughly a day before planting. If you use drip irrigation, install lines along the beds. Mulching with straw or grass after planting helps conserve moisture and suppress weeds. Good bed preparation saves labour and leads to uniform onion stands..Trim()

Module 5 · Irrigation and Water Management

Water Requirements at Different Growth Stages

Onions need different amounts of water at each stage of growth. Getting this right is the difference between a good harvest and a failed crop. In the first 3 weeks after transplanting or seeding, onion roots are shallow and weak. They need light, frequent watering to keep the top 15 cm of soil moist. In dry African conditions, this means watering every 2 days if there is no rain. A simple test: stick your finger 3 cm into the soil. If it feels dry, water. .Trim()

.Trim()

From week 4 to week 8, onions grow fast and start forming bulbs. This is when they need the most water. The roots reach about 30 cm deep, so you need to wet the soil to that depth. In sandy soils, water every 2-3 days; in clay soils, every 4-5 days. A good rule: apply 25-30 mm of water per week (about 25-30 litres per square metre). If you use a watering can, that's 2.5 to 3 full 10-litre cans per square metre per week. .Trim()

.Trim()

When bulbs start to swell (week 9 to week 12), keep the soil evenly moist but not waterlogged. Too much water now can split bulbs or cause rot. Reduce watering to 20-25 mm per week. In hot, dry areas like the Sahel, you may need to water every 2 days, but always check the soil first. .Trim()

.Trim()

In the final 2-3 weeks before harvest, stop watering completely. This allows the necks to dry and the bulbs to mature. If you keep watering, onions will rot in storage. In most African regions, you can stop watering when about half the tops fall over. .Trim()

.Trim()

Remember: these are general guidelines. Always watch your crop and the weather. A rain gauge (costing about $5) helps you track rainfall. If you get 10 mm of rain, you can skip one watering. Overwatering wastes money and can leach nutrients. Underwatering stunts growth. The key is to check soil moisture regularly and adjust..Trim()

Drip Irrigation Systems for Onions

Drip irrigation is the most efficient way to water onions. It saves up to 60% water compared to furrow or sprinkler systems. In Africa, small-scale farmers can use simple drip kits that cost from $50 to $200 for a 100 square metre plot. These kits include a tank, filter, main line, and drip tapes with emitters. .Trim()

.Trim()

To set up drip for onions, first prepare your beds. Onions grow best on raised beds about 1 metre wide. Lay one or two drip lines per bed, spaced 30 cm apart. The emitters should be spaced 20-30 cm along the line. This gives each onion plant access to water. You need a water source at least 1 metre above the field to create pressure, or a small pump. A 200-litre drum raised on blocks works well. .Trim()

.Trim()

Drip irrigation works by releasing water slowly, drop by drop, right at the plant roots. This keeps the soil moist but not soaked. It also reduces weeds because only the onion row gets water. You should run the system for 30-60 minutes per day, depending on your soil and weather. In sandy soil, run it twice a day for 20 minutes. In clay soil, once a day for 40 minutes. Always check that water reaches 20 cm deep. .Trim()

.Trim()

Maintenance is critical. Drip emitters clog easily, especially if your water has sand or dirt. Use a filter and clean it weekly. Flush the lines once a month by opening the end caps. If you see dry spots, check for clogged emitters. In many African countries, you can buy drip tape from agro-dealers or NGOs like KickStart or SolarGen. .Trim()

.Trim()

Drip irrigation also allows you to apply fertiliser through the system (fertigation). Use water-soluble fertilisers and inject them into the tank. This saves labour and gives nutrients directly to the roots. With drip, you can grow onions even in dry seasons, as long as you have water. It is a smart investment for any serious onion farmer..Trim()

Sprinkler and Furrow Irrigation Methods

Sprinkler and furrow irrigation are common for onions in Africa, but they need careful management. Sprinklers spray water like rain. They are good for sandy soils and flat land. You can use a small portable sprinkler connected to a hose or a pump. A basic impact sprinkler costs about $10 and covers a 10-metre circle. Run it for 30-45 minutes to apply 10-15 mm of water. .Trim()

.Trim()

For onions, sprinkler irrigation is best early in the season when plants are small. But as onions grow, sprinklers can cause problems. Wet leaves encourage fungal diseases like downy mildew. Also, sprinklers waste water through evaporation, especially in hot afternoons. So, irrigate early morning or late evening. In windy areas, sprinklers are uneven. You may need to move them around. .Trim()

.Trim()

Furrow irrigation is cheaper but less efficient. You make small channels (furrows) between onion rows and let water flow. This works well in clay soils and where water is plentiful. But it uses a lot of water and can cause soil erosion. For onions, furrows should be shallow (10-15 cm deep) and water should flow slowly. Never let water touch the onion stems directly, as this causes rot. .Trim()

.Trim()

To use furrow irrigation, dig furrows 30-40 cm apart. Let water run for 20-30 minutes, then stop. Check that water has soaked 30 cm deep. In sandy soil, water moves fast, so you may need shorter runs. In clay, water moves slowly, so run longer but less often. .Trim()

.Trim()

Both methods require level land. If your field slopes, water will collect at the bottom and starve the top. You can use basins or borders to control flow. Also, always monitor soil moisture. A simple tensiometer (costing $30) or a shovel can tell you if water reached the roots. Remember, overhead irrigation can wash away seeds or young plants. So, for the first 2 weeks after sowing, use a gentle sprinkle or hand watering. .Trim()

.Trim()

In many African countries, furrow irrigation is common in river valleys. But with climate change, water is scarce. Combining furrow with mulching can reduce water use by 30%. Mulch (dry grass or plastic) covers the soil and slows evaporation. This is a low-cost way to improve any irrigation method..Trim()

Monitoring Soil Moisture

Monitoring soil moisture is the most important skill for onion irrigation. You cannot guess. You must check. The simplest tool is your hand. Dig a small hole 15 cm deep near the onion roots. Take a handful of soil and squeeze it. If it forms a ball and leaves a slight moisture mark on your palm, it is perfect. If it crumbles, it is too dry â water now. If it drips water, it is too wet â stop watering. .Trim()

.Trim()

For more accuracy, use a tensiometer. This is a tube with a gauge that measures how hard roots must work to pull water. For onions, keep the reading between 20 and 30 centibars (cb). If it reads above 40 cb, water immediately. Tensiometers cost about $30-50 and last several seasons. They are available from irrigation suppliers or online. .Trim()

.Trim()

Another tool is a soil moisture meter. These are cheap ($10-20) and have a probe you push into the soil. They give a reading from 1 (dry) to 10 (wet). For onions, aim for 6-7. But these meters are not very accurate in clay or salty soils. Use them as a guide, not a rule. .Trim()

.Trim()

You can also use a simple gypsum block or a homemade device: a wooden stick painted with different colours. When the stick is inserted, the colour changes with moisture. But the hand method is free and reliable if you practice. .Trim()

.Trim()

Monitor at least twice a week, and always before and after irrigation. Keep a notebook. Write down the date, soil moisture, and how much you watered. After a few weeks, you will see patterns. For example, you might learn that in your sandy soil, onions need water every 2 days in hot weather. .Trim()

.Trim()

Also, watch the plants. Onions show stress when they need water: leaves turn a dull grey-green and the tips start to fold. If you see this, water immediately. But do not wait for stress signs â they reduce yield. .Trim()

.Trim()

In Africa, where water is often scarce, monitoring helps you avoid overwatering. Overwatering wastes water, leaches nutrients, and causes root diseases. Underwatering stunts bulbs. The goal is to keep soil moisture steady. A simple rain gauge ($5) also helps you track rainfall. If you get 10 mm of rain, you can skip one irrigation. .Trim()

.Trim()

Finally, remember that soil type matters. Sandy soil dries out fast, so check daily. Clay soil holds water longer, so check every 2-3 days. Loam is in between. Adjust your monitoring to your soil..Trim()

Water Quality and Salinity Issues

Not all water is good for onions. Onions are sensitive to salt. If you irrigate with salty water, your onions will be small, and bulbs may rot. In many African drylands, well water or river water can be salty. You need to test your water before using it. .Trim()

.Trim()

A simple electrical conductivity (EC) meter costs about $30. It measures salt level in water. For onions, the EC should be below 1.2 dS/m (deciSiemens per metre). If it is 1.2 to 2.0, onions will grow but yield may drop. Above 2.0, onions will struggle. If you cannot afford a meter, send a water sample to a government agricultural office or a university lab. They often test for free or low cost. .Trim()

.Trim()

If your water is slightly salty (EC 1.2-2.0), you can still use it with care. Apply extra water to leach salts below the roots. But do not overwater, as this can raise the water table and bring more salt up. Use drip irrigation, which keeps salts away from the seed. Also, add organic matter like compost or manure. It helps the soil hold nutrients and reduces salt damage. .Trim()

.Trim()

Another issue is water with high sodium. Sodium makes soil sticky and hard. You can add gypsum (calcium sulfate) to the soil. Gypsum costs about $20 per 50 kg bag. Apply 200-500 kg per hectare and mix into the top 15 cm. This replaces sodium with calcium. .Trim()

.Trim()

Dirty water is also a problem. Water from rivers or ponds may contain weed seeds, bacteria, or silt. Silt clogs drip emitters. Always filter water. Use a simple sand filter or a cloth filter. For sprinklers, dirty water can block nozzles. Clean them regularly. .Trim()

.Trim()

In coastal areas, seawater intrusion can make wells salty. Never use water with EC above 3.0 dS/m for onions. If you have no other water, consider growing salt-tolerant crops like barley instead. .Trim()

.Trim()

Finally, test your soil too. If your soil is already salty, you may need to flush it with fresh water before planting. Onions grow best in soil with EC below 1.0 dS/m. You can buy a soil test kit for about $15. .Trim()

.Trim()

In summary: test your water, use organic matter, and choose the right irrigation method. If salinity is a big problem, talk to your extension officer. They can help you find solutions like gypsum or better water sources..Trim()

Water is precious in Africa. Every drop counts. Onion farmers can save water and still get a good harvest by using simple conservation methods. The first step is to reduce evaporation. Mulching is the easiest way. Spread a 5 cm layer of dry grass, straw, or plastic mulch around your onions. This keeps the soil cool and cuts water loss by up to 50%. In dry areas, mulch also stops weeds. You can use black plastic mulch, which costs about $30 for a 100-metre roll, or free grass from your farm. .Trim()

.Trim()

Second, water at the right time. Early morning or late evening is best. Midday watering loses up to 30% to evaporation. Also, water deeply but less often. This encourages deep roots. For onions, apply 25-30 mm per week, but split into two or three applications. Do not sprinkle a little every day â that makes shallow roots that dry out fast. .Trim()

.Trim()

Third, use drip irrigation. As we learned, drip saves up to 60% water. If you cannot afford drip, use a watering can or hose with a shut-off nozzle. Never flood your field. .Trim()

.Trim()

Fourth, capture rainwater. In many African regions, rain is seasonal. You can dig small basins or ponds to catch runoff. A 5,000-litre tank can store enough water for a small plot during dry spells. You can also use rooftops to collect rain. A simple gutter system costs about $50. .Trim()

.Trim()

Fifth, fix leaks. Check your pipes, hoses, and tanks for leaks. A leaking pipe can waste hundreds of litres a day. Also, keep your irrigation system clean. Clogged emitters waste water because you run the system longer. .Trim()

.Trim()

Sixth, use soil moisture monitoring. As we discussed, do not water if the soil is already moist. A $5 rain gauge helps you skip watering after rain. .Trim()

.Trim()

Seventh, plant windbreaks. Strong winds dry out soil and plants. A row of tall grasses or trees around your field reduces wind speed and water loss. .Trim()

.Trim()

Eighth, choose drought-tolerant onion varieties. Some varieties like 'Red Creole' or 'Texas Grano' handle heat better. Ask your local agro-dealer. .Trim()

.Trim()

Finally, reuse water. Greywater from your kitchen (without soap) can be used on onions if you filter it. But avoid water with chemicals. .Trim()

By combining these methods, you can cut water use by half. That means you can grow more onions with the same water, or save water for other crops. In the face of climate change, conservation is not just good for the environment â it is good for your pocket..Trim()

Module 8 · Fertilization and Nutrient Management

Nutrient Uptake Patterns in Onions

Onions have a shallow root system, mostly in the top 15-20 cm of soil, so they need nutrients close by and available. Uptake is slow for the first 30-40 days after transplanting or sowing. Then from about day 40 to day 80, the plants take up nutrients rapidly as leaves and bulbs form. After day 80, uptake slows down as bulbs mature. For a 1-hectare crop aiming for 20-30 tonnes of bulbs, onions remove roughly 100-120 kg nitrogen (N), 40-50 kg phosphorus (P2O5), and 80-100 kg potassium (K2O). They also need calcium, magnesium, sulphur, and micronutrients like zinc and boron. In African soils, nitrogen and phosphorus are often low, while potassium may be adequate in some volcanic soils but low in sandy soils. A soil test before planting is your best guide. If you cannot test, a common recommendation for onion in West Africa is 100 kg N, 60 kg P2O5, and 80 kg K2O per hectare, split into 2-3 applications. Onions are sensitive to salt, so do not dump all fertilizer at once. Also, onions have a small root volume, so banding fertilizer 5-10 cm beside the plant row is better than broadcasting. During bulb formation, potassium and sulphur are critical for bulb size, firmness, and storage life. Too much nitrogen late in the season delays maturity and causes thick necks, which rot in storage. So match your fertilizer to the uptake curve: light at start, heavy during rapid growth, and stop nitrogen 3-4 weeks before harvest. This pattern works whether you use organic or mineral fertilizers. In sandy soils, nutrients leach quickly, so split applications are even more important. In heavy clay, roots may struggle, so ensure good soil structure. Always water after applying fertilizer to dissolve it and move it into the root zone. Remember, onions are poor competitors with weeds, so a healthy crop with balanced nutrition will also shade the soil and reduce weed pressure. Start with a soil test, follow the uptake pattern, and you will get bigger, better bulbs..Trim()

Split Fertilizer Applications

Onions need fertilizer in small, frequent doses because their roots are shallow and they cannot take up large amounts at once. Split application means dividing the total fertilizer into 2-4 parts and applying them at different growth stages. For a 1-hectare crop, if your total is 100 kg N, 60 kg P2O5, and 80 kg K2O, you could apply: at planting (basal), 30 kg N, 60 kg P2O5, and 40 kg K2O; at 4-6 weeks after transplanting, 40 kg N and 20 kg K2O; at 8-10 weeks, 30 kg N and 20 kg K2O. This matches the onion's uptake curve. In Africa, common fertilizers are urea (46% N), NPK 15-15-15, and muriate of potash (60% K2O). For the basal, you can use NPK 15-15-15: to supply 30 kg N, 60 kg P2O5, and 40 kg K2O, you would need about 200 kg of NPK 15-15-15 (which gives 30 kg N, 30 kg P2O5, 30 kg K2O) plus additional 30 kg P2O5 from single superphosphate (about 170 kg) and 10 kg K2O from muriate of potash (about 17 kg). That is complicated, so many farmers use a simpler approach: apply 200 kg NPK 15-15-15 at planting, then top-dress with 100 kg urea at 4 weeks and another 100 kg urea at 8 weeks. That gives roughly 30 kg N from NPK plus 46 kg N from first urea and 46 kg N from second urea, total 122 kg N, which is close. Adjust based on your soil test. Split application reduces nutrient loss through leaching and volatilization, especially in sandy soils or high rainfall. It also prevents salt burn to young seedlings. Apply fertilizer in a band 5-10 cm from the plant row and cover lightly with soil, then irrigate. Do not apply on wet leaves; urea can burn them. If you use organic manure, split it too: incorporate well-rotted manure before planting and side-dress with compost or poultry litter at 4 weeks. Splitting is more work but pays off with higher yield and better bulb quality. It also saves money because less fertilizer is wasted. Always keep records of what you apply and when, so you can improve next season..Trim()

Foliar Feeding for Quick Correction

Foliar feeding means spraying liquid fertilizer directly onto onion leaves. It is a fast way to correct nutrient deficiencies because nutrients enter through the leaf within hours, not days. Use it when you see symptoms like yellowing (nitrogen), purple tips (phosphorus), or leaf edge burn (potassium). Foliar feeding is not a substitute for soil fertilization, but a supplement for quick fixes. For onions, common foliar sprays are urea (2-3% solution, i.e., 20-30 g per litre of water), potassium nitrate (1-2%), or commercial micronutrient mixes. To make a 2% urea solution, dissolve 200 g of urea in 10 litres of water. Spray in the early morning or late evening to avoid leaf burn from midday sun. Add a few drops of liquid soap as a sticker to help the spray stick to the waxy onion leaves. Spray until leaves are wet but not dripping. Do not spray if rain is expected within 6 hours. For micronutrient deficiencies like zinc or boron, use chelated products at recommended rates, often 1-2 g per litre. Boron is needed in very small amounts; too much is toxic, so measure carefully. Foliar feeding is especially useful during bulb formation when roots may be less active, or after heavy rain when soil nutrients leached. In Africa, many farmers use foliar sprays based on local materials: e.g., wood ash soaked in water (provides potassium) or compost tea (provides a range of nutrients). To make compost tea, put 1 part mature compost in 5 parts water, let it steep for 3-5 days, strain, and dilute 1:5 before spraying. This is a gentle, organic option. Always test a small area first to check for leaf burn. If you see burning, dilute more. Foliar sprays can also be mixed with some pesticides, but check compatibility. Do not mix with alkaline substances like lime. For best results, spray every 7-10 days during rapid growth if deficiency persists, but no more than 3-4 times per season. Remember, foliar feeding is a rescue tool, not a full feeding program. Use it wisely and combine with good soil management..Trim()

Organic Fertilizers and Amendments

Organic fertilizers come from plant or animal sources: manure, compost, poultry litter, green manures, and crop residues. They release nutrients slowly, improve soil structure, and increase water-holding capacity. For onions, well-rotted cattle manure is a good base. Apply 10-20 tonnes per hectare (about 1-2 kg per square metre) and incorporate into the top 15 cm of soil 2-4 weeks before planting. Fresh manure can burn roots and carry weed seeds, so always compost it first. Poultry litter is richer: apply 5-10 tonnes per hectare. Compost made from kitchen waste, crop residues, and animal manure is excellent. To make compost, layer green and brown materials, keep it moist, and turn every 2 weeks. It is ready in 6-8 weeks when dark and crumbly. In Africa, other organic amendments include wood ash (potassium and calcium), bone meal (phosphorus), and blood meal (nitrogen). Wood ash can be applied at 200-500 kg per hectare, but do not use too much as it raises soil pH. Bone meal is slow-release; apply 200-400 kg per hectare before planting. Blood meal is fast-acting nitrogen; apply 100-200 kg per hectare as a side-dress. Green manures like cowpea or mucuna can be grown before onions and ploughed in to add nitrogen. Organic matter also feeds soil microbes that help onions take up nutrients. However, organic fertilizers alone may not supply enough nutrients for high yields. Combine them with mineral fertilizers. For example, apply manure at planting plus a split of urea. This integrated approach is common in African market gardens. Also, consider biochar: charred organic matter that improves soil fertility and water retention. Apply 1-2 tonnes per hectare. Always test your manure or compost for nutrient content if possible; otherwise, use general recommendations. Store manure under cover to prevent nutrient loss from rain and sun. Do not apply manure to onions close to harvest because of food safety concerns. With organic amendments, you build soil health for the long term while feeding your onions now..Trim()

Preventing Nutrient Deficiencies

Preventing deficiencies is easier and cheaper than curing them. Start with a soil test. In many African countries, soil testing services are available through agricultural extension offices or private labs. A basic test costs about $20-50 and tells you pH, organic matter, and available N, P, K, and micronutrients. Adjust pH to 6.0-6.8 for onions; if too acidic (below 5.5), apply agricultural lime at 1-2 tonnes per hectare. If too alkaline (above 7.5), add gypsum or elemental sulphur. Use a balanced fertilizer program based on test results. Common deficiency symptoms: nitrogen deficiency shows as pale green to yellow older leaves; phosphorus deficiency gives purple leaf tips and stunted growth; potassium deficiency causes leaf edge burn and small bulbs; calcium deficiency leads to leaf tip dieback and poor bulb storage; magnesium deficiency shows yellowing between veins on older leaves; sulphur deficiency looks like nitrogen but on younger leaves. Zinc deficiency causes stunted plants with yellow bands on leaves; boron deficiency leads to hollow stems and brown spots on bulbs. To prevent these, apply a complete fertilizer at planting, then split nitrogen and potassium. For micronutrients, use a soil application of zinc sulphate (10-20 kg/ha) or borax (10-15 kg/ha) if deficient. Foliar sprays can correct quickly. Organic matter helps prevent deficiencies by holding nutrients and releasing them slowly. Crop rotation with cereals or legumes reduces pest and disease buildup and balances nutrient use. Avoid over-irrigation, which leaches nitrogen and sulphur. Keep weeds down because they compete for nutrients. Monitor your crop weekly. If you see early symptoms, act fast. Keep a notebook to record what you see and what you apply. This helps you learn your farm's patterns. In sandy soils, deficiencies are more common because nutrients leach; in clay soils, nutrients may be locked up. So adjust your management. Also, consider using slow-release fertilizers or coated urea to reduce losses. For organic growers, regular additions of compost and manure prevent most deficiencies. Remember, healthy soil grows healthy onions. Prevention is a season-long effort, not a one-time fix..Trim()

Fertigation: Injecting Fertilizers via Irrigation

Fertigation is the practice of injecting soluble fertilizers into irrigation water. It is common in drip irrigation but can also be used with sprinklers or even watering cans. For onions, fertigation saves fertilizer, reduces labor, and delivers nutrients exactly when the plant needs them. You need a fertilizer tank, a injector (venturi or pump), and soluble fertilizers like potassium nitrate, calcium nitrate, urea, and monoammonium phosphate. Do not use granular fertilizers that do not dissolve; they will clog emitters. A simple setup for a small farm: a 200-litre drum, a tap, and a hose connected to a drip line. Mix fertilizer in the drum, let it dissolve, and let it flow into the irrigation water at a controlled rate. For a 1-hectare drip system, you can inject 5-10 kg of fertilizer per 1000 litres of water, depending on your schedule. A common fertigation schedule for onions: week 3-4 after transplanting: 20 kg N, 10 kg P2O5, 20 kg K2O per hectare per week; week 5-8: 25 kg N, 5 kg P2O5, 25 kg K2O per week; week 9-12: 15 kg N, 0 P2O5, 30 kg K2O per week. That totals roughly 100 kg N, 30 kg P2O5, 100 kg K2O. Adjust based on soil test. Always inject fertilizers after the system is full of water, and flush with clean water for 10-15 minutes after injection to prevent salt buildup in the lines. Do not mix calcium fertilizers with phosphate or sulphate fertilizers; they form precipitates that clog emitters. Inject them separately, with a flush in between. Fertigation is efficient but requires careful management. Test the EC (electrical conductivity) of your solution; for onions, keep it below 2.0 dS/m to avoid salt burn. If you see leaf edge burn, reduce concentration. In Africa, fertigation is gaining popularity among commercial onion growers, especially in Kenya, South Africa, and Egypt. For smallholders, a simple bucket drip system can be used. Always use clean water and filter it. Keep records of what you inject and when. Fertigation can increase yields by 20-30% compared to broadcasting, because nutrients are placed right in the root zone. It also reduces weed growth because only the crop gets water and nutrients. However, it requires investment in equipment and regular maintenance. If you are not ready for fertigation, split soil applications are the next best thing..Trim()
