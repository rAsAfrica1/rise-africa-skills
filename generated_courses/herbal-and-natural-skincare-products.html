Module 2 · Botanical Ingredients and Their Properties

Calendula: Soothing and Healing

Calendula, often called pot marigold, is a powerhouse for soothing and healing skin. In Africa, it grows well in many regions, from Kenya to South Africa. You can easily grow it in a small plot or even a container. Harvest the flowers when fully open, ideally in the morning after dew has dried. Dry them in a shaded, well-ventilated area for 5-7 days until brittle. To make a simple infused oil, fill a clean glass jar with dried calendula flowers, cover with a carrier oil like sunflower or olive oil, and place in a warm spot for 2-4 weeks, shaking daily. Strain through a cheesecloth. This oil can be used directly on cuts, burns, and dry skin. For a healing salve, melt 100g of beeswax with 500ml of calendula oil, stir well, and pour into tins. Apply to minor wounds, eczema, or cracked heels. Calendula is anti-inflammatory and antimicrobial, speeding up tissue repair. It's safe for babies and sensitive skin. In rural areas, you can also make a strong tea: steep 2 tablespoons of dried flowers in 250ml hot water for 10 minutes, cool, and use as a compress for irritated skin. Always test on a small area first. This plant is a must-have in your skincare garden..Trim()

Chamomile: Anti-inflammatory Actions

Chamomile is famous for its anti-inflammatory and calming properties, both for the body and the skin. Two main types are German chamomile (Matricaria chamomilla) and Roman chamomile (Chamaemelum nobile). German chamomile is more common in skincare. It grows in many African countries, preferring cool conditions. Harvest the flowers when they are fully open, and dry them quickly in a dehydrator or shaded area. To make a chamomile infusion, pour 250ml of boiling water over 2 tablespoons of dried flowers, cover, and steep for 10 minutes. Strain and cool. Use this as a toner for inflamed or acne-prone skin. For a soothing face mask, mix 1 tablespoon of chamomile infusion with 1 tablespoon of honey and 1 teaspoon of oatmeal. Apply for 15 minutes, then rinse. Chamomile contains bisabolol and chamazulene, which reduce redness and swelling. It's excellent for eczema, sunburn, and rashes. You can also make a chamomile oil by infusing dried flowers in sunflower oil for 2 weeks. Use this oil in creams and lotions. For a quick eye compress, soak cotton pads in cooled chamomile tea and place on closed eyes for 10 minutes to reduce puffiness. Always ensure the flowers are free from pesticides. Chamomile is gentle enough for daily use and can be combined with calendula for enhanced healing..Trim()

Lavender: Calming and Balancing

Lavender is well-known for its calming scent and balancing effects on the skin. It grows abundantly in Mediterranean climates but also thrives in parts of Africa with well-drained soil and full sun, such as the Western Cape and highlands of Kenya. Harvest the flower spikes just as they begin to open, in the morning. Dry them in small bundles hung upside down in a dark, ventilated room for 1-2 weeks. To make lavender infused oil, fill a jar with dried lavender buds, cover with jojoba or sweet almond oil, and let it sit in a warm place for 3-4 weeks. Strain. This oil can be used as a facial serum for oily or combination skin, as it helps regulate sebum production. For a calming bath soak, add 1 cup of dried lavender to a muslin bag and hang under the tap. For a spot treatment, mix 2 drops of lavender essential oil with 1 teaspoon of carrier oil and dab on blemishes. Lavender is antibacterial and anti-inflammatory, making it useful for acne and minor burns. It also promotes relaxation, which can indirectly benefit skin by reducing stress-related breakouts. You can make a simple lavender water by steeping 2 tablespoons of dried flowers in 250ml hot water for 15 minutes, then use as a refreshing mist. Always dilute essential oil before applying to skin. Lavender blends well with chamomile and calendula for a comprehensive skincare routine..Trim()

Rosemary: Antioxidant and Stimulating

Rosemary is a woody herb with needle-like leaves, native to the Mediterranean but now grown across Africa. It is rich in antioxidants like rosmarinic acid and carnosic acid, which protect skin from free radical damage and slow signs of aging. It also stimulates circulation, making it useful for dull skin and hair care. Harvest sprigs year-round, but the best time is just before flowering. Dry the leaves in a shaded area for 3-5 days, then strip them from stems. To make rosemary infused oil, combine 1 cup of dried rosemary with 2 cups of sunflower or olive oil in a double boiler. Heat gently for 2 hours, then strain. This oil can be massaged into the scalp to stimulate hair growth, or added to creams for its antioxidant benefits. For a stimulating facial steam, add 2 tablespoons of dried rosemary to a bowl of hot water, cover your head with a towel, and steam for 5-10 minutes. This opens pores and improves circulation. Rosemary also has mild astringent properties, making it good for oily skin. You can make a rosemary toner by steeping 1 tablespoon of dried leaves in 250ml hot water for 10 minutes, cooling, and applying with a cotton pad. For a hair rinse, use the same tea after shampooing to add shine and strength. Rosemary essential oil should be diluted to 1% for facial use. Combine with green tea for a powerful antioxidant blend..Trim()

Aloe Vera: Hydration and Repair

Aloe vera is a succulent plant that thrives in hot, dry climates across Africa. Its gel is 96% water and packed with vitamins, minerals, and amino acids that hydrate and repair skin. To harvest, cut a mature outer leaf close to the base, stand it upright for 10 minutes to drain the yellow latex (which can irritate skin), then slice open and scoop out the clear gel. Use fresh gel immediately or blend and store in the fridge for up to a week. For a simple hydrating mask, mix 2 tablespoons of fresh aloe gel with 1 teaspoon of honey and apply to face for 15 minutes. Rinse with cool water. Aloe is excellent for sunburn, minor cuts, and dry skin. It contains glucomannan, a compound that stimulates collagen production and speeds healing. For a moisturizing lotion, combine 1/2 cup aloe gel, 1/4 cup coconut oil, and 1 tablespoon beeswax. Melt the oil and wax, then whisk in the aloe gel. Store in a jar. This lotion is great for eczema and psoriasis. Aloe can also be used as a base for other herbal extracts. To make a soothing after-sun spray, mix equal parts aloe gel and cooled chamomile tea in a spray bottle. Always test for allergies. Aloe is safe for all skin types, but if you have sensitive skin, start with a small patch. Grow your own aloe in a pot for a constant supply..Trim()

Green Tea: Protective and Astringent

Green tea is made from the leaves of Camellia sinensis, and it is loaded with polyphenols, especially EGCG, which are powerful antioxidants. It protects skin from UV damage, reduces inflammation, and acts as an astringent to tighten pores. Green tea is not widely grown in Africa, but you can buy high-quality loose leaf tea from local markets or suppliers. To make a green tea toner, steep 1 teaspoon of loose green tea in 250ml hot water (not boiling, about 80Â°C) for 3 minutes. Strain and cool. Apply with a cotton pad after cleansing. For a protective face mask, mix 1 tablespoon of green tea leaves (used, cooled) with 1 tablespoon of yogurt and 1 teaspoon of honey. Apply for 10 minutes, then rinse. Green tea is also great for puffy eyes: chill used tea bags and place on eyes for 10 minutes. For an antioxidant serum, infuse green tea in jojoba oil: combine 2 tablespoons of green tea leaves with 100ml jojoba oil, heat gently for 1 hour, strain, and add 2 drops of vitamin E oil. Use a few drops daily. Green tea can be combined with rosemary for a stimulating and protective blend. Always store green tea in an airtight container away from light. This ingredient is a must for anti-aging and acne-prone skin..Trim()

For example, when harvesting moringa leaves, pick only the lower leaves and never strip the whole tree. Leave at least 30% of the leaves so the tree can photosynthesize. .Trim()

In many African communities, traditional leaders manage land. Respect their rules and local customs. .Trim()

.Trim()
