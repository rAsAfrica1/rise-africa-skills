Introduction and Why It Matters

Hard rock mining is the process of extracting gold from solid rock, rather than from loose soil or river gravel. In many parts of Africa, gold is locked inside quartz veins or sulphide-rich rock. This type of mining is more difficult and expensive than alluvial mining, but it can be very rewarding because the gold is often concentrated in specific veins. For example, a rich quartz vein can contain 5 to 30 grams of gold per tonne of rock, while alluvial gravel might only have 0.1 to 0.5 grams per cubic metre. Hard rock mining allows you to target these high-grade zones..Trim()

.Trim()

Why does it matter for African artisans? Many easy alluvial deposits are becoming exhausted. As surface gravels run out, miners must follow the gold into the bedrock. In countries like Tanzania, Ghana, South Africa, and Zimbabwe, small-scale hard rock mining is a major source of income for rural communities..Trim()
