History and Global Consumption of Yogurt

Yogurt has been made in Africa for thousands of years. Nomadic peoples like the Fulani in West Africa and the Maasai in East Africa fermented milk in calabashes or leather bags. The natural bacteria in the milk, combined with warm temperatures, turned it into a thick, sour food that lasted longer than fresh milk. This was practical: fermentation preserved milk without refrigeration, and the result was easier to digest. Today, yogurt is eaten worldwide, but Africa's consumption is growing fast. In countries like Nigeria, Kenya, and South Africa, urban demand for yogurt is rising, especially among health-conscious consumers and young people. Street vendors sell drinkable yogurt, and supermarkets stock flavored cups. The global yogurt market is worth over $100 billion, and Africa's share is small but expanding. For you as a producer, this history matters: you are joining a long tradition, but with modern tools. Types of Yogurt: Set, Stirred, Greek, and Drinkable

Yogurt comes in four main types, each with different texture and production steps. Set yogurt is fermented in the final container. You pour milk and culture into small cups, incubate them, and the yogurt sets like a custard. It is thick and scoopable. Stirred yogurt is fermented in a large tank, then stirred and poured into containers. It is smoother and less firm. Greek yogurt is strained to remove whey, making it very thick and high in protein. Drinkable yogurt is blended to a liquid consistency, often with fruit or sugar. In Africa, all four types sell well. For set yogurt, you need cups with lids, a warm place (around 43Â°C), and 4-6 hours. For stirred, use a large pot or bucket, then stir gently after fermentation. For Greek, after fermentation, pour the yogurt into a clean cloth bag and hang it for 2-4 hours to drain the whey. You can use the whey in smoothies or animal feed. For drinkable, add 10-20% water or fruit juice and blend. Each type requires different equipment and packaging. Set yogurt is easiest for beginners because you avoid stirring, which can break the gel. Stirred yogurt is good for bulk sales. Greek yogurt earns a higher price but yields less productâ3 liters of milk make about 1 liter of Greek yogurt. Drinkable yogurt is popular for school children and workers. Choose your type based on your market. In hot climates, drinkable yogurt is refreshing, while set yogurt is seen as a treat. You can make all four types with the same basic ingredients: fresh milk, a yogurt culture, and heat. The difference is in the handling after fermentation. Practice small batches of each to see which one you can make consistently well..Trim()

Overview of the Yogurt Production Process

Making yogurt is a simple process with six main steps: heating, cooling, inoculating, incubating, cooling again, and packaging. First, heat fresh milk to 85Â°C for 30 minutes. This kills harmful bacteria and denatures proteins so the yogurt sets thick. Use a thermometer and a pot on a stove or fire. Stir often to prevent burning. Second, cool the milk to 43Â°C. This is the ideal temperature for the yogurt cultures. If it's too hot, it kills the bacteria; if too cold, fermentation is slow. You can cool the pot in a basin of cold water. Third, add your starter culture. This can be a spoonful of previous yogurt or a freeze-dried powder. Use about 2 tablespoons per liter of milk. Mix well. Fourth, incubate. Pour the mixture into clean containers, cover, and keep warm at 43Â°C for 4-6 hours. In Africa, you can use a cooler box with warm water, a wrapped blanket, or a solar incubator. Introduction to Food Safety and Hygiene in Dairy

Chemical Composition of Milk: Proteins, Fats, and Carbohydrates

Milk is mostly water (about 87%), but the solids in that water determine how your yogurt will turn out. The main solids are proteins, fats, and carbohydrates (lactose).
