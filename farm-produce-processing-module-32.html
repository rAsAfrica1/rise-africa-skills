Soybean Processing

8 lessons · 30 quiz questions · Nuts & Seeds

1 Soybean varieties

Soybean (Glycine max) is the world's most important protein crop. Africa is a growing producer, especially in Nigeria, South Africa and Zambia.

Food-grade soybean: Large seeds, light hilum (the "eye" of the bean). Best for tofu, soymilk, edamame. Varieties: TGX, Soya beans (local improved).

Oil-grade soybean: Smaller seeds, higher oil. Used for oil and meal.

For food processing, choose food-grade soybean. Light hilum = whiter end products.

⚠ Raw soybeans contain trypsin inhibitor — must be cooked or fermented. Never eat raw.

2 Cleaning and dehulling

Soybeans come with soil, stones and cracked beans.

  1. Clean — remove stones, chaff, damaged beans
  2. Crack the hull with a roller mill or dehuller
  3. Winnow to separate hulls
  4. For food production, cracked beans are used directly
  5. For oil production, beans are flaked and pressed

Yield: 100 kg cleaned soybeans → 85 kg dehulled + 12 kg hulls + 3 kg loss.

3 Soy milk production

Soy milk is the base for tofu, yoghurt, ice cream and many dairy alternatives.

  1. Soak beans 8–12 hours (or 4 hours in warm water)
  2. Drain and rinse
  3. Grind with hot water (1 part beans to 4–6 parts water)
  4. Filter through cloth — collect the milk
  5. Boil milk 15–20 minutes (destroys trypsin inhibitor)
  6. Add sugar, salt, flavouring
  7. Bottle hot (85°C) or cool and refrigerate

Yield: 1 kg dry soybeans → 6–8 L soy milk.

Retail: $2–$4 per litre. Higher if flavoured or organic.

4 Tofu making

Tofu is curdled soy milk pressed into blocks — the "cheese" of soy.

  1. Make soy milk as above
  2. Heat to 75°C
  3. Add coagulant: gypsum (calcium sulfate, 2% of bean weight) or nigari (magnesium chloride)
  4. Stir gently and let sit 10 minutes — curds form
  5. Cut curds and pour into tofu mould lined with cloth
  6. Press for 30–60 minutes (soft tofu) to 2 hours (firm tofu)
  7. Cool and refrigerate

Yield: 1 kg dry soybeans → 3–4 kg tofu.

Retail: $4–$8 per kg. Firm tofu: $6–$10 per kg.

5 Soy flour

Soy flour is a high-protein flour used in baking, porridge and commercial food production.

  1. Clean and dehull soybean
  2. Roast at 180°C for 15–20 minutes (removes beany flavour, destroys enzymes)
  3. Cool completely
  4. Mill in hammer mill
  5. Sieve to 250 micron
  6. Pack in dark airtight bags

Yield: 100 kg soybeans → 85–90 kg flour.

Retail: $2–$5 per kg. Soy-corn blend for NGOs: $1.50–$3 per kg.

6 Soy oil extraction

Soybean oil is the most-consumed cooking oil worldwide.

Small-scale expeller: Press dehulled, flaked soybeans. 15–18% oil yield.

Hexane extraction (industrial): 98% oil recovery — requires $500K+ plant.

  1. Clean and dehull
  2. Heat and flake
  3. Press in oil press
  4. Filter oil
  5. Bottle in dark glass or PET

Yield: 100 kg soybeans → 15–18 kg oil + 75 kg soy meal.

Retail: $3–$6 per litre. Soy meal: $0.40–$0.80 per kg (animal feed).

7 Textured soy protein (TVP)

Textured soy protein (also called TVP or soy chunks) is made from defatted soy flour, extruded into chunks. Used as meat substitute.

  1. Use defatted soy flour (after oil extraction)
  2. Mix with water
  3. Extrude through a food extruder at 150–180°C
  4. Dry the extruded chunks
  5. Pack in poly bags

Yield: 100 kg defatted flour → 70–80 kg TVP.

Retail: $4–$10 per kg. Requires extruder ($2,000–$10,000).

⚠ TVP production requires significant equipment. Start with soymilk and tofu first.

8 Health food market

Soy is a top-5 health food globally, valued for high protein and isoflavones.

Positioning:

  • High protein — 36–40% protein in dry weight
  • Complete protein — all essential amino acids
  • Isoflavones — claimed menopausal and heart benefits
  • Gluten-free
  • Non-GMO or organic — big price premiums

Export markets: US, EU, Middle East. Bulk soy flour: $1.50–$3 per kg FOB. Organic premium: $4–$8 per kg.

💰 Best soy products for small processors: soy milk, tofu, soy flour. All three use simple equipment and have strong local demand.
