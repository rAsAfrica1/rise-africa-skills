Carrot Processing

8 lessons · 30 quiz questions · Tubers & Roots

1 Carrot varieties for processing

Carrots (Daucus carota) come in many varieties. For processing you want high carotene content (orange), uniform size and sweet flavour.

Nantes: Cylindrical, sweet, high carotene. Best for juice and puree.

Imperator: Long, tapered. Common in supermarkets. Good for chips and dicing.

Chantenay: Short, broad, very sweet. Best for baby food and puree.

Kuroda: Tropical variety, grows well in Africa. Deep orange, juicy. Good all-round processing.

Avoid purple, yellow or white carrots for processing — they have lower carotene and less demand.

2 Washing and topping

Carrots are washed immediately after harvest to prevent wilting.

  1. Cut off the leafy top 5 mm above the crown
  2. Soak in water for 10 minutes
  3. Brush wash (drum washer for volume)
  4. Sort — remove cracked, forked or green-shouldered carrots
  5. Grade by size — chips need uniform diameter

Store fresh carrots at 0–4°C and 95% humidity. They hold for 4–6 months under proper cold storage.

⚠ Green tops on carrots mean sun exposure. Cut them off — they taste bitter.

3 Carrot juice

Carrot juice is the second-largest juice market in the world after orange. Popular for its sweet taste and carotene content.

  1. Wash and peel (optional — peeling gives lighter colour)
  2. Blanch in steam for 3 minutes (increases juice yield)
  3. Cool quickly
  4. Extract with a juicer or screw press
  5. Strain through 200 micron
  6. Add 0.2% citric acid + lemon juice to taste
  7. Pasteurise at 90°C for 30 seconds
  8. Hot-fill into bottles

Yield: 100 kg carrots → 60–70 litres juice. Shelf life (pasteurised): 9–12 months.

4 Dried carrot flakes

Dried carrot is used by soup companies, food manufacturers and instant noodles. It's a bulk commodity that sells steadily.

  1. Wash, peel and top carrots
  2. Blanch for 3 minutes (prevents darkening during drying)
  3. Dice into 5 mm cubes or slice into 3 mm rounds
  4. Dry in dehydrator at 60°C for 6–8 hours
  5. Target moisture: 6–8%
  6. Cool, then package in moisture-barrier bags

Yield: 100 kg fresh → 10–13 kg dried.

Bulk price: $6–$12 per kg. Manufacturers buy in tonnes.

5 Carrot puree for baby food

Carrot puree is a first food for babies from 6 months. This is a premium product with strong margins.

  1. Wash, peel and chop
  2. Steam cook until very soft (15 minutes)
  3. Blend into smooth puree (no lumps for stage 1)
  4. Add water to reach desired consistency
  5. For stage 2 (6–8 months), leave small pieces
  6. Heat to 90°C
  7. Hot-fill into baby food jars or retort pouches
  8. Vacuum-seal lids and cool

Yield: 100 kg carrots → 90–95 kg puree (carrots are already high moisture).

Retail: $3–$6 per 200 g jar. Highest-margin carrot product.

6 Carrot cake and baking mixes

Value-added dry mixes with carrot powder as a base.

Carrot cake mix: 60% flour + 25% carrot powder + 10% sugar + 5% spice blend. Pack in 500 g bags. Add eggs, oil and water to bake.

Carrot muffin mix: similar formulation with extra cinnamon.

Retail: $4–$8 per 500 g bag. Sold at bakeries and health food shops.

7 Carrot powder

Carrot powder is a concentrated source of carotene and natural orange colour.

  1. Wash and peel carrots
  2. Blanch 3 minutes
  3. Slice 3 mm thick
  4. Dry at 55–60°C for 8–12 hours
  5. Mill to fine powder
  6. Sieve through 250 micron
  7. Package in dark airtight packaging

Yield: 100 kg fresh → 8–12 kg powder.

Retail: $15–$35 per kg. Used in supplements, pet food, natural colourants.

8 Retail and wholesale

Retail channels:

  • Health food shops — carrot powder, juice, puree
  • Baby food aisles — puree in jars
  • Farmer's markets — fresh and juice
  • Online — direct-to-consumer in cities

Wholesale channels:

  • Soup and food manufacturers — dried flakes
  • Juice companies — carrot juice concentrate
  • Baby food companies — carrot puree
  • Animal feed — rejected carrots
💰 Sell to manufacturers in tonnes for steady cash. Sell retail for higher margin but more work.
