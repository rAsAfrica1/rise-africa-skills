Lessons

1. L1

In this lesson, you will learn how to connect your car engine to the water pump. The engine's flywheel spins at high speed, but the pump needs a slower, steady rotation. The first step is to measure the engine's output shaft. Use a vernier caliper or a good ruler to get the exact diameter. Most car engines have a crankshaft pulley or flywheel with a center bore of about 30-50mm. Write down this measurement. Next, measure the pump's input shaft. It might be a keyed shaft of 20-30mm. These two sizes are different, so you need an adapter. You can make one on a lathe, or buy a flexible coupling that matches both sizes. In many African workshops, you can find a machinist who will turn a custom adapter for a low price. Bring both shafts to him. He will cut a piece of steel round bar to fit the engine side, then bore the other end to fit the pump shaft. Make sure the adapter has a keyway if the shafts have keys. A keyway is a slot cut into the shaft and the adapter to lock them together. Without it, the coupling will slip. Use a 6mm or 8mm key steel, depending on the size. Also, check the runout: the adapter must be concentric within 0.05mm. If it wobbles, the pump will vibrate and wear out bearings quickly. Finally, balance the adapter. A simple static balance by removing metal from the heavy side is enough for speeds under 1500 RPM. Remember, safety first: always disconnect the battery before working on the engine. Wear gloves and eye protection when grinding or machining.

2. L2

Now that you have an adapter, you need to join it to the pump shaft. There are three common methods: direct coupling, belt drive, and chain drive. For small pumps up to 5 kW, a direct coupling is best. It is simple and efficient. You will need a flexible coupling, like a jaw coupling or a tyre coupling. These allow for small misalignments. To install, slide the coupling hub onto the engine adapter and the other hub onto the pump shaft. Use a dial indicator to check alignment. The maximum parallel misalignment should be less than 0.1mm, and angular misalignment less than 0.5 degrees. If you don't have a dial indicator, use a straight edge and feeler gauges. Tighten the set screws on the hubs. Then insert the flexible spider or tyre between the jaws. For larger pumps or when you need to change speed, use a belt drive. A V-belt with two pulleys is common. Calculate the pulley sizes: if the engine runs at 1500 RPM and you want the pump at 750 RPM, the pump pulley must be twice the diameter of the engine pulley. For example, engine pulley 100mm, pump pulley 200mm. Use a B-section belt for up to 10 kW. Mount the pump on a sliding base so you can adjust belt tension. The belt should deflect about 10mm when you press it with your thumb midway between pulleys. Too tight, and you damage bearings; too loose, and it slips. In dusty African conditions, belt drives are more forgiving than direct couplings, but you must check tension weekly.

3. L3

A drive system is only as good as its alignment. Misalignment is the number one cause of coupling failure. In this lesson, you will learn how to align the engine and pump properly. First, mount both on a sturdy base. Use a steel frame or a thick wooden plank at least 50mm thick. The base must be rigid; if it flexes, alignment will change when the engine runs. Place the pump so its shaft is roughly in line with the engine shaft. Use shims under the feet to adjust height. Shims can be made from sheet metal, washers, or even flattened tin cans. Start with a straight edge across the two coupling halves. Check the gap at four points: top, bottom, left, right. Adjust shims until the gap is equal within 0.05mm. Then check angular alignment: the gap between the coupling faces should be the same at top and bottom, and at left and right. If not, adjust the shims on one side. For belt drives, alignment means the pulleys must be in the same plane. Use a straight edge across the pulley faces. The belt should run straight, not at an angle. If the pulleys are misaligned, the belt will wear quickly and may come off. After alignment, tighten all bolts to the correct torque. Use a torque wrench if available. For M12 bolts, torque to about 80 Nm. Finally, turn the engine by hand for a few revolutions to check for binding. If it turns freely, you are ready to connect the battery and test. Always recheck alignment after the first hour of running, as the base may settle.

4. L4

Belts and pulleys are the most common drive system in African workshops because they are cheap, easy to repair, and handle shock loads. In this lesson, you will learn how to select and install them. First, determine the power you need. A typical small irrigation pump might need 2-5 kW. For that, a single B-section V-belt can transmit up to 5 kW at 1500 RPM. If you need more power, use two or three belts in parallel. The pulley diameter matters: smaller pulleys have less grip. The minimum pitch diameter for a B-section pulley is 75mm. If you go smaller, the belt will slip and wear out fast. To calculate speed, use this formula: Engine RPM Engine Pulley Diameter = Pump RPM Pump Pulley Diameter. For example, if the engine runs at 1800 RPM and has a 100mm pulley, and you want the pump at 900 RPM, the pump pulley must be 200mm. Always round to the nearest standard size. When installing, loosen the motor mount bolts and slide the engine to remove the belt. Never pry the belt on with a screwdriver; that damages the cords. Use a belt tension gauge or the thumb method: press the belt midway between pulleys, it should deflect about 10mm per meter of span. For a 500mm span, deflection should be 5mm. Check belt tension weekly. In dusty conditions, belts wear faster, so keep spares. Also, align pulleys with a straight edge. Misalignment of just 1mm can cut belt life by half. Finally, use belt dressing only as a temporary fix; it can attract dust and cause slipping. Replace belts when they show cracks or glazing.

5. L5

Sometimes you need a right-angle drive or a compact setup. That's where chain and gear drives come in. Chain drives are stronger than belts and don't slip. They are good for low-speed, high-torque applications. For example, if your pump runs at 300 RPM and needs high torque, a chain drive is ideal. You will need two sprockets and a roller chain. The sprocket sizes determine the speed ratio. If the engine sprocket has 15 teeth and the pump sprocket has 45 teeth, the pump will run at one-third engine speed. Use a chain size like 08B or 10B, which are common in Africa. To install, align the sprockets carefully. Use a straight edge across the sprocket faces. The chain should have about 2% slack; too tight and it wears the bearings, too loose and it jumps off. Lubricate the chain with engine oil every 50 hours. In dusty conditions, use a dry lubricant or clean the chain often. Gear drives are less common because they are expensive and need a sealed oil bath. But if you have a gearbox from a car or truck, you can adapt it. For example, a differential can be used as a right-angle drive. Weld the differential to the frame, connect the engine to the input shaft, and the pump to one of the output shafts. Lock the other output shaft. This gives you a 90-degree turn and a gear reduction. However, welding cast iron requires special rods and preheating. If you are not experienced, get a professional welder. Always check for oil leaks and maintain the oil level. Chain and gear drives are noisy, so use guards to protect yourself and others.

6. L6

You have connected the engine to the pump, but you must ensure the whole system is safe and reliable. In this lesson, you will learn about guards, couplings, and maintenance. First, install guards over all rotating parts: belts, pulleys, couplings, and shafts. A guard can be made from expanded metal or sheet metal. It must be strong enough to prevent fingers from entering. Use bolts, not screws, so it doesn't vibrate loose. Next, check the coupling regularly. For flexible couplings, inspect the rubber spider or tyre for cracks every 100 hours. Replace if worn. For direct couplings, check the set screws and keys. They can loosen due to vibration. Use thread-locking compound on set screws. For belt drives, check tension and alignment weekly. For chain drives, check slack and lubrication. Also, listen for unusual noises. A squealing belt means it's slipping; a grinding noise means bearings are failing. Address these immediately. Finally, create a maintenance schedule. Every day: check for leaks and unusual noise. Every week: check belt tension, chain slack, and coupling alignment. Every month: grease bearings, change oil in gearboxes, and inspect guards. Keep a logbook. In rural Africa, spare parts may be far away, so keep critical spares: a spare belt, a spare coupling spider, and a spare key. Train everyone who operates the pump on safety: never wear loose clothing near rotating parts, always disconnect the battery before maintenance, and never touch a running pump. With proper care, your converted engine will pump water for years.

7. Practical Reference

Practical Reference: Mechanical Coupling and Drive System

In the African context, converting a car engine into an irrigation water pump is a pragmatic response to two realities: unreliable grid electricity and the high cost of purpose-built pumping sets. The mechanical coupling and drive system is the heart of this conversion. It determines whether your engine's torque reaches the pump efficiently, whether the setup survives dusty and humid conditions, and whether you can repair it with locally available parts. This module gives you the practical reference data, dimensions, and field-tested guidance you need to design, build, and troubleshoot a belt-driven or direct-coupled system using common salvage components.

This lesson assumes you have already selected a suitable engine (typically a 515 HP diesel or petrol engine from a car, pickup, or stationary application) and a centrifugal or self-priming pump. Your task now is to connect them reliably. We will focus on two dominant drive configurations in African workshops: V-belt drive and direct shaft coupling. We will also cover alignment tolerances, pulley sizing, belt selection, guarding, and maintenance intervals that keep pumps running through the dry season.

Drive Configuration Options

Two configurations dominate African workshops: V-belt drive and direct coupling. V-belt drive is the most practical for field conversions because it tolerates slight misalignment, allows speed adjustment through pulley sizing, and absorbs shock loads. Direct coupling is more efficient but demands precise alignment and is less forgiving of frame flex or thermal expansion.

Pulley Sizing and Speed Calculation

The pump must run at its designed speed to deliver the required flow and head. If your engine runs at 1,800 RPM and your pump needs 1,450 RPM, you need a pulley ratio of 1,800/1,450 1.24. This means the driven pulley diameter should be 1.24 times the driver pulley diameter. Always verify pump curves before sizing.

Formula: Ddriven = (Nengine / Npump) Ddriver

For example, a 100 mm driver pulley at 1,800 RPM driving a pump at 1,200 RPM requires a driven pulley of (1800/1200) 100 = 150 mm. Use standard pulley sizes available in local markets (e.g., 75, 100, 125, 150, 200 mm) and adjust with belt length or a tensioner.

SAFETY WARNING: Rotating Equipment Hazard

Never operate an engine-driven pump without a properly secured belt guard or coupling guard. In African field conditions, loose clothing, hands, and tools can be caught in exposed belts and pulleys, causing severe injury or death. Guards must be made of metal or rigid plastic, cover all rotating parts, and be boltednot wiredin place. Before any maintenance, shut off the engine, remove the key, and wait for all moving parts to stop completely. Do not use your hand to check belt tension while the engine is running. Train all operators on lockout/tagout procedures. This is non-negotiable.

Alignment and Tensioning

Misalignment is the leading cause of premature belt and bearing failure in field conversions. Use a straight edge or laser alignment tool across both pulley faces. The maximum allowable offset is 0.5 mm per 100 mm of pulley width. Angular misalignment should not exceed 0.5 degrees. For direct coupling, use a dial indicator and shim the engine or pump feet until radial and angular alignment are within 0.05 mm and 0.02 degrees respectively.

Belt tension: apply the "deflection" rule. Press the belt midway between pulleys with moderate thumb pressure (about 10 kg). Deflection should be 1015 mm per metre of span. Too loose causes slip and glazing; too tight overloads bearings. Re-tension after the first 24 hours of operation, then every 100 hours.

Mounting and Frame Construction

Build a rigid base frame from 50505 mm angle iron or 100 mm channel. Weld or bolt it to a concrete plinth at least 150 mm thick. The frame must be level within 1 mm per metre. Use anti-vibration mounts (rubber pads or spring mounts) between engine/pump and frame to reduce fatigue. In sandy or muddy locations, raise the frame at least 200 mm above ground and provide drainage.

For direct coupling, use a flexible coupling (e.g., jaw coupling or tyre coupling) to accommodate minor misalignment and thermal growth. Never use a rigid flange coupling unless alignment is perfect and the frame is extremely stiff.

Field Troubleshooting Quick Guide

  • Belt squealing: Insufficient tension or glazed belt. Re-tension or replace.
  • Excessive vibration: Misalignment, worn bearings, or loose mounts. Check alignment and bolt torque.
  • Pump not priming: Check suction line for air leaks, foot valve, and impeller clearance. Drive system may be slipping.
  • Overheating bearings: Over-tensioned belt or misalignment. Reduce tension and re-align.
  • Belt flipping off: Severe misalignment or worn pulley grooves. Replace pulleys or re-align.

Quick Reference Checklist

  1. Verify engine and pump speeds match required flow/head using pump curve and pulley ratio formula.
  2. Select correct belt section and number of belts based on engine HP (see Table 2).
  3. Align pulleys or coupling within tolerance using straight edge or dial indicator.
  4. Tension belts to 1015 mm deflection per metre of span; re-check after 24 hours.
  5. Install rigid metal guards over all rotating parts and bolt securely.
  6. Mount engine and pump on a rigid, level frame with anti-vibration pads.
  7. Lubricate bearings per manufacturer schedule; check weekly in dusty conditions.
  8. Keep spare belts, coupling inserts, and alignment tools in the pump house.
