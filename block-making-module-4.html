Expanded Clay (LECA) Block

8 lessons · 30 quiz questions · Medium difficulty

1 What is expanded clay

Expanded clay (LECA — Lightweight Expanded Clay Aggregate) is made by heating clay to 1,100–1,200°C in a rotary kiln. At that temperature, the clay softens and trapped gases (from organic matter in the clay) expand it like popcorn.

Result: round, porous, lightweight pebbles with a hard ceramic shell. Typical sizes: 4–10 mm (for blocks) and 10–20 mm (for drainage and horticulture).

Key properties:

  • Bulk density: 300–500 kg/m³ (vs 1,500 for gravel)
  • Fire resistant (already fired at 1,200°C)
  • Chemically inert — no reaction with cement
  • High thermal insulation
  • Rot-proof, pest-proof

LECA is used both for making lightweight blocks AND as loose fill for insulation in floors and roofs.

2 Sourcing or making LECA

Two paths for getting LECA:

Buy it: LECA is produced by specialist plants. In Africa, supply is limited but growing (South Africa, Egypt, Nigeria). Price: $80–$250 per m³ (bulk bags). Import from Europe: $200–$400 per m³ landed.

Make it: Requires a rotary kiln at 1,100–1,200°C. Investment: $500,000–$2,000,000. Only viable at scale — minimum 20,000 m³ per year. Not for small producers.

For small block-makers: buy LECA. Focus your capital on the block-making equipment, not the raw material.

If LECA is not available, substitutes include:

  • Pumice (volcanic rock, naturally lightweight)
  • Expanded perlite (similar process, smaller scale possible)
  • Recycled polystyrene beads (cheap but weaker)
💡 South Africa and Egypt have local LECA. In East and West Africa, pumice or imported LECA is common.

3 Mixing LECA with cement

LECA block mix (by volume):

  • Cement: 1 part
  • Sand: 1 part
  • LECA (4–10 mm): 4 parts
  • Water: as needed for a workable mix

Key rules:

  • Pre-wet the LECA. LECA absorbs 10–20% of its weight in water. If it's dry when mixed, it steals water from the cement, weakening the block.
  • Measure by volume, not weight. LECA is very light — 1 m³ weighs only 350 kg vs 1,500 kg for gravel.
  • More cement than dense blocks. LECA blocks need a richer cement matrix because the aggregate doesn't contribute strength.

Cement use: 1 bag (50 kg) makes 35–45 LECA blocks of 440 × 215 × 100 mm.

4 Lightweight block mould design

LECA blocks are made on the same machines as dense blocks. Two differences:

1. Taller moulds. Because LECA blocks are lighter, you can make them larger. Typical LECA block: 600 × 200 × 250 mm (vs 440 × 215 × 100 for concrete). Fewer blocks per m² = faster wall construction.

2. Deeper fill cavity. LECA mix is bulkier than dense concrete — a mould of the same outer size needs a deeper cavity to hold the same weight of material.

Vibration times: 3–5 seconds (shorter than for dense blocks). LECA is friable — over-vibrating breaks the pebbles and reduces insulation value.

Many plants also make LECA blocks with tongue-and-groove edges — the interlocking edges allow thinner mortar joints.

5 Why LECA walls are warm

The thermal conductivity (λ value) of a material determines how much heat passes through it.

λ values (W/m·K, lower is better):

  • Dense concrete: 1.6–1.8
  • Hollow concrete block: 0.8–1.0
  • LECA block: 0.3–0.5
  • AAC block: 0.10–0.15
  • Mineral wool: 0.04

LECA blocks are 3–5× more insulating than dense concrete. A house built with LECA walls stays 4–8°C cooler in summer and warmer in winter compared to a solid concrete house of the same thickness.

In hot African climates, this means less air conditioning, lower electricity bills, and more comfortable living. That's the selling point.

6 Cost per LECA block

LECA blocks cost more than dense blocks — but the value is in the insulation and the speed of laying.

Typical cost breakdown (per 600×200×250 mm block):

  • Cement: $0.30
  • Sand: $0.05
  • LECA: $0.60–$1.20 (the biggest cost — depends on local LECA price)
  • Labour: $0.15
  • Overheads: $0.10
  • Total: $1.20–$1.80 per block

Selling price: $2.00–$3.50 per block.

But because LECA blocks are bigger, you need fewer blocks per m² of wall. A 4 m² wall needs 30 LECA blocks vs 50 dense blocks. Even at a higher per-block price, the cost per m² can be similar.

7 Curing lightweight blocks

LECA blocks cure the same way as dense blocks — 7 days damp, 28 days to full strength. But watch these differences:

  • Faster drying. LECA blocks dry faster because they have less water in the mix. Over-spraying can waterlog them.
  • Lighter handling. You can move LECA blocks sooner (12–18 hours after casting) than dense blocks (24 hours).
  • Lower pallet pressure. Because they're light, you can stack 12–15 layers high vs 8–10 for dense blocks.
  • Protect from rain. LECA blocks can absorb water if left uncovered. Keep them under plastic or a roof.

Strength test: same drop test. A good LECA block chips at the corner.

8 Selling LECA on insulation

Don't sell LECA blocks as "cheap concrete". Sell them as insulated blocks.

Marketing angles:

  • "Cooler in summer, warmer in winter"
  • "Lower electricity bills"
  • "Fast to build — one LECA block = two concrete blocks"
  • "Fire resistant"
  • "Light on foundations — smaller footings needed"

Target markets:

  • Homeowners building in hot climates
  • Architects designing energy-efficient buildings
  • Commercial buildings with insulation requirements
  • Second-storey additions (weight-critical)

Price premium vs dense blocks: 30–80% per block, but customer saves on wall thickness, labour, and air conditioning.

💰 LECA blocks are a premium product. Sell on value, not price. Target educated buyers who understand insulation.
