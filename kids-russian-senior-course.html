Russian for Teens

Crack Cyrillic in a week, then go after the vocabulary that opens laboratories.

Ages 12–168 modules 🪨 Starts with no device

Cyrillic takes about a week if you attack it in the right order — the friendly letters, the liars, then the strange shapes. After that Russian rewards you fast, because thousands of scientific and technical words came into Russian from the same roots as English. This course takes you from zero to introducing yourself, reading real technical labels, and saying why you want to study.

The Words You Can Already Read

Hundreds of Russian words are borrowed from the same international vocabulary as English, so your first real reading is easier than it looks.

СПОРТ · ФУТБОЛ · БАНК · МЕТРО · ТЕЛЕФОН

🪨 No device needed

The Five Letters That Lie to You

Р С Н В У look like Latin letters and are not, and until you kill that reflex your reading will stay slow.

Р=r С=s Н=n В=v У=oo
3
🪨 No device needed

The Shapes With No Twin

Ж Ш Щ Ц Ч Э look like nothing else on earth, which makes them the last hurdle and the easiest one.

Ж zh · Ш sh · Щ shch · Ц ts · Ч ch · Э e
4
📱 Shared phone

Stress, and Why Your O Turns Into an A

Unstressed Russian vowels change their sound, so pronouncing every letter as written is the fastest way to sound wrong.

молоко → written m-o-l-o-k-o → said ma-la-KÓ

📱 Shared phone

The Laboratory and the Hospital

The Russian words for medicine and science are the reason many students learn this language at all, and they are unusually easy to recognise.

физика · химия · биология · наука
6
📱 Shared phone

Five Sentences That Get You Out of Trouble

A handful of first-person verb phrases will carry you through most of a real conversation long before you understand any grammar.

я знаю · я не знаю · я хочу · я могу · помогите!

🤖 Salvage kit

Read the Machine

Russian-made and Russian-labelled equipment turns up in workshops, clinics and generators, and safety words are the first real Russian most engineers read.

ВКЛ ⏻ on ВЫКЛ ⭘ off ОПАСНО ⚠️
8
🤖 Salvage kit

Capstone: The Scholarship Introduction

Today you deliver, out loud and unscripted, the exact self-introduction a Russian university interviewer would ask you for.

Здравствуйте. Меня зовут… Я из… Я учу русский язык. Я хочу учиться в России.
For a parent or teacher

Your teenager will finish able to read Cyrillic fluently aloud, handle greetings and basic requests with correct stress, read technical and safety labelling, and give a spoken self-introduction suitable for a scholarship interview. Russian universities admit substantial numbers of African students each year in medicine and engineering, and language preparation is a formal part of that route.
