Insulated Concrete Form (ICF)

8 lessons · 30 quiz questions · Medium difficulty

1 What is ICF

ICF stands for Insulated Concrete Form. It's a system where walls are built from hollow polystyrene blocks that are then filled with concrete.

The result: a reinforced concrete wall that's already insulated on both sides. No separate insulation step needed.

Basic ICF system:

  • Hollow EPS blocks (like giant Lego pieces) stack together to form the wall shape
  • Steel reinforcement is placed in the hollow centres
  • Concrete is poured into the hollows
  • The concrete cures, becoming a solid structural wall
  • The EPS stays in place as permanent insulation

Popular in Europe, North America, and increasingly in South Africa and North Africa.

💡 ICF walls can be built by unskilled labour in days. Structure + insulation + formwork all in one step.

2 Polystyrene block design

ICF blocks look like large hollow bricks made of white foam.

Typical block dimensions:

  • Length: 1,200 mm
  • Height: 250–400 mm
  • Width: 200–400 mm (depends on wall thickness required)
  • Concrete core: 100–250 mm wide inside the foam
  • EPS thickness: 40–100 mm on each side

Blocks interlock with tongue-and-groove edges and vertical slots for rebar. Some have internal webbing (plastic or metal) that holds the two EPS panels together.

Producers sell blocks with different core sizes: 100 mm core for interior walls, 200 mm+ for load-bearing exterior walls.

3 Site assembly basics

ICF is a site-based system — most of the work happens on the build site, not in a factory.

Assembly steps:

  1. Pour a level concrete foundation with starter rebar sticking up
  2. Place the first course of ICF blocks over the starter rebar
  3. Stack subsequent courses — blocks click together with no mortar
  4. Build up to 3 m high per pour cycle
  5. Insert vertical and horizontal rebar into the block slots
  6. Prepare for concrete pour (bracing, props to keep walls straight)
  7. Pump concrete into the cores — from bottom to top of each section
  8. Vibrate the concrete gently to remove air pockets
  9. Cure, then remove bracing
  10. Finish interior and exterior surfaces

Two people can stack the ICF blocks for a small house in 2–3 days.

4 Reinforcement placement

ICF walls get their strength from concrete + steel, not from the foam.

Standard reinforcement:

  • Vertical rebar: 12 mm diameter at 200–400 mm spacing, running the full wall height
  • Horizontal rebar: 10 mm diameter in every course or every second course
  • Corner rebar: extra bars at every corner and T-junction
  • Opening rebar: extra bars at every door and window opening

Rebar sits in pre-formed slots in the block — easy to place accurately. Cover to the outside face of the concrete must be 40 mm minimum.

Reinforcement design for a full house requires a structural engineer in most countries.

5 Pouring concrete into ICF

The concrete pour is the critical moment — every mistake shows up here.

Concrete specification:

  • Strength: 25–30 N/mm²
  • Slump: 100–150 mm (pourable, but not too wet)
  • Aggregate: 10 mm maximum (to flow around rebar)
  • Additive: plasticiser for flow

Pour rules:

  • Pour in layers 1 m high — not full height in one go
  • Use a pump or chute — never dump from height
  • Never pour from more than 1.5 m above the wall
  • Vibrate gently — no more than 3–5 seconds per spot
  • Pour continuously if possible — cold joints weaken the wall

After pour, the wall may shift — check alignment with a level and straight edge, adjust bracing.

6 Curing the concrete core

The EPS foam does most of the curing job for you — it holds moisture in and keeps temperature stable.

Curing rules:

  • Do not remove bracing for 24–48 hours
  • Keep EPS forms wet externally for first 3 days (spray the outside)
  • Do not drill into the wall for 7 days
  • Full strength at 28 days

After 48 hours, walls can be cut for openings and prepared for roof structure. Interior finishes can be applied after 7 days.

Because the foam surrounds the concrete, curing is naturally well-controlled — one of the hidden advantages of ICF.

7 Finishing ICF walls

ICF walls need finishes on both sides — EPS alone isn't a wall finish.

Interior finishes:

  • Drywall (screwed directly to the EPS or via a furring strip)
  • Rendered plaster — a bonding coat then topcoat
  • Wood panelling on furring strips

Exterior finishes:

  • Traditional render — mesh + base coat + topcoat
  • Siding, stone, or brick veneer on furring
  • Stucco, EIFS systems

Because EPS is soft, mechanical fixings (screws, bolts) need care. Use plastic or metal anchors specifically rated for foam.

Finish cost: $30–$80 per m² per side, similar to standard construction.

8 Selling to house builders

ICF is a premium construction system. Selling points:

Advantages over block + insulation:

  • Faster construction (3–4 weeks faster for a house)
  • Better insulation (U-value 0.15–0.25 W/m²K)
  • Stronger (reinforced concrete core)
  • Less labour
  • Lower energy bills for life of building

Who buys ICF:

  • High-end residential builders
  • Eco-builders focusing on energy efficiency
  • Commercial developers of hotels, offices
  • Cold-storage and food-processing facilities
  • Any building where energy cost matters

Pricing (Africa, 2026):

  • ICF block set per m² of wall: $35–$70
  • Complete ICF wall (blocks + concrete + labour + finish): $120–$250 per m²

Cost to make ICF blocks (if producing): $15–$35 per m².

Selling tools:

  • Energy efficiency comparison calculator
  • Completed project photos and testimonials
  • Sample ICF wall section cut open
  • Partnership with ICF-trained builders
💰 ICF is a high-margin, low-competition market. Only worth it if you can produce quality EPS blocks and have local building demand. Partnership with an ICF-trained contractor is key.
