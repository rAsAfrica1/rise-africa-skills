Grass Paver (Grass-crete)

8 lessons · 30 quiz questions · Easy difficulty

1 What are grass pavers

Grass pavers (also called grasscrete or grass-crete) are concrete blocks with open cells through which grass grows. They give a paved surface that looks and behaves like grass.

The result: a car park, driveway, or access road that drains water naturally and looks green. Grass grows through the holes, supported by the concrete grid.

Used worldwide for:

  • Eco-friendly car parks (hotels, churches, offices)
  • Fire access lanes on grass
  • Residential driveways (council-approved where impermeable paving is banned)
  • Drainage areas — the cells absorb water
  • Event overflow parking (temporary)

In Africa, grass pavers are a growing market as cities enforce drainage regulations to reduce flooding.

2 Open-cell mould design

Grass paver moulds create blocks with 20–30% open space.

Common designs:

  • Grid pattern: 4×4 grid of square cells — 200 × 200 × 80 mm block
  • Honeycomb: Hexagonal cells — decorative
  • Cross ("+") pattern: Centre cross with cells around it — common in Africa
  • Round-cell pattern: Circular cells

Moulds have:

  • Solid outer frame
  • Internal cores (create the open cells)
  • Flat top for the surface
  • Depth 80–100 mm (deeper than normal pavers — cells need depth)

Cost: £250–£900 per mould (multi-cavity for volume).

3 Drainage hole layout

The open cells are the point. Design rules:

  • Cell size: 30–50 mm across. Smaller cells fill with soil and grass better; larger cells drain more water.
  • Cell spacing: 40–60 mm wall thickness between cells.
  • Open area: Target 25–35% open. Below 20% = poor grass growth. Above 40% = weak block.
  • Shape: Square and hexagonal are strongest. Round cells drain fastest.

For car parks: 30 mm cells, 40 mm walls, 80 mm thick block. For fire lanes: 40 mm cells, 50 mm walls, 100 mm thick block.

💡 Design a pattern that looks good in photos — most buyers decide from a picture, not a spec sheet.

4 Concrete mix for grass pavers

Standard grass paver mix by weight:

  • Cement: 1 part
  • Sharp sand (0–4 mm): 1.5 parts
  • Crushed stone (4–6 mm): 2.5 parts
  • Water: earth-damp

Cement is about 15–17% of total weight. Same as regular pavers.

Grass pavers must be strong enough to carry vehicles while having thin walls between cells. The walls are the weak point — never use stone larger than 6 mm.

Target strength: 30–40 N/mm² at 28 days. Stronger than standard pavers is not needed — the soil under the paver does most of the load-bearing.

5 Filling the mould

Grass pavers need careful filling — thin cell walls trap air bubbles easily.

  1. Fill in two layers.
  2. Compact first layer with a rod — poke into every cell.
  3. Fill second layer.
  4. Vibrate 5–8 seconds (longer than standard pavers).
  5. Level top with a straight edge.
  6. Demould straight up.

Common fault: honeycomb between cells. Cause: mix too dry. Fix: add water slowly and vibrate longer.

Second common fault: broken cell walls during demoulding. Cause: mix too wet, or the mould's inner cores are worn. Fix: check the cores.

6 Demoulding delicate cells

Cell walls are only 40–60 mm wide — much thinner than regular pavers. Demoulding needs care:

  • Wait 24 hours before demoulding (longer than standard pavers).
  • Lift the mould straight up slowly — no tilting.
  • Inner cores must release cleanly — if they drag, the walls break.
  • Green pavers stay on the pallet. Move to curing after 24 hours.
  • Never stack green grass pavers more than 3 high — the cell walls can't take weight.

Broken cell walls mean the paver is scrap. Handle it like eggs until it's cured.

7 Curing

Standard curing: 7 days damp, 28 days full strength. Grass pavers specifically:

  • Keep covered with plastic sheet for the first 3 days.
  • Spray 2× daily for 7 days.
  • Do not stack more than 4 high until 14 days old.
  • After 14 days, stack 8 high.
  • After 28 days, ready to sell.

Grass pavers are usually sold after 14 days (75% strength) — enough for the sales channel. Full strength is for actual installation.

8 Selling as eco drainage

The market is growing fast. Sell on the environmental story:

Pitch to buyers:

  • "Reduce flooding — water drains through, not into storm drains"
  • "Council compliant — meets permeable paving rules"
  • "Green car park — looks better than concrete"
  • "Lower drainage cost — no need for extra soakaways"

Who buys:

  • Churches and schools (grass car parks)
  • Hotels and guesthouses (eco landscaping)
  • Office parks with sustainability targets
  • Homeowners facing new drainage rules
  • Environmental landscaping contractors

Pricing (Africa, 2026):

  • Standard grass paver: $8–$15 per m²
  • Premium/heavy-duty: $15–$25 per m²
  • Delivered + laid: add $4–$8 per m²

Production cost: $4–$7 per m². Margin: 40–55%.

💰 Grass pavers are a niche but growing market. Premium price, less competition. Perfect addition to a paver business.
