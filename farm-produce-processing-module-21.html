Tomato Processing

8 lessons · 30 quiz questions · Fruit Vegetables

1 Tomato varieties for processing

Not all tomatoes are good for processing. Fresh-market tomatoes are bred for looks and shipping; processing tomatoes are bred for high solids, deep colour, and uniform ripening.

Roma / plum varieties: Elongated, thick walls, low water. Best for sauce, paste, canning. Varieties: Roma VF, San Marzano, Heinz 1370.

Round processing: Bigger fruit, good for diced and juice. Varieties: Hypeel, Campbell 33.

Cherry tomatoes: Small, sweet. Only for premium dried tomato products.

For sauce and paste: Roma types. Look for 5–7 brix — higher is better.

2 Washing and sorting

Tomatoes arrive with soil, stems and possibly damaged fruit.

  1. Dump into a washing tank (or use a drum washer for volume)
  2. Add 100 ppm chlorine to the wash water
  3. Rub gently to remove soil (don't bruise)
  4. Rinse in clean water
  5. Sort on a rolling table — remove green, damaged, mouldy fruit
  6. Grade by ripeness (red = sauce, slightly orange = ketchup)

Use tomatoes within 24 hours of harvest. Refrigerate between 10–15°C if not processing immediately.

⚠ One mouldy tomato can spoil an entire 50 kg batch. Sort carefully.

3 Crushing and pulping

The goal is to break down tomatoes into juice + pulp, separating out skins and seeds.

  1. Cut tomatoes in half (removes core and any hard green parts)
  2. Blanch in steam at 90°C for 3 minutes (loosens skins)
  3. Pass through a pulper-finisher (or crush by hand for small batches)
  4. The pulper separates juice+pulp from skins and seeds
  5. Collect the juice

Yield: 100 kg fresh tomatoes → 65–75 kg juice/pulp.

Save the skins and seeds — they're valuable for seed saving or animal feed.

4 Sauce and paste concentration

Tomato sauce (passata): 8–10 brix. Cook juice for 30 minutes, stirring.

Tomato paste: 28–30 brix. Cook juice for 2–3 hours until reduced to 1/4 volume. You can tell by colour — deep red, thick.

Concentrate: 12–15 brix. Middle ground.

The simplest test: drop a spoonful on a cold plate — it should hold its shape without running.

Yield: 100 kg juice → 25–30 kg paste (28 brix).

💡 Add 0.5% salt and 0.3% citric acid before cooking. Both preserve and improve taste.

5 Ketchup production

Ketchup is tomato paste + sugar + vinegar + spices. Highly profitable retail product.

Recipe per 10 kg paste:

  • 10 kg tomato paste (28 brix)
  • 1.5 kg sugar
  • 1.2 L white vinegar (5%)
  • 200 g salt
  • Spice bag: onion powder, garlic, cloves, cinnamon, paprika
  • 0.1% potassium sorbate (preservative)

Simmer 20 minutes, remove spices, hot-fill at 90°C into bottles.

Retail: $3–$6 per 500 ml bottle. Big market.

6 Sun-dried tomatoes

Sun-dried tomatoes are a premium product — high margin, low volume.

  1. Cut Roma tomatoes in half lengthwise
  2. Salt lightly and place cut-side up on drying racks
  3. Sun-dry 4–7 days (or dehydrate at 60°C for 8–12 hours)
  4. Target moisture: 15–18%
  5. Pack in oil (olive or sunflower) or vacuum-seal dry

Yield: 100 kg fresh → 6–9 kg dried.

Retail: $25–$50 per kg. Oil-packed: $40–$70 per kg jar.

7 Acidification and safety

Tomatoes are borderline acidic (pH 4.0–4.6). To be safely canned, they must be acidified.

  • Target pH: Below 4.6
  • Add: 1 tbsp lemon juice or 1/4 tsp citric acid per 500 ml jar
  • Test: pH meter or pH strips
  • Hot fill: 90°C into sterilised jars
  • Process: Water-bath 35 minutes (for pint jars)

Never skip acidification — botulism risk with low-acid tomatoes.

⚠ Modern tomato varieties are less acidic than heirloom ones. Always test pH.

8 Bottling and pasteurisation

Sealing and pasteurising the finished product is critical for shelf life.

  1. Sterilise glass jars in boiling water for 10 minutes
  2. Fill with hot sauce/paste, leaving 1 cm headspace
  3. Wipe rims clean
  4. Seal with new lids
  5. Process in water bath: 35 minutes for 500 ml, 45 minutes for 1 L
  6. Cool overnight — check seals (lid should be concave and not flex)

Shelf life: 12–24 months unopened. Refrigerate after opening.

💰 Tomato is the #1 processed vegetable worldwide. Sauce and ketchup are the biggest markets. Start with sauce, add ketchup once volume grows.
