1. Booking a Hotel Room

Booking a hotel in Chinese is straightforward if you know the key phrases. Start by learning 'I want to book a room' (w xing dng y g fngjin). For dates, use ...... (cng...d o...) meaning 'from...to...'. For example, (cng w yu y h o d o w yu sn h o) means from May 1st to May 3rd. Specify room type: (dn rn jin) single room, (shung rn jin) double room, (bio zhn jin) standard room. Ask for price: ? (dusho qin y wn?) How much per night? If you need a cheaper option, say ? (yu mi yu piny y din de?) Do you have a cheaper one? In many African cities like Nairobi or Lagos, you can use these phrases when calling Chinese-run hotels or using apps like Booking.com with Chinese interface. Always confirm: ? (hn zo cn ma?) Is breakfast included? And Wi-Fi? (yu min fi Wi-Fi ma?) Is there free Wi-Fi? For payment, ask ? (k y shu k ma?) Can I pay by card? If you need to cancel, say (w xing q xio y dng) I want to cancel my reservation. Practice with a friend: one plays receptionist, the other books a room for a business trip to Guangzhou or a holiday in Cape Town. Write down your own dates and room preferences. Use a translation app like Pleco to check tones. Remember, Chinese numbers are essential: y, r, sn, s, w, li, q, b, ji, sh. Combine them for 11 to 99: sh y, r sh, etc. For dates, say (h o) for day of month. So May 1st is . For months, (yu) means month: January, February, etc. Practice saying your check-in and check-out dates aloud. Then, when you call or message a hotel, you'll be understood. Many hotels in Africa that cater to Chinese tourists have Mandarin-speaking staff, so this skill is directly useful. Also, learn to ask for a quiet room: ? (yu mi yu n jng de fngjin?) This helps if you need to work or rest. Finally, always repeat back the details to confirm: , , , ? (z i qu rn y xi , w yu y h o r zh, w yu sn h o tu fng, du ma?) Let me confirm: check-in May 1st, check-out May 3rd, correct? This avoids mistakes. With these phrases, you can book confidently.

2. Checking In and Out

Checking in and out of a hotel in Chinese requires a few key phrases. At check-in, say (w yu y dng) I have a reservation. Then give your name: ... (w ji o...) My name is... If you don't have a reservation, say (w xing zh y wn) I want to stay one night. The receptionist may ask for your passport: (qng ch sh nn de h zh o) Please show your passport. Hand it over and say (gi n) Here you are. They might ask you to fill a form: (qng tin zh zhng bio) Please fill this form. Learn to write your name, nationality, and passport number in Chinese characters if possible, but you can also write in English. For check-out, say (w y o tu fng) I want to check out. They will ask ? (nn zh de zn me y ng?) How was your stay? You can reply , (hn ho, xi xi) Very good, thanks. Then they may say (zh sh nn de zh ng dn) Here is your bill. Check the total: ? (y gng du sho qin?) How much in total? If you need a receipt, say (qng gi w f pi o) Please give me a receipt. If you want to leave luggage after check-out, ask ? (w k y j cn xng l ma?) Can I store my luggage? They may say , (k y, min fi) Yes, free. Or (shu fi) For a fee. For late check-out, ask ? (k y wn din tu fng ma?) Can I check out later? Usually until 2 PM is possible. In many African hotels, check-in time is 2 PM and check-out is 12 PM. If you arrive early, ask ? (k y t qin r zh ma?) Can I check in early? They might say (fng jin hi mi zhn bi ho) The room is not ready yet. Then you can wait in the lobby: (w z i d tng dng) I'll wait in the lobby. Practice these dialogues with a partner. Use role-play: one is the guest, one is the receptionist. Switch roles. Also, learn to ask for help with your bags: (qng bng w n xng l) Please help me with my luggage. Tipping is not mandatory but appreciated. A simple is enough. Remember to always be polite: use (qng) please, (xi xi) thank you, and (du b q) sorry. These go a long way. With these phrases, you can handle check-in and check-out smoothly in any Chinese-speaking environment, whether in Africa or abroad.

3. Asking About Hotel Amenities

When staying in a hotel, you need to know what facilities are available. Start with the question ? (ji di n yu shn me sh sh?) What facilities does the hotel have? Common amenities include (yu yng ch) swimming pool, (ji n shn fng) gym, (cn tng) restaurant, (ji b) bar, (x y f w) laundry service, and (hu y sh) meeting room. To ask if they have a specific amenity, say ...? (yu...ma?) For example, ? (yu yu yng ch ma?) Is there a swimming pool? If you need to know the hours, ask ? (yu yng ch j din ki mn?) What time does the pool open? Or ? (cn tng j din ki sh gng yng zo cn?) What time does the restaurant start serving breakfast? For Wi-Fi, ask Wi-Fi? (fng jin l yu Wi-Fi ma?) Is there Wi-Fi in the room? And ? (m m sh du sho?) What is the password? For laundry, ask ? (x y f w zn me shu fi?) How much does laundry service cost? If you need an iron, ask ? (yu yn du ma?) Is there an iron? For a hairdryer, ? (yu chu fng j ma?) Is there a hairdryer? If you want to know about airport shuttle, ask ? (yu j chng ji sng ma?) Is there airport shuttle service? For parking, ? (yu tng ch chng ma?) Is there a parking lot? In many African hotels, amenities may be limited. So also learn to ask ? (f jn yu cho sh ma?) Is there a supermarket nearby? Or ? (f jn yu cn gun ma?) Is there a restaurant nearby? If something is not working, say (kng tio hu i le) The air conditioner is broken. Or (mi yu r shu) There is no hot water. Then ask ? (k y xi y xi ma?) Can you fix it? Practice by making a list of amenities you care about. Then ask about each one. Use a dictionary app to find words for specific items. When you travel, take a photo of the hotel's business card so you can show the address to a taxi driver. Also, learn to say (w y o q ji di n) I want to go to the hotel. With these phrases, you can make the most of your stay and get what you need.

4. Travel Destinations and Attractions

Talking about travel destinations in Chinese opens up conversations. Start with ... (w xing q...) I want to go to... For example, (w xing q chng chng) I want to go to the Great Wall. In Africa, popular destinations for Chinese tourists include (i j jn z t) Egyptian Pyramids, (nn fi ho w ng jio) Cape of Good Hope in South Africa, (kn n y m s i m l) Maasai Mara in Kenya, and (tn sng n y q l m zh lu shn) Mount Kilimanjaro in Tanzania. To ask about attractions, say ? (zh l yu shn me ho wn de d fng?) What fun places are there here? Or ? (f jn yu shn me jng din?) What scenic spots are nearby? For directions, ask ? (zn me q?) How to get there? You can say (zu ch z ch) by taxi, (zu gng jio ch) by bus, or (zu d ti) by subway. For opening hours, ask ? (j din ki mn?) What time does it open? And ? (mn pi o du sho qin?) How much is the ticket? If you want a guide, ask ? (yu do yu ma?) Is there a guide? For photos, say ? (k y pi zh o ma?) Can I take photos? To express admiration, say ! (t i mi le!) So beautiful! Or ! (zhn zhu ng gun!) Really spectacular! When planning a trip, use phrases like ... (w d su n q...) I plan to go to... and ? (shn me sh hu q zu ho?) When is the best time to go? For example, , (q kn n y k n dng w d qin x, q yu d o ji yu zu ho) To see the Great Migration in Kenya, July to September is best. Practice by describing your dream trip. Write a short paragraph in Chinese. Use a map app in Chinese to find places. Learn the names of African countries in Chinese: (n r l y ) Nigeria, (ji n ) Ghana, (i si b y ) Ethiopia, etc. This helps when talking to Chinese tourists or business partners. Also, learn to ask for recommendations: ? (n yu shn me tu ji n?) What do you recommend? With these phrases, you can discuss travel plans and make your trips more enjoyable.

5. Buying Train and Plane Tickets

Buying tickets in Chinese is a practical skill. For train tickets, go to the train station or use an app like 12306 (China's official train app). Say ... (w y o mi y zhng q...de hu ch pi o) I want to buy a train ticket to... For example, (w y o mi y zhng q ni lu b de hu ch pi o) I want to buy a train ticket to Nairobi. Specify date and time: (mng tin de) tomorrow's, (sh ng w) morning, (xi w) afternoon. For class: (yng zu) hard seat, (run zu) soft seat, (yng w) hard sleeper, (run w) soft sleeper. For plane tickets, say ... (w y o mi y zhng q...de fi j pi o) I want to buy a plane ticket to... Ask for price: ? (du sho qin?) How much? For one-way or round-trip: (dn chng) one-way, (wng fn) round-trip. For direct flight: (zh fi) direct flight. For transfer: (zhun j) transfer. If you need to change or cancel, say (w xing gi qin) I want to change my ticket, or (w xing tu pi o) I want to refund. For passport, say (zh sh w de h zh o) This is my passport. In Africa, you can buy tickets at the airport or through travel agents. Learn to ask ? (yu zh ku ma?) Is there a discount? For students or seniors, ask ? (yu xu shng pi o ma?) Is there a student ticket? For luggage, ask ? (k y d i du sho xng l?) How much luggage can I bring? If you need a window seat, say (w xing y o k o chung de zu wi) I want a window seat. For aisle seat: (k o gu d o de zu wi). Practice by role-playing at a ticket counter. One person is the seller, one is the buyer. Use real dates and destinations. Also, learn to read train and flight numbers: (ch c) train number, (hng bn) flight number. When you hear the announcement, you can understand: ... (ki wng...de li ch) train bound for... With these phrases, you can travel by train or plane confidently in Chinese-speaking regions or with Chinese-speaking agents.

6. Role-Play: At the Hotel Front Desk

This role-play simulates a conversation at a hotel front desk. Practice with a partner. One plays the guest, one the receptionist. Use the phrases from previous lessons. Start with the guest: , (n ho, w yu y dng) Hello, I have a reservation. Receptionist: ? (qng wn nn gu xng?) May I ask your surname? Guest: (w xng wng) My surname is Wang. Receptionist: , (qng sho dng, w ch y xi ) Please wait, let me check. Then: , , , ? (wng xin shng, nn y dng le y g dn rn jin, cng w yu y h o d o sn h o, du ma?) Mr. Wang, you booked a single room from May 1st to 3rd, correct? Guest: (du) Correct. Receptionist: (qng ch sh nn de h zh o) Please show your passport. Guest hands it over: (gi n) Here you are. Receptionist: (qng tin zh zhng bio) Please fill this form. Guest fills it. Receptionist: , 305 (zh sh nn de fng k, fng jin h o sh sn lng w) This is your room card, room number 305. Guest: , ? (xi xi, qng wn zo cn j din?) Thank you, what time is breakfast? Receptionist: (zo shang q din d o sh din) 7 to 10 AM. Guest: Wi-Fi? (yu Wi-Fi ma?) Is there Wi-Fi? Receptionist: , 12345678 (yu, m m sh y r sn s w li q b) Yes, password is 12345678. Guest: , (ho de, xi xi) Okay, thanks. Now practice check-out. Guest: (w y o tu fng) I want to check out. Receptionist: , (qng sho dng, zh sh nn de zh ng dn) Please wait, here is your bill. Guest: ? (y gng du sho qin?) How much in total? Receptionist: 500 (y gng w bi yun) 500 yuan total. Guest: ? (k y shu k ma?) Can I pay by card? Receptionist: (k y) Yes. Guest pays. Receptionist: (zh sh nn de f pi o) Here is your receipt. Guest: , ? (xi xi, w k y j cn xng l ma?) Thanks, can I store my luggage? Receptionist: , (k y, min fi) Yes, free. Guest: , (t i ho le, xi xi) Great, thanks. Switch roles and repeat. Add variations: ask for a late check-out, report a broken AC, ask for a taxi. This role-play builds confidence. Record yourself and listen for tones. Use apps like HelloChinese or ChineseSkill for more practice. With repetition, these dialogues become automatic.

<h2>1. Booking a Hotel Room ()</h2>
<p>Book before you fly, and book in Chinese where possible. Platforms such as Ctrip () and Meituan () list business hotels near exhibition centres like the Canton Fair Complex in Guangzhou or the National Exhibition and Convention Center in Shanghai. For agribusiness buyers, prioritise hotels with reliable Wi-Fi, a business centre for printing contracts, and a 24-hour front desk essential when your flight from Nairobi, Lagos or Addis Ababa lands after midnight.</p>

<p><strong>Core phrases:</strong> (w xing ydng y jin fng I would like to book a room); / / (dn rn jin / shung rn jin / t ofng single / double / suite); XXXX (from [date] to [date]); (hn zof n ma is breakfast included?); (ky ki fpi o ma can you issue a VAT invoice?). The last question matters: Chinese companies need a (fpi o) to reimburse suppliers, and your African company may need it for tax records.</p>

<h2>2. Checking In and Out ()</h2>
<p>At check-in, present your passport and visa. Say: (w yu ydng I have a reservation); (w ji o my name is); (qngwn j din tufng what time is check-out?). Standard check-out in China is 12:00 noon; late check-out (, ynch tufng) is often chargeable by the hour. If you need to store luggage before an evening train, ask: (ky jcn xngli ma can I store my luggage?).</p>

<p>On check-out, request the invoice () and a detailed bill (, zh ngdn). If you consumed the minibar or used the laundry, verify charges before paying. Business travellers should also ask for a (zhs zhngmng accommodation certificate), sometimes required for visa extensions or company reimbursement.</p>

<h2>3. Asking About Hotel Amenities ()</h2>
<p>Agribusiness trips are working trips. You will need to know whether the hotel can support late-night calls to your farm managers, print shipping documents, or arrange a car to a cold-storage facility. Useful questions: Wi-Fi(yu minfi Wi-Fi ma free Wi-Fi?); (shngw zhngxn z i nl where is the business centre?); (yu ji nshnfng ma gym?); (ky ji oxng fw ma wake-up call?); (fjn yu cntng ma restaurants nearby?).</p>

<p>For halal or vegetarian needs common for travellers from Muslim-majority African countries ask: (yu qngzhn cntng ma is there a halal restaurant?) or (yu ssh ma vegetarian food?). Larger Chinese cities such as Yiwu, Guangzhou and Xi'an have established halal restaurants and mosques, but smaller agricultural hubs may not.</p>

<h2>4. Travel Destinations and Attractions ()</h2>
<p>Combine business with targeted site visits. If you are sourcing tea-processing machinery, a detour to Hangzhou or Fujian is worthwhile. If you are in agrochemicals, visit the Yangtze River Delta industrial parks. Key destination vocabulary: (jngdin scenic spot); (bwgun museum); (nngy zhnlnhu agricultural exhibition); (pf shchng wholesale market). Ask: (znme q how do I get there?); (mnpi o dushao qin ticket price?); (kif ng shjin sh shnme opening hours?).</p>

<p>For African buyers, the most commercially relevant "attractions" are often production bases, wholesale markets and testing laboratories rather than classical gardens. Build these into your itinerary and confirm access in advance with your Chinese host.</p>

<h2>5. Buying Train and Plane Tickets ()</h2>
<p>China's high-speed rail network (, goti) is faster and more reliable than domestic flights for journeys under 1,200 km. Tickets can be bought on 12306 (the official railway app, available in English), Trip.com, or at station counters. You must present your passport; Chinese ID is not required for foreign travellers, but your passport number must match the booking exactly.</p>

<p><strong>Train vocabulary:</strong> / / (ydngzu / rdngzu / shngwzu first / second / business class); (wp sleeper); (goti high-speed); (dngch bullet train); (huchzh n railway station); (tupi o refund); (giqin change ticket).</p>

<p><strong>Flight vocabulary:</strong> / / (jngjcng / shngwcng / tudngcng economy / business / first class); / (dnchng / wngfn one-way / return); (xngli tuyn checked baggage); (dngjku boarding gate); (hngbn ynw flight delay).</p>

<p style="margin:0; color:#b30000; font-weight:bold;"> SAFETY WARNING Visa, Registration and Payment</p>
<p style="margin:8px 0 0 0; color:#333;">Foreign travellers in China must register their accommodation with local police within 24 hours of arrival. Hotels do this automatically; if you stay in a private residence, you must register at the local police station () yourself. Carry your passport at all times it is required for hotel check-in, train boarding and police checks. Never overstay your visa: penalties include fines of up to RMB 10,000, detention and future entry bans. For payments, WeChat Pay and Alipay now accept international cards, but carry some cash (RMB) for small vendors and taxis. Keep digital and paper copies of your passport, visa and hotel bookings in case of loss.</p>
</div>
