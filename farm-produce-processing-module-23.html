Garlic Processing

8 lessons · 30 quiz questions · Fruit Vegetables

1 Garlic varieties

Garlic (Allium sativum) comes in two main types:

Hardneck: Cold-hardy, single ring of cloves around a central stalk. Stronger flavour. Best for cold climates.

Softneck: Warmer climates, multiple rings of cloves, no central stalk. Stores longer. Best for braiding and processing in warm regions.

Varieties: California Early, Italian Late (softneck); Music, Spanish Roja (hardneck).

For Africa and warm markets, softneck varieties are ideal — better storage and processing yield.

2 Curing and drying

Fresh garlic must be cured before processing — this develops flavour and extends shelf life.

  1. Harvest when lower leaves turn brown but tops still green
  2. Shake off soil — do not wash
  3. Bundle and hang in a dry, well-ventilated, shaded area
  4. Cure 3–6 weeks until skins are papery
  5. Trim roots and cut tops to 3 cm

Well-cured garlic stores 6–9 months at 0–4°C, 60–70% humidity.

⚠ Don't cure garlic in direct sun — it gets sunscald and won't store.

3 Peeling methods

Peeling is the bottleneck in garlic processing.

Manual: Crush clove with knife, peel skin. 2 kg/hour per person.

Shake method: Two metal bowls, shake cloves hard — skins separate. 15 kg/hour.

Hot water blanch: Boil 30 seconds, then ice bath. Skins slip. Fast.

Mechanical peeler: 100+ kg/hour. $500–$3,000 depending on size.

For volume production, blanch-and-peel is the sweet spot: cheap, fast, good quality.

4 Garlic powder

Garlic powder is a top-selling spice globally.

  1. Peel garlic cloves
  2. Slice 2 mm thick
  3. Dehydrate at 55°C for 8–10 hours
  4. Target moisture: 5–6%
  5. Mill in a hammer mill
  6. Sieve through 250 micron
  7. Package in dark jars or foil bags

Yield: 100 kg fresh garlic → 25–30 kg dried → 22–27 kg powder.

Retail: $15–$35 per kg. Bulk: $8–$15 per kg.

5 Garlic paste

Garlic paste is a huge market in Africa, India and the Middle East.

  1. Peel garlic cloves
  2. Blend with oil (10–15%) and salt (2–3%) into smooth paste
  3. Add 0.1% citric acid and 0.1% potassium sorbate
  4. Hot-fill into jars at 85°C
  5. Seal and cool

Yield: 100 kg fresh → 110 kg paste (oil adds weight).

Retail: $5–$10 per 200 g jar. Big market with diaspora and food service.

6 Pickled and fermented garlic

Pickled garlic: Peel cloves, pack in jars, cover with brine (vinegar + water + sugar + salt). Seal, refrigerate 2 weeks. Retails $8–$15 per jar.

Fermented black garlic: Whole bulbs aged at 60°C and 70% humidity for 30–40 days. Cloves turn black, sweet, with umami. Sells for $50–$120 per kg in health food markets. Takes patience and temperature control.

Honey garlic: Garlic cloves in honey, fermented 2–4 weeks. Popular wellness product.

7 Garlic oil infusion

Garlic-infused oil is a cooking product with strong retail demand.

  1. Use dried garlic only (fresh causes botulism risk)
  2. Warm olive or sunflower oil to 60°C
  3. Add 10% dried garlic flakes
  4. Infuse 2 hours at 60°C
  5. Strain through fine cloth
  6. Bottle in amber glass

Retail: $12–$25 per 250 ml bottle.

⚠ Never make garlic-in-oil with fresh garlic at room temperature. Botulism risk. Use dried garlic and heat.

8 Retail packaging

Packaging by product:

  • Whole cured garlic — mesh bags (500 g, 1 kg)
  • Peeled fresh — vacuum packs (500 g)
  • Powder — dark glass jars, foil pouches (50 g–1 kg)
  • Paste — glass jars (200 g, 500 g)
  • Pickled — glass jars (350 g)
  • Black garlic — vacuum-sealed pouches (100 g)
  • Infused oil — dark glass bottles (250 ml)

Export requirements: Phytosanitary certificate, pesticide residue test, food safety cert. Garlic powder to EU/US often requires steam sterilisation for microbial safety.

💰 Highest-margin garlic products: black garlic, garlic powder, infused oils. Fresh garlic is low margin.
