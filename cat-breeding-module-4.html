4. Feeding Cats for Health and Reproduction

Cats are obligate carnivores. Feeding them right is 40-60% of running a breeding cattery.

Module 4 of 12 · Cat Breeding for Profit

Why feeding cats is different

Cats are obligate carnivores. They cannot survive on plant-based diets. They need specific nutrients that only come from animal sources:

NutrientWhy cats need it
TaurineHeart, eyes, reproduction - cats cannot make it
Arachidonic acidCell membranes - only from animal fat
Vitamin A (preformed)Cats cannot convert beta-carotene
NiacinCats cannot convert tryptophan efficiently
ArginineDetoxifies ammonia - deficiency is fatal
High proteinCats need 2-3x the protein of dogs or humans

Feed cost is 40-60% of running a cattery. Getting it right shows directly in kitten health and weight.

Target protein (dry matter): adult 35-40%, kitten 40-50%, lactating queen 45-50%.

Never feed cats these foods. Toxic: onions, garlic, chives, chocolate, grapes, raisins, alcohol, coffee, raw yeast dough, dog food (lacks taurine), cow milk (most cats are lactose intolerant).

Raw feeding benefits: shinier coat, cleaner teeth, smaller stool, more energy. Risks: bacterial contamination, unbalanced diet, bones causing injury if not size-appropriate.

Special feeding for reproduction

Breeding queen - before mating

  • Feed to body condition score 3 (ribs felt but not seen).
  • Reduce carbohydrates slightly 2 weeks before mating - lean queens conceive better.
  • Ensure adequate vitamin E and taurine - critical for fertility.

Pregnancy

Week of pregnancyFeed
Week 1-4Normal maintenance diet
Week 5-6Gradually increase 10-25%, switch to kitten food
Week 7-9Increase 25-50%, split into smaller meals
Do not overfeed a pregnant queen. Fat queens have more difficult queening and higher stillborn rates. Target BCS 3 at queening.

Lactation

  • Feed free-choice or 4-5 small meals per day.
  • Kitten food for the higher protein and fat.
  • Constant access to fresh water - lactating queens drink 2-3x normal.
  • Continue high-quality feeding until kittens are weaned.

Stud male

  • Maintain BCS 3.
  • Extra protein and taurine.
  • Do not overfeed - fat studs have lower fertility and difficulty mating.

Kittens

  • Introduce solid food at 3-4 weeks (gruel - wet food + kitten milk replacer).
  • Feed 4 meals per day from 6-12 weeks.
  • Kitten food until 12 months (large breeds 15 months).
  • Free access to dry food + 3-4 wet meals.

Water - critical for cats

  • Cats have a naturally low thirst drive - they evolved as desert animals.
  • They get most of their water from food in the wild.
  • Dry-fed cats are chronically under-hydrated - a major cause of urinary problems.
  • Provide fresh water in multiple places.
  • Use wide, ceramic, or stainless steel bowls - not plastic (retains odours).
  • Consider a water fountain - cats drink more from moving water.
  • Add water to wet food - "gravy".

The feed rule: Feed to body condition, not to a fixed amount. A cat that looks right is being fed right. Adjust portions weekly based on ribs, waist, and energy.
Practical exercise 4: Weigh your adult cat. Body condition score 1-5. Adjust portions so every adult is at score 3. Record weekly weights for 4 weeks.
