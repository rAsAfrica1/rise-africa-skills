🧠 Brain Opener

Two identical seeds. One in a plain plastic bag. One in a labelled, branded packet. The branded one sells for 5 times more. The seed is the same.

  • What must a legal seed packet include?
  • Why does branding matter for seed?
  • What is the cheapest professional packaging?
🎁 Reveal a strong answer

A legal packet must include: variety name, batch number, germination percentage, moisture content, net weight, producer name and contact, production date, and certification body. Branding matters because trust is built visually. The cheapest professional packaging is a printed label applied to a zip-lock bag with a heat sealer.

📖 Rudo Designs a Packet

Rudo had 20 kilograms of certified organic kale seed ready to sell. She had been selling it in plain transparent zip-lock bags with a marker label. Buyers paid 25 dollars per kilogram.

Her cousin came to visit from Nairobi. She brought a professional seed packet from a Kenyan company.

The packet had a photo of a kale plant. A green and gold banner. A logo. The variety name in big letters. Small text at the bottom with batch, germination, moisture, and the company name.

Rudo looked at her plastic bag and then at the Kenyan packet. The quality of the seed was the same. The quality of the presentation was 20 times different.

She asked her cousin how much it cost to make the packet. Her cousin said: 15 cents per packet when printed in hundreds.

Rudo had 400 packets worth of seed. 400 packets at 15 cents each: 60 dollars.

She designed the packet. She hired a local printer. She printed 500.

The design was simple. Her farm name in a gold band. A photo of kale. Variety name in bold. Batch number in the corner. Legal information on the back.

She filled the first 100 packets and took them to a farmers market in Harare.

By midday she had sold 60 packets at 3 dollars each - for 10-gram packets. That is 300 dollars per kilogram, not 25.

Same seed. Different packaging. Twelve times the price.

💡 The Big Idea

Packaging is the price you command

Seed is sold in two ways: bulk (by kilogram, low margin) and packaged (by gram, high margin). A plastic bag of seed sells as bulk. A branded packet sells as a product. The packaging costs a few cents. The price difference is hundreds of percent.

📚 Three Deep Lessons

1

What must appear on a legal seed packet

Every African country has minimum legal requirements. Most require: variety name, batch number, germination percentage, moisture content, net weight, production date, producer name and address, and contact information.

Additional requirements for organic: certification body name and certificate number. Some countries require purity percentage. Some require a physical address.

Check with your national seed authority for the exact list. Fines for missing information can be heavy.

2

The design of trust

Buyers judge seed quality by the packet before they open it. A packet that looks professional is assumed to be good seed. A packet that looks homemade is assumed to be risky.

Five design elements build trust: a clear photograph of the plant, a strong colour (green, gold, and white are standard), a clean font, a batch number visible, and a certification mark if applicable.

Do not overload the packet. Five pieces of information visible. The rest can go on the back. Simple designs look more professional than crowded ones.

3

Printing and filling at low cost

A local printer in any African city can print a full-colour design on 200-gram paper for 10 to 20 cents per packet in quantities of 500 or more. Set up a template with the printer - not the whole design every time, just the template. Then print the batch-specific information yourself.

Filling is done by hand: weigh the seed on a small scale (10g, 25g, 50g, 100g), pour into the packet, seal with heat or tape. A heat sealer costs 30 to 50 dollars and pays for itself in one season.

Standard sizes: 10g (gardeners), 25g (smallholder), 50g (large garden), 100g (small farm), 500g and 1kg (bulk to agro-dealers).

🛠 Hands-On - Design Your First Seed Packet

You need

  • A4 paper or card
  • Scissors
  • A pen
  • A zip-lock bag or paper envelope
  1. Draw your packet template on paper. Front and back.
  2. Front: plant photo or drawing, variety name, brand name.
  3. Back: batch number, germination, moisture, weight, production date, your name and phone.
  4. Add a certification mark if you have one.
  5. Take the drawing to a local printer and get a quote for 500 copies.
  6. Buy 500 zip-lock bags or heat-seal bags.
  7. Fill 10 test packets with seed.
  8. Show them to three potential buyers. Ask if they would pay a premium.
Why it works: Testing your packet with real buyers before printing 500 is free market research.

🌍 Real Stories from Africa

Kenya Seed Company packets

Kenya Seed Company prints 10 million seed packets a year. Their packet design has not changed in 20 years - photo, green banner, variety name. Consistency builds trust. Small producers can learn the same lesson with their own consistent design.

Rwanda small producer packaging

A Rwandan cooperative of small seed producers buys packets in bulk from a Kigali printer at 12 cents each. They fill by hand and sell at 3 dollars per 10-gram packet. The cooperative now sells 200,000 packets a year.

🏆 Project Challenge

Package 100 Units

Take 1 kilogram of seed and package it into 100 units of 10 grams each.

  1. Weigh 1 kilogram of clean, dry, tested seed.
  2. Buy or print 100 small packets.
  3. Buy 100 zip-lock bags or heat-seal bags.
  4. Fill each bag with 10 grams using a small scale.
  5. Seal, label, and stack.
  6. Price each packet at 3 dollars.
  7. Offer to 5 potential buyers.
  8. If 3 out of 5 buy, you have a product. If not, adjust.
