Lessons

1. Standard Cleaning Services: What to Offer

Standard cleaning is your bread and butter. It covers regular upkeep: dusting, sweeping, mopping, vacuuming, wiping surfaces, cleaning bathrooms and kitchens, and taking out trash. In African homes, this often includes sweeping courtyards, wiping down verandas, and cleaning outdoor furniture. For offices, it means emptying bins, sanitising desks, and cleaning shared spaces like kitchens and restrooms. You need basic tools: brooms, mops, buckets, cloths, disinfectant, glass cleaner, and a vacuum if you can afford one. Start with a checklist to ensure consistency. For a 3-bedroom home, standard cleaning takes 2-3 hours with one cleaner. For a small office (10 desks), about 1-2 hours. Price per session, not per hour, for standard jobsclients prefer flat rates. In Nairobi, a standard home clean might go for KES 1,500-2,500; in Lagos, NGN 8,000-15,000. Always ask about pets, children, and special surfaces (e.g., marble) to avoid damage. Offer add-ons like inside fridge or oven cleaning for extra fees. Remember: reliability beats perfection. Show up on time, use your own supplies, and leave a fresh scent. Thats how you get repeat clients.

2. Deep Cleaning and Specialized Services (Move-Out, Post-Construction)

Deep cleaning goes beyond surface level: scrubbing grout, behind appliances, inside cabinets, ceiling fans, light fixtures, and windows (inside and out). Move-out cleans are for tenants leaving a rentallandlords expect spotless. Post-construction cleans remove cement dust, paint splatters, and debris. These jobs take 2-3 times longer than standard. For a 2-bedroom move-out, budget 4-6 hours with two cleaners. Post-construction for a 3-bedroom house: 6-8 hours with a team of three. Tools: stiff brushes, scraper, heavy-duty degreaser, window squeegee, and a wet/dry vacuum. In South Africa, a move-out clean for a 2-bedroom flat costs R800-1,500; post-construction for a small house R1,500-3,000. In Ghana, similar jobs go for GHS 500-1,200. Always do a walkthrough with the client before quotinghidden messes cost you. Charge by the job, not by the hour, because deep cleans are unpredictable. Upsell: carpet shampooing, upholstery cleaning, and wall washing. These services build your reputation as a specialist and justify higher rates.

3. Pricing Models: Hourly, Flat Rate, and Square Footage

Three main pricing models: hourly, flat rate, and square footage. Hourly is simple: you charge per hour per cleaner. In Kenya, KES 300-500 per hour per cleaner; in Nigeria, NGN 1,500-2,500. But clients hate open-ended billsthey fear youll drag the job. Flat rate is a fixed price for a defined scope. Its the most popular for homes and offices because clients know the cost upfront. To set a flat rate, estimate hours hourly rate + supplies + profit margin (20-30%). Square footage works for large commercial spaces: charge per square meter, e.g., KES 50-100 per m in Nairobi, or NGN 200-400 in Lagos. For a 100 m office, thats KES 5,000-10,000. Square footage pricing is easy to quote but must account for furniture density and floor type. Many cleaners use a hybrid: flat rate for standard, square footage for big contracts. Always clarify whats includede.g., floors, dusting, bathrooms, kitchen but not inside oven. Get a signed agreement. Adjust for frequency: weekly clients get a discount (10-15%), one-time deep cleans cost more. Never underpriceyoull resent the work and cut corners.

4. Calculating Costs and Setting Profitable Rates

To set profitable rates, you must know your costs. Direct costs: labour (wages), supplies (detergents, cloths, mop heads), transport, and equipment depreciation. Indirect costs: phone, marketing, insurance, and admin time. Example: In Accra, a cleaner earns GHS 20 per hour. For a 3-hour job, labour = GHS 60. Supplies: GHS 10. Transport: GHS 15. Total direct cost = GHS 85. Add 30% overhead and profit = GHS 110. So quote GHS 120-150. If you charge GHS 80, you lose money. In Lagos, a cleaner earns NGN 1,000 per hour. A 4-hour job: labour NGN 4,000, supplies NGN 500, transport NGN 800 = NGN 5,300. With 30% markup, price at NGN 7,000. Always track your timemost new cleaners underestimate by 30%. Use a simple notebook or app like Wave to record expenses. Set a minimum job fee (e.g., KES 1,500) to avoid tiny jobs that eat your day. Review rates every 6 months. If youre fully booked, raise prices by 10%. If youre desperate, dont loweradd value instead (e.g., free fridge clean). Profitable rates let you pay fair wages, buy good supplies, and grow.

5. Creating Service Packages and Upsells

Packages make buying easy and increase your average sale. Offer three tiers: Basic, Standard, and Premium. Basic: sweep, mop, dust, trash, bathrooms. Standard: Basic + kitchen surfaces, inside microwave, and window sills. Premium: Standard + inside oven, inside fridge, and baseboards. Price Basic at KES 2,000, Standard at KES 3,000, Premium at KES 4,500 for a 2-bedroom in Nairobi. In Nigeria, Basic NGN 10,000, Standard NGN 15,000, Premium NGN 22,000. Upsells: carpet shampooing (KES 500 per room), upholstery cleaning (KES 1,000 per sofa), and wall washing (KES 800 per room). For offices, upsell sanitising high-touch points (door handles, light switches) and restroom deep clean. Always present the premium firstanchoring makes the standard look affordable. Train your team to ask: Would you like the inside oven cleaned for an extra KES 500? That one question can add 20% to your revenue. Bundle recurring services: Weekly cleaning at 10% off, plus one free deep clean per quarter. Use simple printed flyers or WhatsApp catalogues to show packages. Clients love clarity and choices. Upsells should solve a problem, not feel pushyfocus on health and time savings.

6. Adjusting Prices for Commercial Contracts and Recurring Clients

Commercial contracts are the backbone of a stable cleaning business. Offices, schools, clinics, and shops need daily or weekly cleaning. Price per square meter or per desk. For a 200 m office in Johannesburg, charge R3,000-5,000 per month for daily cleaning (after hours). In Nairobi, KES 30,000-50,000 per month for a similar size. Recurring residential clients: weekly cleaning gets 10-15% off the flat rate; bi-weekly gets 5-10% off. Why? Because you save on travel and marketing. Always sign a contract with scope, frequency, price, and payment terms (e.g., invoice on the 1st, payment within 7 days). For commercial, add a clause for price adjustments after 12 months (inflation). Offer value-added services: restroom sanitising, floor polishing, and window cleaning at a discount when bundled. For large contracts, consider a team of 2-3 cleaners. Example: A 3-cleaner team for a 500 m office in Lagos: monthly fee NGN 250,000-400,000. Calculate your costs: labour (NGN 150,000), supplies (NGN 20,000), transport (NGN 30,000), profit (NGN 50,000+). Never undercut to win a contractyoull regret it. Instead, offer a trial month at a slight discount, then adjust. Recurring clients are gold: they pay your bills. Treat them well with consistent quality and small freebies (e.g., occasional fridge clean).

7. Practical Reference

Practical Reference: Services and Pricing Strategies for Cleaning Businesses in Africa

This practical reference consolidates the core pricing and service-design decisions you must make to run a profitable home and office cleaning business in an African market. It is written for operators in cities such as Lagos, Nairobi, Accra, Kigali, Dar es Salaam, and Johannesburg, where informal competition is high, payment cycles vary, and clients range from single households to multinational commercial tenants. Use this module alongside the earlier lessons on Standard Cleaning Services, Deep Cleaning, Pricing Models, Cost Calculation, Service Packages, and Commercial Contracts.

The central business principle is simple: your price must cover labour, transport, consumables, equipment depreciation, downtime, tax, and profit in that order. Many African cleaning businesses fail not because they lack clients, but because they price below cost and scale losses. This reference gives you the numbers, structures, and checklists to avoid that trap.

Calculating Your True Cost Per Job

Before you quote, calculate your break-even cost. This is the minimum you must charge to avoid losing money. Use this formula:

True Cost = (Labour Hours Wage) + Transport + Consumables + Equipment Depreciation + Admin Overhead + Tax

Example (Nairobi, 3-bedroom deep clean):

  • 2 cleaners 6 hours $4/hour = $48
  • Transport (2 trips) = $8
  • Chemicals and consumables = $6
  • Equipment wear (machine, mops, cloths) = $4
  • Admin/phone/booking overhead = $3
  • Tax (16% VAT where applicable) = $11
  • Total cost = $80
  • Target margin 35% Quote price = $108$115

If you quoted $70 for this job, you would be subsidising the client. This is the single most common error in African cleaning SMEs.

SAFETY WARNING: Chemical Handling and Worker Protection

Cleaning chemicals commonly used in African markets including bleach (sodium hypochlorite), hydrochloric acid descalers, and ammonia-based glass cleaners can cause severe respiratory damage, chemical burns, and poisoning when mixed or used without ventilation. Never mix bleach with ammonia or acid. Always provide nitrile gloves, eye protection, and N95 masks for deep cleaning and post-construction work. Ensure workers are trained on Safety Data Sheets (SDS) and that premises are ventilated. In many African jurisdictions, failure to provide PPE violates occupational health and safety law (e.g., Kenya's OSHA 2007, Nigeria's Factories Act, South Africa's OHS Act 85 of 1993).

Creating Service Packages and Upsells

Packages increase average job value without increasing marketing cost. Structure them in three tiers:

  • Essential (Standard Clean): Entry price. Weekly or bi-weekly. Low margin, high volume.
  • Premium (Deep Clean + Standard): Quarterly deep clean bundled with routine visits. Higher margin.
  • Elite (Full Property Care): Deep clean + window cleaning + fridge/oven + balcony + pest-control coordination. Premium pricing.

Upsells to train your team to offer:

  1. Inside oven cleaning (+$15$25)
  2. Inside fridge cleaning (+$10$18)
  3. Interior window cleaning (+$12$20)
  4. Mattress vacuuming and deodorising (+$10$15)
  5. Balcony/patio pressure washing (+$20$40)
  6. Laundry and ironing add-on (+$8$15)
  7. Monthly subscription discount lock-in (reduces churn)

Adjusting Prices for Commercial and Recurring Clients

Commercial clients offices, clinics, schools, Airbnb managers want predictable invoices and SLAs. Price them differently:

  • Offer 1020% discount on the one-off equivalent, but lock them into a 612 month contract.
  • Charge a mobilisation fee for the first deep clean before daily service begins.
  • Include a price adjustment clause tied to fuel or inflation (e.g., "rates reviewed every 6 months against CPI").
  • Invoice monthly in advance where possible. In markets with slow payment, require a deposit or first-month upfront.
  • Never discount below your true cost to win a contract. A loss-making contract is worse than no contract.

Quick Reference Checklist

  • Have you written a clear scope of work for every service type?
  • Have you calculated true cost (labour + transport + chemicals + equipment + admin + tax) before quoting?
  • Is your margin at least 30% on one-off jobs and 20% on recurring contracts?
  • Do you have a signed contract or written agreement for commercial clients?
  • Are your staff trained on chemical safety and provided with PPE?
  • Do you review prices every 6 months against fuel and inflation?
  • Are you tracking actual hours vs. quoted hours to refine future pricing?
  • Do you have at least two upsell services offered on every job?
