Mango Processing

8 lessons · 30 quiz questions · Fruits

1 Mango varieties

Mango (Mangifera indica) is one of Africa's biggest fruit crops. Over 100 varieties exist, but for processing only a few matter.

Kent: Sweet, fibreless, deep orange flesh. Best for pulp and juice.

Tommy Atkins: Firm, resistant to bruising. Best for export and pulp.

Keitt: Large, sweet, fibreless. Best for pulp and drying.

Apple mango (local African): Small, fibrous but very sweet. Best for juice and local pulp.

Ngowe, Sabre, Peach (Zimbabwe): Local varieties for juice and drying.

For juice and pulp, choose fibreless varieties (Kent, Keitt, Tommy). For local markets, use available sweet varieties.

2 Peeling and pulping

Mango has a large stone in the middle — separating flesh from stone is the key processing step.

  1. Wash mangoes thoroughly
  2. Sort by ripeness — processing mangoes should be firm-ripe
  3. Peel with a knife or steam peeler (30 seconds of steam loosens skin)
  4. Cut flesh from stone (two "cheeks" plus the two thin side pieces)
  5. Cut off any fibrous parts near the stone
  6. Blend or pass through a pulper-finisher to remove remaining fibres

Yield: 100 kg fresh mangoes → 55–65 kg pulp.

⚠ Stone and skin are 30–40% of mango weight. Plan for this in your costing.

3 Mango juice and nectar

Mango juice is one of the top 5 juices globally. Africa is a major producer of mango pulp for export.

Mango juice (100%):

  1. Use fresh mango pulp
  2. Add water (1 part pulp to 1.5 parts water)
  3. Add sugar to reach 12–14 brix
  4. Add citric acid to reach pH 3.6–3.8
  5. Pasteurise at 90°C for 30 seconds
  6. Hot-fill into bottles

Mango nectar (30–50% pulp): Same process but less pulp. Cheaper, popular in Africa.

Yield: 100 kg pulp → 250 L juice or 200 L nectar.

Retail: $1.50–$3 per litre for juice; $0.80–$1.50 for nectar.

4 Mango pulp for export

Mango pulp (also called mango puree) is a major export commodity — sold to juice companies, yoghurt makers, and food manufacturers.

  1. Process mangoes as above
  2. Refine pulp — pass through fine sieve
  3. Add 0.1% ascorbic acid (prevents browning)
  4. Heat to 90°C
  5. Fill into aseptic bags or cans
  6. Cool rapidly

Two forms:

  • Aseptic pulp: Sterilised, in 200 kg drums. 12-month shelf life at room temp. Exported worldwide.
  • Frozen pulp: Frozen at −18°C in bags or drums. 18-month shelf life.

Export price: $1.20–$2.50 per kg FOB. Top buyers: EU, US, Middle East.

💰 Aseptic mango pulp exports are worth billions globally. Africa produces a fraction of world supply — huge opportunity for organised processors.

5 Dried mango slices

Dried mango is a premium snack — sweet, chewy, and profitable.

  1. Peel mangoes
  2. Cut into 5 mm slices (uniform for even drying)
  3. Soak in 20% sugar syrup with citric acid (0.5%) for 4 hours (osmotic pre-treatment)
  4. Drain and pat dry
  5. Dry at 55°C for 10–14 hours
  6. Target moisture: 15–18% (chewy, not brittle)
  7. Cool and pack in foil pouches

Yield: 100 kg fresh → 12–18 kg dried.

Retail: $25–$50 per kg. Premium organic: $40–$70 per kg.

6 Mango chutney

Mango chutney is a classic Indian and African condiment. Simple to make, long shelf life.

  1. Use slightly under-ripe mangoes (firmer texture)
  2. Peel and dice into 1 cm cubes
  3. Cook with vinegar, sugar, ginger, garlic, chilli, raisins
  4. Simmer 1 hour until thick
  5. Fill into jars at 90°C
  6. Seal and cool

Yield: 100 kg mangoes → 130 kg chutney (added vinegar and sugar).

Retail: $6–$12 per 350 g jar.

7 Mango jam

Mango jam is a value-added product with strong retail demand.

  1. Use ripe mango pulp
  2. Add sugar (1 kg sugar per 1.5 kg pulp)
  3. Add lemon juice (juice of 2 lemons per kg)
  4. Add commercial pectin (0.5%)
  5. Simmer until thick (65 brix)
  6. Fill into jars at 90°C
  7. Seal and cool

Yield: 100 kg pulp → 130–140 kg jam.

Retail: $6–$12 per 350 g jar.

8 Packaging and cold chain

Packaging by product:

  • Fresh mangoes — mesh bags or boxes
  • Juice — glass or PET bottles (250 ml–1 L)
  • Aseptic pulp — 200 kg sterile drums or bags
  • Frozen pulp — 1 kg, 5 kg bags
  • Dried slices — foil pouches (100 g–1 kg)
  • Chutney/jam — glass jars (350 g)

Storage:

  • Fresh mangoes: 10–13°C, 5–7 days
  • Juice: 12 months unopened
  • Aseptic pulp: 12 months at room temp
  • Frozen pulp: 18 months at −18°C
  • Dried slices: 12 months in foil
  • Chutney/jam: 24 months unopened
💰 Highest-margin products: dried mango slices ($25–50/kg) and aseptic pulp for export. Mango juice is high volume, moderate margin.
