🧠 Brain Opener

You ask an AI to draw a giraffe wearing a school uniform. It draws one in ten seconds. Who made that drawing? Not the AI. Not you. Something in between. This module is about who owns what.

  • If you describe a picture and AI draws it, who made it?
  • Is it fair to copy a famous artist's style?
  • What should you always tell people about AI-made pictures?
🎁 Reveal a good answer

The picture belongs to the person who described it, mostly - but you should always say "made with AI." Copying a real artist's style without credit is unfair. Copying a real person's face to make them look bad is wrong. The rule is: use AI to make things, but say what you did and do not hurt real people.

📖 Kudakwashe and the Lion Poster

Kudakwashe was eleven. His school announced a competition: design a poster for a new wildlife club. Winner gets a certificate and their poster printed for the school gate.

Kudakwashe asked AI to draw a lion, a sunset, and a group of children watching. The AI made a beautiful picture. He printed it. He put his name on it.

The day of the judging, the teacher asked him one question: "Kudakwashe, did you draw this?"

He hesitated. He said, "AI drew it. I told it what to draw."

The teacher nodded slowly. "Then you should tell the judges that. It might change the answer. It might not. But you must say what you did."

Kudakwashe told the judges. They thanked him. Then they asked him to make a new poster - one where he did the drawing and the AI only helped with ideas.

He spent two hours drawing a lion himself. It was not as beautiful as the AI picture. But it was his.

He did not win the competition. But the teacher kept both his posters - the AI one and his own - and put them side by side in the classroom. She wrote underneath them: "The first is impressive. The second is honest."

Kudakwashe understood something that day. The AI made a better picture. But his picture was the one he was proud of.

💡 The Big Idea

AI images are made, not created

AI image tools learn from millions of pictures made by real people. When you ask them to draw, they are mixing and matching what they learned. The result is beautiful. But it is not original in the way a human drawing is. Always say when a picture was made with AI. This is honest, and it is becoming the normal rule everywhere.

📚 Three Deep Lessons

1

How AI image tools work

AI image tools have seen millions of real pictures - photos, drawings, paintings. They learn what a "tree" looks like by seeing thousands of trees. When you ask for a tree, they mix what they learned into a new picture.

This is why AI pictures sometimes have strange fingers or extra legs. The AI has not really understood "hand." It has only seen thousands of hand-shaped pixels. It guesses. Most guesses are good. Some are not.

2

The ethical rules of AI images

Three rules every young artist should follow. First, always say when a picture was made with AI - in your signature, in a caption, in your description. Second, never use AI to copy a real artist's style and pretend it is your own. Third, never use AI to make fake pictures of real people doing things they did not do.

These rules are becoming laws in many countries. Following them protects you and respects other people.

3

AI as a thinking tool, not a drawing tool

Use AI to help you think, not to replace your hand. Ask it to describe what a lion looks like from different angles. Ask it for ideas for the background. Then draw the picture yourself.

The drawing will be worse than the AI version. That does not matter. What matters is that you are learning. In five years, your drawings will be good - because you practised. The people who only use AI will still not know how to draw.

🛠 Hands-On - AI Draws, You Draw

You need

  • A phone with an AI image tool
  • Paper
  • A pencil or crayons
  1. Pick a subject - a lion, a house, a machine.
  2. Ask the AI to draw it. Save the AI picture.
  3. Now draw your own version on paper. Take your time.
  4. Put the two side by side.
  5. Write one sentence under each: how are they different?
  6. Keep your drawing. Show it to your family.
Why it works: The AI picture is prettier. Your drawing is more valuable - because you made it, and because it will get better.

🤖 AI-Era Skill

AI image tools are powerful. Use them to help, not to replace your own drawing.

Try this prompt: "Describe a lion in detail - its mane, its face, its body, the background - so that I can draw it myself. Include the shapes and the colours."

Read the description and draw from it. Then compare with an AI drawing of the same lion. Which looks more like your own understanding?

🌍 Real Stories from Africa

Anime artists in Japan

Japanese anime artists have led a global movement to label AI-made pictures. Their rule: AI art is fine, but it must be labelled. Artists whose styles were copied without permission fought back. The world is starting to listen.

South African comic artists

A group of South African comic book artists uses AI to draft backgrounds, then draws the characters themselves. They always credit AI in the credits page. Their fans respect them more for being honest.

🏆 Project Challenge

The Honest Art Gallery

Create a small gallery of five drawings. At least two must be your own.

  1. Pick five subjects - animals, places, people, things.
  2. For each one, make a picture.
  3. For two, use an AI tool. Write "AI-assisted" under them.
  4. For three, draw them yourself. Write "my own work" under them.
  5. Show the gallery to your family or class.
  6. Talk about which pictures you are proudest of and why.
