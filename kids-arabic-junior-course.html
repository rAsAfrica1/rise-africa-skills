Marhaba! Arabic

Arabic letters hold hands, and some of them are the same shape wearing different dots.

Ages 7–128 modules 🪨 Starts with no device

Arabic runs right to left, and its letters join up like people holding hands in a line. Even better: many letters are the SAME shape, and only the dots tell them apart. Learn one shape and you get three letters free. You will be writing your own name in Arabic much sooner than you think.

Letters Hold Hands

Arabic letters change shape depending on whether they are at the start, the middle or the end of a word — because they are joining onto their neighbours.

ب + ي + ت → بيت
3
🪨 No device needed

Peace Be Upon You

The Arabic greeting is a whole sentence with a fixed reply, and knowing the reply is what makes you sound like you belong.

السلام عليكم 🫱 → 🫲 وعليكم السلام

The Sounds From Deeper Down

Arabic has sounds made further back in the throat than any English sound, and you need a real voice in your ear to find them.

ع 😮 ح 😤 خ 😾 ق 🗣️

Everyone in the Family

Arabic family words are short, ancient and used constantly — and two of them sound almost like baby words in every language on earth.

أب 👨 أم 👩 أخ 👦 أخت 👧

Arabic numbers are the words you will hear first at any market from Nouakchott to Khartoum, so learn them by ear before you meet them on paper.

For a parent or teacher

Your child will finish able to write and recognise a solid group of Arabic letters in their joined forms, greet and reply in Arabic, count to ten, and read simple words on real packaging. Arabic is spoken right across North Africa, the Sahel and the Horn, is a working language of the African Union, and is the language of the Qur'an for families where that matters.
