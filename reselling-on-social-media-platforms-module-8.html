1. Responding to Comments and DMs Professionally

When a customer comments on your post or sends a DM, they are deciding whether to trust you with their money. Reply within 30 minutes during business hours (9amâ6pm). If you sell on Instagram, WhatsApp, or Facebook, set a notification sound for each app. For comments, always reply publicly first: "Thanks for asking, @name! I've sent you a DM with details." This shows other buyers you are active. Then send a private DM with the answer. Never reply with just "DM sent" and nothing elseâthat looks lazy.

Use a friendly but professional tone. Start with "Hello [Name]" or "Hi [Name], thanks for reaching out." Avoid slang like "hey guy" or "my dear." If you don't know the answer, say: "Good question. Let me check and get back to you within 2 hours." Then actually do it. In WhatsApp Business, set a greeting message and quick replies for common questions like price, delivery, and payment methods. For example: "Our price for the 10kg bag of rice is N8,500. Delivery in Lagos is N1,500, and we deliver in 2 days."

If a customer asks for a discount, don't ignore it. Say: "I can't reduce the price, but I can offer free delivery if you order two items." This keeps the conversation moving. If someone leaves a negative comment, reply calmly: "We're sorry you're unhappy. Please check your DM so we can fix this." Never argue in public. Screenshot the conversation and move to private.

Track your response time. A simple notebook or Google Sheet with columns for Date, Customer Name, Platform, Question, and Time Taken to Reply helps you improve. Aim to answer 90% of DMs within 1 hour. If you get more than 20 DMs a day, use Instagram's saved replies or WhatsApp Business quick replies to save time. Remember: every unanswered DM is a lost sale. Treat each message like a customer walking into your shop.

2. Handling Returns and Refunds

Returns and refunds are part of doing business. In Africa, many buyers fear scams, so a clear return policy builds trust. Write your policy in simple language and post it on your page. Example: "Returns accepted within 3 days if item is unused and in original packaging. Buyer pays return delivery. Refund processed within 48 hours via mobile money or bank transfer." Keep it fair but protect yourself. If you sell perishable goods like food, state "No returns on food items unless damaged on arrival."

When a customer requests a return, respond within 2 hours. Ask for a photo or video of the item. If it's your mistake (wrong size, damaged), apologise and offer a full refund or replacement. If it's the customer's mistake (changed mind, ordered wrong size), offer a partial refund or exchange, but they pay return shipping. For example: "I'm sorry the dress doesn't fit. You can return it for an exchange, but you'll cover the N1,000 delivery. Or I can give you a 50% refund and you keep the dress."

Use mobile money like M-Pesa, MTN MoMo, or Airtel Money for fast refunds. Keep a record of every return: date, customer name, reason, amount refunded, and method. This helps you spot patterns. If you get many returns for the same product, stop selling it or change your supplier. For high-value items (over $50), consider asking for a video call before refunding to confirm the issue. Never block a customer who asks for a refundâthat kills your reputation. Instead, solve the problem and ask them to update their review. A good refund experience can turn a complainer into a loyal repeat buyer.

3. Dealing with Difficult Customers

Difficult customers are angry, impatient, or unreasonable. They may insult you or threaten to report you. Your job is to stay calm and solve the problem. First, listen without interrupting. Let them finish. Then repeat back what they said: "So you're saying the shoes arrived with a torn sole, and you want a full refund. Is that correct?" This shows you heard them. Never take it personally. They are angry at the situation, not at you.

If they insult you, don't insult back. Say: "I understand you're upset. I want to help. Let's focus on fixing this." If they threaten to post negative reviews, reply: "I'd rather solve this for you than have you leave unhappy. Give me 24 hours to make it right." Then do it. For example, if a customer claims they never received a package, check your delivery records. If you used a courier like GIG Logistics or Sendbox, share the tracking number. If it's lost, refund or resend. If they are lying, stay professional: "Our records show delivery was made on 5th June at 2pm. Can you check with your neighbour?"

Some customers will try to bully you into free items. Set boundaries. Say: "I can offer a 10% discount on your next order, but I cannot give a full refund without a valid reason." If they become abusive, you can end the conversation: "I'm happy to help when you're ready to speak respectfully. I'll be here." Then stop replying for 2 hours. Document everythingâscreenshots, dates, and what you offered. If a customer becomes a serial returner or scammer, add them to a blacklist in your notes. In Africa, word of mouth is powerful. One bad experience shared in a WhatsApp group can cost you 10 sales. So fix problems fast, but don't let anyone take advantage of you.

4. Building Trust with Reviews and Testimonials

In social media reselling, trust is your currency. Buyers can't touch your product, so they rely on what others say. After every successful sale, ask for a review. The best time is right after they confirm they received the item and are happy. Send a simple message: "Hi [Name], so glad you love the bag! Would you mind leaving a short review on my page? It helps me a lot. Here's the link." If they say yes, make it easyâsend a screenshot of what to write or a direct link to your Google Business profile or Facebook reviews section.

Offer a small incentive, but never buy fake reviews. For example: "Leave a review and get 5% off your next order." That's fair. When you get a review, screenshot it and post it on your page, Instagram Story, or WhatsApp Status. Add a thank-you message: "Big thanks to Amina for the kind words!" This encourages others. Keep a folder on your phone called "Testimonials" with all screenshots. Use them in your product posts: put a review next to the product photo.

If you get a negative review, respond publicly and politely. Example: "Thank you for your feedback, John. We're sorry the delivery was late. We've refunded your delivery fee and will do better next time." Then fix the issue. A bad review handled well can actually increase trust. Don't delete negative commentsâthat looks suspicious. Instead, show you care. For WhatsApp sellers, ask happy customers to send a voice note or video testimonial. Post those as Status updates. Real voices and faces are more convincing than text. Aim for at least 5 reviews in your first month. After 6 months, you should have 30+ reviews. That's social proof that turns followers into buyers.

5. Creating FAQ and Auto-Reply Templates

You answer the same questions every day: price, delivery time, payment methods, return policy. Instead of typing each time, create an FAQ and auto-reply templates. Start by listing your top 10 questions. For example: 1) How much is this? 2) Do you deliver to my area? 3) What payment methods do you accept? 4) How long does delivery take? 5) Can I return it? 6) Do you have this in another colour? 7) Is it original? 8) Do you offer discounts? 9) Where are you located? 10) Can I pay on delivery?

Write clear, short answers. For WhatsApp Business, set up a greeting message: "Hello! Thanks for contacting [Your Shop]. We sell [product]. Please tell us what you need." Also set an away message for after hours: "We're closed now. We'll reply by 9am tomorrow." Use quick replies (shortcuts) for common answers. For example, type "/price" to send: "Our price for the 5kg bag is N4,500. Delivery is N1,000 in Lagos, N2,000 outside Lagos." On Instagram, use saved replies in the DM settings. On Facebook, use automated responses in Messenger.

Make your FAQ a pinned post on your page and a highlight on Instagram. Write it in simple English, and if you sell to a local market, add a version in your local language (e.g., Pidgin, Swahili, Hausa). For example: "How much? = How much be this?" This makes customers feel at home. Update your FAQ every month based on new questions. If you get a question three times, add it to the FAQ. Also, create a PDF or image with your FAQ and send it to new customers automatically. This saves you hours. A good auto-reply system can cut your response time from 10 minutes to 10 seconds. That means more sales and less stress.

6. Managing Multiple Conversations Efficiently

If you sell on Instagram, Facebook, WhatsApp, and TikTok, you can get 50+ messages a day. Managing them one by one will burn you out. Use a system. First, set specific times to check messages: 9am, 12pm, 3pm, and 6pm. Outside those times, use auto-replies. Second, use labels or folders. On WhatsApp Business, label chats as "New Order," "Paid," "Delivered," or "Problem." On Instagram, use the "Flag" or "Mark as Unread" feature. On Facebook, use "Labels" in your inbox. This helps you see what needs action.

Third, use a simple notebook or Google Sheet to track orders. Columns: Date, Customer Name, Platform, Item, Amount, Payment Status, Delivery Status, Notes. When a new order comes in, write it down immediately. This prevents you from mixing up customers. Fourth, use a chatbot or auto-reply for the first message. For example, on WhatsApp, set a greeting that asks: "Are you asking about price, delivery, or returns?" Then send the relevant FAQ. On Instagram, use ManyChat or Chatfuel to automate common questions. These tools are free for basic use.

Fifth, prioritise paying customers. If you have 20 messages, answer the ones from people who have already paid or are ready to pay first. Then answer inquiries. Sixth, batch similar tasks. For example, reply to all price questions at once using copy-paste. Seventh, don't be afraid to hire a virtual assistant once you get more than 100 messages a day. In Africa, you can find trained VAs on platforms like Upwork or local Facebook groups for $100â$200 per month. They can handle DMs while you focus on sourcing and packing. Finally, take breaks. Customer service is draining. Set your phone to "Do Not Disturb" for 1 hour a day. Your mental health matters. A tired seller makes mistakes.

2. Handling Returns and Refunds

Returns are inevitable. The goal is not to avoid them but to handle them so the customer comes back. A clear, published policy reduces disputes before they start. In African markets, mobile money (M-Pesa, MTN MoMo, Airtel Money, Flutterwave) is the dominant payment method, so refunds must be processed through the same channel where possible.

  • Window: 3â7 days for local, 14 days for cross-border.

  • Exclusions: Perishables, intimate items, custom orders.
  • Process: Customer sends photo/video evidence â you approve â arrange pickup or drop-off â inspect â refund or replace.

Never process a refund without verifying the original payment. In African social commerce, common fraud patterns include: (1) fake payment screenshots, (2) refund requests to a different mobile money number than the one used to pay, (3) "item not received" claims when tracking shows delivery, and (4) chargeback abuse on card payments. Always: verify payment in your own records before refunding, refund only to the original payment source, keep screenshots of all transactions for 90 days, and require photo/video evidence for damage claims. If a dispute exceeds 2,000 USD equivalent, escalate to your payment provider's dispute resolution team.

3. Dealing with Difficult Customers

Difficult customers fall into four types: the angry complainer, the serial returner, the price haggler, and the time-waster. Each needs a different script.

The HEARD method works for angry customers:

  • Hear â let them finish without interrupting.
  • Empathize â "I understand why you're frustrated."
  • Apologize â "I'm sorry this happened."
  • Resolve â "Here's what I'll do right now."
  • Diagnose â "How can I prevent this for you next time?"

For serial returners, apply your policy consistently and document every interaction. For hagglers, hold your price with confidence and offer value instead of discount â free delivery, a small gift, or a bundle. For time-wasters, set a boundary: "I can hold this for 24 hours with a 50% deposit."

Never argue publicly. If a customer attacks you in comments, reply once with empathy and move to DM. If they become abusive, you have the right to disengage: "I want to help, but I need us to keep this respectful. I'll be here when you're ready."

4. Building Trust with Reviews and Testimonials

In African social commerce, trust is the currency. A single screenshot of a happy customer's WhatsApp message can convert more sales than a professional product photo. Actively collect reviews after every successful delivery.

Review collection script: "Hi [Name], I'm glad your [product] arrived! If you're happy with it, would you mind sending a quick voice note or text I can share? It helps other buyers trust us. Thank you!"

Display reviews in three places: your bio link, pinned Instagram Stories, and a dedicated "Reviews" highlight. For WhatsApp Business, use the catalog feature and status updates. Always ask permission before sharing a customer's name or photo.

5. Creating FAQ and Auto-Reply Templates

An FAQ document saves hours every week. Build one covering: pricing, delivery times, payment methods, return policy, product authenticity, and location. Pin it to your profile and use WhatsApp Business "Quick Replies" for instant answers.

Auto-reply template examples:

  • Greeting: "Hi [Name]! Thanks for reaching out. I'll reply within 30 minutes. Meanwhile, here's our catalog: [link]"
  • Away: "We're offline until 8 AM. Leave your question and we'll reply first thing. For urgent orders, WhatsApp [number]."
  • Price inquiry: "The [product] is [price] including delivery within [city]. Would you like to order?"

6. Managing Multiple Conversations Efficiently

Use a single inbox tool where possible. WhatsApp Business, Instagram, and Facebook can be managed from Meta Business Suite. For Telegram and TikTok, use a shared spreadsheet or a CRM like HubSpot's free tier. Label conversations: New Lead, Awaiting Payment, Shipped, Resolved, Follow-up.

Batch your responses: check messages at 8 AM, 1 PM, and 7 PM. Use voice notes for complex explanations â they are faster than typing and build rapport. For cross-border customers, note their time zone and set expectations: "I'll reply within 4 hours during your daytime."
