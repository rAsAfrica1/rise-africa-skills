Quality Control and Testing

8 lessons · 30 quiz questions · Business

1 What QC means in a small yard

Quality Control (QC) means checking your blocks — every batch, every day — to catch problems before they reach customers.

Why QC matters:

  • Prevents weak blocks leaving the yard
  • Protects reputation and prevents claims
  • Finds problems early (cheaper than recalls)
  • Builds customer trust
  • Required for certified supply

Three levels of QC:

  1. Incoming: Check raw materials (cement, sand, stone, water)
  2. In-process: Check the mix, the fill, the demoulding
  3. Finished: Check the cured block (dimensions, weight, strength)

A small yard needs all three, run daily. Total time: 30–60 minutes per day.

💡 QC is not optional. One bad batch to a builder can cost you future orders worth 10× the batch value.

2 Test blocks per batch

Every production batch needs sample blocks set aside for testing. A "batch" is one mixing cycle — typically 20–30 minutes of production.

Standard sample set:

  • 5 blocks for size and weight check (24 hours after casting)
  • 3 blocks for 7-day strength test
  • 3 blocks for 28-day strength test
  • 3 blocks kept as reference (for complaints)

Mark every sample with: batch number, date, time, mix design.

For 5,000 blocks/day, you'll make ~20 batches. That's 200+ sample blocks per week. Keep records organized.

3 Crush test basics

The crush test (compressive strength test) is the definitive quality check.

How it works:

  1. Blocks are cured for 7 or 28 days
  2. Sent to an accredited lab
  3. Each block is placed in a compression machine
  4. Load applied until block breaks
  5. Result is recorded in N/mm² or MPa

Typical results:

  • Dense block at 7 days: 5–6 N/mm²
  • Dense block at 28 days: 7–10 N/mm²
  • Paver at 28 days: 35–50 N/mm²
  • CSEB at 28 days: 3–6 N/mm²

Lab cost: $50–$200 per test. Send 3 blocks per test (average the results).

⚠ A single test can be misleading. Test 3 blocks and average them for reliable data.

4 Weight and dimension checks

Simple daily checks catch most problems before they get serious.

Size check (5 blocks per batch):

  • Measure length, width, height
  • Tolerance: ±2 mm for standard blocks, ±1 mm for fair-faced
  • Record any out-of-tolerance blocks

Weight check (5 blocks per batch):

  • Standard dense block: 15–20 kg
  • Hollow block: 10–15 kg
  • Lightweight block: 8–12 kg

If blocks are lighter than expected: too little cement, wrong mix, or over-vibrated. Check immediately.

Weight varies ±5% between batches. Beyond that, investigate.

5 Recording results

QC records prove your quality. Without them, you can't tell a customer "this block meets spec".

QC log entries per batch:

  • Batch number and date
  • Mix design used
  • Cement, sand, stone, water quantities
  • Ambient temperature and humidity
  • Sample block dimensions and weights
  • Any issues noted
  • Test results (when available)

Tools: A simple lined notebook works. A spreadsheet is better. Store in a locked cabinet.

Keep records for at least 2 years (longer for structural or exported blocks — up to 5 years).

6 Rejecting bad batches

Not every batch is sellable. Learn to reject without regret.

Reject the batch if:

  • Average crush strength is below spec by more than 10%
  • More than 5% of blocks have cracks, honeycomb, or size issues
  • Cement was stale or wrong type
  • Mix was over-wet (blocks slumped)
  • Weather stopped curing

What to do with rejects:

  • Crush them and return as aggregate (best use — no waste)
  • Sell as second-grade at reduced price with clear disclosure
  • Use internally for non-critical fill
  • Never sell as first-grade

Rejecting a batch is expensive. Keeping records helps you spot repeat causes and prevent them.

⚠ Selling weak blocks damages your reputation permanently. Better to lose one batch than lose the business.

7 Certificates of conformity

A "Certificate of Conformity" (CoC) is a document stating that a specific batch meets a defined standard.

What goes on a CoC:

  • Your business name and details
  • Product name and dimensions
  • Batch number and date
  • Test results (strength, dimensions, weight)
  • Standard the block complies with (e.g. BS EN 771-3, SAZS 612)
  • Signature and date

Issue a CoC for every order over a certain size (say, 500 blocks). Customers use these for warranty claims and building inspections.

For major projects (schools, hospitals), you may need third-party certification. That means HACCP-style audits of your QC system by an external body.

8 Complaint handling

Even with good QC, complaints happen. Handle them well and they become loyalty.

The 5-step process:

  1. Listen — let the customer explain fully without interrupting
  2. Verify — ask for batch number, photos, or return a sample
  3. Check records — pull the QC records for that batch
  4. Resolve — replace, refund, or repair depending on the fault
  5. Prevent — adjust process to prevent recurrence

How fast to respond: Within 24 hours for initial contact. Within 7 days for resolution.

What NOT to do:

  • Ignore complaints — they escalate to social media
  • Blame the customer without checking
  • Refuse refunds on clearly faulty product
💰 Good QC is cheap; bad QC is expensive. A $50 lab test prevents a $5,000 recall. Every time.
