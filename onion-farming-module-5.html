5. Pests and Diseases

Thrips and purple blotch are the two biggest threats. Here is the prevention and control plan.

Module 5 of 6 · Onion Farming for Profit

Onion thrips - the number one pest

Thrips are tiny (1-2 mm) insects that suck sap from leaves. Signs:

  • Silvery streaks or patches on leaves
  • Leaves curl and twist
  • Overall stunted growth
  • Reduced bulb size
  • Thrips visible on close inspection - thin, dark, fast-moving

The four major diseases

DiseaseSignsPrevention
Purple blotchWhite sunken spots with purple centres on leavesMancozeb or chlorothalonil preventively, spacing
Downy mildewPale patches on leaves, grey-purple fuzz underneathDrip irrigation, copper spray, resistant varieties
Neck rotSoft brown rot at the neck after storageCure properly, stop nitrogen early, dry storage
Basal rotRot at base of bulb, yellowing leavesClean seed, rotation, well-drained soil

Thrips develop resistance fast. Rotate active ingredients - never use the same chemical twice in a season.

IPM order of operations

  1. Prevent - rotation, drip irrigation, spacing, resistant varieties
  2. Monitor - blue sticky traps, weekly leaf checks
  3. Cultural - remove crop debris, avoid fresh manure
  4. Biological - neem, predatory mites
  5. Targeted chemical - correct product, rate, PHI, rotate actives
The rotation rule: Never plant onion in the same bed two seasons in a row. Neck rot and basal rot live in soil and crop debris.
Practical exercise 5: Install 10 blue sticky traps per hectare. Check weekly. Spray only at threshold.
