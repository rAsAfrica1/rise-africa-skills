1. Making and Receiving Phone Calls

When you call a business in Spanish-speaking Africa, like Equatorial Guinea or Spain, start with a clear greeting: 'Buenos das, le habla [your name] de [your company].' If you're calling a supplier in Malabo, they may answer 'Dgame?' or 'Al?' both mean 'Hello?' on the phone. Always state your purpose within the first 10 seconds. For example: 'Llamo para confirmar el pedido de 50 sacos de cemento.' Speak slowly and repeat numbers: 'El precio es 250.000 francos CFA' (in Equatorial Guinea) or '3.500 nairas' (if calling Nigeria). Write down key details: order number, quantity, price, delivery date. If you don't understand, say: 'Puede repetir ms despacio, por favor?' or 'Puede deletrear eso?' For receiving calls, answer with your company name: 'Buenos das, Ferretera El Progreso, en qu puedo ayudarle?' Keep a pen and paper ready. Confirm every detail by repeating it back: 'Entonces, entrega el martes 15 a las 10 de la maana?' If the line is bad, say: 'La conexin est mala. Podemos hablar por WhatsApp?' In many African countries, phone credit is expensive, so keep calls under 3 minutes. End politely: 'Gracias por su tiempo. Hasta luego.' Practice with a colleague using a script. Record yourself and listen for clarity. Common phrases: 'Me escucha?' (Can you hear me?), 'No cuelgue, por favor' (Don't hang up), 'Le devuelvo la llamada' (I'll call you back). Remember, in business Spanish, use 'usted' not 't' unless you know the person well. A simple 'Cmo est?' is fine after greeting. Avoid slang. If you need to transfer, say: 'Un momento, le paso con el gerente.' Always confirm the best number to call back. This builds trust and saves money.

2. Writing a Professional Email in Spanish

A professional email in Spanish follows a clear structure. Start with a formal greeting: 'Estimado Sr. [Apellido]:' or 'Estimada Sra. [Apellido]:' if you know the name. If not, use 'Estimados seores:' or 'A quien corresponda:'. In the first line, state your purpose: 'Me dirijo a usted para solicitar una cotizacin de 200 unidades de aceite de palma.' Use 'usted' throughout. Keep paragraphs short 2 to 3 sentences each. For example: 'Somos una empresa de distribucin en Ghana. Necesitamos entrega en Accra antes del 30 de junio.' If you attach files, write: 'Adjunto encontrar nuestra lista de precios.' End with a polite closing: 'Agradezco su pronta respuesta.' Then 'Atentamente,' or 'Cordialmente,' followed by your full name, position, company, phone, and email. In Africa, email is often read on mobile phones, so use a clear subject line: 'Solicitud de cotizacin Pedido 1234'. Avoid long sentences. Use bullet points for lists: ' 50 cajas de tomate 30 kg de cebolla'. If you need a reply by a certain date, say: 'Por favor, confirme antes del viernes 10 de mayo.' For follow-ups, reply to the same email thread. If you don't get a response in 3 days, send a short reminder: 'Le escribo nuevamente para saber si recibi mi correo anterior.' Common mistakes: using 't' instead of 'usted', forgetting accents (, , , , ), and writing in all caps. Use a professional email address like nombre@empresa.com. Sign off with 'Quedo atento a su respuesta.' Practice by drafting an email to a supplier in Spanish, then use Google Translate to check, but always verify with a fluent speaker. Save templates for common requests: quotes, orders, complaints. This saves time and reduces errors.

3. Scheduling Meetings and Appointments

Scheduling in Spanish requires clarity about time, date, and place. Use the 24-hour clock to avoid confusion: 'a las 15:00' means 3 PM. Days of the week: lunes, martes, mircoles, jueves, viernes. Months: enero, febrero, marzo, etc. When proposing a meeting, say: 'Le viene bien el mircoles 12 a las 10:00?' or 'Podemos reunirnos el prximo lunes?' If you need to reschedule, say: 'Lo siento, no puedo el martes. Podemos cambiar al jueves?' Confirm the meeting 24 hours in advance: 'Le confirmo nuestra reunin maana a las 14:00 en su oficina.' In Africa, meetings may start late, but always arrive on time. For virtual meetings, specify the platform: 'Usaremos Zoom. Le enviar el enlace.' Common phrases: 'A qu hora le conviene?' (What time suits you?), 'Estoy disponible el viernes por la maana' (I'm available Friday morning), 'Dnde nos reunimos?' (Where shall we meet?). If you need to cancel, do it early: 'Lamento cancelar nuestra reunin. Podemos reprogramar para la prxima semana?' Always send a written confirmation via email or WhatsApp. For example: 'Confirmado: Reunin el 20 de mayo a las 11:00 en la oficina de [empresa].' Use a calendar app like Google Calendar and set reminders. In business Spanish, use 'quedar' for informal appointments: 'Quedamos a las 10?' But for formal meetings, use 'reunirse'. Practice role-playing with a colleague: one proposes a time, the other accepts or suggests another. Learn to say 'No puedo, lo siento' politely. If you need to bring documents, say: 'Traer el contrato y la factura.' End with 'Gracias, nos vemos el mircoles.' This saves time and builds professional relationships.

4. Confirming Orders and Following Up

Confirming orders in Spanish prevents costly mistakes. After placing an order, send a confirmation message: 'Confirmo el pedido de 100 cajas de leche a 5.000 francos CFA cada una. Total: 500.000 francos CFA. Entrega el 15 de junio.' Use numbers clearly: 'cien' (100), 'mil' (1,000), 'un milln' (1,000,000). If you need to follow up, wait 2-3 business days, then call or email: 'Llamo para confirmar si recibi nuestro pedido.' If the supplier hasn't responded, say: 'Necesito saber si el pedido est en camino.' For late deliveries, be polite but firm: 'El pedido deba llegar ayer. Cundo llegar?' Ask for a tracking number: 'Me puede dar el nmero de seguimiento?' In Africa, follow up by WhatsApp if email fails. Use voice notes for clarity. Common phrases: 'Est confirmado mi pedido?' (Is my order confirmed?), 'Cundo sale el envo?' (When does the shipment leave?), 'Necesito la factura proforma' (I need the proforma invoice). Always keep a record: order date, quantity, price, delivery date, and any changes. If there's a problem, say: 'Faltan 10 cajas. Por favor, envenlas en el prximo camin.' For payment follow-up: 'Han recibido el pago?' (Have you received the payment?). If you need to change an order, do it before shipping: 'Puedo aumentar el pedido a 150 cajas?' After delivery, confirm receipt: 'Recib el pedido completo. Gracias.' This builds trust. Practice writing a confirmation email template: 'Asunto: Confirmacin de pedido #1234 Cuerpo: Estimado Sr. Prez, Confirmo el pedido de 50 sacos de arroz a 20.000 francos CFA cada uno. Total: 1.000.000 francos CFA. Entrega el 10 de julio. Gracias.' Use this for every order.

5. Using WhatsApp and Text Messaging for Business

WhatsApp is essential for business in Africa. Use it for quick confirmations, reminders, and sharing documents. Start with a polite greeting: 'Buenos das, Sr. Gmez. Le escribo por WhatsApp para confirmar la reunin de maana.' Avoid sending voice notes longer than 30 seconds many people can't listen at work. Instead, type short messages. For orders: 'Puede enviar 20 cajas de tomate maana?' Use emojis sparingly a thumbs up () is fine, but no smileys in formal contexts. Create a business profile with your company name and logo. Use WhatsApp Business for auto-replies: 'Gracias por su mensaje. Le responderemos en breve.' For urgent matters, call instead of texting. If you need to send a document, use PDF, not Word, to avoid formatting issues. Common phrases: 'Me confirma, por favor?' (Please confirm), 'Quedo atento' (I'll wait), 'Gracias por su respuesta.' For reminders: 'Le recuerdo nuestra cita el jueves a las 10:00.' If you don't get a reply in 2 hours, send a gentle follow-up: 'Recibi mi mensaje anterior?' Never send messages after 8 PM or before 7 AM unless urgent. In group chats, address the person directly: '@Juan, necesito el precio.' Keep messages short: 1-2 sentences. For payment confirmations: 'Pago enviado. Por favor, confirme recepcin.' Use 'usted' for business contacts. Save contacts with company names: 'Ferretera El Progreso Juan'. Backup chats regularly. Practice sending a professional WhatsApp: 'Buenos das, Sra. Lpez. Soy [your name] de [company]. Necesito cotizacin de 100 kg de caf. Me puede ayudar?' This is fast, cheap, and effective.

6. Practice: Draft a Business Email to a Supplier

Now you will draft a complete business email in Spanish to a supplier. Follow this template. First, choose a real supplier for example, a coffee exporter in Ethiopia or a textile manufacturer in Ghana. Write the subject line: 'Solicitud de cotizacin [producto]'. Then greet: 'Estimado Sr. [Apellido]:' or 'Estimados seores:'. In the first paragraph, introduce yourself: 'Me llamo [your name] y represento a [your company] en [city, country].' State your request: 'Necesito una cotizacin para 500 unidades de [producto].' Include specifications: size, color, quality. Ask for price, delivery time, and payment terms: 'Cul es el precio por unidad? Cunto tiempo tarda la entrega a [your city]? Aceptan pago contra entrega?' Mention quantity discounts: 'Ofrecen descuento por volumen?' If you need samples, ask: 'Pueden enviar una muestra?' Then close: 'Agradezco su pronta respuesta. Quedo atento a su cotizacin.' Sign off: 'Atentamente, [your full name], [position], [company], [phone], [email].' After writing, check for accents and formal 'usted'. Use a spell checker. Then send it to yourself first to see how it looks. If you have a Spanish-speaking colleague, ask them to review. Common errors: mixing 't' and 'usted', forgetting to attach files, and writing too much. Keep it under 150 words. Practice with different scenarios: complaining about a late delivery, requesting a refund, or confirming an order. For example: 'Asunto: Reclamo por pedido #5678 Cuerpo: Estimado Sr. Ruiz, El pedido #5678 lleg con 20 unidades faltantes. Solicito el envo inmediato de las unidades restantes o un reembolso. Gracias.' Save this template and adapt it. In Africa, email is often the first contact, so make it professional. Review your draft three times before sending.

7. Practical Reference

Practical Reference: Business Communication in Spanish Phone, Email, WhatsApp & Follow-Up

For African agribusiness professionals trading with Spanish-speaking partners from importers in Madrid and Valencia to cooperatives in Peru, Chile, and Argentina Spanish is not a "nice to have." It is a commercial instrument. A single well-worded email can confirm a container of cocoa, a poorly phrased WhatsApp message can delay payment for a shipment of green beans, and a missed follow-up call can hand a contract to a competitor. This practical reference module gives you the vocabulary, structures, and workflows to handle phone calls, professional emails, meeting scheduling, order confirmation, and instant messaging with confidence.

The module is built around the six lessons in this course: Making and Receiving Phone Calls, Writing a Professional Email, Scheduling Meetings and Appointments, Confirming Orders and Following Up, Using WhatsApp and Text Messaging for Business, and the Practice exercise (Draft a Business Email to a Supplier). Use this reference as a desk aid print it, pin it near your phone, and keep it open when drafting messages to clients in Latin America or Spain.

2. Making and Receiving Phone Calls

Phone calls in Spanish business culture are shorter than in Anglophone contexts but more formal in the opening. Always identify yourself, your company, and your country before stating your purpose. For African exporters, network reliability matters if the call drops, follow up immediately with a WhatsApp voice note or email so the client knows the interruption was technical, not personal.

3. Writing a Professional Email in Spanish

Spanish business emails follow a predictable structure: salutation, context, request or confirmation, deadline, and formal closing. Unlike English, Spanish emails often use longer courtesy formulas this is not flattery, it is professional register. For African exporters dealing with Latin American importers, using "usted" (formal you) throughout is safest until the client switches to "t."

Standard structure:

  1. Asunto (Subject): short, specific, with reference number. Example: "Confirmacin pedido #AG-2024-118 Cacao en grano".
  2. Saludo: "Estimado/a Sr./Sra. [Apellido]:" or "Estimados seores:".
  3. Contexto: one sentence reminding them of the relationship. "En relacin con nuestra conversacin telefnica del 3 de junio"
  4. Cuerpo: the request, confirmation, or information bulleted if multiple items.
  5. Plazo: explicit date. "Le agradecera su respuesta antes del viernes 14 de junio."
  6. Cierre: "Quedo a su disposicin para cualquier aclaracin."
  7. Despedida: "Atentamente," / "Reciba un cordial saludo," + full name, title, company, phone, country.

4. Scheduling Meetings and Appointments

Time zones are the single biggest source of friction between Africa and Latin America. Ghana (GMT) is 3 hours ahead of Peru, 5 ahead of Mexico City, and 1 hour behind Spain. Always state the time in both zones and confirm in writing. Spanish uses the 24-hour clock in business contexts: "a las 15:00 horas (hora de Accra) / 10:00 (hora de Lima)."

5. Confirming Orders and Following Up

Order confirmation is where African exporters most often lose money through vague language. Spanish commercial correspondence uses precise terms: pedido (order), albarn (delivery note), factura proforma (proforma invoice), plazo de entrega (delivery time), incoterm (Incoterm, unchanged). Always repeat back the quantity, unit, price, Incoterm, port, and payment terms in the confirmation email this creates a written record that protects both parties.

Follow-up timing that works: send the confirmation within 24 hours of verbal agreement; follow up on the proforma after 3 working days; follow up on payment after 7 days past due; escalate politely after 14 days. In many Latin American markets, a phone call after an unanswered email is expected, not intrusive.

Safety & Fraud Warning Payment and Identity Verification

African exporters are frequently targeted by fraudulent "buyers" using Spanish-language emails that mimic legitimate Latin American importers. Never change bank details based on email alone always verify by phone call to a number you already hold. Be alert to urgent language ("pago inmediato", "cambio de cuenta bancaria"), requests for advance fees, and free email domains (gmail, hotmail, outlook) claiming to represent a company. Cross-check the company against the official chamber of commerce of the buyer's country (e.g., Cmara de Comercio de Espaa, Cmara de Comercio de Lima). Report suspected fraud to your national export promotion agency (e.g., GFDA Ghana, EPC Ghana, or your country's equivalent).

6. Using WhatsApp and Text Messaging for Business

In Latin America, WhatsApp is the default business channel often more used than email. In Spain, it is increasingly accepted for logistics coordination but less for contracts. For African exporters, WhatsApp is a powerful tool but must be used with discipline:

  • Open with identity: "Buenos das, Sr. [Apellido], le escribe [Nombre] de [Empresa] en [Pas]."
  • Keep voice notes under 60 seconds long voice notes are ignored.
  • Use text for anything with numbers (prices, quantities, dates) so there is a written record.
  • Confirm important decisions by email "Le confirmo por correo lo acordado por WhatsApp."
  • Respect working hours in the client's time zone; avoid messages after 19:00 local time.
  • Use professional abbreviations sparingly: "Sr.", "Ud.", "x" (por), but avoid "q" for "que" in first contact.

Quick Reference Checklist

  • Identify yourself, company, and country in the first 10 seconds of any call.
  • Use "usted" until the client switches to "t"; mirror their register.
  • State times in both zones (e.g., "15:00 Accra / 10:00 Lima").
  • Confirm every verbal agreement in writing within 24 hours.
  • Repeat quantity, price, Incoterm, port, and payment terms in order confirmations.
  • Verify bank detail changes by phone never by email alone.
  • Keep WhatsApp voice notes under 60 seconds; use text for numbers.
  • Follow up on proformas after 3 days and on payments after 7 days past due.
