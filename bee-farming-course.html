Hive construction, colony management, honey harvesting, and wax product creation for African beekeepers.

1
Why Beekeeping is Essential
Start

Bees pollinate 75% of global crops. As a farmer, keeping bees boosts your own yields while producing honey, wax, and propolis for sale.

  • One hive can produce 20-40kg of honey per year in good forage areas.
  • Wax sells to cosmetics, candle makers, and pharmaceutical companies.
  • Propolis (bee glue) is a natural antibiotic with high value in health markets.
Pro Tip: Even 2 hives make a difference. Start small, learn the bees' behavior, then expand.

Key Takeaway: Beekeeping improves crop pollination AND creates independent products. It is a double-income activity.

Hive Types: Langstroth vs Top-Bar
Locked

Your hive choice determines your management style, honey yield, and startup cost. Choose based on your goals and budget.

  • Langstroth: frame-based, high honey yield, standard for commercial beekeepers. More expensive.
  • Top-Bar: simpler, cheaper, easier to build locally. Lower yield but perfect for beginners.
  • Kenya Top-Bar (KTB) is the most popular in Africa — locally made with local wood.
Pro Tip: If you have carpentry skills, build KTB hives yourself. Cost drops from ₦15,000 to ₦3,000 per hive.

Key Takeaway: Begin with Kenya Top-Bar hives. Upgrade to Langstroth when you scale to 20+ hives.

Sourcing Bees & Colony Establishment
Locked

You can buy bees, catch wild swarms, or split existing colonies. Each method has different costs and risks.

  • Buying a colony: fastest, most reliable. Costs ₦10,000-₦25,000 per colony.
  • Catching swarms: free, but unpredictable. Use bait hives with lemon grass oil attractant.
  • Splitting: divide a strong colony into two. Requires experience but costs nothing.
Pro Tip: Place bait hives 3-5 meters high, under shade, near water. Swarm season is typically during the rainy season.

Key Takeaway: Bait hives cost almost nothing and can capture free swarms during peak season.

Hive Placement & Forage
Locked

Bees fly up to 3km to find nectar. Your farm's location relative to flowers, water, and shade determines your harvest.

  • Place hives near diverse flowering plants: citrus, mango, sunflower, sesame, and wildflowers.
  • Water source within 500m is essential. Bees use water to cool the hive.
  • Face hive entrances east/southeast for morning sun. Avoid flood-prone areas.
Pro Tip: Plant a bee forage garden: sunflowers, cowpea, and basil flower continuously and boost honey yield.

Key Takeaway: Diverse forage + water + morning sun = maximum honey production.

Hive Inspection & Colony Health
Locked

Inspect hives every 2-4 weeks during active season. You are checking for queen presence, brood pattern, disease, and space.

  • Look for eggs: one per cell, standing upright at the bottom. No eggs = queenless hive.
  • Brood pattern should be compact and continuous. Spotty brood means a failing queen or disease.
  • Check for pests: hive beetles, wax moths, and ants are common African threats.
Pro Tip: Smoke the entrance lightly before opening. Calm bees are easier to inspect and less likely to sting.

Key Takeaway: Regular, gentle inspections catch problems early. A queenless hive will collapse within weeks.

Honey Harvesting & Extraction
Locked

Harvest at the right time, with the right method, and your honey will be pure, clear, and command top price.

  • Harvest when 80% of comb cells are capped with wax. Uncapped honey is too watery and ferments.
  • Use a bee escape board 24 hours before harvest to clear bees from the honey super.
  • Extract by crushing comb (top-bar) or spinning frames (Langstroth). Filter through cheesecloth.
Pro Tip: Never harvest during rainy season. Bees need stored honey to survive the dearth period.

Key Takeaway: Capped comb + dry season harvest + clean extraction = premium honey.

Wax & Propolis Products
Locked

Don't throw away wax cappings and scrapings. They are valuable raw materials for secondary products.

  • Melt wax in a double boiler (never direct heat — it burns). Filter through cloth and mold into blocks.
  • Wax products: candles, shoe polish, cosmetics, and wood sealants.
  • Propolis: collect from hive cracks and frames. Soak in alcohol to make tinctures.
Pro Tip: A 1kg wax block sells for ₦3,000-₦5,000 to candle makers. It is pure profit from waste.

Key Takeaway: Every byproduct has value. Wax and propolis turn waste into additional income.

Marketing & Value-Added Products
Locked

Raw honey in a plastic bottle sells cheap. Packaged, branded honey with a story sells for 3x the price.

  • Package in glass jars with labels showing harvest date, floral source, and your farm name.
  • Value-added: honey wine (mead), honey soap, propolis throat spray, and beeswax lip balm.
  • Sell at farmers markets, health stores, online (Instagram/WhatsApp), and to hotels.
Pro Tip: Get your honey tested for moisture content and purity. A lab certificate justifies premium pricing.

Key Takeaway: Branding and certification transform honey from a commodity into a premium product.
