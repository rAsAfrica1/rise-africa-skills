1. Creating a Marketing Plan for Errand Services

A marketing plan is your roadmap to getting clients. Start by defining your target market. In your area, who needs errand services? Busy professionals, small business owners, elderly people, or students? For example, in Lagos, you might target busy market traders who need someone to buy supplies. In Nairobi, target tech workers in Westlands who need lunch delivered. Write down 2-3 specific groups.

Next, set a monthly marketing budget. Even 5,000 Naira or 1,000 KES can work. Allocate it: 40% for data and airtime, 30% for printing flyers, 20% for transport to network, 10% for a small Facebook ad. Track every cedi, shilling, or naira spent. Use a notebook or free app like Wave.

Your key message should be clear: 'I save you time.' For example, 'I do your shopping, pay bills, and deliver packages so you can focus on work.' Include your phone number and a call to action: 'Call or WhatsApp today for a free quote.'

Use offline and online methods. Print 100 flyers for 2,000 NGN and post them at bus stops, markets, and office buildings. Join WhatsApp groups for your estate or church. Offer a 10% discount for first-time clients. Ask satisfied clients for referrals. Set a goal: get 5 new clients this month. Review your plan weekly and adjust. If flyers don't work, try posting in Facebook groups like 'Lagos Entrepreneurs' or 'Nairobi Business Network'. Keep it simple and consistent.

2. Networking and Building Referral Partnerships

Networking is about building relationships, not just collecting contacts. Start with people you know: family, friends, church members, neighbours. Tell them exactly what you do. For example, 'I run errands like paying bills, shopping, and delivering items. If you know anyone who needs help, please refer them.' Give them your business card or WhatsApp number.

Attend local business meetings, trade fairs, or community events. In Accra, go to the Makola Market traders' association meetings. In Johannesburg, join a local chamber of commerce event. Introduce yourself and exchange contacts. Follow up within 48 hours with a WhatsApp message: 'Great meeting you. Let me know if I can help with any errands.'

Partner with complementary businesses. For example, a cleaning service, a laundry, or a delivery company. Offer to refer clients to them if they refer to you. Create a simple referral agreement: for every client they send, you give them 10% of the first job's fee. Print small cards with your details to leave at their shop.

Use a simple contact list. Write names, phone numbers, and how they can help. Follow up monthly with a friendly message or a small gift like airtime. Remember, people refer those they trust. Be reliable and professional. If you get a referral, thank the referrer immediately and send them a small token like 500 NGN or 100 KES. This encourages more referrals.

3. Using Social Media to Attract Clients

Social media is free and powerful. Focus on Facebook, WhatsApp, and Instagram. Create a business page on Facebook. Use a clear profile photo (your face or logo) and a cover photo showing you working. Write a description: 'Errand and PA services in [your city]. We save you time. Call 080XXXXXXX.'

Post 3-4 times a week. Share useful content: tips like 'How to save time on bill payments', photos of you running errands (with client permission), and testimonials. Use local hashtags: #LagosErrands #NairobiPA #AccraErrands. Join Facebook groups for your city, like 'Lagos Buy and Sell' or 'Nairobi Entrepreneurs'. Post your services there, but follow group rules.

On WhatsApp, create a broadcast list of potential clients. Send a weekly message with your services and a special offer. For example, 'This week only: 10% off grocery shopping.' Use WhatsApp Status to show your daily work. Post short videos of you delivering a package or helping a client.

Instagram is visual. Post photos of neatly arranged shopping or a happy client (with permission). Use Stories to show behind-the-scenes. Run a small ad: 1,000 NGN can reach 5,000 people in your city. Target by location and interests. Respond to messages quickly. If someone comments, reply within an hour. Use a simple scheduling tool like Meta Business Suite to plan posts. Track what works: if a post gets many likes, do more like it.

4. Writing Effective Service Descriptions and Proposals

A service description tells clients exactly what you do and why they should hire you. Start with a headline: 'Errand and Personal Assistant Services in [City] Save 10 Hours a Week.' Then list your services: grocery shopping, bill payments, package delivery, queue standing, event planning, etc. Be specific: 'We shop at Shoprite, Spar, and local markets.'

Include your pricing. For example: 'Grocery shopping: 1,000 NGN per trip plus transport. Bill payment: 500 NGN per bill. Delivery within city: 1,500 NGN.' If you can't give fixed prices, say 'Starting from 1,000 NGN.' Add a call to action: 'Call or WhatsApp 080XXXXXXX to book.'

When writing a proposal for a corporate client, use a simple template. Start with a polite greeting and thank them for the opportunity. State their problem: 'You need someone to handle office errands so your staff can focus on core work.' Then offer your solution: 'I will provide a dedicated assistant for 4 hours daily, Monday to Friday.' List deliverables: 'Daily pickups, bank deposits, lunch orders, and supply runs.' Give your price: '50,000 NGN per month.' Add why you're reliable: 'I have 2 years experience and references.' End with a request: 'I would like to meet to discuss further.'

Keep language simple and professional. Avoid jargon. Use bullet points. Proofread for spelling. If you have a smartphone, use a free template from Canva or Google Docs. Always customize the proposal with the client's name and specific needs. Follow up after 3 days with a polite call.

5. Customer Reviews and Testimonials

Reviews build trust. When a client is happy, ask them for a review. Do it immediately after a job. Say, 'If you're satisfied, could you write a short review on my Facebook page or send me a WhatsApp message I can share?' Make it easy: send them a direct link to your Facebook page or Google Business Profile.

If they agree, give them a guide: 'Please mention what I did and how it helped you.' For example, 'John paid my bills on time and saved me a trip.' Collect these and post them on your social media, website, and proposals. Take screenshots of WhatsApp praise and turn them into images using Canva.

Offer a small incentive for reviews: a 5% discount on their next job or a free small errand. But never buy fake reviews. Authenticity matters. Respond to all reviews, even negative ones. If a client complains, apologize publicly and offer to make it right. For example, 'We're sorry for the delay. We'll give you a free delivery next time.' This shows you care.

Use testimonials in your marketing. Add a 'What Clients Say' section to your flyers and Facebook page. In proposals, include 2-3 quotes. For example, 'Amina saved me 5 hours last week.' Mrs. Okafor, Lagos. Keep a folder on your phone with all reviews. Update it monthly. Ask repeat clients for updated reviews after 3 months. Positive reviews lead to referrals and more business.

6. Scaling Your Business: Hiring and Training Assistants

When you have more clients than you can handle, it's time to hire. Start by calculating your revenue. If you earn 100,000 NGN per month and work 10 hours a day, you can hire an assistant for 40,000 NGN and take on more clients. Hire slowly: start with one part-time assistant.

Find assistants through referrals. Ask your network for reliable people. Post in church, community groups, or WhatsApp groups. Interview them: ask about their experience, reliability, and how they handle challenges. Do a background check: call their previous employer or pastor. Start with a trial period of 2 weeks. Pay them daily or weekly at first: 2,000 NGN per day.

Train your assistant thoroughly. Create a simple manual with your processes: how to shop, how to handle cash, how to communicate with clients. Use a notebook or Google Doc. Shadow them for the first 3 days. Teach them your standards: be on time, dress neatly, and send updates via WhatsApp. Give them a script for client interactions: 'Good morning, I'm here to pick up your package.'

Use a simple system to manage jobs. A WhatsApp group with your assistant works. Assign tasks, share locations, and ask for photos as proof. Pay them fairly and on time. Motivate them with bonuses for good reviews or extra jobs. As you grow, hire more assistants. Eventually, you can become a coordinator. Set clear rules: no stealing, no lateness. If they break rules, give a warning or let them go. Scaling means you work on the business, not just in it.

7. Practical Reference

Practical Reference: Marketing and Growing Your Errand Business

In African cities and peri-urban corridors, errand and personal assistant services are growing fast because time-poor professionals, market traders, diaspora families, and small firms need reliable people to handle queues, deliveries, bill payments, school pickups, and government paperwork. This practical reference gives you the tools to market that service, win repeat clients, and scale without losing quality. It is written for operators in Lagos, Nairobi, Accra, Kampala, Kigali, Dar es Salaam, Johannesburg, and similar markets where trust, mobile money, and WhatsApp are central to business.

Creating a Marketing Plan for Errand Services

Your marketing plan should answer four questions: Who pays? What pain do they have? Where can I reach them cheaply? How will I measure results? A simple one-page plan is enough. Define your niche: corporate errands (bank deposits, document filing, procurement), household errands (groceries, school runs, bill payments), or event support. Then set a monthly target: e.g., 20 new clients, 60 repeat jobs, 15 referrals. Allocate a small budget: 1015% of revenue in the first six months. Track every lead source in a notebook or Google Sheet. In many African markets, the cheapest acquisition channels are referrals, WhatsApp status, community groups, and partnerships with complementary businesses such as cleaning companies, security firms, and online vendors.

Networking and Building Referral Partnerships

Referrals close faster and cost less than cold outreach. Build a network of 1015 referral partners who already serve your ideal clients: estate agents, property managers, HR officers, accountants, event planners, online boutiques, and delivery riders. Offer a clear referral fee or reciprocal service. For example, a property manager who refers tenants for bill payments might receive 510% of the first job value or free errands monthly. Join local business associations, chamber of commerce meetings, and online groups such as LinkedIn, Facebook business groups, and WhatsApp community groups. Follow up within 24 hours. Keep a simple CRM: name, contact, referral date, job value, thank-you sent.

Using Social Media to Attract Clients

Social media works when it shows proof, not just promises. Post three types of content weekly: (1) proof of work photos of completed errands, receipts, and happy clients (with permission); (2) useful tips how to renew a passport, pay a utility bill, or avoid queue times; (3) offers package deals, referral bonuses, and limited slots. WhatsApp Business is often the highest-converting channel in Africa because clients can chat, send locations, and pay via mobile money. Use a clear business profile, catalogue, and auto-reply. On Instagram, TikTok, and Facebook, use local hashtags and short videos showing your route, your team, and your reliability. Respond to every message within one hour during business hours.

Writing Effective Service Descriptions and Proposals

Clients buy clarity and confidence. A strong service description states what you do, what you do not do, turnaround time, price, and payment terms. Example: Same-day bill payment and bank deposit within Lagos Mainland. Pickup by 10am, receipt sent by 4pm. Fee: 3,500 per run plus transport. Payment via transfer or cash on delivery. For corporate proposals, include: client problem, proposed scope, timeline, pricing table, service level agreement (response time, confidentiality, replacement of staff), and two references. Always put your price in writing and ask for a signed acceptance or WhatsApp confirmation before starting.

Customer Reviews and Testimonials

Reviews are your cheapest marketing asset. Ask for a review immediately after a successful job, when the client is happiest. Make it easy: send a short WhatsApp message with a link or ask them to reply with one sentence. Offer a small incentive such as a discount on the next errand. Display reviews on your WhatsApp catalogue, Instagram highlights, Google Business Profile, and proposals. Respond professionally to negative feedback: acknowledge, apologise, explain the fix, and follow up offline. In African markets, trust is relational; a named testimonial from a known client or company carries more weight than anonymous stars.

Scaling Your Business: Hiring and Training Assistants

Scale when you have more demand than you can personally serve and repeat clients who trust your brand. Hire slowly: start with one part-time assistant or rider. Recruit through referrals, community groups, and local vocational centres. Screen for honesty, punctuality, local knowledge, and smartphone literacy. Verify identity, address, and references. Train using a simple manual: your service standards, customer scripts, safety rules, expense limits, receipt handling, and reporting format. Pay fairly and on time; in many markets, paying weekly or bi-weekly builds loyalty. Use a tracker for jobs, cash advances, and client feedback. Consider a franchise or subcontractor model only after your own processes are documented and profitable.

SAFETY WARNING:

Never send an assistant to collect cash, documents, or valuables without a written authorisation, a receipt system, and a verified client identity. In high-risk areas, avoid late-night errands, unregistered addresses, and carrying large sums. Use mobile money or bank transfers where possible, keep a paper trail, and share your route with a supervisor. Errand staff must never disclose client addresses, account details, or family routines. A single breach can destroy trust and expose your team to danger.

Quick Reference Checklist

  • Write a one-page marketing plan with niche, target clients, channels, and monthly targets.
  • Set up a WhatsApp Business profile with catalogue, auto-reply, and mobile money payment link.
  • Recruit at least five referral partners and agree on a clear commission or reciprocal service.
  • Create a standard service description and a simple proposal template with scope, price, and terms.
  • Ask every satisfied client for a short review and display it on your profile and proposals.
  • Track leads, jobs, expenses, and feedback in a simple sheet or notebook.
  • Hire only after demand is consistent; verify identity, references, and train with a written manual.
  • Review your pricing and safety rules every quarter, especially when fuel or transport costs change.
