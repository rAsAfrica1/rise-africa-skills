Agriculture · 6-Month Program

Sunflower Farming

The oilseed of opportunity — full stands and the boron rule, the bee partnership, sclerotinia rotation discipline, cut-and-dry harvesting past the birds, and the pressing business where the margin lives.

Course Overview

Sunflower points a 90–130 day drought-tolerant crop straight at Africa's cooking-oil import gap — and hands you a protein cake by-product that feeds this college's poultry, pig and dairy enterprises. This course teaches the crop and the business around it. In the field: full stands at the head-size sweet spot, modest N with real P, the boron rule that fills empty head centres on sandy soils, and the bee partnership worth 20–40% of seed set. Against its enemies: the rotation discipline that starves sclerotinia's black sclerotia bank, and the harvest logic — cut at brown-bract maturity, dry heads on racks — that is itself the crop's best bird control. Then the oilseed laws of 9–10% moisture and months-not-years storage, and the business ladder from raw seed through toll pressing to the motorised expeller that makes a farm the area's oilseed anchor, selling oil by the litre and cake by the kilogram.

What You Will Learn

  • Choose oil hybrids vs OPVs and site the crop on its deep-taproot strengths
  • Build the 45–55,000/ha stand at 75 × 25–30 cm and guard emergence from doves and rodents
  • Feed with manure, P and light N — and apply the boron rule on sandy soils, measured not guessed
  • Run the bee partnership: hives at flowering, the no-daytime-spray law, honey plus 20–40% seed set
  • Hold the sclerotinia rotation line (4+ years, volunteers hoed) and manage mildew, rust and head pests
  • Harvest by the cut-and-dry method at brown-bract maturity — the bird defence that works
  • Thresh, winnow and store to the oilseed laws: 9–10% moisture, cool, short, checked weekly
  • Climb the business ladder — raw seed, toll pressing, own press — selling oil, cake and toll services

Month 1: The Crop, Types & the Oil Market
Start

Lesson 1.1 — The oil crop that fits the gap

Sunflower is the smallholder's oilseed of opportunity: 90–130 days, drought-tolerant once established (its taproot mines moisture 2 m down), happy on moderate-fertility land, and pointed straight at one of Africa's most reliable markets — cooking oil, which most countries import at scale. Every litre pressed locally replaces an import, and the press's by-product, sunflower cake, feeds straight into the poultry, pig and dairy enterprises of this college. Sunflower is also the beekeeper's crop: a flowering field is a honey flow, and bees repay it with a measurable seed-set bonus.

  • Yields: typical smallholder ~0.8–1.2 t/ha; good management with improved seed reaches 1.5–2.5 t/ha (well-run hybrid crops higher).
  • Oil content is the crop's real product: modern varieties carry 35–45% oil; a hand press extracts roughly a third of seed weight as oil, motorised expellers more.
  • Rotation friend: a deep-rooted break crop after cereals — but never after itself or too often (disease build-up, Module 4).

Lesson 1.3 — One myth retired

Young sunflower buds do track the sun across the sky — but mature heads stop and face EAST. This matters practically: rows and field orientation don't need engineering around the 'following the sun' story, and an east-facing wall of ripening heads dries evenly in morning sun while shading afternoon-laid bird ambushes... nothing you can control changes it. What you CAN control fills the rest of this course.

Lesson 2.2 — Feeding: modest N, real P... and the boron rule

  • Sunflower forages nutrients deep and wide, so it flatters poor soils — but responds honestly to manure ploughed in and a P-strong basal at planting, plus a light N top-dress at knee height. Heavy N grows stems that lodge and delays maturity.
  • Boron is sunflower's signature micronutrient. Deficiency (common on sandy leached soils) shows as deformed heads, hollow stems near the head, poor seed set at the head's centre — and it can halve yield invisibly. Where sandy soils or past symptoms suggest it, a small borax application (soil at planting, ~5–10 kg/ha borax, or foliar per label at bud stage) is among the highest-return single inputs in this course. Do not overdose: boron's window between deficiency and toxicity is narrow — measure, don't guess.

Lesson 2.3 — Water rhythm

Critical windows: establishment (shallow young roots) and above all flowering through early seed fill (~55–85 days) — stress there empties the head's centre. The deep taproot handles mid-vegetative dry spells that would wound maize. Rain-fed timing rule: plant so flowering meets the heart of the rains, not their tail.

Lesson 2.4 — Weeds: two passes and canopy

Weed at 2–3 weeks (with thinning) and again at 5–6 weeks; after that the crop's big leaves close the row and finish the job. Shallow hoeing only near rows — sunflower's feeding roots run close beneath the surface. The final pass can pull a little soil to the stem base, steadying tall stands against wind.

Month 3: Bees, Growth Stages & the Standing Crop
Locked

Lesson 3.1 — Bees: the input that pays you twice

Sunflower is partially self-fertile but insect pollination lifts seed set substantially — well-pollinated fields commonly gain 20–40% more filled seed, with fuller head centres and better oil. The practical programme:

  • Site hives (yours or a beekeeper partner's) within flight range at flowering; 2–4 colonies per hectare is a working benchmark.
  • Never spray insecticide during flowering — if a treatment is ever unavoidable, late evening after bee flight, and told to the beekeeper first. A poisoned pollinator force is yield burned twice: seed set now, honey partnership forever.
  • The beekeeper's side of the trade: sunflower honey is a fast, distinctive crop. This is the Beekeeping course's favourite partnership — formalise it.

Lesson 3.3 — Support acts

  • Wind: tall crops on light soils lodge in storms — full stands support each other; the soil banked at the last weeding helps; windbreak lines pay on exposed sites.
  • Intercropping: sunflower's shade is heavy — classic companions are early short crops (beans finishing before the canopy) or field-edge arrangements rather than dense mixtures.
  • Volunteer control: shattered seed grows next season's volunteers, which carry disease across the rotation gap — hoe them out wherever they appear.

Month 4: Birds, Sclerotinia & Plant Health
Locked

Lesson 4.1 — Birds again — but with a calendar trick

From seed hardening to harvest, doves, parrots, weavers and quelea treat a sunflower field as a feeding platform (the bowed east-facing heads are literally perches with meals). The defence mirrors the dryland cereals — neighbourhood synchrony, active scaring through the 2–3 critical weeks, reflective tape and platforms — plus sunflower's own lever: because maturity is visible on the head's back and the crop can be cut at physiological maturity and dried off the field (Module 5), harvesting promptly at maturity is itself the biggest bird-control measure. Every extra field day after brown-bract stage is a toll paid in seed.

Lesson 4.2 — Diseases: rotation is the medicine

Lesson 4.3 — Insects worth knowing

  • Cutworms at emergence: evening collection, clean seedbeds.
  • Head-feeding caterpillars (bollworm etc.): scout flowering heads; treat only real infestations, and then only in the evening for the bees' sake.
  • Aphids and leafhoppers: rarely economic on a healthy crop; natural enemies usually finish them.
  • Termites on drought-stressed late crops: timely harvest again.
The rotation rule carries this crop. Sunflower after sunflower — or squeezed too often into the rotation — builds sclerotinia's black sclerotia bank in your soil until the crop fails no matter what you spray (and there is nothing effective to spray). Four-plus years between sunflower crops, volunteers hoed, infected plants burned: cheap discipline now, or a lost enterprise later.

Month 5: Cut-and-Dry Harvest, Storage & Pressing
Locked

Lesson 5.1 — Harvest logic: cut at maturity, dry off the field

Physiological maturity: head backs yellow-brown, bracts brown, seeds hard and black (oil types) — moisture still ~15–25%. From here the field only loses: birds, shattering, lodging, head rot in late rain. The smallholder method that beats them all:

  1. Cut heads with a knife (leave 10–20 cm of stalk as a handle), load into baskets/bags.
  2. Dry heads face-up on racks, mats or roofs — off the ground — for days to two weeks until seed rubs out freely.
  3. Thresh: rub heads over a ridged board, beat gently in sacks, or hire the small threshers appearing in oilseed areas.
  4. Winnow clean, then finish drying seed to 9–10% moisture — the oilseed storage law (oily seed spoils faster than starchy grain; the rub-and-sniff test: no clumping, no oily rancid smell, seeds crack cleanly).

Lesson 5.2 — Storage: short, cool, dry, and sold with a plan

  • Sunflower seed stores months, not years: high oil turns rancid with heat, moisture and time. Cool, dry, ventilated, in clean sacks off the floor; hermetic storage works well for seed stock and short holds.
  • Rats love it and heat kills it — the store checks are weekly, not monthly.
  • The price logic: crushers and traders buy year-round, but prices firm in the months after harvest; a 2–4 month hold in good conditions is usually the sweet spot — beyond that, quality risk outruns price gain. Selling as OIL (Lesson 5.3) escapes the seed-quality clock almost entirely.

Lesson 5.3 — Pressing: where the margin lives

RouteWhat happensEconomics
Sell raw seedTo traders/crushers at farm gate or townSimplest; lowest margin
Hire pressing / toll pressingTown expeller presses your seed for a fee or shareYou sell oil + cake at retail — usually +30–60% over raw seed value

Crude sunflower oil for local sale is settled/filtered simply; frying-grade clarity comes from careful filtering. Follow local food-sale rules (clean containers, no reused pesticide bottles — ever). The cake: sell fresh or dry it; mouldy cake is toxic feed (the aflatoxin law crosses crops), so the drying-and-storage discipline applies to the by-product too.

Month 6: The Pressing Business & the Plan
Locked

Lesson 6.1 — The pressing-town business model

The strongest sunflower enterprises are built backwards from the press:

  1. Year one: grow 1–2 ha well; sell part raw for cash flow; toll-press the rest and learn the oil-and-cake retail trade (who buys oil by the litre? who buys cake — poultry keepers, pig farmers, dairy?).
  2. Year two: contract or coordinate neighbours' plantings (same variety, synchronized calendar — bird logic AND supply logic); hold seed for the price rise in proper storage.
  3. Year three: the press decision — hand ram press (low cost, low volume) or small motorised expeller (real volume, toll income from the whole area). A pressing point with reliable seed supply becomes the area's oilseed anchor: buying seed, selling oil and cake, pressing for fees.

Lesson 6.3 — The customer map

  • Households and food stalls: oil by the litre and half-litre — repeat weekly customers; clean bottles and consistent quality are the brand.
  • Poultry, pig and dairy keepers: the cake line — often pre-sold before pressing day in livestock areas; your own animals (companion courses) eat it too.
  • Crushers and traders: the raw-seed floor price and the overflow channel; know their moisture and cleanliness specs.
  • Bakers and snack makers: confectionery-type seed where that niche exists.
  • The beekeeper partnership: honey revenue on their side, 20–40% seed set on yours — write the flowering calendar together.

Lesson 6.4 — Records and report card

Per plot per season: variety/seed source; planting date and stand count at 3 weeks; boron and fertility applications; flowering dates and hive presence; bird-watch days; harvest date versus brown-bract date (the gap IS your bird loss meter); seed weight, drying days, moisture at store; pressing yields (oil litres and cake kg per 100 kg seed); sales by product and price. Report card: stand count, the maturity-to-harvest gap in days, and oil litres per 100 kg seed. Those three numbers are the field, the birds, and the press — the whole business.

🏆 Final Project: Your Sunflower Business Plan

The final project is your complete sunflower enterprise plan — from stand architecture and the bee calendar to the pressing business the field is built to feed.

  2. Field plan: variety and seed source, deep-cultivation and stand plan with target population, boron decision with evidence, feeding programme and calendar.
  3. Pollination and health page: the bee arrangement and flowering rules, the four-year rotation map with sclerotinia hosts marked, volunteer control and scouting routine — written as commitments.
  4. Harvest and storage plan: the brown-bract trigger, cut-and-dry logistics (racks, labour), threshing and drying to 9–10%, storage setup with the weekly check log and the 2–4 month sales window.
  5. Processing plan: toll or own-press route, recovery targets (oil litres per 100 kg), bottling and food-safety rules, cake handling and its customer list.
  6. Budget and cash-flow: the full budget both routes, the press investment case if chosen, and the coordination plan for neighbour supply.
  7. Risk page and year-3 vision: three risks with mitigations, and the staged ladder — grow well, toll-press, then anchor the area's oilseed trade.

Submission standard: A plan you could hand to a savings group, a bank officer, or a family investor without embarrassment. Real local prices, real named buyers, honest risks.

📚 Glossary

Oilseed
A crop grown primarily for extractable oil — sunflower's seed is 35–45% oil.
Hybrid / OPV
Fresh-seed-yearly high performers versus saveable open-pollinated varieties — the standard seed choice.
Taproot
Sunflower's deep central root, mining moisture ~2 m down — its drought insurance.
Head (capitulum)
The 'flower' — actually hundreds of disc flowers opening in rings over 1–2 weeks.
Heliotropism
Sun-tracking by young buds only; mature heads fix facing east.
Boron
Sunflower's signature micronutrient; deficiency deforms heads and empties centres — corrected with measured borax.
Sclerotinia (white mould)
The rotation disease: cottony rot leaving black sclerotia that persist years in soil; no cure, only prevention.
Sclerotia
The hard black survival bodies of sclerotinia — 'rat droppings' in stem and head.
Volunteer
Self-sown plants from shattered seed — the disease bridge across rotation gaps; hoed out.
Physiological maturity
Head backs yellow-brown, bracts brown, seeds hard — the cut-and-dry trigger at ~15–25% moisture.
Cut-and-dry harvest
Cutting heads at maturity and rack-drying off the field — sunflower's main bird defence.
Oilseed moisture law
Storage at 9–10% — drier than grain, because oil turns rancid.
Rancidity
Oil spoilage from heat, moisture and time — why sunflower seed stores months, not years.
Toll pressing
Paying a press to process your seed, keeping the oil and cake to sell — the accessible middle rung.
Expeller
A motorised screw press — volume oil extraction and the anchor of an area oilseed business.
Press cake
The protein-rich residue after pressing — feed for poultry, pigs and dairy; dried and stored like food.
