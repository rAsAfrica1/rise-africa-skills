10 businesses you can start in Africa with under 100 dollars

6 min read - Beginner - Updated 2026

The most common reason people do not start a business is that they think they need money first. They do not. They need a customer first, and small money second.

Every business on this list has been started in Africa by somebody with less than 100 dollars. None of them require a loan, a licence, or a partner. All of them can produce your first dollar within 30 days.

The rule is the same for every business here: find one customer before you spend anything. If you cannot find one person who wants the thing, do not buy the materials.

1. Organic vegetable seed packets (from $30)

Buy 100 grams of organic tomato or kale seed from a registered producer. Divide it into 10 small packets of 10 grams each. Sell each packet for $3 to local gardeners and small farmers.

Cost: $30 for seed, $5 for packaging, $0 for delivery if you walk. Revenue: $30 in first week if you sell all 10. Scale: $300 a month once you have 100 regular customers.

Why it works

Small farmers cannot buy 100 grams at a time. They can afford 10 grams. You are selling the small size, not the seed itself.

2. Mobile phone screen protector fitting ($50)

Buy 20 tempered glass protectors online for $40. Buy a cleaning cloth and a card for $10. Set up outside any market or bus stop. Fit a protector for $3 each.

Cost: $50. Revenue: $60 on a Saturday. Scale: $400 a month from 130 fittings.

3. WhatsApp customer support for small shops ($0)

Walk into ten small shops. Offer to manage their WhatsApp business account - replies, order taking, and delivery coordination. Charge $15 per shop per week.

Cost: $0. Revenue: $150 per week from 10 shops. Scale: $600 a month with 10 active shops.

4. Print-and-deliver homework service ($60)

Buy a small printer and 200 sheets of paper. Advertise on WhatsApp to parents in your area. Students send you homework by phone, you print it, and you deliver it for $0.50 per page.

Cost: $60 for printer and paper. Revenue: $30 to $50 per week. Scale: $200 a month.

5. Bulk cooking oil repackaging ($40)

Buy a 20-litre tin of cooking oil for $30. Buy 20 small 1-litre bottles for $10. Repackage into single litres and sell each for $2 at markets and door to door.

Cost: $40 per cycle. Revenue: $40 per cycle. Scale: Two cycles per week = $80 profit per week.

Why it works

Buyers who cannot afford $30 for a tin can afford $2 for a bottle. You are selling convenience, not oil.

6. Organic compost sales ($30)

Build three compost piles using free waste from markets and farms. After eight weeks, sell the finished compost at $5 per 50-kilogram bag. Each pile produces 30 to 50 bags.

Cost: $30 for tools and transport. Revenue: $150 per pile. Scale: $600 per season from four piles.

7. Second-hand book resale ($50)

Buy 30 books from market vendors at $1 each. Clean them up. Sell each one on WhatsApp or at a school gate for $3 to $5.

Cost: $50. Revenue: $120 when all sold. Scale: $500 a month with four cycles.

8. Small plumbing and electrical fixes ($80)

Buy a basic toolkit - pliers, screwdrivers, tape, torch, plumber tape - for $60. Print 200 flyers for $20. Offer tap repair, light fitting, and toilet fixes at $5 to $15 per job.

Cost: $80. Revenue: $40 to $80 per day. Scale: $1,000 a month with three jobs a day.

9. Vegetables on a bicycle ($40)

Buy vegetables in bulk from farmers outside town in the morning. Sell them door to door in a residential area. The margin is usually 40 to 50 percent.

Cost: $40 per day. Revenue: $60 per day. Scale: $600 per month if you work six days a week.

10. Coaching for school exams ($0)

Offer one hour of maths or English coaching to a group of three students at $5 per hour each. That is $15 per hour. Do this four times a week.

Cost: $0. Revenue: $60 per week. Scale: $240 a month from one class.

How to choose which one is right for you

Do not pick the business with the biggest profit. Pick the one where you already know one customer. If you know a gardener, choose seed packets. If you know a shop owner, choose WhatsApp support. If you know a parent with school children, choose coaching.

Your first customer is free. Use them to test. If they pay, you have a business. If they do not, you have learned something at no cost.
