Ostrich Farming Guide

Ostrich chick rearing, nutrition, leather and meat production, and export opportunities for African farmers.

1
Why Ostrich Farming?
Start

Ostriches are the world's largest birds and one of the most profitable livestock ventures per hectare. Leather, meat, feathers, and eggs all generate income.

  • Ostrich leather is the strongest commercial leather — used for luxury handbags and boots.
  • Ostrich meat is red, low in fat, and commands premium prices in Europe and Asia.
  • One breeding pair can be worth $5,000-$15,000 depending on genetics and age.
Pro Tip: Ostrich farming requires patience. First returns come at 18-24 months, not weeks.

Key Takeaway: Ostriches are a high-value, long-term investment. Multiple products = multiple revenue streams.

Breeds & Sourcing Chicks
Locked

The African Black ostrich is the primary commercial breed. Sourcing quality chicks from certified breeders is critical.

  • African Black: best leather quality, most common in commercial farms.
  • Red-necked and Blue-necked: less common, different climate adaptations.
  • Day-old chicks are fragile. Buy 3-6 month old chicks for higher survival rates.
Pro Tip: Always inspect the breeder's farm. Ask for vaccination records and parent stock photos.

Key Takeaway: Buy from certified breeders. Older chicks survive better than day-olds.

Housing & Fencing
Locked

Ostriches are fast (70 km/h), strong, and curious. Your fencing must be serious — they will test it.

  • Perimeter fence: 2.2m high, buried 30cm deep (they dig), with small mesh at the base.
  • Night shelters: 3m x 3m per pair, with sand floors and good ventilation.
  • Chick brooders: heated to 30°C for the first 4 weeks, then reduce gradually.
Pro Tip: Use smooth wire, not barbed wire. Ostriches run into fences and barbs cause severe leg injuries.

Key Takeaway: Ostrich fencing is a capital investment. Do it right once, or lose birds repeatedly.

Nutrition & Feeding
Locked

Ostriches are omnivores with a simple digestive system. Their diet changes dramatically from chick to adult.

  • Chicks (0-3 months): 20-22% protein starter pellets + chopped greens.
  • Growers (3-14 months): 16-18% protein, plus lucerne/alfalfa and whole grain.
  • Adults: 14% protein maintenance diet. They also eat stones (grit) to aid digestion.
Pro Tip: Never feed moldy grain. Ostriches are extremely susceptible to aflatoxin poisoning.

Key Takeaway: Protein decreases with age. Clean, fresh feed always. No mold ever.

Health & Disease Management
Locked

Ostriches are hardy but vulnerable to specific diseases in their first year. Vigilance and vaccination are essential.

  • Aspergillosis (mold-related lung infection): the #1 killer of chicks. Keep bedding dry.
  • Avian influenza: reportable disease. Strict biosecurity prevents introduction.
  • Impaction: chicks eat sand, stones, or foreign objects. Use raised feeders and clean pens.
Pro Tip: Observe chicks daily. Lethargy, drooping wings, or labored breathing are emergency signs.

Key Takeaway: Dry bedding, clean water, and biosecurity prevent 95% of ostrich chick mortality.

Breeding & Incubation
Locked

Ostriches lay the largest eggs in the world. Natural incubation is possible, but artificial incubation gives you control.

  • A female lays 40-60 eggs per season (April-September in the Northern Hemisphere).
  • Incubation: 36.5°C, 25% humidity, turning every 2 hours. Hatch at 42 days.
  • A male sits at night, females during the day in natural systems.
Pro Tip: Store eggs at 15°C for up to 7 days before incubation without significant hatch loss.

Key Takeaway: Artificial incubation maximizes hatch rate. Control temperature and humidity precisely.

Leather, Meat & Feather Processing
Locked

The value is in processing. Raw products sell cheap; processed products export for dollars.

  • Leather: skin at 10-14 months. Tanning requires specialized chemicals or a tannery partner.
  • Meat: process at 10-14 months. Each bird yields 35-45kg of prime red meat.
  • Feathers: pluck during processing. Sold to fashion, costume, and duster manufacturers.
Pro Tip: Partner with an established tannery before you start. Leather quality depends entirely on post-slaughter handling.

Key Takeaway: Processing partnerships multiply your revenue. Never sell raw if you can process.

Export Market & Business Model
Locked

The real money in ostrich farming is export. Local markets pay pennies compared to European and Asian buyers.

  • EU and Japan are the largest markets for ostrich leather and meat.
  • You need CITES permits for international trade in ostrich products.
  • Join the International Ostrich Association for market access and breeding stock connections.
Pro Tip: Start with a tourism model: farm tours, egg painting, and photo ops generate cash flow while birds mature.

Key Takeaway: Export is the goal, but tourism and local sales fund your growth while you wait.
