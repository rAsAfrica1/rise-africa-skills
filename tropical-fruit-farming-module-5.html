5. Guava, Litchi and Macadamia

Three longer-term tropical crops with premium pricing and strong demand.

Module 5 of 6 · Tropical Fruit Farming for Profit

Guava

Guava is the most underrated tropical fruit. It produces within 2-3 years and yields heavily. Guava is high in vitamin C, high in demand for juice and jam, and tolerant of poor soil.

MetricValue
Years to fruit2-3
Yield per tree100-300 fruits/year
Yield per hectare20-40 t/year
Trees per hectare200-400
Price per kg$0.60-1.20
Productive life20-30 years

Why guava works

  • Hardy - tolerates poor soil, drought, and heat
  • Heavy bearer - 100-300 fruits per tree
  • Long life - 20-30 years
  • Multiple markets - fresh, juice, jam, puree, nectar
  • Year-round harvest in warm climates

Litchi

Litchi is a premium fruit with strong demand in Asia and Europe. Trees produce for 50+ years. Litchi requires cooler subtropical conditions at higher altitude.

MetricValue
Years to fruit4-6
Yield per mature tree50-150 kg
Yield per hectare8-15 t
Trees per hectare100-200
Price per kg$1.00-2.50
Productive life50+ years

Site requirements

  • Altitude 800-1,500 m (cool dry winter triggers flowering)
  • Rainfall 1,200-2,000 mm
  • Deep, well-drained soil pH 5.5-6.5
  • Frost-free
  • Dry spell before flowering essential (no rain for 4-6 weeks)

Macadamia

Macadamia is the most valuable tree nut in the world. It takes 5-7 years to first harvest but trees produce for 40-60 years. Premium prices and export demand make it attractive for patient farmers.

MetricValue
Years to first harvest5-7
Years to full production10-12
Yield per mature tree15-40 kg nut-in-shell
Yield per hectare3-5 t (nut-in-shell)
Trees per hectare200-300
Price per kg (NIS)$3.00-6.00
Productive life40-60 years

Site requirements

  • Altitude 600-1,500 m
  • Rainfall 1,200-2,500 mm/year, well-distributed
  • Deep, well-drained soil (over 1 m), pH 5.0-6.5
  • Frost-free
  • Shelter from strong wind (branches are brittle)

Macadamia processing

  1. Harvest nuts as they drop - do not pick from tree.
  2. De-husk within 24 hours.
  3. Dry to 10% moisture in 2-3 weeks.
  4. Store in cool, dry conditions or sell to a processor.
  5. Most smallholders sell nut-in-shell to a processor.
The patient tree rule: Litchi and macadamia take 5-7 years to first income. Plant them alongside shorter crops (passion fruit, papaya) so you have income while the trees establish.
Practical exercise 5: Visit a litchi or macadamia orchard. Ask about the years to first income, yield per tree, and current market price. Decide if the long wait fits your cash flow.
