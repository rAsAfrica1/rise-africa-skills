1. Creating a TikTok Account for Reselling

To start selling on TikTok, you need a business account. Download TikTok from Google Play Store or Apple App Store. Open the app and sign up using your phone number or email. Use a phone number that you check often because TikTok sends verification codes. After signing up, go to your profile and tap the three lines at the top right. Select 'Settings and privacy', then 'Account', then 'Switch to Business Account'. Choose 'Business' and select a category like 'Shopping and Retail' or 'Product/Service'. This gives you access to analytics, including how many people view your videos and their demographics. Use a clear profile photo, like your logo or a picture of yourself holding your product. Write a bio that tells people what you sell and how to order. For example: 'Quality sneakers in Lagos. DM to order. Pay on delivery.' Add a link to your WhatsApp or Instagram if you have one. In your settings, enable 'Allow others to find me' and 'Allow direct messages' so customers can contact you. Also set up two-factor authentication for security. If you plan to use TikTok Shop, you will need to connect it later, but for now, focus on building your presence. Post your first video within 24 hours of creating the account. TikTok's algorithm favours new accounts that post consistently. Aim to post at least once a day for the first week. Use hashtags like #TikTokMadeMeBuyIt, #ShopSmall, and #AfricanBusiness. Engage with other sellers by commenting on their videos. This helps you learn and get noticed. Remember, your account is your shop. Keep it professional but friendly. Respond to comments and messages quickly. In Africa, many successful resellers started with just a phone and a few products. You can do the same.

2. Filming Engaging Product Videos

Your product videos must grab attention in the first 3 seconds. Use your smartphone camera. Natural light is best: film near a window during the day, or outside in the shade. Avoid direct sunlight as it creates harsh shadows. Clean your lens before filming. For product demos, show the item in use. If you sell clothes, wear them and show how they fit. If you sell phone accessories, plug them in and show them working. Use a tripod or stack books to keep your phone steady. Film in short clips of 5-10 seconds each, then edit them together in TikTok or CapCut. Add text overlays to highlight key points: price, size, colour, and how to order. Speak clearly in English or a local language your audience understands. Keep videos between 15 and 30 seconds. Longer videos work for tutorials but not for quick sales. Use trending sounds from TikTok's sound library. To find them, tap 'Add sound' and look for songs with an arrow icon. Show the product from different angles. For example, for a handbag, show the front, inside, and strap. Include a call to action: 'DM me to order' or 'Click the link in my bio'. Post at times when your audience is active. In many African countries, peak times are 7-9 AM, 1-2 PM, and 7-10 PM. Test different times. Use hashtags: #TikTokMadeMeBuyIt, #ShopSmallBusiness, #AfricanFashion, #LagosMarket. Respond to comments with video replies to boost engagement. If someone asks a question, make a video answering it. This creates more content and builds trust. Always show the real product, not stock photos. Customers want to see what they will get. If you dropship, order a sample first to film. For handmade items, show the process. People love seeing how things are made. Keep your background clean and uncluttered. A simple wall or fabric works well. Add captions for people watching without sound. Use TikTok's auto-captions or add them manually. Finally, be consistent. Post at least one video daily. The more you post, the better you get and the more the algorithm favours you.

3. Using TikTok Trends to Boost Sales

TikTok trends are challenges, sounds, or formats that many users copy. Using them can get your product seen by more people. To find trends, open TikTok and tap the 'For You' page. Look for videos with a music note icon and an arrow. Also check the 'Discover' tab for hashtags like #TikTokMadeMeBuyIt, #Trending, #Challenge. When you see a trend, think about how to adapt it to your product. For example, the 'What I ordered vs What I got' trend is perfect for showing real products. Film a split screen: on one side, the product photo from your supplier; on the other, the actual item you received. This builds trust. Another trend is 'Things TikTok Made Me Buy' where you show items you bought because of TikTok. You can feature your own product as if you bought it. Use trending sounds even if they are in another language; the algorithm promotes them. But make sure the sound fits your video. For dance trends, you can dance while holding your product. For text-based trends, like 'Tell me you're a reseller without telling me', write text on screen and show your products. Participate in challenges like #SmallBusinessCheck where you show your workspace. Always add your own twist. Don't just copy; make it relevant to your product. Use 3-5 hashtags per video: one broad (#TikTokMadeMeBuyIt), one niche (#AfricanPrint), one location (#NairobiBusiness). Post your trend video within 24-48 hours of the trend peaking. Use TikTok's 'Analytics' to see which trends drive views to your profile. If a trend works, make more videos with it. Engage with other users' trend videos by commenting and duetting. Duetting means you film your reaction alongside their video. If someone shows a problem your product solves, duet it and show your solution. This puts your product in front of their audience. Be careful not to jump on trends that don't match your brand. For example, a serious business might avoid silly dance trends. But for many resellers, fun trends work well. Track your results: use TikTok's built-in analytics to see views, likes, shares, and profile visits. If a trend brings sales, note it. If not, try another. Trends change fast, so check daily. Set aside 15 minutes each morning to scroll and find trends. Save sounds you might use. Then film your version. Consistency is key: even if a trend doesn't go viral, it keeps your account active.

4. Going Live on TikTok to Sell

TikTok Live lets you sell in real time. You need at least 1,000 followers to go live. To start, tap the '+' button at the bottom, then swipe to 'LIVE'. Before going live, plan your session. Choose 5-10 products to show. Write down key details: price, sizes, colours, and any discounts. Set up your filming area with good lighting and a clean background. Use a tripod or prop your phone. Test your internet connection; a stable Wi-Fi or 4G is best. Tell your followers in advance by posting a video or story: 'Going live at 7 PM to show new arrivals. Don't miss it!' During the live, greet viewers by name. Show each product clearly. Demonstrate how it works. Mention the price and how to order: 'DM me the word "BUY" and your size.' Offer live-only discounts to encourage immediate purchases. For example, 'First 5 buyers get 10% off.' Use a countdown timer for flash sales. Interact with comments: answer questions, thank people for gifts, and acknowledge shares. If someone buys, shout them out: 'Thank you, Ama from Accra, for buying the red dress!' This encourages others. Keep the energy high. Speak clearly and enthusiastically. You can also do giveaways: 'I'll give a free gift to one person who shares this live.' To receive payments, share your mobile money number or payment link in the comments. For TikTok Shop, you can add products to your live so viewers can tap to buy. After the live, review the recording to see what worked. TikTok saves your live for 30 days. Post clips from the live as regular videos to reach more people. Go live at least twice a week. Consistency builds an audience. In Africa, many sellers go live in the evening when people are home. Test different times to see when you get the most viewers. Use a wired earphone with a mic for better audio. Keep a notepad to track orders. After the live, send payment details to buyers and confirm their orders. Ship quickly to build trust. Live selling is powerful because it creates urgency and personal connection. With practice, you can sell out your stock in one session.

5. TikTok Shop Setup and Integration

TikTok Shop lets you sell directly in the app. It is available in some African countries like South Africa, Nigeria, and Kenya, but check if it's available in yours. To set up, go to your profile, tap the three lines, then 'Settings and privacy', then 'Account', then 'TikTok Shop'. Follow the prompts to register as a seller. You will need a business license or ID, a bank account or mobile money account, and product photos. If TikTok Shop is not available in your country, you can still use other methods: add a link to your WhatsApp or Instagram in your bio, or use a third-party link like Linktree. For TikTok Shop, once approved, you can list products. Each listing needs a title, description, price, and up to 9 images. Use clear photos and include keywords in the title, like 'Men's Leather Shoes Size 42-45'. Set shipping options: you can ship yourself or use TikTok's logistics partners. Offer free shipping if possible; it increases sales. You can also run promotions like discounts and bundles. To integrate with your videos, when you film, tap 'Add link' and select your product. Viewers can then tap the product icon to buy. In live videos, you can pin products so viewers can shop while watching. Use TikTok's analytics to track views, clicks, and sales. If a product isn't selling, change the photos or price. Respond to customer messages within 24 hours. Ship orders within 2-3 days. Good reviews boost your shop's ranking. Ask buyers to leave a review after delivery. If you don't have TikTok Shop, you can still use TikTok to drive sales to your WhatsApp. Put your WhatsApp link in your bio and say 'DM to order'. Use a WhatsApp Business account to automate replies and catalogue products. Many African resellers use this method successfully. For payments, mobile money like M-Pesa, MTN MoMo, and Airtel Money are common. Always confirm payment before shipping. Keep records of orders and expenses. As you grow, consider registering your business for legal protection. TikTok Shop is a powerful tool, but even without it, you can sell effectively by directing traffic to your preferred platform.

6. Building a Following for Repeat Sales

To get repeat sales, you need a loyal following. Start by posting consistently. Aim for 1-2 videos daily. Use a content mix: 60% entertaining or educational, 30% product showcases, 10% personal stories. For example, show how you package orders, share customer reviews, or give tips on choosing products. Engage with your audience. Reply to every comment within a few hours. Like and reply to DMs. Ask questions in your videos: 'Which colour do you prefer?' This encourages comments and boosts your video. Collaborate with other small businesses. Do shoutouts for shoutouts: you promote their product, they promote yours. This exposes you to new audiences. Use TikTok's 'Duet' and 'Stitch' features to interact with other creators. Stitch means you clip a small part of their video and add your own commentary. If a customer posts a video using your product, duet it and thank them. This makes them feel valued and encourages others to post. Run contests: 'Tag 3 friends in the comments to win a free gift.' This increases reach. Offer a loyalty program: 'Buy 3 items, get 1 free' or 'Repeat customers get 10% off.' Keep a customer list (with permission) and send them WhatsApp messages about new stock. Use TikTok's 'Analytics' to see your top viewers' locations and times. Post when they are most active. Use hashtags that your audience follows: #TikTokMadeMeBuyIt, #ShopSmall, #AfricanBusiness, #SupportLocal. Create a series: 'Day 1 of building my fashion business' or 'Pack an order with me.' Series keep people coming back. Show your face and personality. People buy from people they trust. Share your struggles and wins. Be authentic. If you make a mistake, apologise and fix it. Good customer service leads to repeat sales. After a sale, follow up: 'Did you receive your order? How is it?' Ask for a review. Offer a discount on their next purchase. Build a community, not just a customer base. Respond to negative comments politely and take conversations to DM. Finally, be patient. Building a following takes time. Many successful African TikTok sellers started with zero followers. Post, learn, and improve. Celebrate small wins. With consistency, you will build a loyal following that buys from you again and again.

<h3>Why Fulfilment Decides Whether You Make Money</h3>

<p>Most new resellers obsess over content and ignore logistics. That is backwards. A video that gets 50,000 views but leads to 40 orders you cannot pack on time will destroy your shop rating faster than a video with 2,000 views and 10 clean deliveries. On TikTok Shop, your seller rating is driven by order defect rate, late dispatch, and cancellation rate. In most markets, a shop that drops below a threshold on these metrics loses visibility in the algorithm meaning your content stops being shown to buyers.</p>

<p>The practical rule: <strong>only scale your content when your fulfilment can absorb the volume.</strong> If you can pack and dispatch 15 orders a day reliably, do not run a promotion designed to generate 100 orders a day. Build in batches.</p>

Successful resellers in Nairobi and Lagos typically batch these: confirm and pack orders in the morning, dispatch by midday, follow up in the evening.</p>

<h3>Managing Returns Without Losing Your Margin</h3>

<p>Returns are normal in e-commerce, but in social commerce they can be brutal because your content created an emotional purchase. Three practices reduce return rates:</p>

<ul>
<li><strong>Show the product honestly on camera.</strong> Zoom in on stitching, fabric, size, and flaws. Buyers who know what they are getting return less.</li>
<li><strong>State size and fit clearly in every video.</strong> "This runs small order one size up" prevents a large share of returns.</li>
<li><strong>Confirm the order before dispatch.</strong> A 30-second WhatsApp voice note confirming colour, size, and address costs almost nothing and prevents costly failed deliveries.</li>
</ul>

<p>Set a clear, written return policy and put it in your TikTok bio and order confirmation message. Ambiguity creates disputes; disputes damage your shop rating.</p>

<div style="border:3px solid #b00020; background:#fdecea; padding:16px; margin:20px 0; font-family:Arial, sans-serif;">
<strong style="color:#b00020; font-size:16px;">SAFETY WARNING RESTRICTED AND REGULATED PRODUCTS</strong>
<p style="margin-top:8px; color:#7a0016;">Do <strong>not</strong> sell medicines, veterinary drugs, pesticides, unregistered cosmetics, food supplements making health claims, or any product requiring regulatory approval through TikTok Shop or social media unless you hold the required licence and the product is registered with your national regulator (e.g. NAFDAC in Nigeria, PPB in Kenya, FDA Ghana, SAHPRA in South Africa). Selling unregistered or counterfeit health, agricultural, or chemical products is illegal, can seriously harm buyers and animals, and exposes you to prosecution and permanent platform bans. If a supplier offers you "fast-moving" medicines, agrochemicals, or slimming products at unusually low prices, treat it as a red flag. Verify registration numbers directly with the national regulator before listing.</p>
</div>

<h3>Quick Reference Checklist</h3>

<ol>
<li><strong>Confirm TikTok Shop or hybrid model</strong> for your country before promising buyers a checkout method.</li>
<li><strong>Link a verified payout account</strong> (bank or mobile money) and confirm settlement timing in your seller dashboard.</li>
<li><strong>Calculate your true margin</strong>: selling price cost of goods platform fee payment fee delivery expected returns.</li>
<li><strong>Set a daily packing capacity</strong> and do not run promotions that exceed it.</li>
<li><strong>Batch your day</strong>: confirm and pack in the morning, dispatch by midday, follow up in the evening.</li>
<li><strong>Confirm every order in writing</strong> (WhatsApp or DM) with colour, size, and address before dispatch.</li>
<li><strong>Publish a clear return policy</strong> in your bio and order confirmation message.</li>
<li><strong>Never list restricted products</strong> (medicines, agrochemicals, unregistered cosmetics) without a valid licence and registration.</li>
</ol>
