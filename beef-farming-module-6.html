6. Health, Parasites and Disease Control

Tick-borne disease kills more cattle in Africa than anything else. Here is how to prevent it.

Module 6 of 12 · Beef Farming for Profit

Tick control - the foundation

Ticks transmit ECF, heartwater, anaplasmosis, and babesiosis. Tick control is the most important health task on any African cattle farm.

Tick resistance is real. Rotate acaricide classes every 6-12 months. Never use the same chemical year after year.

The ECF vaccine (ITM)

Infection and Treatment Method (ITM) is the standard vaccine for East Coast Fever.

  • Calves are vaccinated at 3-6 weeks old.
  • Vaccine is live - must be used within hours of preparation.
  • Calves must be monitored for 2-3 weeks after vaccination.
  • Some develop mild ECF and need treatment.
  • Lifelong immunity after successful vaccination.
  • Available in Kenya, Uganda, Tanzania, Malawi, Zambia, Zimbabwe, and others.

Foot care

  • Check hooves monthly - especially in wet season.
  • Trim overgrown hooves with hoof shears.
  • Footbath with 10% zinc sulphate or 5% copper sulphate for foot rot.
  • Provide dry, well-drained areas around troughs and gates.
  • Treat lameness early - a lame bull cannot serve, a lame cow cannot graze.

Biosecurity

  1. Quarantine new cattle for 30 days in a separate pen.
  2. Test for brucellosis and TB before introducing.
  3. Limit visitors - especially from other cattle farms.
  4. Footbath at the entrance for vehicles and people.
  5. Isolate sick animals immediately.
  6. Do not share bulls between herds without quarantine.
  7. Control movement of cattle within the farm.
  8. Disinfect handling facilities between uses.

Daily health routine

  1. Morning: Walk the herd. Check for limping, dull eyes, off-feed animals, coughing.
  2. Midday: Check water and count - a missing animal means a problem.
  3. Evening: Observe feeding behaviour. Sick cattle eat last or not at all.
  4. Weekly: Dip or spray for ticks. Check new cases of lameness or discharge.
  5. Monthly: Body condition score. Hoof check. Weigh sample cattle.
  6. Quarterly: Deworm, check tick resistance, review records.
  7. Annually: Vaccinations per calendar.

Emergency kit for the farm

  • Oxytetracycline (long-acting) - 500 ml
  • Ivermectin injectable - 250 ml
  • Albendazole oral - 1 L
  • Foot rot treatment (zinc sulphate) - 5 kg
  • Iodine, bandages, syringes, needles
  • Dipping chemical - concentrate
  • Mineral supplements - free-choice lick
  • Thermometer, hoof shears, obstetrical chains

When to call the vet

  • Multiple sudden deaths
  • Suspected ECF, heartwater, or FMD
  • Difficult calving
  • Abortion outbreak
  • Severe lameness in multiple animals
  • Nervous signs (circling, staggering)
The tick rule: Tick control is the foundation of cattle health in Africa. Weekly dipping is not optional - it is the difference between a healthy herd and losing cattle to ECF, heartwater, and anaplasmosis.
Practical exercise 6: Set up a health calendar. Mark dipping days, deworm dates, and vaccination months. Post it where you see it daily. Count the ticks on 5 cows weekly.
