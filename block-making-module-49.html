1 Understanding your market

Know who buys blocks and why. Your market includes builders, contractors, homeowners, and government projects.

  • What block types are in demand?
  • What prices do competitors charge?
  • What delivery times do customers expect?
  • What quality standards are required?

Visit construction sites, talk to builders, and observe trends.

2 Identifying your target customers

Not everyone is your customer. Focus on those who value what you offer.

  • Small local builders — regular orders, need quick delivery
  • Large contractors — bulk orders, may require credit
  • Homeowners — small orders, want advice
  • Landscapers — specialty blocks, edging
  • Government — strict specs, formal tenders

Create a simple customer profile for each type.

3 Pricing strategies

Price to win business and make profit.

  • Cost-plus: cost + margin (simple)
  • Competitive: match or beat competitors (risky)
  • Value-based: charge more for quality or special features
  • Volume discounts: lower price for large orders

Never compete on price alone. Compete on reliability and quality.

4 Promoting your business (offline)

Let people know you exist.

  • Signage at your yard
  • Business cards and flyers
  • Signs on delivery vehicles
  • Attend local builders' meetings
  • Word of mouth — ask happy customers to refer
  • Sponsor local events

5 Digital marketing basics

Use your phone to reach more customers.

  • WhatsApp Business: catalogue, quick replies
  • Facebook page: photos of blocks, projects, testimonials
  • Google Maps: list your business, get directions
  • Simple website or landing page (optional)

Post regularly. Respond fast.

6 Building relationships and repeat business

It costs less to keep a customer than to find a new one.

  • Deliver on time
  • Provide consistent quality
  • Follow up after delivery
  • Offer loyalty discounts
  • Remember their preferences

Happy customers become your salesforce.

7 Handling inquiries and closing sales

Turn inquiries into orders.

  1. Respond quickly — within an hour if possible
  2. Ask questions: block type, quantity, delivery date
  3. Provide a clear quote
  4. Follow up if no response
  5. Confirm order in writing (WhatsApp or email)

Be professional. First impression counts.

8 Customer service and complaints

Handle complaints well to keep customers.

  • Listen without arguing
  • Acknowledge the problem
  • Offer a solution (replacement, refund, discount)
  • Act quickly
  • Learn from mistakes

A complaint handled well can create a loyal customer.
