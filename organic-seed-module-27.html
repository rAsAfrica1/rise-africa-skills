🧠 Brain Opener

You have proven the model works. Now you want to grow. Most seed businesses fail at this exact moment - not because they cannot grow, but because they grow in the wrong order.

  • What is the right order for scaling a seed business?
  • What is contract growing and when does it make sense?
  • What is the biggest risk when scaling?
🎁 Reveal a strong answer

The right order is: customers first, then systems, then land, then people. Contract growing means working with other farmers who grow seed for you under your quality control. The biggest risk is that quality drops as volume grows - one bad batch at scale can lose every customer.

📖 Rudo Scales Without Falling

Rudo had one hectare of organic tomato seed. She had 12 customers. She had 92 percent germination. She was earning 8,000 dollars per season. She wanted to earn 30,000.

Her first plan was to rent 3 more hectares. Her aunt stopped her.

"Before you add land, add customers. Land without buyers is debt."

Rudo did the maths. She had 12 customers. If she doubled her customer list to 24, she could sell 3 hectares of production. But she did not have 3 hectares.

She had a smaller idea. What if she stayed on one hectare but produced more per hectare?

She improved her seeding rate, her irrigation, her weeding. Her yield per hectare rose from 15 to 22 kilograms. That was a 46 percent increase with the same land.

But she still needed more volume. So she asked two neighbouring farmers if they would grow seed for her under her instructions. She would supply the foundation seed, provide the training, do the quality control, and buy the harvest at a fixed price.

Both agreed. That was the beginning of her contract growing.

By year three, Rudo had her own 1 hectare plus 4 contract farmers. Total production: 5 hectares of tomato seed. She had 40 customers.

Revenue: 42,000 dollars. Her net: 14,000 dollars.

She had scaled by adding customers, then systems, then land, then people. In that order. And she had never lost a single quality batch.

💡 The Big Idea

Scale in the right order

Customers first. Systems second. Land third. People fourth. Most businesses try to scale land first because it is visible. But land without customers is cost, and land without systems is chaos. Scale in order and the business grows without breaking.

📚 Three Deep Lessons

1

The four stages of scaling

Stage 1 - Customer growth. Before adding anything to production, double your customer list. More customers means more certainty. It also means you can say no to bad buyers and yes to good ones.

Stage 2 - System growth. Before adding land, make your existing operations more efficient. Better seed rate. Better irrigation. Better weeding. Better processing. Every 10 percent improvement in efficiency is like getting 10 percent more land for free.

Stage 3 - Land growth. Now add land. Own it or rent it. One hectare at a time. Each hectare you add should have a customer lined up already.

Stage 4 - People growth. Hire. Train. Delegate. Your first hire should be someone who can do what you do not want to do. Your second hire should be someone who can do what you cannot do. Your third hire should be someone who can run the business without you.

2

Contract growing - the African way to scale

Contract growing means other farmers grow seed for you under your specifications. You supply the starter seed, the training, the quality standards, and often the inputs. They supply the land and labour. You buy the harvest at a pre-agreed price.

This is how most African seed companies scale. Kenya Seed Company works with thousands of contract growers. Seed Co in Zimbabwe and Zambia works with hundreds. Farmers earn 40 percent more than open market prices. Companies get volume without owning land.

The keys to contract growing: clear quality standards, regular monitoring, one year trial before scaling, written agreement, and payment on time every time.

3

The scaling risks

Quality risk. At 1 hectare, you control everything. At 5 hectares, you control less. At 10 hectares, you control nothing directly. Quality can drop silently. Test every batch. Never skip a quality check.

Cash risk. Scaling doubles your costs before it doubles your revenue. You need working capital to bridge the gap. Keep 3 months of cash reserve minimum. 6 is safer.

People risk. New hires may not care about quality the way you do. Write down your systems. Train your people. Pay them well. Fire quickly if quality slips.

Market risk. If one buyer is 60 percent of your revenue at 1 hectare, they will be 80 percent at 5 hectares. Do not scale with a concentrated customer base. Broaden first.

🛠 Hands-On - Write Your 3-Year Scale Plan

You need

  • A notebook
  • A pen
  • Your last season numbers
  1. Write your current state: hectares, customers, revenue, profit.
  2. Write your 3-year goal: hectares, customers, revenue, profit.
  3. Work backward. What must be true in year 2 to reach year 3?
  4. What must be true in year 1 to reach year 2?
  5. Write the three most important actions for year 1.
  6. For each, write the cost and the expected return.
  7. Write your top risk for scaling and your mitigation.
  8. Share the plan with one mentor or experienced farmer.
Why it works: A 3-year plan in writing is worth 100 times a plan in your head.

🎮 Game - Scale Simulation

3 to 12 players - 30 min

Each has costs and risks. Players must build to 5 hectares over 5 simulated years without running out of cash or quality. Most successful scale wins.

🌍 Real Stories from Africa

Kenya Seed Company contract model

Kenya Seed Company works with over 30,000 contract seed growers across Kenya. Farmers supply land and labour. The company supplies foundation seed, technical support, and a guaranteed purchase price. The model has scaled to over 20,000 hectares of seed production.

Rwanda cooperative scaling

A Rwandan women cooperative of 40 farmers scaled from 2 to 45 hectares in 5 years. Their method: one hectare at a time, three new customers per year, one new system improvement per season. Never rushed. Never failed.

🏆 Project Challenge

Scale by Customer First

Add 10 new customers before you add 1 hectare.

  1. Write down your current customer list.
  2. Set a goal to double it in 6 months.
  3. Find 5 new potential customers each month.
  4. Visit them. Learn what they need.
  5. Add them to your list.
  6. Only when your customer list doubles, add your first hectare of production.
  7. Repeat.
