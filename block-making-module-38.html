Pricing Your Blocks

8 lessons · 30 quiz questions · Business

1 Cost per block breakdown

You cannot price without knowing your cost. Break down every block into four categories:

  • Direct materials: Cement, sand, stone, water
  • Direct labour: Wages for making blocks
  • Overheads: Rent, power, maintenance, admin
  • Waste: Rejected blocks, spillage, breakage

Example — dense 440 × 215 × 100 mm block:

  • Cement (2 kg × $0.20): $0.40
  • Sand and stone (17 kg × $0.02): $0.34
  • Water, power, pallets: $0.10
  • Labour (5 staff × 500 blocks): $0.20
  • Overheads (rent, insurance, admin): $0.15
  • Waste allowance (5%): $0.06
  • Total cost: $1.25 per block

Every time a raw material price changes, recalculate.

2 Cement, sand, labour, water

The four biggest inputs to track.

Cement: 30–40% of cost. Biggest variable. Track weekly.

Sand and stone: 15–25% of cost. Track per delivery.

Labour: 10–20% of cost. Use piece rate or hourly. Calculate per-block labour cost.

Water: 1–3% of cost. Often overlooked, but adds up.

Track as % of selling price:

  • Cement: 25–30%
  • Sand/stone: 15–20%
  • Labour: 10–15%
  • Overheads: 10–15%
  • Profit: 20–30%

If any line drifts above these ranges, investigate immediately.

3 Overhead allocation

Overheads are costs you pay whether you produce 1,000 blocks or 10,000 — they don't change with volume. Per-unit they shrink as you scale.

Monthly overheads:

  • Rent
  • Electricity, water fixed charges
  • Insurance
  • Licences and permits
  • Admin (phone, internet, banking)
  • Equipment depreciation
  • Security

How to allocate: Total monthly overhead ÷ monthly production = overhead per block.

Example: $3,000 overheads ÷ 100,000 blocks = $0.03 per block. At 200,000 blocks = $0.015 per block. Doubling production halves overhead per block.

💡 Volume is the fastest way to cut cost per block. Fixed overheads stay flat; only material and labour scale.

4 Profit margin targets

Set a target margin before pricing — not after.

Standard margins by market:

  • Commodity blocks (dense, hollow): 15–25%
  • Pavers: 30–40%
  • Decorative (coloured, split-face): 40–55%
  • Specialty (fire, acoustic, refractory): 50–70%
  • Fair-faced architectural: 60–80%

Below 15% margin, you're working for free. Above 60%, you're in a specialist niche.

Gross vs net margin:

  • Gross margin = (Selling price − Variable cost) ÷ Selling price
  • Net margin = (Selling price − All costs) ÷ Selling price

Track net margin monthly. It's what pays you.

5 Market price survey

Your cost tells you the minimum price. The market tells you the maximum.

How to survey the market:

  • Call 5 hardware stores — ask their retail price per block
  • Call 3 competitors — ask their trade price per 1,000 blocks
  • Visit building sites — ask builders what they pay
  • Check online listings (Facebook Marketplace, Gumtree, etc.)

Compare your cost vs market:

  • If your cost is 40% below market → healthy margin
  • If your cost is 20% below market → thin margin, work on efficiency
  • If your cost is above market → you have a problem — reduce cost or exit

Undercut market by 5–10% to win initial customers. Not more.

6 Volume vs margin

Two ways to make money: high volume with low margin, or low volume with high margin.

Volume play: Standard dense blocks at 15–20% margin. Sell 100,000+ per month to builders and merchants. Thin margin, big total profit.

Margin play: Decorative, coloured, or specialty blocks at 40–60% margin. Sell 5,000 per month to architects and designers. High margin, lower volume.

Best strategy: Both. Standard blocks pay the bills (fixed costs). Specialty blocks generate the profit.

Don't try to be everything to everyone. Pick your products, know your margins, focus.

7 Discounts that hurt

Discounts cut straight into profit. The math is brutal.

Example: You make $0.75 profit at $2.00 selling price. A 20% discount ($0.40 off) leaves $0.35 profit — you'd need to sell 2.1× more blocks to make the same total profit.

Safe discounts:

  • Bulk discount: 5% off for 5,000+ blocks (buyer commits more money)
  • Pre-payment discount: 3% off for cash up front (improves cash flow)
  • Seasonal clearance: End-of-year stock clearance (frees warehouse space)
  • Loyalty discount: After 12 months of regular orders (retains good customers)

Unsafe discounts:

  • Discount on first enquiry (train the customer to haggle)
  • "Match any price" policies (race to the bottom)
  • Discount without reducing cost (just loses money)
⚠ Every discount needs a reason. No reason = no discount. Be polite but firm.

8 Raise prices without losing clients

Costs rise; prices must follow. Handle it carefully.

Steps to a successful price rise:

  1. Give notice — 30 days minimum. Not "effective immediately".
  2. Explain why — cement cost, fuel, exchange rate. Show, don't just tell.
  3. Adjust one product at a time — spread the impact
  4. Communicate personally — call your top 10 customers, don't just send a mass email
  5. Watch for drops — if sales fall over 30% in the first month, review

What to expect: Most customers understand. A few will leave — but they were price-only customers anyway. The rest stay because of reliability and quality.

Never absorb a rise indefinitely. It's better to lose 10% of customers than go bankrupt.

Bonus tactic: Bundle — raise price 8% but throw in free delivery for orders over 1,000 blocks. Feels fair, protects margin.

💰 Pricing is the highest-leverage decision in your business. A 10% price rise is often 40%+ more net profit. Get it right.
