Vision and Language Models - How AI Sees and Reads

~15 min read20 min gameIntermediate
Shared phone

🧠 Brain Opener

Two kinds of AI are changing the world fastest: vision models, which see images, and language models, which read and write text. Sometimes they work together - and that combination is what makes AI feel almost human.

  • How does a vision model see an image?
  • How does a language model understand words?
  • What happens when you combine them?
🎁 Reveal a good answer

A vision model turns an image into a grid of numbers (pixels), then learns patterns in those numbers. A language model turns words into lists of numbers (tokens), then learns patterns in those numbers. When combined, a model can look at a photo and describe it in words, or read a description and draw the photo.

📖 Vimbai Builds a Photo Sorter

Vimbai was seventeen. Her aunt was a wedding photographer with 40,000 photos on her laptop. Every wedding produced 500 to 800 photos. The aunt spent hours sorting them into albums.

Vimbai offered to help. She wanted to build a small AI that could sort the photos automatically - by faces, by location, by time of day.

She started with faces. She learned about vision models. A vision model takes a photo and turns it into a grid of numbers. It looks for patterns in those numbers. After training, it can detect faces.

She learned about language models too. A language model takes words and turns them into tokens. It learns patterns in text.

Then she discovered multimodal models - models that do both. She could send a photo and a question. The model would answer in text.

She tested it. "How many people are in this photo?" The model answered. "Is this indoors or outdoors?" The model answered. "Is this a wedding or a birthday?" The model answered correctly 8 times out of 10.

She built a small system. For every photo, ask three questions. Tag the photo with the answers. Then sort by tags.

It was not perfect. The model made mistakes. But it cut sorting time from three hours per wedding to forty minutes. Her aunt was delighted.

Vimbai learned the difference between vision and language the hard way - by trying to build with both.

💡 The Big Idea

Images and words become numbers

Vision models turn pixels into numbers and find patterns. Language models turn words into numbers and find patterns. Neither understands in the human sense. Both find patterns in numbers at scale. When you combine them, you get AI that can look at a photo and write a caption, or read a caption and generate a photo.

📚 Three Deep Lessons

1

How vision models see

An image is a grid of pixels. Each pixel is three numbers (red, green, blue). A 1000 by 1000 photo has 3 million numbers. That is the input to a vision model.

The model learns patterns in those numbers. Early layers detect edges. Middle layers detect shapes. Late layers detect objects - faces, cars, cats. The model does not know what a cat is. It learns which pixel patterns go with the label cat.

2

How language models read

A language model breaks text into tokens - usually words or word pieces. Each token becomes a list of numbers - an embedding. The model looks at patterns between tokens. It learns which tokens tend to appear near which other tokens.

When you ask a question, the model generates an answer token by token. Each new token is chosen because it fits the pattern of the input. This is why language models can be wrong: they are predicting the next likely token, not checking the truth.

3

Multimodal - when they work together

A multimodal model has both a vision part and a language part. The vision part turns images into embeddings. The language part turns text into embeddings. The two are combined so the model can reason across both.

This is what allows AI to look at a photo and answer questions. It is also what allows AI to generate images from text descriptions. The two modalities are aligned through training on millions of image-text pairs.

🛠 Hands-On - Test a Vision Model

You need

  • A phone with an AI app that supports images
  • Ten photos from your home
  • A notebook
  1. Pick ten photos: family, objects, places, animals, food.
  2. For each, ask the AI three questions: what is in this photo, is it indoor or outdoor, how many people are in it.
  3. Write down the answers.
  4. Compare with what you know is true. Count how many the AI got right.
  5. Try the same questions with the AI in a different language (Shona, Ndebele, French). Note what changes.
  6. Write 3 sentences about what worked and what failed.
Why it works: Multimodal models are powerful and imperfect. Testing them yourself shows you where they fail - faces, colours, small details, local objects.

🎮 Game - Vision vs Language

3 to 10 players - 20 min

Split into two teams. Team A describes an image using only words (no drawing). Team B has to draw the image from the description. Then compare. Discuss: what was lost in translation from image to words to image?

🤖 AI-Era Skill

Multimodal AI is best tested with your own photos and your own questions.

Try this prompt: "Here is a photo of my street. Describe what you see in detail, then tell me what you do not know about this place."

Check the AI description against what you know. The second part - what it does not know - is often more interesting than the first.

🌍 Real Stories from Africa

Zimbabwe crop disease detection

Zimbabwean farmers take a photo of a sick plant with their phone. A vision model classifies the disease with 90 percent accuracy. The model was trained on 50,000 labelled photos from across Southern Africa. Farmers get a diagnosis in seconds.

Kenyan wildlife monitoring

In Kenya, camera traps in national parks take millions of photos. Vision models sort the photos by animal species. What used to take researchers months now takes hours. The models were trained on 200,000 labelled images by Kenyan zoologists.

🏆 Project Challenge

Build a Photo Tagger

Build a small system to tag and sort photos.

  1. Pick 50 photos from your phone or family.
  2. Decide on five tags: person, place, activity, mood, time of day.
  3. For each photo, ask a multimodal AI one question per tag.
  4. Record the AI answer and whether it was right.
  5. Count the AI accuracy per tag. Which tag is hardest?
