1. Introducing Yourself and Your Business

In Spanish-speaking business settings, a proper introduction starts with a firm handshake and direct eye contact. Begin with "Buenos das" (good morning) or "Buenas tardes" (good afternoon), then say "Me llamo [your name]" (My name is...). For your business, use "Soy el fundador de [business name]" (I am the founder of...) or "Trabajo con [business name]" (I work with...). If you're in agriculture, say "Nos dedicamos a la agricultura" (We are dedicated to agriculture).

Africans often have three names, so practice saying your full name slowly. Example: "Me llamo Amara Okonkwo. Soy de Nigeria. Mi empresa se llama Green Harvest y vendemos mango orgnico a Espaa." (My name is Amara Okonkwo. I am from Nigeria. My company is called Green Harvest and we sell organic mango to Spain.) Notice the use of "se llama" for the company name.

When someone introduces themselves, respond with "Mucho gusto" (nice to meet you) or "Encantado/Encantada" (delighted). To ask about their business: "A qu se dedica su empresa?" (What does your company do?). If they speak fast, say "Ms despacio, por favor" (Slower, please).

In many African cultures, we value relationship before business. In Spanish-speaking countries like Equatorial Guinea (where Spanish is official), they also appreciate a personal touch. So after the basics, add "Llevo cinco aos en este negocio" (I've been in this business for five years) or "Tenemos clientes en tres pases" (We have clients in three countries). Numbers build credibility.

Practice with a partner: record yourself on your phone using the voice memo app. Listen for clear pronunciation of "ll" (sounds like 'y' in 'yes') and "" (sounds like 'ny' in 'canyon'). Repeat until you can introduce yourself in under 30 seconds. This is your elevator pitch in Spanish.

2. Small Talk with Other Entrepreneurs

Small talk in Spanish business settings often revolves around family, travel, food, and football. Start with "Cmo est?" (How are you?) or "Qu tal?" (How's it going?). A safe topic is the weather: "Hace mucho calor hoy, no?" (It's very hot today, right?). In Africa, we talk about rain and harvest; in Spanish, "la temporada de lluvias" (rainy season) is common.

Ask about their journey: "De dnde es usted?" (Where are you from?). If they say "Soy de Colombia," you can respond "He odo que el caf colombiano es excelente" (I've heard Colombian coffee is excellent). This shows you know their country. For Africans, mention "Me encantara visitar [country] algn da" (I would love to visit [country] someday).

Food is a great connector. Say "La comida de mi pas es muy picante" (The food of my country is very spicy) or "Me encanta el arroz con pollo" (I love rice with chicken). Ask "Cul es su plato tpico?" (What is your typical dish?). If they mention a dish you don't know, say "No conozco ese plato, cmo se prepara?" (I don't know that dish, how is it prepared?).

Football (soccer) is universal. "Le gusta el ftbol?" (Do you like football?) works. In Spanish-speaking Africa, like Equatorial Guinea, they follow La Liga. You can say "Soy fantico del Barcelona" (I'm a Barcelona fan) or "Apoyo al Real Madrid" (I support Real Madrid). Avoid politics and religion until you know them better.

Useful phrases: "Cunteme ms" (Tell me more), "Y usted?" (And you?), "Interesante" (Interesting). When you don't understand, say "No entiendo, puede repetir?" (I don't understand, can you repeat?). Practice with a friend: set a timer for 5 minutes and only speak Spanish about your day. This builds fluency for networking events.

3. Discussing Collaboration Opportunities

When discussing collaboration, use clear, direct language. Start with "Me gustara explorar una colaboracin" (I would like to explore a collaboration). Then explain what you offer: "Nosotros podemos proporcionar [product/service]" (We can provide...). For example, "Podemos proporcionar caf de Etiopa" (We can provide coffee from Ethiopia).

Ask about their needs: "Qu busca en un socio?" (What do you look for in a partner?). Listen for keywords like "distribuidor" (distributor), "inversor" (investor), "socio local" (local partner). If they say "Necesitamos un distribuidor en frica Occidental," respond "Tenemos contactos en Ghana y Nigeria" (We have contacts in Ghana and Nigeria).

Discuss terms carefully. Use numbers: "Podemos empezar con 100 unidades" (We can start with 100 units). Ask about timelines: "Cundo podramos firmar un acuerdo?" (When could we sign an agreement?). If you need to think, say "Necesito consultarlo con mi equipo" (I need to consult with my team).

Common collaboration models: "joint venture" (empresa conjunta), "acuerdo de distribucin" (distribution agreement), "franquicia" (franchise). For African businesses, highlight unique advantages: "Tenemos acceso al mercado de la SADC" (We have access to the SADC market). SADC is the Southern African Development Community.

Be prepared to talk about logistics: "Cmo manejamos el envo?" (How do we handle shipping?). Mention Incoterms like FOB (Free on Board) or CIF (Cost, Insurance, Freight). If they use a term you don't know, ask "Qu significa eso?" (What does that mean?).

End with next steps: "Le enviar una propuesta por correo electrnico" (I will send you a proposal by email). Or "Podemos agendar una videollamada?" (Can we schedule a video call?). Use tools like Google Meet or Zoom. Practice this dialogue with a colleague playing the other entrepreneur.

4. Attending Trade Fairs and Exhibitions

At trade fairs, you need quick, effective Spanish. When you arrive at a booth, say "Buenos das, puedo hacerle algunas preguntas?" (Good morning, can I ask you some questions?). If they say yes, start with "Qu productos ofrece su empresa?" (What products does your company offer?).

If you are exhibiting, greet visitors: "Bienvenido a nuestro stand" (Welcome to our booth). Hand them a brochure and say "Aqu tiene nuestro catlogo" (Here is our catalog). Ask "Qu tipo de productos busca?" (What type of products are you looking for?). If they are interested, say "Podemos ofrecerle un precio especial para pedidos grandes" (We can offer you a special price for large orders).

Key phrases for trade fairs: "Cul es su precio por unidad?" (What is your price per unit?), "Cul es el pedido mnimo?" (What is the minimum order?), "Tienen representantes en frica?" (Do you have representatives in Africa?). Write down answers. Use a notebook or your phone's notes app.

Collect business cards. Say "Me puede dar su tarjeta?" (Can you give me your card?). If they don't have one, ask for their email: "Cul es su correo electrnico?" (What is your email?). Repeat it back to confirm: "Entonces, es juan@empresa.com, verdad?" (So it's juan@empresa.com, right?).

Common trade fairs in Africa: "Feria Internacional de Dakar" (Dakar International Fair), "Feria de Lagos" (Lagos Fair). In Spanish-speaking countries: "Feria de Madrid" (Madrid Fair), "Expo Guadalajara" (Guadalajara Expo). Mention these to show you know the scene.

After the fair, follow up within 48 hours. Send an email: "Fue un placer conocerle en la feria. Adjunto nuestra presentacin." (It was a pleasure meeting you at the fair. I attach our presentation.) Use a tool like Mailtrack to see if they opened it. Practice a 2-minute pitch in Spanish before the event.

5. Exchanging Contact Information and References

Exchanging contact info in Spanish is straightforward. Say "Me permite su nmero de telfono?" (May I have your phone number?). Or "Podemos intercambiar WhatsApp?" (Can we exchange WhatsApp?). WhatsApp is widely used in Africa and Latin America. If they agree, say "Mi nmero es +234 801 234 5678" (My number is...). In Spanish, read numbers digit by digit: "dos, tres, cuatro..."

For email, say "Mi correo electrnico es amara@greenharvest.com" (My email is...). Spell it if needed: "a, m, a, r, a..." Use the Spanish alphabet: a, be, ce, che, de, e, efe, ge, hache, i, jota, ka, ele, eme, ene, ee, o, pe, cu, erre, ese, te, u, uve, doble uve, equis, ye, zeta. Practice spelling your name and email.

Ask for references: "Conoce a alguien que pueda recomendarme?" (Do you know someone who can recommend me?). Or "Puede ser mi referencia?" (Can you be my reference?). If they say yes, provide their name and contact to potential partners. In African business culture, personal references are powerful.

Useful phrases: "Le presento a mi socio, [name]" (Let me introduce my partner, ...). "Trabajamos con [company] en Kenia" (We work with ... in Kenya). "Puede contactar a [name] para ms informacin" (You can contact ... for more information).

Store contacts properly. In your phone, add a note: "Conocido en Feria de Dakar, interesado en caf." Use a CRM like HubSpot (free) or a simple Google Sheet. Follow up with a message: "Un placer conocerle. Aqu est mi contacto." (Nice to meet you. Here is my contact.)

If someone gives you a reference, thank them: "Muchas gracias por la referencia." Then follow up with the referred person: "[Name] me sugiri que hablara con usted." ( ... suggested I speak with you.) This opens doors. Practice this exchange with a friend until it feels natural.

6. Practice: Role-play a Networking Event

Now put everything together. Find a partner (friend, colleague, or language exchange partner on Tandem or HelloTalk). Set up a mock networking event. One person plays an African entrepreneur (you), the other plays a Spanish-speaking investor or distributor. Use a timer: 10 minutes total.

Round 1: Introduction. Walk up, shake hands, and say: "Buenos das, me llamo [name]. Soy de [country]. Mi empresa se llama [name] y nos dedicamos a [sector]." Then ask: "Y usted?" (And you?). Practice until smooth.

Round 2: Small talk. Talk about weather, food, or football. Use phrases like "De dnde es?" "Le gusta el ftbol?" "Cul es su plato tpico?" Keep it light. After 3 minutes, move to business.

Round 3: Collaboration. Say: "Me gustara explorar una colaboracin. Nosotros podemos proporcionar [product]. Qu busca usted?" Discuss numbers, timelines, and next steps. Use "Le parece bien si...?" (Does it seem good to you if...?).

Round 4: Exchange contacts. Say: "Me permite su WhatsApp?" and "Cul es su correo electrnico?" Write them down. Then say: "Le enviar una propuesta maana." (I will send you a proposal tomorrow.)

After the role-play, review. Record it on your phone. Listen for mistakes. Common errors: using "t" instead of "usted" (use "usted" for formal networking), forgetting to conjugate verbs, or speaking too fast. Correct them.

Repeat the role-play with different scenarios: one where you are the buyer, one where you are the seller. Use real numbers from your business. For example, "Vendemos 500 kg de cacao al mes" (We sell 500 kg of cocoa per month). This builds confidence.

Finally, create a cheat sheet with 10 key phrases and keep it in your pocket at real events. Practice 15 minutes daily for one week. You'll be ready to network in Spanish.

<h2>Practical Reference: Networking and Partnerships in Spanish-Speaking African Agribusiness</h2>

<p>This practical reference supports the module <strong>Networking and Partnerships</strong> in the RISE AFRICA Skills course <em>Spanish Language</em>. It is designed for African agribusiness owners, cooperative managers, exporters and agri-service providers who need to build commercial relationships with Spanish-speaking counterparts in Equatorial Guinea, Spain, Latin America and the wider Hispanic business world. The focus is business-first: how to introduce yourself, make small talk, discuss collaboration, attend trade fairs, and exchange contacts all in practical Spanish.</p>

<p>Africa's trade with Spanish-speaking markets is growing. Equatorial Guinea is the only sovereign African state where Spanish is an official language, but Spanish is also used in business across Morocco, Western Sahara, and increasingly in Angola and Mozambique due to Latin American investment. Meanwhile, Spain is a major importer of African cocoa, coffee, fish and horticultural products. According to <strong>UN Comtrade</strong> (2023), Spain imported over USD 1.2 billion in agricultural goods from Sub-Saharan Africa, with cocoa, coffee and fish leading. This makes Spanish-language networking a direct commercial skill, not a cultural extra.</p>

<h3>3. Discussing Collaboration Opportunities</h3>
<p>Move from rapport to proposal with structured language. Use <em>propongo</em> (I propose), <em>sugiero</em> (I suggest), <em>le interesara?</em> (would you be interested?). Always clarify volume, price, delivery, and payment terms. African exporters should prepare a one-page Spanish-language offer sheet.</p>

<p>Key collaboration models:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Distribucin:</strong> <em>Buscamos un distribuidor exclusivo en Espaa.</em> (We seek an exclusive distributor in Spain.)</li>
<li><strong>Joint venture:</strong> <em>Proponemos una empresa conjunta para procesar aqu.</em> (We propose a joint venture to process here.)</li>
<li><strong>Suministro:</strong> <em>Podemos suministrar 20 toneladas mensuales.</em> (We can supply 20 tonnes monthly.)</li>
<li><strong>Inversin:</strong> <em>Necesitamos inversin para una planta de envasado.</em> (We need investment for a packaging plant.)</li>
</ul>

<h3>4. Attending Trade Fairs and Exhibitions</h3>
<p>Spanish-language trade fairs relevant to African agribusiness include <strong>Fruit Attraction</strong> (Madrid), <strong>Alimentaria</strong> (Barcelona), and <strong>Saln del Gourmet</strong> (Madrid). In Africa, the <strong>Feria Internacional de Malabo</strong> (Equatorial Guinea) and <strong>SIAM</strong> (Morocco) offer Spanish-language environments.</p>

<p>At a fair, use the <strong>30-second pitch</strong>: <em>Buenos das. Soy [name] de [company] en [country]. Producimos [product] de alta calidad. Buscamos [partner type] en [market]. Tiene cinco minutos?</em></p>

<h3>5. Exchanging Contact Information and References</h3>
<p>Always ask for a business card and a reference. In Spanish: <em>Me puede dar su tarjeta?</em> (Can you give me your card?) and <em>Podra recomendarme a alguien en su red?</em> (Could you recommend someone in your network?). Follow up within 48 hours with a short Spanish email.</p>

<p>Template follow-up email:</p>

<p><em>Estimado/a [nombre]:</em></p>

<p><em>Saludos cordiales,<br/>[Su nombre]<br/>[Empresa], [Pas]</em></p>
