2. Guinea Fowl - Housing, Feeding, Breeding

Guinea fowl is hardier than chickens but has specific habits that must be managed.

Module 2 of 6 · Guinea Fowl and Quail Farming for Profit

Housing

Guinea fowl are semi-wild. They fly well, roost in trees if allowed, and wander far if not contained. Good housing is essential.

RequirementSpecification
Floor space0.3-0.4 m2 per adult bird
Roof height2.2-2.5 m (they fly up to roost)
Perches1 m per 5 birds, 60-90 cm off ground
Run area2-3 m2 per bird, covered on top
WallsWire mesh 2 cm, at least 1.5 m high
RoofSolid, prevents rain and wild birds
NestsGround nests, 30 x 30 cm, one per 4 hens

Guinea fowl are prone to panicking and "piling" at night, which smothers birds. Dim lighting, perches, and low-stress handling prevent this.

Guinea fowl thrive on free-range - they eat ticks, beetles, grasshoppers, seeds, and greens. Free-range reduces feed cost by 30-50%.

Brooding keets

Keets (baby guinea fowl) are fragile and need care:

  1. Temperature: 35 C in week 1, reduce 3 C per week.
  2. Water: dip each keet's beak in water at arrival.
  3. Feed: chick mash crumble on flat trays for first 3 days.
  4. Space: 0.05 m2 per keet for first 4 weeks.
  5. Brooder guard: cardboard ring, 40 cm high, first 2 weeks.
  6. Shade and shelter - keets chill easily.
  7. Vaccination: Newcastle at day 7, 21, and week 8.
Keets are fragile. First-week mortality can reach 30% if they get cold or wet. Pre-heat the brooder 24 hours before arrival and keep it dry.

Breeding

ParameterValue
Age at first egg24-32 weeks
Eggs per year80-150
Breeding ratio1 male to 3-4 females
Incubation26-28 days
Laying seasonSpring-summer (long days)
Egg hatch rate60-75% (natural), 80-90% (incubator)

Guinea fowl are seasonal breeders - they lay mainly in the long-day months. Artificial lighting can extend the laying period.

Egg collection

  • Guinea fowl hide their nests. Provide dark, quiet nest boxes.
  • Collect eggs twice daily - they crack easily on the ground.
  • Handle gently - shells are thick but pointed ends crack.
  • Store at 15-18 C, 75% humidity, for up to 7 days.

Sexing

Guinea fowl are difficult to sex until 8-12 weeks:

  • Voice: males have a one-note call ("chi-chi-chi"), females two-note ("buck-wheat")
  • Helmet: males have a larger, more upright helmet
  • Wattles: males have larger wattles
  • Body: males stand taller, more upright posture

The free-range rule: Guinea fowl are 40-50% cheaper to feed if allowed to forage. But guard against predators - they are easy prey for hawks, snakes, and dogs.
