Teaching a Machine - Training with Paper Cards

🧠 Brain Opener

You can teach a machine without a computer. All you need is a stack of cards and two friends. This module shows you how training really works.

  • What is training?
  • Who teaches the machine?
  • What happens if you teach badly?
🎁 Reveal a good answer

Training is showing the machine many examples and telling it the right answer. You teach it. If you teach badly - with few examples, or with wrong answers - the machine learns badly. The teacher matters as much as the machine.

📖 Tawanda Trains the Bean Sorter

Tawanda was ten. His grandmother sold beans at the market. Every week, Tawanda helped her sort the beans into two piles - good beans and bad beans.

"This one is good," his grandmother said, holding up a bean. "This one has a hole."

Tawanda got very fast at sorting. He could sort a whole bowl in five minutes. But his little brother could not.

"What if we taught a machine to do it?" Tawanda asked.

His grandmother laughed. "Machines are expensive."

"But I can teach it with cards," Tawanda said. "Like this."

He made fifty small cards. On each card he drew a bean - some good, some with holes, some too small. He wrote "good" or "bad" at the bottom of each card.

Then he asked his brother: "Look at this card. Do not read the bottom. What do you think it is?"

His brother guessed. Some right, some wrong. Tawanda showed him the answer. After twenty cards, his brother guessed almost every one. After fifty cards, he never got it wrong.

"Now you know how to sort," Tawanda said. "I taught you with cards."

"Then I can sort the beans?"

"Yes. And if I want to teach a real machine, I would do the same thing. Just a hundred times more cards."

💡 The Big Idea

Training is teaching with examples

Training means showing a machine lots of examples, each one with the right answer. The machine looks at the examples. If it sees a hundred drawings of good beans, it learns what a good bean looks like. When it sees a new card, it can guess the right answer. The more examples, the better the machine.

📚 Three Deep Lessons

1

What training looks like

Imagine fifty cards, each with a bean drawn on it. At the bottom of each card, the right answer: "good" or "bad." You hold up a card without showing the answer. The machine (or your friend) guesses. Then you flip the card and show the answer.

If the guess is wrong, the machine corrects itself. This is called learning. Do it a hundred times and the machine will get most new cards right. This is the whole process. No magic. Just examples and corrections, over and over.

2

Good training needs good examples

If all your cards show green beans, the machine will only know green beans. Show it a brown bean and it will be confused. This is why trainers work so hard to collect examples that cover everything.

If you want a machine to recognise all African handwritings, you have to show it handwriting from all African countries. Otherwise it will only recognise one style. The data is the training. Without good data, no amount of clever machine will work.

3

When the machine gets it wrong

Even after good training, machines make mistakes. A card that is neither good nor bad. A new shape the machine has never seen. The machine will guess, and sometimes guess wrong.

This is normal. Real AI trainers keep a stack of "test cards" - cards the machine has never seen. They test the machine on these. If it passes, it is ready. If it fails, they train more.

🛠 Hands-On - Train a Friend to Sort

You need

  • 30 small cards or papers
  • A pencil
  • Two friends
  1. Draw or write one example on each card - for beans, animals, fruit, anything with two categories.
  2. Write the right answer on the back.
  3. Show a card to your friend, front side only. Ask them to guess.
  4. Flip the card and show the answer.
  5. Do this with 15 cards. Then test with 5 new cards your friend has never seen.
  6. Count how many they get right on the new cards.
Why it works: This is exactly how AI training works. Examples with answers, then a test on new examples. The friend is the machine.

🎮 Game - Train the Class

4 to 20 players - 15 min

One player is the trainer. They hold up cards one by one. The class guesses. The trainer reveals the answer. After twenty cards, the trainer holds up five new cards. Score the class on how many they get right. Then swap the trainer. Which trainer produced the best score? Discuss why.

🤖 AI-Era Skill

AI needs examples to learn. Give it a clear task with a few examples and see how it does.

Try this prompt: "I will show you ten short sentences and tell you whether each one is happy or sad. Then I will give you five new sentences and you guess. Are you ready? Sentence 1: The sun is shining today and I feel good - happy."

After ten examples and five guesses, check the AI score. Any mistakes are because the examples did not cover enough cases.

🌍 Real Stories from Africa

Zimbabwe crop disease training

Zimbabwean farmers take photos of sick plants. AI trainers collect thousands of these photos, each labelled with the disease name. The AI learns to spot the disease from the photo. A farmer takes one picture, and the phone tells them what is wrong.

Kenyan handwriting recognition

A Kenyan startup collected handwriting samples from 5,000 students across the country. They trained an AI to read all their writing. Now the AI marks exam papers in minutes instead of weeks. The training took three months.

🏆 Project Challenge

Train Someone in Your Family

Teach one skill to one person using the training method.

  1. Pick a simple skill - sorting laundry, matching socks, identifying trees.
  2. Make 20 example cards. Write the answer on the back.
  3. Sit with your family member. Do 15 training cards.
  4. Test with 5 new cards they have never seen.
  5. Count how many they get right.
  6. Write down what worked and what did not.
