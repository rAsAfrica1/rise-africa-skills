1. Identifying Your Ideal Client: Homes vs. Offices

Start by deciding whether you want to clean homes, offices, or both. This choice shapes everything: your pricing, your tools, your schedule. 3. Conducting Market Surveys to Understand Client Needs

This practical reference gives you the tools to identify who will actually pay for cleaning services in your city, what they will pay, and how to prove demand before you buy equipment or hire staff. In most African markets, cleaning is a relationship business: trust, reliability and referrals matter more than glossy branding. Your research must therefore focus on three questions: Who has the money and the pain? What are they currently paying or sacrificing? How will they hear about you? 1. Identifying Your Ideal Client: Homes vs. Offices

Residential and commercial clients buy cleaning for different reasons. A busy two-income household in Nairobi's Kilimani or Lagos's Lekki buys time and peace of mind. A 40-person office in Accra's Airport City buys professional appearance, staff health and compliance. Your ideal client is the segment where your strengths (location, transport, staff availability, price point) match their willingness to pay.

Start by mapping your catchment area within a realistic travel radius â usually 30â45 minutes by matatu, danfo, tro-tro or boda boda. Beyond that, transport cost and lateness destroy margins. Visit or call at least 20 potential clients in each segment before deciding.

2. Analyzing Local Competition and Pricing Strategies

In most African cities, your competition falls into four types: informal individual cleaners (cheapest, unreliable), small registered cleaning firms (your direct rivals), in-house staff hired directly by offices, and international facility-management companies (premium, out of reach for most SMEs). Call or visit at least 10 competitors and ask, as a prospective client, for a quote. Record what they charge, what is included, how they are insured, and how quickly they respond. Then benchmark against your own cost structure.
