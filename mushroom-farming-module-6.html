1. Oyster Mushroom Cultivation

Oyster mushrooms are the easiest to grow in Africa. You need pasteurised straw, a dark humid room, and spawn. Start by chopping wheat or rice straw into 2-5 cm pieces. Soak 10 kg of straw in 100 litres of water with 1 kg of lime for 18 hours. Drain and steam for 1 hour at 80Â°C, or soak in hot water at 65Â°C for 30 minutes. Cool to room temperature.

Mix 500 g of oyster spawn (grain or sawdust) with the straw. Pack into clear plastic bags (30x50 cm) with holes every 10 cm. Keep in a dark room at 25-28Â°C. After 14 days, the straw turns white with mycelium. Move bags to a fruiting room with 85-95% humidity and 20-24Â°C. Provide 12 hours of light daily (a fluorescent bulb works).

Mist bags twice daily. Mushrooms appear in 3-5 days. Harvest when caps are flat but edges still curled. Twist and pull. Each bag yields 500-800 g over 3 flushes. Soak bags in water for 12 hours between flushes. In Africa, use a simple greenhouse with shade net and plastic sheet. A 10 kg straw batch gives about 3 kg of mushrooms. Sell fresh in local markets or dry for longer storage.

2. Button Mushroom Cultivation

Button mushrooms need composted manure, not straw. In Africa, use horse or cow manure mixed with wheat straw and gypsum. Build a compost pile: 500 kg manure, 500 kg straw, 20 kg gypsum, and 10 kg urea. Turn every 2 days for 14 days, keeping moisture at 70%. Pasteurise at 60Â°C for 8 hours, then cool to 25Â°C.

Mix 5 litres of button spawn per 100 kg compost. Fill into trays or beds 20 cm deep. Cover with plastic for 14 days. When white mycelium runs through, add a 3 cm casing layer of peat moss and lime (pH 7.5). Keep at 18Â°C and 90% humidity. After 10 days, drop temperature to 16Â°C and increase fresh air. Mushrooms appear in 3-4 days.

Harvest buttons when caps are 3-5 cm wide, before they open. Twist and pull. Each 100 kg compost yields 15-25 kg over 3 flushes. In hot African climates, grow in caves or insulated rooms. Button mushrooms are less forgiving than oysters, so start with a small trial. Use a thermometer and hygrometer. Sell fresh to hotels and supermarkets.

3. Shiitake Mushroom Cultivation

Shiitake grows on hardwood logs or sawdust blocks. In Africa, use oak, eucalyptus, or mango logs cut 1 metre long, 10-15 cm diameter. Drill holes 2 cm deep every 15 cm. Insert shiitake plug spawn and seal with wax. Stack logs in a shady, moist area. Water daily. After 6-12 months, logs are fully colonised (white mycelium). Soak logs in cold water for 24 hours to shock them into fruiting. Then keep at 15-20Â°C and 85% humidity. Mushrooms appear in 7-10 days.

For sawdust blocks: mix 80% hardwood sawdust, 18% wheat bran, 2% gypsum. Add water to 60% moisture. Fill into polypropylene bags, sterilise at 121Â°C for 2 hours. Inoculate with 200 g shiitake spawn per 2 kg block. Incubate in dark at 25Â°C for 60 days. Remove bags and soak in water for 12 hours. Fruit at 18Â°C and 90% humidity with 12 hours light. Harvest when caps are 5-10 cm and edges still curled. Each block yields 300-500 g over 3 flushes. Logs yield for 3-5 years. Dry shiitake for 24 hours in sun or 8 hours at 50Â°C. Sell as medicinal or gourmet.

4. Milky Mushroom Cultivation

Milky mushrooms love heat. They grow well in African tropics at 30-35Â°C. Use pasteurised straw or cotton waste. Soak 10 kg straw in lime water (1 kg lime in 100 L water) for 18 hours. Drain and steam at 80Â°C for 1 hour. Cool.

Mix 500 g milky mushroom spawn with straw. Pack into bags or trays. Incubate in dark at 30-35Â°C for 14 days. When white mycelium covers the substrate, case with 2 cm of sterilised soil or peat. Keep humidity at 85-90% and temperature at 30-35Â°C. Provide 12 hours of light. Mushrooms appear in 4-6 days. Harvest when caps are 8-12 cm wide, before they flatten completely. Twist and pull.

Each 10 kg bag yields 1-1.5 kg over 3 flushes. Milky mushrooms are less common but fetch high prices in local markets. They are easy because they tolerate high heat. Use a simple shade house with misting. Avoid direct sunlight. Sell fresh or dry. In West Africa, milky mushrooms are called 'olu' or 'okro' mushroom. Start with 20 bags to test your market.

5. Reishi and Medicinal Mushrooms

Reishi (Ganoderma lucidum) is grown for medicine, not food. It needs hardwood sawdust and a longer cycle. Mix 78% hardwood sawdust, 20% wheat bran, 2% gypsum. Add water to 60%. Fill into polypropylene bags (1 kg each). Sterilise at 121Â°C for 2 hours. Inoculate with 100 g reishi spawn per bag. Incubate in dark at 25-28Â°C for 30-45 days. When mycelium is white, cut bags and move to fruiting room at 25-30Â°C, 85-90% humidity, and 12 hours light. Reishi forms a conk (hard mushroom) in 30-60 days. Harvest when the edge turns brown and stops growing. Dry at 50Â°C for 12 hours. Grind into powder. Use 5 g per day for tea or capsules. Sell to herbalists or health shops.

Other medicinal mushrooms: Turkey tail (Trametes versicolor) grows on logs. Lion's mane (Hericium erinaceus) grows on sawdust blocks like shiitake. In Africa, grow these for local traditional medicine markets. Always dry mushrooms completely to avoid mould. Store in airtight jars. A 1 kg bag of reishi yields 100-150 g dry. Start with 10 bags. Label with dosage instructions. Consult local regulations for selling medicinal products.

6. Specialty and Exotic Mushrooms

Specialty mushrooms fetch premium prices. In Africa, try king oyster (Pleurotus eryngii), pink oyster (Pleurotus djamor), and shimeji (Hypsizygus tessellatus). King oyster grows on sawdust blocks like shiitake. Mix 80% sawdust, 18% bran, 2% gypsum. Sterilise, inoculate, incubate at 25Â°C for 30 days. Fruit at 15-18Â°C and 90% humidity. Harvest when stems are thick and caps small. Each 2 kg block yields 400-600 g. Pink oyster grows on straw like regular oyster but needs 25-30Â°C. It fruits in 10 days. Harvest when caps are pink and flat. Shimeji grows on sawdust at 15-20Â°C. Harvest in clusters.

Exotic mushrooms need precise conditions. Use a small air conditioner or evaporative cooler. Test with 5 blocks before scaling. Sell to restaurants, hotels, and farmers' markets. Package in paper bags or cling film. For drying, slice 5 mm thick and dry at 45Â°C for 8 hours. Store in airtight containers. In Africa, specialty mushrooms can sell for 3-5 times the price of oyster mushrooms. Start with one exotic type, master it, then add another. Keep records of temperature, humidity, and yield. Join a mushroom growers' association for support.

Mushroom cultivation involves high-temperature sterilisation and, in some protocols, chemical disinfectants. In African field conditions, the following risks are real and have caused serious injuries:

  • Steam and boiling water burns: Never open a sterilisation drum or autoclave while pressurised or before it has cooled below 50Â°C. Use heat-resistant gloves and a face shield.
  • Formalin and bleach fumes: Formaldehyde is a known carcinogen. If used for room disinfection, wear a respirator, ensure forced ventilation, and vacate the area for at least 4 hours. Never mix bleach with ammonia-based cleaners.
  • Lime (calcium oxide) burns: Agricultural lime used for substrate soaking causes severe skin and eye burns. Wear goggles, gloves, and a dust mask when handling dry lime.
  • Carbon dioxide in sealed rooms: High COâ during colonisation can cause dizziness and asphyxiation. Never enter a sealed growing room without ventilating it first.
  • Spore inhalation: Reishi and Shiitake spores can cause allergic alveolitis (mushroom worker's lung). Harvest before full spore release and wear an N95 mask in enclosed growing rooms.

First aid: For chemical burns, flush with clean water for 15 minutes and seek medical attention. For steam burns, cool with water and cover with sterile dressing.

3. Quick Reference Checklist

Use this checklist before every production cycle. It is designed for a 50â200 bag operation, the most common commercial scale for African small and medium enterprises.

  1. Substrate moisture test: Squeeze a handful of substrate. A few drops should appear between fingers â no stream, no dry crumble. Target 65â70% moisture.
  2. Pasteurisation temperature log: Record temperature every 15 minutes. Hold at 80â90Â°C for a minimum of 2 hours. Do not guess.
  3. Cool-down check: Substrate must be below 30Â°C before spawning. Spawning into warm substrate kills mycelium and invites bacteria.
  4. Spawn rate: Use 5â10% spawn by wet weight. Higher rates (10%) speed colonisation and reduce contamination risk â worth the extra cost for beginners.
  5. Hygiene protocol: Wash hands, sanitise tools with 70% alcohol, and wear clean clothing. No eating, smoking, or walking between contaminated and clean areas without changing.
  6. Colonisation monitoring: Check bags every 3 days. Remove any bag showing green, black, or pink contamination immediately â do not open it inside the growing room.
  7. Fruiting trigger: Introduce fresh air, light (500â1000 lux), and lower temperature only after full colonisation (white mycelium throughout). Premature fruiting reduces yield.
  8. Harvest timing: Harvest oyster mushrooms before caps flatten and release spores. For button mushrooms, harvest when veils are intact. Late harvest reduces shelf life and market price.
