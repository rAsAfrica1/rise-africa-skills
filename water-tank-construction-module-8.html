1. Sizing and Selecting Inlet, Outlet and Overflow Pipes for African Water Tanks

Every water tank fails or succeeds at its connections. In Kenya, Nigeria, Ghana and South Africa, a 10,000 litre tank that cost KES 85,000 to build can be rendered useless by a KES 800 inlet pipe that is too small, an outlet that sucks air, or an overflow that dumps water onto the foundation. This lesson teaches you how to size, select and price the three critical pipes before you cut a single hole. Start with demand. A typical rural household in Kisumu uses 80 to 120 litres per person per day. For a family of six, that is 600 litres daily. If you fill the tank from a borehole pump running 2 hours per day, your inlet must deliver 300 litres per hour, or 5 litres per minute. A 20 mm (3/4 inch) PVC pipe at gravity head of 2 metres delivers about 15 litres per minute, so it is more than enough. But if you are filling from a 5,000 litre tanker truck that empties in 15 minutes, you need 333 litres per minute. That requires a 50 mm (2 inch) pipe minimum. Use this rule: flow in litres per minute equals 0.003 times diameter in mm squared times the square root of head in metres. For a 25 mm pipe under 3 m head, that is 0.003 x 625 x 1.73, about 3.2 litres per minute. That is too slow for tanker filling. Always size for the fastest fill event, not the average day. Outlet sizing depends on peak draw-off. A single tap with a 15 mm opening at 2 m head flows about 12 litres per minute. If three taps run at once, you need a 25 mm outlet manifold. In many African homes, the outlet feeds a gravity-fed shower and a kitchen tap. A 20 mm outlet pipe is the minimum; 25 mm is better because it reduces friction loss. Friction loss matters. In a 30 m run of 20 mm PVC at 10 litres per minute, you lose about 0.8 m of head. That is the difference between a shower that works and one that dribbles. Overflow sizing is about safety, not performance. The overflow must handle the maximum inflow if the inlet valve fails. If your inlet can deliver 300 litres per minute, your overflow must handle 300 litres per minute. A 50 mm overflow pipe at 0.5 m head handles about 180 litres per minute. So you need two 50 mm overflows, or one 75 mm. Never undersize the overflow. In Lagos, a 5,000 litre plastic tank with a 25 mm overflow and a 50 mm inlet burst its base when the float valve stuck. The overflow could not keep up. The tank cost NGN 180,000; the flood damage cost NGN 450,000. Materials and costs. In Kenya, 20 mm PVC pipe costs KES 120 per metre, 25 mm KES 180, 50 mm KES 450, 75 mm KES 750. In Nigeria, 20 mm PVC is NGN 350 per metre, 50 mm NGN 1,200, 75 mm NGN 2,000. In Ghana, 25 mm PVC is GHS 12 per metre, 50 mm GHS 28. In South Africa, 50 mm uPVC is ZAR 65 per metre, 75 mm ZAR 110. Fittings add 30 to 50 percent. A complete inlet, outlet and overflow kit for a 5,000 litre tank costs KES 4,500 in Nairobi, NGN 12,000 in Lagos, GHS 350 in Accra, ZAR 900 in Johannesburg. That is cheap insurance. For ferrocement and concrete tanks, you can cast pipes into the wall. Use PVC sleeves or cast iron pipes. For plastic tanks, use bulkhead fittings with rubber gaskets. For brick tanks, embed pipes in mortar with a 20 mm clearance and seal with waterproof grout. Always install a strainer on the outlet inside the tank to stop debris. A 50 mm strainer costs KES 300. A float valve on the inlet costs KES 1,200 in Kenya, NGN 3,500 in Nigeria. Safety note: never drill a hole in a plastic tank below the water line without a proper bulkhead fitting. A rubber gasket and a backing nut are essential. A naked hole will crack within weeks. Also, install a mosquito screen on the overflow. In Ghana, open overflows breed mosquitoes and cause malaria. A stainless steel mesh screen costs GHS 15. Finally, label every pipe. Inlet is blue or green, outlet is white or black, overflow is red. This prevents cross-connection during repairs. A plumber who charges KES 3,000 per day will take 2 days to install these pipes. If you do it yourself, you save that labour but you must buy a hole saw, a pipe wrench and Teflon tape. Total tool cost: KES 2,500. The lesson: spend 5 percent of your tank budget on plumbing. It protects 100 percent of your investment.

2. Installing Inlet Plumbing: Float Valves, Cisterns and Gravity Lines

The inlet is the most abused part of any water tank. In Africa, we often fill tanks from boreholes, wells, rivers, municipal lines and tanker trucks. Each source needs a different inlet strategy. This lesson gives you practical installations for ferrocement, concrete, plastic and brick tanks, with real costs in KES, NGN, ZAR and GHS. Start with gravity inlets. If your source is a spring or a roof catchment, the inlet pipe comes from above. You need a first-flush diverter and a leaf screen. A 100 mm PVC first-flush diverter costs KES 1,800 in Nairobi, NGN 4,500 in Lagos, GHS 180 in Accra, ZAR 350 in Johannesburg. Install it before the tank. It dumps the first 20 litres of dirty roof water. Then the clean water enters the tank through a 50 mm inlet pipe. The inlet should enter at the top of the tank, not the side. This reduces backpressure and allows air to escape. For a plastic tank, cut a hole in the lid or use the pre-moulded inlet. For a ferrocement tank, cast a 50 mm PVC pipe into the wall at the top, angled down 10 degrees. For a concrete tank, embed the pipe in the formwork. For a brick tank, leave a gap in the brickwork and mortar the pipe in place. Always seal with a flexible waterproof sealant, not just cement. Cement cracks. Sealant costs KES 600 per cartridge. Now pressurised inlets. If you fill from a municipal line or a pump, you must use a float valve. A float valve stops the water when the tank is full. The most common is the 20 mm brass float valve, costing KES 1,200 in Kenya, NGN 3,500 in Nigeria, GHS 120 in Ghana, ZAR 280 in South Africa. Install it inside the tank on the inlet pipe. The float must move freely. In a plastic tank, mount the float valve on a bracket. In a concrete tank, bolt it to the wall with stainless steel anchors. Never use mild steel; it rusts. For high-pressure lines above 4 bar, use a pressure reducer before the float valve. A 20 mm pressure reducer costs KES 2,500. Without it, the float valve will hammer and fail within months. In Lagos, a 10,000 litre plastic tank on a 6 bar municipal line blew its float valve in 3 weeks. The replacement cost NGN 8,000 plus water damage. A pressure reducer would have cost NGN 6,000. For tanker filling, use a 50 mm or 75 mm camlock fitting. A 50 mm camlock costs KES 1,500, NGN 4,000, GHS 150, ZAR 320. Install it on a short stub pipe at the top of the tank. The tanker hose connects and fills fast. But you must have an overflow that can handle the flow. Also, install a shut-off valve before the camlock. A 50 mm gate valve costs KES 2,200. For borehole filling, the inlet pipe comes from the pump. Use a 32 mm or 40 mm HDPE pipe. HDPE is flexible and cheap. A 32 mm HDPE pipe costs KES 90 per metre in Kenya, NGN 250 in Nigeria, GHS 10 in Ghana, ZAR 45 in South Africa. Bury it 30 cm deep to protect from sun and animals. Connect to the tank with a bulkhead fitting. A 32 mm bulkhead fitting costs KES 800. Always install a non-return valve on the inlet if the pump is below the tank. This stops water from flowing back and draining the tank. A 32 mm non-return valve costs KES 1,500. For ferrocement tanks, you can build a small inlet chamber or cistern. This is a 200 litre concrete box that receives the inlet water and then overflows into the main tank. It acts as a sediment trap. The cistern costs KES 8,000 to build with concrete and mesh. It is common in Ghana and Nigeria. The inlet pipe enters the cistern, and a 50 mm pipe connects the cistern to the main tank. Clean the cistern every 3 months. Safety notes: never connect a municipal line directly to a tank without a float valve and an air gap. An air gap prevents backflow contamination. In South Africa, this is required by SANS 10252. Also, install a vacuum breaker on the inlet if the pipe can siphon. A 20 mm vacuum breaker costs ZAR 180. For plastic tanks, do not over-tighten bulkhead fittings. Hand tight plus a quarter turn is enough. Over-tightening cracks the plastic. Use silicone grease on the gasket. A tube costs KES 400. Finally, insulate exposed inlet pipes in hot areas. In northern Kenya, UV destroys PVC in 2 years. Paint the pipe with UV-resistant paint or wrap it with foil tape. A roll of foil tape costs KES 500. Labour: a plumber charges KES 2,500 to install an inlet with float valve. In Nigeria, NGN 7,000. In Ghana, GHS 250. In South Africa, ZAR 600. This is a 3-hour job. If you do it yourself, watch a video and buy the right fittings. The cost of failure is a flooded foundation and a cracked tank. The cost of doing it right is a few thousand shillings.

3. Outlet Plumbing: Taps, Manifolds and Gravity Feed Systems

The outlet is where your tank earns its keep. If the outlet is too small, too high or clogged, your family suffers. This lesson covers outlet design, installation and maintenance for all tank types, with real African prices and practical tips. Start with outlet height. The outlet should be 100 mm above the tank floor. This leaves a dead zone for sediment. But you must be able to drain the tank completely for cleaning. So install two outlets: a high outlet for daily use and a low drain outlet at the very bottom. The high outlet is a 25 mm or 32 mm bulkhead fitting. The low drain is a 50 mm gate valve. In Kenya, a 25 mm bulkhead costs KES 600, a 50 mm gate valve KES 2,200. In Nigeria, NGN 1,800 and NGN 6,500. In Ghana, GHS 60 and GHS 220. In South Africa, ZAR 150 and ZAR 450. For ferrocement and concrete tanks, cast the high outlet pipe into the wall at the desired height. Use a 32 mm PVC pipe with a 90-degree bend inside the tank. The bend should point down, and you attach a strainer. A 32 mm strainer costs KES 400. For brick tanks, leave a hole in the brickwork and mortar the pipe in. For plastic tanks, use the pre-moulded outlet or drill a hole and install a bulkhead fitting. Always use a rubber gasket on both sides of the tank wall. Now the manifold. Most homes need more than one tap. A manifold splits the outlet into multiple lines. Use a 32 mm PVC pipe as the main line, then reduce to 20 mm for each tap. A 32x20x20 tee costs KES 250. A 20 mm ball valve costs KES 450. Install a ball valve on each line so you can isolate repairs. In a typical Kenyan home, you need one line to the kitchen, one to the bathroom, one to the toilet and one to the garden. That is four ball valves, four tees, and 20 metres of 20 mm pipe. Total cost: KES 6,500. In Nigeria, NGN 18,000. In Ghana, GHS 550. In South Africa, ZAR 1,400. For gravity feed, height is everything. Every 1 metre of height gives 0.1 bar of pressure. A shower needs 0.2 bar to work well. So your tank must be at least 2 metres above the shower head. If your tank is on the ground, you need a pump. A 0.5 HP booster pump costs KES 18,000 in Kenya, NGN 45,000 in Nigeria, GHS 1,800 in Ghana, ZAR 3,500 in South Africa. Install the pump after the outlet, with a pressure tank. A 24 litre pressure tank costs KES 12,000. This gives you consistent pressure. For gravity systems, use a 32 mm outlet pipe, not 20 mm. The larger pipe reduces friction loss. A 32 mm pipe at 20 litres per minute loses 0.3 m of head over 30 metres. A 20 mm pipe loses 2.5 m. That is a huge difference. In Ethiopia and Tanzania, many rural homes use 32 mm outlet pipes for this reason. Strainers are critical. A strainer stops leaves, sand and insects from clogging your taps. Install a Y-strainer on the main outlet line. A 32 mm Y-strainer costs KES 1,200, NGN 3,500, GHS 120, ZAR 280. Clean it every month. If you have a concrete tank, install a sediment trap before the outlet. This is a small concrete box inside the tank that lets sand settle. It costs KES 5,000 to build. In Ghana, this is common in rural communities. Safety notes: never use lead pipes or lead solder. Lead is toxic. Use PVC, HDPE or copper. In South Africa, SANS 10252 prohibits lead. Also, support your outlet pipes. A full 32 mm pipe weighs 1 kg per metre. If unsupported, it will sag and crack the tank wall. Use pipe clips every 1 metre. A bag of 20 clips costs KES 300. For plastic tanks, do not use metal fittings directly on the plastic. Use plastic bulkhead fittings with rubber gaskets. Metal expands and cracks plastic. If you must use metal, use brass, not steel. Brass costs more but lasts. A 25 mm brass bulkhead costs KES 1,500. Maintenance: every 6 months, open the low drain and flush the tank. This removes sediment. Every year, replace the rubber gaskets on the outlet. A pack of 10 gaskets costs KES 500. If you see water dripping from the outlet, tighten the bulkhead nut by a quarter turn. If it still leaks, replace the gasket. Do not use cement to seal a leak. Cement will crack and fail. Use silicone sealant or plumber's tape. Labour: a plumber charges KES 3,000 to install an outlet manifold with 4 taps. In Nigeria, NGN 8,000. In Ghana, GHS 300. In South Africa, ZAR 700. This is a 4-hour job. If you do it yourself, buy a pipe cutter, a deburring tool and a wrench. Total tool cost: KES 3,500. The lesson: your outlet is your interface with the user. Make it big, make it clean, and make it easy to repair.

4. Overflow and Venting: Protecting Your Tank from Disaster

The overflow is the most ignored pipe in African water tank construction. It is also the most important for safety. A tank without a proper overflow is a bomb waiting to burst. This lesson covers overflow sizing, installation, venting and emergency drainage for ferrocement, concrete, plastic and brick tanks. Start with the physics. Water expands when it freezes, but in Africa we do not worry about freezing. We worry about overfilling. If the inlet valve fails, water keeps coming. The tank fills to the brim. Then the weight of the water pushes against the walls. A 10,000 litre tank holds 10 tonnes of water. That is 10,000 kg. If the overflow cannot handle the inflow, the tank will crack or burst. In Lagos, a 5,000 litre plastic tank burst because the overflow was blocked by a bird nest. The water destroyed a wall and flooded a shop. The loss was NGN 800,000. The overflow screen cost NGN 2,000. Sizing the overflow: the overflow must handle the maximum inflow. If your inlet is a 50 mm pipe at 3 bar, it delivers about 200 litres per minute. A 50 mm overflow at 0.5 m head handles 180 litres per minute. So you need a 75 mm overflow, or two 50 mm overflows. A 75 mm PVC pipe costs KES 750 per metre in Kenya, NGN 2,000 in Nigeria, GHS 280 in Ghana, ZAR 110 in South Africa. A 75 mm elbow costs KES 350. Install the overflow at the top of the tank, at least 100 mm below the top rim. This gives you a safety margin. For plastic tanks, use the pre-moulded overflow or drill a hole and install a bulkhead fitting. For concrete and ferrocement tanks, cast a 75 mm PVC pipe into the wall. For brick tanks, leave a gap and mortar the pipe in. Always slope the overflow pipe downward, away from the tank. A slope of 1 in 50 is enough. The overflow must discharge to a safe place. Do not discharge onto the foundation. Discharge to a drain, a garden, or a soakaway. In Kenya, a soakaway pit costs KES 15,000 to dig and fill with stones. In Nigeria, NGN 40,000. In Ghana, GHS 1,200. In South Africa, ZAR 2,500. If you discharge onto the ground, you will erode the soil and undermine the tank. Venting: every tank needs a vent. The vent lets air in when you draw water and lets air out when you fill. Without a vent, the tank will glug and the outlet will surge. A 50 mm vent pipe with a mosquito screen is enough. Install it on the top of the tank, opposite the inlet. A 50 mm vent cowl costs KES 800. A stainless steel screen costs KES 300. In Ghana, a vent is called an air pipe. In South Africa, it is an air vent. Do not confuse the vent with the overflow. The vent is small; the overflow is large. The vent can be 50 mm; the overflow must be 75 mm or larger. Emergency drainage: if the overflow fails, you need a backup. Install a low-level drain valve at the bottom of the tank. A 50 mm gate valve costs KES 2,200. If the tank is overfilling, open the drain. This is a manual backup. Also, install a float switch on the pump. A float switch costs KES 1,500. It turns off the pump when the tank is full. This prevents overfilling at the source. In Nigeria, a float switch costs NGN 4,000. In Ghana, GHS 150. In South Africa, ZAR 350. For municipal lines, use a float valve. But float valves fail. So install a second float valve in series. Two float valves cost KES 2,400. That is cheaper than a flood. Safety notes: never block an overflow. Never reduce the overflow size. Never use a flexible hose as an overflow. Flexible hose collapses and kinks. Use rigid PVC. Also, install a screen on the overflow to stop insects and rodents. In Kenya, a mesh screen costs KES 200. In Nigeria, NGN 500. In Ghana, GHS 20. In South Africa, ZAR 50. Maintenance: every 3 months, check the overflow for blockages. Pour a bucket of water into the overflow to test it. If it drains slowly, clean it. Every year, replace the mosquito screen. A roll of screen costs KES 500. Labour: a plumber charges KES 2,000 to install an overflow and vent. In Nigeria, NGN 5,000. In Ghana, GHS 200. In South Africa, ZAR 450. This is a 2-hour job. If you do it yourself, buy a hole saw, a PVC cutter and a ladder. Total tool cost: KES 4,000. The lesson: the overflow is your insurance policy. Spend 10 percent of your plumbing budget on it. It will save you 100 percent of your tank.

5. Testing, Maintenance and Repair of Tank Plumbing Systems

You have installed the inlet, outlet and overflow. Now you must test them. A plumbing system that is not tested will fail when you least expect it. This lesson gives you a step-by-step testing and maintenance routine for African water tanks, with real costs and safety notes. Start with the fill test. Close all outlets and the low drain. Open the inlet. Fill the tank to the overflow level. Watch for leaks at every connection. A leak shows as a wet spot or a drip. If you see a leak, mark it with chalk. Do not try to fix it while the tank is full. Drain the tank to below the leak, then repair. For bulkhead fittings, tighten the nut by a quarter turn. If it still leaks, replace the gasket. A pack of 10 gaskets costs KES 500. For cast-in pipes, apply waterproof sealant around the pipe where it meets the wall. A cartridge of sealant costs KES 600. For brick tanks, repoint the mortar with a waterproof mix. A bag of waterproof cement costs KES 1,200. Next, test the overflow. With the tank full, open the inlet fully. The overflow should carry the water away without backing up. If water rises above the overflow, your overflow is too small. You need a bigger pipe or a second overflow. In Lagos, a 10,000 litre tank had a 50 mm overflow and a 50 mm inlet. The overflow could not keep up. The fix was to add a second 50 mm overflow. The cost was NGN 8,000. The flood damage would have been NGN 300,000. Test the vent. Close the outlet and open the inlet. You should hear air escaping from the vent. If you hear gurgling, the vent is blocked. Clean it with a wire brush. A wire brush costs KES 200. Test the outlet. Open each tap one by one. Check the flow. A 20 mm tap should fill a 10 litre bucket in 50 seconds. If it takes longer, your outlet pipe is too small or clogged. Clean the strainer. If the strainer is clean, check for a kink in the pipe. In Kenya, a kinked HDPE pipe reduced flow by 70 percent. The fix was to replace 5 metres of pipe. The cost was KES 450. Now maintenance. Every month, clean the strainer on the outlet. Every 3 months, clean the overflow screen. Every 6 months, flush the tank through the low drain. Every year, replace all rubber gaskets. Every 2 years, repaint exposed metal fittings. A can of rust paint costs KES 800. In Nigeria, NGN 2,500. In Ghana, GHS 80. In South Africa, ZAR 150. For concrete and ferrocement tanks, inspect the inside of the tank every year. Look for cracks. A crack wider than 1 mm needs repair. Use a waterproof grout. A 5 kg bag costs KES 1,500. In Ghana, GHS 120. In South Africa, ZAR 250. For plastic tanks, check for UV damage. UV makes plastic brittle. If you see chalky white patches, the tank is degrading. Paint it with UV-resistant paint. A 5 litre can costs KES 3,500. In Nigeria, NGN 9,000. In Ghana, GHS 250. In South Africa, ZAR 600. Safety notes: never enter a tank without a second person watching. Tanks are confined spaces. You can suffocate. In Kenya, a man died inside a 10,000 litre tank because he entered alone to clean it. Always have a rope and a harness. A harness costs KES 4,500. Also, never use a generator inside a tank. The fumes will kill you. Use a battery-powered light. A rechargeable LED light costs KES 1,500. Repairing a leak in a plastic tank: drain the tank below the leak. Clean the area with sandpaper. Apply a plastic repair patch. A patch kit costs KES 1,200. In Nigeria, NGN 3,000. In Ghana, GHS 100. In South Africa, ZAR 220. Do not use cement on plastic. It will not stick. Repairing a leak in a concrete tank: drain the tank. Chisel out the crack into a V shape. Fill with waterproof mortar. Cure for 3 days. Then apply a waterproof coating. A 5 kg bag of coating costs KES 2,500. In Nigeria, NGN 6,000. In Ghana, GHS 200. In South Africa, ZAR 450. Repairing a leak in a ferrocement tank: the same as concrete, but you must also check the wire mesh. If the mesh is rusted, cut it out and replace it. A roll of chicken wire costs KES 1,800. In Nigeria, NGN 4,500. In Ghana, GHS 150. In South Africa, ZAR 300. Labour: a plumber charges KES 3,000 per day for repairs. In Nigeria, NGN 8,000. In Ghana, GHS 300. In South Africa, ZAR 700. If you do it yourself, buy a pipe wrench, a hacksaw and a bucket. Total tool cost: KES 2,500. The lesson: test everything before you use it. Maintain everything before it breaks. A well-maintained plumbing system lasts 20 years. A neglected one lasts 2 years. The choice is yours.

SAFETY WARNING: Overflow and vent pipes must never be capped, valved or blocked. A blocked overflow can pressurise a tank and cause catastrophic rupture, and a blocked vent can cause a tank to implode during rapid emptying or to collapse under vacuum. Always route overflow to a safe soakpit or drain at least 3 m from the tank foundation. In areas with mosquito-borne disease (e.g. malaria-endemic zones), fit a 1 mm mesh screen on the overflow and vent outlets to prevent mosquito breeding and vector entry. Never enter a tank without confined-space precautions: hydrogen sulphide and oxygen deficiency kill silently.

Quick Reference Checklist

  • 1. Confirm tank volume and daily demand before choosing pipe sizes; use Table 1.
  • 2. Inlet float valve set at least 50 mm below overflow invert; overflow invert at least 50 mm below tank top.
  • 3. Outlet positioned 50-100 mm above tank floor to avoid drawing sediment; fit a washout at floor level.
  • 4. Overflow diameter at least one size larger than inlet; never downsize.
  • 5. Fit 1 mm mesh screens on overflow and vent; inspect and clean every 3 months.
  • 6. Support horizontal runs every 1 m and vertical runs every 2 m; use UV-stabilised pipe outdoors.
  • 7. Pressure-test new plumbing at 1.5 times working pressure for 1 hour before backfilling.
  • 8. Label all pipes (inlet, outlet, overflow, vent, washout) with UV-resistant paint or tags.
