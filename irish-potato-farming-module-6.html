The dominant cost is seed, at 40–50% of total. Then fungicides. Then labour.

Revenue

Net margin

$2,000–8,000 per hectare. Potato has higher net margin per hectare than any cereal, at higher capital risk.

Lesson 2

Reading the Potato Price Calendar

In this lesson
  • Map the seasonal price cycle
  • Use storage to hit high-price months
  • Time plantings to sell into peaks

Typical pattern in African markets

  • Main harvest (June–August in many regions): lowest prices, $0.20–0.35/kg
  • Post-harvest storage (Sept–Nov): prices rise, $0.40–0.60/kg
  • Late dry season (Dec–Feb): peak prices, $0.60–1.00/kg
  • Planting season (March–May): supply low, prices high until new harvest

Using storage to capture the price

Diffuse-light storage lets you hold 3–5 months, cold storage 6–10 months. If you harvest at $0.25/kg and sell in January at $0.70/kg, storage nearly triples your revenue on the stored portion. Cold storage pays for itself in one season if you have 20+ tonnes.

Off-season production

With irrigation and cool-season planting, you can produce potato for the late dry-season market when prices are highest. Requires cool nights (altitude) or heavy irrigation to manage soil temperature.

Lesson 3

The Seed Potato Business

In this lesson
  • Understand seed potato certification
  • Decide whether to grow seed instead of table potato
  • Calculate the margin difference

Seed potato production

Grow certified seed to sell to other farmers. Seed sells at 2–3x food price. But requirements are strict:

  • Isolation from other potato fields (200 m minimum, more for high virus pressure)
  • Only certified pre-basic or basic seed as starting material
  • Strict virus and disease control — inspection by the national certification body
  • Roguing of off-types and infected plants
  • Harvest, grade, store separately
  • Formal certification (fee and inspection)

The economics

  • Table potato: 25 t/ha at $0.35/kg = $8,750
  • Seed potato (certified): 20 t/ha at $0.80/kg = $16,000

Seed costs more to produce — more inputs, more inspections, more labour — but net margin is 1.5–2x table potato.

Starting small

You can produce seed-quality potato for your own use without formal certification. Save the best tubers from a clean field, store them properly, and use as seed next season. This saves $800–1,600/ha on certified seed.

Lesson 4

The Four Buyer Types

In this lesson
  • Name the four buyer types
  • Match grade to buyer
  • Build buyer loyalty

1. Local market vendors

Buy daily, cash. $0.25–0.40/kg. Volume buyer for grade 2.

2. Urban retailers and supermarkets

Buy weekly on contract, $0.40–0.70/kg, 30-day terms. Require uniform grade 1, packaging. Register your business if you want to supply.

3. Chip and crisp processors

Buy in 5–50 tonne lots on contract. $0.40–0.80/kg. Require specific varieties and aflatoxin-free, low-sugar tubers.

4. Seed potato distributors

Buy certified seed in bulk. $0.60–1.00/kg. Require certification, isolation, and virus-free status.

5. Institutional buyers

Schools, hospitals, army — buy on tender, 30–60 day terms. Require business registration and often collateral or references.

The rule: sell to at least two buyer types. Grade 1 to supermarket, grade 2 to local market, small tubers to processing. Every grade becomes cash.
Lesson 5

The One-Page Business Plan

In this lesson
  • Complete the one-page plan
  • Set targets for year 1 and year 2
  • Decide scale triggers

Before next season, fill in this one page:

  1. Business type: table potato, processing, or seed?
  2. Capital tier: how much will you invest?
  3. Land: hectares and site (cool, well-drained, no potato history)
  4. Variety: which variety and why
  5. Target yield: realistic kg/ha (start with 15 t/ha target)
  6. Buyers: three named contacts
  7. Revenue target: price × yield
  8. Total cost: every line
  9. Net margin: revenue − cost
  10. Value step: what single step to add in 12 months (storage, seed business, processing contract)
  11. Scaling trigger: what condition before expanding

Read it at harvest. Read it again next season. Compare against reality.

Knowledge check — Module 6

1. What is the biggest cost line in potato production?

Correct: seed. 40–50% of total cost.

2. When are potato prices highest?

Correct: late dry season. Storage captures the premium.

3. How much does certified seed sell for?

Correct: 2–3x. Higher margin but strict certification requirements.

4. What must a seed potato grower have?

Correct: isolation, clean seed, roguing, certification. Strict but the highest margin.

5. What is the target year-1 yield for a beginner?

Correct: 15 t/ha. Conservative starting point, then refine.
