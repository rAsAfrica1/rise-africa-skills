Pig Farming - The Complete Course

Twelve modules. Seventy-two written lessons. Twelve knowledge checks. A farrow-to-market capstone. Real gestation, farrowing and mortality numbers, sourced and dated, with ranges where the science gives ranges.

How this course works

Every module has six written lessons, a five-question knowledge check, and one written capstone piece. Work through them in order: modules 1 to 7 are the breeding business, modules 8 to 12 are the growing-to-market business, and skipping either half leaves real gaps.

These are written lessons, not video lessons. Approximately 43,794 words of teaching text, plus checklists, tables and quizzes. Lifetime access.

The numbers this course is built on

  • Gestation is fixed by pig biology at 114 days (three months, three weeks, three days), regardless of breed or country.
  • Pre-weaning piglet mortality typically runs 10 to 20 percent, and can reach 25 percent in poorly managed systems. Crushing by the sow is the single biggest cause, responsible for roughly half of liveborn deaths.
  • A sow returns to fertile heat 4 to 7 days after weaning if she is in good body condition. This wean-to-service interval decides how many litters she gives you per year.
  • Smallholder litters in East and Southern Africa commonly run 10 to 14 piglets born. Well-managed commercial herds elsewhere reach higher.
  • Feed is typically 60 to 70 percent of the cost of producing a pig.
  • African Swine Fever has no vaccine and no cure. It can wipe out a herd in days. Biosecurity is not optional reading, it is survival.

Sources: peer-reviewed swine research, the Merck Veterinary Manual, and smallholder pig surveys from Uganda, Nigeria and southern Africa. Figures are ranges. Your herd, breed and management will move them. Verify against your own vet and local extension officer.

Iron-clad price check. Before you buy a boar, a sow, feed, or build housing, call three suppliers and compare. This course teaches the method and the real biology. You confirm the local prices and market demand.
