✅ Iron-Clad Price Check

Before you buy a boar, a sow, feed, or build housing, call 3 suppliers and compare. Prices and breed availability vary widely between regions and seasons. This course teaches you the method and the real biology — you confirm the local prices and market demand.

The numbers this course is built on, sourced and dated:
  • Gestation is fixed by pig biology at 114 days — three months, three weeks, three days — regardless of breed or country.
  • Pre-weaning piglet mortality typically runs 10–20%, and can reach 25% in poorly managed systems. Crushing by the sow is the single biggest cause, responsible for roughly 55% of liveborn deaths.
  • A sow returns to fertile heat 4–7 days after weaning if she is in good body condition — this "wean-to-service interval" is the number that decides how many litters she gives you per year.
  • Smallholder litters in East and Southern Africa commonly run 10–14 piglets born; well-managed commercial herds elsewhere reach considerably higher.
  • African Swine Fever has no vaccine and no cure. It can wipe out a herd in days. Biosecurity is not optional reading — it is survival.
Sources: peer-reviewed swine research (Muns et al. 2016; PMC studies on farrowing duration and crushing mortality), Merck Veterinary Manual, Uganda/Nigeria smallholder pig surveys. Figures are ranges — your herd, breed and management will move them. Verify against your own vet and local extension officer.

MODULE 6  Farrowing: The Birth Itself6 lessons

What you will be able to do:

  • Know when a farrowing is normal and when you must intervene — and the limits of what to attempt yourself before calling a vet
  • Reduce crushing, the single largest cause of piglet death, through pen design and simply being present during farrowing
  • Handle a stuck or difficult birth safely, understanding real risk to both sow and piglets

THE 6 LESSONS IN THIS MODULE

  5. When to intervene, and when to call a vet instead

MODULE 7  The First Three Weeks: Piglet Care6 lessons

What you will be able to do:

  • Get every piglet onto colostrum within the first hours — the single most protective thing you can do for newborn survival

  • Clip needle teeth and manage tail docking or castration where your context calls for it, done correctly and with real welfare awareness
  • Cross-foster piglets between litters when one sow has too many and another too few, done within the correct 24-hour window
  • Spot the early signs of scours (diarrhoea) and chilling, the two biggest killers after crushing

THE 6 LESSONS IN THIS MODULE

  • Manage the stress of weaning: separation from the sow and mixing with unfamiliar litters both cause real setbacks if rushed
  • Feed a nursery diet correctly through the vulnerable post-weaning weeks
  • Track average daily gain from day one, the number that tells you if something is wrong before it becomes a crisis
