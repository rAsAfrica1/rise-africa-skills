Concrete Brick

8 lessons · 30 quiz questions · Easy difficulty

1 Brick vs block

Bricks and blocks are both masonry units, but they differ in size and use.

FeatureBrickBlock
Size215 × 102 × 65 mm440 × 215 × 100 mm
Weight2.5-3 kg15-20 kg
Wall per m²60 bricks12.5 blocks
Laying speedSlower (5× more units)Faster
LookTraditional, fine scaleIndustrial, larger

Concrete bricks use the same basic materials as blocks — cement, sand, aggregate. But they're shaped to match the traditional brick size for design flexibility.

2 Concrete brick mix

Concrete brick mix by weight:

  • Cement: 1 part
  • Sharp sand (0–3 mm, finer than block sand): 3 parts
  • Small aggregate (3–5 mm): 2 parts
  • Water: earth-damp

Finer sand gives a smoother finish and better surface detail. Cement is about 15–17% of weight.

A 50 kg bag of cement makes 80–100 concrete bricks of 215 × 102 × 65 mm.

Target strength: 10–15 N/mm² at 28 days. Higher than standard blocks because bricks are used for facing and are visible.

3 Small-brick moulds

Brick moulds are small, and multi-cavity is the standard:

  • Single cavity: One brick per cycle. 4–6 seconds per brick.
  • 6-cavity: Common mould. 6 bricks per cycle.
  • 12-cavity: High-output mould on a larger vibrating table.

Mould materials: steel (best) or heavy-duty fibreglass. Cost: £200–£800 per 6-cavity mould.

Brick dimensions vary slightly by country:

  • UK: 215 × 102.5 × 65 mm
  • US: 203 × 92 × 57 mm (modular)
  • Africa: 220 × 105 × 70 mm (common)

Match your local market — call a hardware store and ask what size bricks they stock.

4 Fast batch production

Concrete bricks are small, so production is fast. Optimise the flow:

  1. Pre-mix a large batch in a pan mixer (200–400 kg per batch).
  2. Fill the multi-cavity mould on a vibrating table.
  3. Vibrate 4–6 seconds.
  4. Level top with a straight edge.
  5. Demould immediately onto a fresh pallet.
  6. Move pallet to curing area.
  7. Repeat every 30–60 seconds.

With a 6-cavity mould and 3 people, you can produce 500–1,000 bricks per hour of continuous work.

Realistic daily output: 2,500–4,000 bricks with a small crew and one machine.

5 Curing concrete bricks

Same as blocks: 7 days damp, 28 days full strength.

  • Cover pallets with plastic sheet for the first 3 days.
  • Spray 2× daily for 7 days.
  • Stack 8–10 high after 24 hours.
  • Full strength at 28 days.

Because they're small, bricks dry faster than blocks — but they still need 7 days of curing. Skipping this is the #1 cause of weak bricks.

Strength test: drop a brick from knee height onto concrete. A good brick chips at the corner. A bad one shatters.

6 Colour options

Concrete bricks can be coloured just like pavers.

Pigments:

  • Iron oxide red — the traditional brick colour
  • Iron oxide black — charcoal, modern look
  • Iron oxide yellow — sandstone, cream
  • Titanium dioxide — off-white
  • Chromium oxide — green

Dose: 3–5% of cement weight. Mix pigment dry with a small amount of cement first, then add to the batch.

Coloured brick retails at 2–3× plain grey brick. Cost increase: about £0.05–£0.10 per brick.

7 Retail packaging

Concrete bricks are sold in several formats:

  • Loose on pallets: 500 bricks per pallet, sold to builders.
  • Shrink-wrapped packs: 100 or 500 bricks per pack for hardware stores.
  • Small DIY packs: 10 or 20 bricks for small jobs.

Barcoded labelling with brick dimensions, quantity, and price helps hardware stores sell.

Retail price: $0.30–$0.60 per brick plain grey; $0.60–$1.20 per brick coloured or textured.

8 Selling for small jobs

Concrete brick sells to:

  • Homeowners doing small jobs: garden walls, planters, BBQ surrounds, repairs.
  • Hardware stores: stock small quantities for retail.
  • Repair contractors: matching bricks for old buildings, small wall repairs.
  • Feature walls: decorative brick used for interiors.

Advantages over fired clay brick:

  • Cheaper (no kiln needed)
  • Consistent size and colour
  • Can be made locally anywhere
  • Coloured to order

Pricing (Africa, 2026):

  • Plain concrete brick: $0.30–$0.50 each
  • Coloured/textured: $0.50–$1.00 each
  • Premium architectural: $1.00–$2.00 each
💰 Concrete brick is a small-margin, high-volume product. Best for filling order gaps and satisfying local demand near hardware stores.
