1. Setting Up a Mobile Money Merchant Account

To accept mobile money payments from customers, you need a merchant account. In most African countries, you can register with the dominant mobile money provider: M-Pesa in Kenya, Tanzania, and Mozambique; MTN Mobile Money in Uganda, Ghana, and Cameroon; Airtel Money in Zambia, Malawi, and Niger; Orange Money in Senegal, Mali, and CÃ´te d'Ivoire. Start by visiting an authorized agent or the provider's branch with your national ID, business registration certificate (if you have one), and a letter from your local brick makers' association. For a small brick business, you can register as a sole proprietor. The registration fee is usually between 5,000 and 20,000 shillings (or equivalent), and you may need to deposit an initial float of 50,000 to 100,000 shillings into your merchant wallet. The agent will help you set a merchant code (also called a till number or paybill). For example, M-Pesa uses a 5-digit till number for Buy Goods and Services. Once registered, you will receive a merchant SIM card and a simple phone or POS device. Train yourself and your staff on how to initiate a payment: the customer dials *182*1*1# (M-Pesa) or *165# (MTN), enters your merchant code, the amount, and their PIN. You will receive an SMS confirmation. Keep your merchant SIM in a safe place and never share your PIN. Also, link your merchant account to your bank account so you can transfer funds easily. Many providers allow automatic settlement to your bank at a small fee (e.g., 1% of the transfer). Within a week, you can start accepting digital payments, which reduces the risk of handling cash and attracts more customers.

2. Receiving Customer Payments via Mobile Money

Once your merchant account is active, receiving payments is straightforward. When a customer wants to pay for bricks, ask them to send money to your merchant code. For M-Pesa, they select 'Lipa na M-Pesa' then 'Buy Goods and Services', enter your till number, the amount (e.g., 15,000 KES for 500 bricks at 30 KES each), and their PIN. You will get an SMS like 'Confirmed. Ksh15,000.00 received from 2547XXXXXX on 12/5/23 at 2:30 PM. Transaction ID: QWE123.' Always verify the SMS before releasing bricks. For MTN, the customer dials *165*3# and follows prompts. If you have a smartphone, you can use the provider's merchant app (e.g., M-Pesa Business App) to see real-time transactions. For record-keeping, write down each transaction in a notebook: date, customer name, amount, and transaction ID. At the end of the day, reconcile the total mobile money received with your sales. If you have multiple employees, assign one person to handle payments and give them a unique PIN. You can also generate a QR code from your merchant app that customers can scan to pay. This is faster and reduces errors. For bulk orders, ask customers to pay a deposit (e.g., 50%) via mobile money and the balance on delivery. If a customer claims they paid but you didn't receive an SMS, check your app or dial *334# to query recent transactions. Never accept a screenshot as proof; only the SMS or app confirmation is valid. With practice, you can process a payment in under a minute.

3. Sending Payments to Suppliers and Employees

Mobile money is not just for receiving money; you can also pay suppliers and employees quickly and safely. For suppliers, such as clay or sand providers, you can send money directly from your merchant wallet or your personal wallet. For example, if you buy 10 tonnes of clay for 50,000 KES, you can send the amount to the supplier's mobile number. Use the 'Send Money' option: M-Pesa *334#, MTN *165*1#, Airtel *185#. You will need the supplier's phone number and name. Always confirm the number with the supplier before sending. Transaction fees for sending money are typically 1-2% of the amount (e.g., sending 50,000 KES costs about 500 KES). For employees, you can pay wages via mobile money. If you have 5 workers earning 800 KES per day, you can send each 4,000 KES at the end of the week. This reduces the risk of theft and saves time. Set up a schedule: every Friday at 5 PM, send payments. Keep a record of each transaction: date, recipient, amount, and purpose. For recurring payments, some providers offer bulk payment services (e.g., M-Pesa Business Bulk Payment) where you upload a CSV file with multiple recipients. This is useful if you have many employees. Also, you can use mobile money to pay for transport (e.g., lorry hire) and utilities. Always double-check the phone number before confirming. If you make a mistake, call the provider's customer care immediately; they may reverse the transaction if the recipient hasn't withdrawn the funds. By using mobile money for payments, you build a digital record that can help you get loans in the future.

4. Mobile Money Savings and Investment Options

Mobile money platforms offer savings and investment products that can help your brick business grow. For savings, many providers have a 'Savings' or 'M-Kesho' account linked to your mobile wallet. For example, M-Pesa has M-Shwari in Kenya, where you can save as little as 10 KES and earn interest (around 3-5% per year). You can lock funds for a fixed period to earn higher interest. To save, dial *334# and select 'M-Shwari', then 'Save'. You can also set up automatic savings: every day after sales, transfer 10% of your daily revenue to savings. For investments, some providers offer government securities or unit trusts via mobile. For instance, in Kenya, you can buy M-Akiba bonds via M-Pesa. In Ghana, you can invest in treasury bills via MTN Mobile Money. These typically require a minimum of 100 GHS and offer returns of 10-15% per year. Another option is to join a village savings and loan association (VSLA) that uses mobile money to pool funds. You can also invest in your own business: use mobile money to buy a brick-making machine or extra moulds. For long-term savings, consider a mobile money pension product like Mbao Pension Plan in Kenya, where you save as little as 20 KES per day. Always keep an emergency fund of at least 3 months' expenses in an accessible savings account. Avoid keeping large amounts in your mobile wallet because it may not earn interest and is vulnerable to fraud. Move excess funds to a bank or savings account regularly. With consistent saving, you can accumulate enough to expand your brick yard or buy a truck.

5. Mobile Money Loans and Overdrafts

Mobile money providers offer loans and overdrafts based on your transaction history. For example, M-Shwari in Kenya offers loans from 100 KES to 500,000 KES with a one-time facilitation fee of 7.5% and daily interest of 0.5% (about 180% APR). To qualify, you need to use M-Pesa regularly for at least 6 months. The loan limit grows as you save and repay on time. In Ghana, MTN offers 'MTN Qwik Loan' with limits up to 10,000 GHS. In Tanzania, Tigo offers 'Tigo Nivushe' up to 1,000,000 TZS. To access a loan, dial the provider's code (e.g., *334# for M-Shwari) and select 'Loan'. The money is deposited into your mobile wallet instantly. You can use it to buy raw materials, pay labour, or invest in equipment. Repayment is automatic: when you receive money in your wallet, the loan is deducted first. Overdrafts are also available: you can spend more than your wallet balance up to a limit, and repay with a fee. For example, M-Pesa has 'Fuliza' overdraft in Kenya, which charges 1% of the amount borrowed per day. Use loans wisely: only borrow for business needs that will generate income. Avoid borrowing to pay for personal expenses. Before taking a loan, calculate if the expected profit from the bricks covers the loan fee and interest. For instance, if you borrow 20,000 KES for 30 days, the fee is 1,500 KES (7.5%) plus daily interest, so total repayment could be around 23,000 KES. Ensure you can sell enough bricks to cover that. Always repay on time to increase your credit limit and avoid penalties. Keep a record of all loans and repayments.

6. Security and Fraud Prevention in Mobile Money

Mobile money fraud is common, but you can protect yourself. First, never share your PIN with anyone, not even a provider's agent or customer care. Your PIN is your signature. Second, beware of SIM swap fraud: fraudsters may call pretending to be from your provider and ask for your ID number or PIN to 'upgrade' your account. Hang up and call the official customer care line. Third, use a strong PIN (not 1234 or your birth year) and change it regularly. Fourth, when receiving payments, always check the SMS or app notification yourself; don't rely on the customer's phone screen. Fifth, for large transactions, confirm the sender's name and transaction ID. Sixth, avoid using public Wi-Fi for mobile money transactions; use your mobile data. Seventh, keep your phone and SIM safe; if you lose your phone, report to your provider immediately to block your line. Eighth, register for a mobile money account in your own name with your ID; don't use someone else's account. Ninth, be cautious of overpayment scams: a customer may send more money than agreed and ask for a refund; the original payment may be reversed later. Always wait for the transaction to settle before refunding. Tenth, educate your employees about fraud and set clear rules: only you or a trusted manager should handle mobile money. Finally, keep records of all transactions and reconcile daily. If you suspect fraud, report to the provider and local police. Many providers have a fraud hotline (e.g., M-Pesa 100 in Kenya). By following these steps, you can protect your brick business's money.

Practical Reference: Mobile Money Integration for Brick Businesses

This practical reference consolidates the operational essentials from the module Mobile Money Integration for Brick Businesses. It is written for brick producers, yard managers and small agribusiness entrepreneurs in Africa who want to move cash-heavy operations onto mobile money rails without losing control of margins, records or security. Use it as a desk reference, a training handout, or a checklist before you open a merchant account, pay a supplier, or take a loan.

Mobile money is now the default payment layer for millions of small businesses across Sub-Saharan Africa. According to the GSMA State of the Industry Report on Mobile Money 2024, the region accounted for roughly 1.1 billion registered mobile money accounts and about $912 billion in transaction value in 2023, with merchant payments one of the fastest-growing use cases. For a brick business â where a single order can be a few hundred or several thousand bricks â mobile money reduces cash-handling risk, speeds up settlement, and creates a transaction trail you can use to qualify for credit.

1. Setting Up a Mobile Money Merchant Account

Most brick businesses start with a personal wallet and quickly outgrow it. Merchant accounts (also called "till," "paybill," "merchant number" or "business wallet" depending on the country and provider) separate business funds from personal funds, give you a printed or digital receipt trail, and usually reduce transaction fees at higher volumes.

To open one you will typically need: national ID or passport, business registration certificate (or a trading licence), a physical address or GPS location of the yard, and a phone number registered in the business name. In Kenya, for example, Safaricom M-PESA requires a valid business permit and ID for a Paybill or Till number; in Ghana, MTN MoMo merchant accounts require a Ghana Card and business registration; in Nigeria, providers such as OPay, PalmPay and Moniepoint require a CAC certificate and BVN.

2. Receiving Customer Payments via Mobile Money

Brick buyers â contractors, developers, homeowners, hardware shops â increasingly prefer mobile money because it removes the need to carry cash to a rural yard. A merchant account gives you three practical benefits:

  • Instant confirmation: the customer receives an SMS and you receive a merchant notification, which reduces disputes about "I paid" versus "I did not receive."
  • Automatic receipts: most merchant accounts can issue digital receipts, which helps with VAT and tax filing.
  • Reconciliation: a dedicated merchant number keeps business income separate from personal spending.

Practical tip: display your merchant number, QR code and USSD string clearly at the yard gate, on the delivery truck, and on invoices. For bulk orders, ask for a 50% deposit via mobile money before loading bricks, and the balance on delivery. This protects you against default and gives you working capital to buy clay and fuel.

3. Sending Payments to Suppliers and Employees

Paying suppliers and workers by mobile money is faster and safer than cash, especially when your clay supplier or casual labourers are in different towns. Use bulk payment or "send to many" features where your provider offers them. Keep a simple payment register: date, payee, phone number, amount, purpose, and the mobile money transaction code. This register becomes your evidence when applying for a loan.

4. Mobile Money Savings and Investment Options

Many providers now offer wallet-linked savings products â M-Shwari (Kenya), MoMo Save (Ghana), M-Pesa Savings (Tanzania), and similar. Interest rates are usually modest (often 2%â6% per year), but the real value for a brick business is discipline: locking away a portion of each sale so you are not tempted to spend it on non-business items. Some providers also offer target savings for equipment (e.g., a brick moulding machine) or group savings for cooperatives.

For longer-term investment, consider treasury bills or money market funds accessed through mobile-linked investment accounts, or chama/SACCO group savings. Always compare the return against the cost of borrowing â if a mobile money loan costs 3% per month and savings earn 0.4% per month, paying down the loan is the better "investment."

5. Mobile Money Loans and Overdrafts

Mobile money transaction history is now a credit score. Providers such as M-Shwari, KCB M-Pesa, Fuliza, MoMo Loan, and Airtel Money loans use your wallet activity to offer small, short-term loans and overdrafts. For a brick business, these are useful for bridging gaps: buying clay before a big order, paying wages before a customer settles, or repairing a kiln.

However, the cost is high. Annualised interest rates on mobile money loans frequently exceed 30%â100%, and overdraft facility fees (like Fuliza's daily fee) can quietly erode margins. Rule of thumb: use mobile money credit only for short, self-liquidating needs â a loan that will be repaid by a specific confirmed order â not for long-term equipment or operating losses.

6. Security and Fraud Prevention in Mobile Money

Common frauds affecting brick businesses include: fake payment SMS ("I have sent the money" when no funds arrived), SIM-swap attacks, social engineering calls, and dishonest employees who share the merchant PIN. Practical controls:

  • Verify every incoming payment in your own wallet or merchant portal before releasing bricks.
  • Use a separate merchant PIN known only to the owner or a trusted manager; change it regularly.
  • Register for transaction alerts and review statements weekly.
  • Never click links in SMS or WhatsApp claiming to be from your provider.
  • Keep a written or digital ledger of all mobile money transactions â this is your defence if a dispute arises.
