Words ending in -/- are feminine (, ). Words ending in -/- are neuter (, ). This helps you use correct pronouns. For example, ' . .' (This is bread. It is fresh.) Practice with a friend: one says a word, the other gives the gender. Do this for 15 minutes daily. Within a month, you'll be confident.

4. Russian Cuisine and Traditional Dishes

Russian cuisine is hearty and uses simple ingredients. Start with soups: (borscht) is beetroot soup, often with cabbage, potatoes, and meat. Served with sour cream ''. (shchi) is cabbage soup. (ukha) is fish soup. For main dishes, (pelmeni) are dumplings filled with meat, eaten with butter or sour cream. (vareniki) are similar but often filled with potato, cheese, or cherries. (blini) are thin pancakes, served with caviar, jam, or sour cream. (pirozhki) are stuffed buns. (kasha) is porridge, made from buckwheat, rice, or oats. For protein, (shashlik) is marinated meat grilled on skewers. (plov) is rice pilaf with meat and carrots. (herring under a fur coat) is a layered salad with herring, potatoes, carrots, beets, and mayonnaise. For drinks, (kompot) is fruit juice, (kvas) is fermented bread drink, and (tea) is common. Vodka '' is popular but strong. In African context, you can adapt: use local ingredients. For borscht, use beetroot and cabbage available in your market. For pelmeni, use ground beef or chicken. For shashlik, use goat or beef. Host a Russian dinner: make borscht, pelmeni, and blini. Invite friends. Practice saying the names in Russian. For example, ' . .' (This is borscht. It is tasty.) Learn adjectives: (tasty), (sweet), (salty), (spicy), (sour). Say ' ' (I like borscht). Watch YouTube cooking videos: 'Russian borscht recipe', 'how to make pelmeni'. Follow along. Write down ingredients in Russian. After cooking, describe the dish: ' .' (Borscht is red and tasty.) This lesson connects language to culture. You'll not only speak but also cook. Do this once a week. Within a month, you'll know 10 traditional dishes and their names.
