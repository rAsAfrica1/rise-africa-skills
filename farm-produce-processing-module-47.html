Marketing & Branding

8 lessons · 30 quiz questions · Business

1 Naming your food brand

Your brand name is the first thing customers see. Good names are memorable, easy to pronounce, and hint at what you do.

Good names: Short (1–3 words), easy to spell, distinctive (not generic like "Best Foods").

Ideas:

  • Place-based — "Harare Harvest", "Kano Kitchens"
  • Family names — "Moyo & Sons"
  • Product-based — "Golden Groundnut"
  • Made-up words — "Zuri", "Nala"

Check before committing:

  • Google the name — someone else using it?
  • Check domain name available (.com or local)
  • Check trademark registry — don't infringe
  • Check social handles available

Register the trademark once you're serious. Protects your brand from copycats.

2 Logo and packaging design

Your logo and packaging are how you stand out on a crowded shelf.

Logo principles:

  • Simple — works on a tiny label and a billboard
  • Scalable — clean vector file (.svg, .ai, .eps)
  • Recognisable at a glance
  • 2–3 colours maximum

Packaging design:

  • Clearly visible product name (customers scan for 3 seconds)
  • Front shows: brand name, product, weight, key benefit
  • Consistent look across your range (same font, colours)
  • Photography — real product photos, not stock
  • Space for legal label info on back

Hire a designer for $100–$500. Worth every cent. Bad design costs you sales forever.

💡 Print 500 units of any new packaging — never 10,000 until you test the design in the market.

3 Photos and product styling

Photography sells food. Phone photos with good light beat expensive photos with bad light.

How to take great product photos:

  • Natural light near a window — never flash
  • Clean white or textured background
  • Photo from multiple angles: front, side, in-use
  • Show product in a setting (jar of peanut butter on toast)
  • Show scale — hand or person using product
  • Edit lightly — brighten, sharpen, crop

Take 20 photos, use the best 3. Consistent style across your range builds brand recognition.

4 Social media content ideas

Social media costs nothing but time. It builds trust and keeps you top-of-mind.

10 posts to rotate:

  1. Product photo with price
  2. Behind-the-scenes in the factory
  3. Customer testimonial
  4. Farmer partner spotlight
  5. Recipe using your product
  6. Special offer or discount
  7. Question to engage ("What's your favourite way to use peanut butter?")
  8. Education — health benefits of your product
  9. New batch announcement
  10. Team spotlight

Post 3–5 times a week. Reply to every comment and message. Consistency beats perfection.

5 WhatsApp catalogue and ordering

In Africa, WhatsApp is the #1 business tool. Set it up properly.

WhatsApp Business (free app):

  • Business profile — name, hours, address, website
  • Catalogue feature — up to 500 products with photos and prices
  • Auto-replies — greet new customers, out-of-hours message
  • Quick replies — save common answers as templates
  • Labels — organise chats (new order, paid, delivered)
  • Away message — for times you're not available

Broadcast lists let you message many customers at once (up to 256 per list). Use for new product announcements, but don't spam — 2–3 messages a week maximum.

6 Local radio, newspaper and events

Traditional media still works in Africa — often cheaper than you think.

Radio: Community and local stations cost $20–$200 for a 30-second ad. Best for reaching older and rural customers.

Newspaper: Adverts $50–$500 depending on circulation. Best for professional credibility.

Events:

  • Farmers' markets — sell, sample, get feedback
  • Agricultural shows — booth with free samples
  • Trade fairs — meet wholesalers and distributors
  • Church and community events — loyal local customers

Sponsor a local event — football team, school, church function. Cheap advertising and community goodwill.

7 Customer reviews and referrals

Word of mouth is the strongest marketing. Actively generate it.

Ask for reviews: After delivery, message: "Thanks for your order! If you're happy, would you mind posting a photo or sharing with friends?"

Referral rewards: "Refer a friend — get $1 off your next order." Both referrer and new customer benefit.

Collect testimonials: Ask happy customers for a 1-line quote and photo. Use on packaging, website, social media.

Reviews build trust. A single authentic customer photo can outsell any branded image.

💡 Reply to negative reviews quickly and professionally. How you handle a complaint shows your character — often wins back the customer.

8 Building brand reputation

Brand is what customers say about you when you're not in the room. Build it deliberately.

What creates a strong brand:

  • Consistency — same quality every batch, same voice every post
  • Transparency — show how products are made, who makes them
  • Trust — never compromise on food safety
  • Story — why did you start? What problem are you solving?
  • Community — support local farmers, give back

Strong brands can charge 20–50% more than generic competitors. Worth the effort.

💰 Marketing doesn't need a big budget. It needs consistency. Post daily, be present, be helpful. That beats any paid ad.
